# Lateral Femoral Condyle

The shape of the lateral condyle and the part it plays in knee motion,
oriented rather than analysed, with the kinematics left to Section 3.

## For Medical Students

### Structure and Location

The lateral condyle is flatter in the sagittal plane than the medial and
broader from side to side. It projects further anteriorly, which is why the
lateral trochlear facet stands higher than the medial.

Its outer wall carries the lateral epicondyle, and below and behind that a
groove, the popliteal sulcus, in which the popliteus tendon lies. Its inner
wall forms the lateral wall of the intercondylar notch.

The articular surface carries a shallow groove across it, the lateral condylar
notch, marking the line where the meniscus sits in extension.

### Relations

Laterally, outside the capsule: the lateral collateral ligament from the
epicondyle, the lateral head of gastrocnemius from above and behind the
condyle, and the lateral intermuscular septum along the shaft.

The popliteus tendon lies in its sulcus inside the capsule, passing from its
origin forward of the collateral down and back to the muscle belly.

Inside the notch, the medial surface of this condyle carries the femoral
attachment of the anterior cruciate ligament.

The common peroneal nerve is not a relation of the condyle itself. It lies
further distally and laterally, around the fibular neck.

### Function

Because the lateral condyle is flatter in profile, the lateral side of the
joint translates further through flexion than the medial. The combination of a
travelling lateral side and a pivoting medial side produces rotation of the
tibia as the knee bends and straightens.

Its anterior projection gives the patella a lateral wall to work against,
which is the main bony restraint to lateral patellar translation.

### Clinical Relevance

The lateral epicondyle locates the collateral origin, and the relationship
between that origin and the popliteus origin is the key to the posterolateral
corner.

The anterior cruciate attaches on the inner wall of this condyle, so the
lateral condyle is the bone that the femoral side of a reconstruction is
placed in.

### Key Learning Points

- The lateral condyle is flatter, broader and projects further anteriorly than the medial.
- Its inner wall carries the femoral attachment of the anterior cruciate ligament.
- The popliteus tendon lies in a sulcus on its outer wall, inside the capsule.
- A flatter lateral condyle lets the lateral side travel, which produces rotation with flexion.

## MRCS Level

### Structure and Location

Name three things on the outer wall. The lateral epicondyle carries the
collateral origin. The popliteal sulcus, anterior and distal to it, carries
the popliteus origin. The supracondylar area above and behind carries the
lateral head of gastrocnemius.

The order of the first two is the point: the collateral origin is proximal and
posterior to the popliteus origin, and the two tendons therefore cross as they
run distally.

On the articular surface, the shallow transverse groove marks the extension
position of the lateral meniscus and is a normal feature rather than a defect.

The inner wall is flat, slightly concave, and faces the medial condyle across
the notch. The anterior cruciate attachment sits on its posterior part, close
to the articular margin.

### Relations

The posterolateral corner structures all take their bearings from this
condyle: the collateral ligament, the popliteus and its popliteofibular
extension, and the arcuate complex.

The capsule attaches around the articular margin and the popliteus tendon
enters the joint through a hiatus in it, so the tendon is intra-articular but
extrasynovial along part of its course.

The lateral geniculate vessels run on the capsule at this level, with the
superior lateral genicular artery passing above the condyle deep to the
iliotibial band.

### Blood Supply and Innervation

The lateral condylar epiphysis is supplied by the superior lateral and
inferior lateral genicular arteries, with the descending branch of the lateral
circumflex femoral contributing above.

The notch surface and the anterior cruciate attachment are supplied by the
middle genicular artery, which is the vessel to name for any intra-articular
structure here.

Articular innervation is from the common peroneal and tibial nerves
posterolaterally and the femoral nerve anteriorly. The recurrent branch of the
common peroneal supplies the anterolateral capsule.

### Function

The lateral compartment is the less constrained of the two: a flatter femoral
surface on a convex tibial surface with a mobile meniscus. It permits
translation and it depends more on soft tissue restraint for it.

In extension the anterior projection of the condyle resists lateral patellar
translation, and that restraint is lost as the patella leaves the trochlea in
flexion. 3.13 and Section 3 own the kinematic account.

### Clinical Relevance

The femoral attachment of the anterior cruciate is on this condyle, which
makes its inner wall the most studied surface in the knee. 2.7.6 owns the
footprint.

The collateral and popliteus origins are the two fixed points for
posterolateral corner reconstruction, and their relative positions are what
makes the reconstruction anatomical or not.

An impression on the articular surface of the lateral condyle is a recognised
finding after a pivoting injury, and distinguishing it from the normal
transverse groove matters. The finding belongs to Section 5; the normal groove
belongs here.

The superior lateral genicular artery lies deep to the iliotibial band above
the condyle and is encountered in lateral approaches.

### Key Learning Points

- The collateral origin is proximal and posterior to the popliteus origin, so the tendons cross.
- The anterior cruciate attaches to the posterior part of the inner wall, near the articular margin.
- The lateral compartment is less constrained: a flat femur on a convex tibia with a mobile meniscus.
- The transverse groove on the lateral articular surface is normal, not a defect.
- The superior lateral genicular artery runs deep to the iliotibial band above the condyle.

## FRCS Level

### Relations

The posterolateral corner is the region where the greatest number of named
structures attach in the smallest area, and all of them refer to this condyle.
Reconstruction there is a matter of restoring two or three fixed points rather
than reproducing every structure.

The popliteus hiatus in the capsule is the route by which the tendon becomes
intra-articular, and it is also the route by which a lateral meniscal tear can
extend into a region with no meniscal attachment.

### Function

Lateral condylar geometry is the reason the lateral compartment can be left
more mobile than the medial in a reconstructed or replaced knee without the
same penalty.

It is also why lateral restraint is soft tissue dependent. Removing or failing
to restore the lateral structures has a larger effect than the equivalent on
the medial side, because the bone was never doing the work.

### Clinical Relevance

Anterolateral rotational control is a live argument, and the anatomy
underneath it is the relationship between the lateral condyle, the capsule,
and the iliotibial band's deep fibres. The anatomical description belongs
here; whether an added lateral procedure helps is an evidence question owned
by Sections 6 and 7.

In a lateral approach, the structures at risk in order of depth are the
iliotibial band, the superior lateral genicular artery, the capsule, and then
the popliteus tendon. Knowing the order is what makes the exposure deliberate.

The femoral tunnel for anterior cruciate reconstruction is drilled through the
inner wall and exits on the outer surface, so the condyle's width and the
position of the lateral structures both constrain the trajectory.

### Key Learning Points

- Posterolateral reconstruction restores two or three fixed points rather than every structure.
- Lateral restraint is soft tissue dependent, so failing to restore it costs more than on the medial side.
- The popliteus hiatus is both the tendon's route into the joint and a route for a tear to extend.
- In a lateral approach the order of depth is band, superior lateral genicular artery, capsule, popliteus.
