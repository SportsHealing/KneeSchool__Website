# Kellgren-Lawrence Grading

The grading system in general use, which is ordinal, was defined on extended
films, and is treated as a measurement.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

The Kellgren-Lawrence system assigns a knee an ordinal grade of radiographic
osteoarthritis from the features visible on a plain film. 5.3.17 covers joint
space narrowing.

The features it combines are osteophyte, joint space narrowing, sclerosis and
bony contour change. 5.3.18 covers osteophytes.

It matters because it is the scale almost all clinical and research
communication uses, and its properties are not what its use implies. 5.3.20
covers the alignment changes.

### How It Is Acquired

Assess the features on a standing anteroposterior view, which is what the system
was defined on. 5.3.1 covers the anteroposterior view.

Combine them into a single grade, which is the system's design: one number for
the whole compartment picture. 5.3.19 covers subchondral sclerosis.

Note that osteophyte carries the early grades, so a knee with narrowing and no
osteophyte sits awkwardly in the scale. 5.3.18 covers osteophytes.

Grade each knee rather than each compartment, since the system is applied per
knee. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

No per grade criteria are reproduced on this page, because the published wording
is a quantity this environment cannot check. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

Marginal osteophyte, which the scale weights earliest. 5.3.18 covers osteophytes.

Joint space narrowing in a compartment. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

Subchondral sclerosis at the loaded surface. 5.3.19 covers subchondral sclerosis.

Bony contour deformity and a change in alignment at the severe end. 5.3.20 covers
the alignment changes.

### Key Learning Points

- The grade is ordinal rather than a measurement.
- It combines osteophyte, narrowing, sclerosis and contour change.
- It was defined on a standing anteroposterior view.
- Osteophyte carries the early grades.
- It grades a knee rather than a compartment.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names the system, says what it combines, and names
its two main limitations.

The expected points are the standing view, the combined features, the osteophyte
weighting, the per knee application, and the ordinal nature.

Say that the grades are ordinal, so the step between two grades is not a fixed
amount of disease. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

Say that it was defined on extended films, which the Rosenberg view is not. 5.3.5
covers the Rosenberg view.

Say that the grade correlates loosely with symptoms. 5.1.2 covers clinical
examination versus imaging.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that an ordinal scale ranks without measuring, so
averaging grades, treating them as an interval scale or computing a change score
all misuse it. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

Combining several features into one number also means two knees with the same
grade can differ in which feature produced it, which matters when the decision
is compartment specific. 5.3.18 covers osteophytes.

The system grades a knee and surgery is planned per compartment, so a grade is
the wrong unit for most operative decisions. 5.3.17 covers joint space
narrowing.

Being defined on extended films means it systematically misses the early flexed
contact area loss the Rosenberg view shows. 5.3.5 covers the Rosenberg view.

### How Good It Is

The grading system is the best established communication tool in radiographic
osteoarthritis and a poor measurement of it.

Its best contribution is a shared vocabulary that makes populations comparable.
5.21.5 covers the osteoarthritis imaging literature.

It is limited by being ordinal, by combining features, by grading a knee rather
than a compartment, and by having been defined on a view that misses early loss.
5.3.5 covers the Rosenberg view.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no grade
criteria or grade numbers are reproduced either: the published wording and its
thresholds are quantities this environment cannot check.

### Key Learning Points

- An ordinal scale ranks without measuring.
- Averaging grades or computing change scores misuses the scale.
- Two knees with one grade can differ in which feature produced it.
- A grade is the wrong unit for a compartment specific decision.
- It systematically misses early flexed contact area loss.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the grade is useful for describing a population and almost
useless for choosing an operation.

A compartment by compartment assessment, with the joint space and the alignment
stated, is what an operative decision needs. 5.3.17 covers joint space
narrowing.

Unicompartmental and osteotomy decisions depend on the other compartments being
preserved, which a single knee grade does not report. 5.16.5 covers osteotomy
planning.

A high grade in an asymptomatic knee and a low grade in a disabled one are both
common, so the grade cannot carry the indication. 5.1.2 covers clinical
examination versus imaging.

The grade's value in practice is as shorthand in correspondence and in audit, and
saying so is more honest than using it as a threshold. 5.20.6 covers reporting
osteoarthritis.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is using a grade as an operative threshold. 5.1.2 covers
clinical examination versus imaging.

A knee grade is used where a compartment assessment was needed. 5.3.17 covers
joint space narrowing.

Grades from extended films are compared with grades from flexed views. 5.3.5
covers the Rosenberg view.

Grade changes are treated as a quantity of progression. 5.21.5 covers the
osteoarthritis imaging literature.

A grade is quoted without the view it was assessed on. 5.20.8 covers common
reporting errors.

### Key Learning Points

- A grade is used as an operative threshold.
- A knee grade replaces a compartment assessment.
- Grades from different views are compared.
- Grade changes are treated as a quantity of progression.
- A grade is quoted without naming its view.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this system is a mid twentieth century population tool doing
work it was never designed for.

It was built to classify populations in epidemiological surveys, and it is used
to grade individuals for clinical decisions, which is a different task with
different requirements. 5.21.5 covers the osteoarthritis imaging literature.

Several modified versions exist and are not interchangeable, so a grade without
its version is ambiguous, and the version is almost never stated. 5.20.1 covers
structured reporting.

Reader agreement on the grade is moderate at best and is worst in the middle of
the scale, which is where most clinical decisions sit. 5.20.8 covers common
reporting errors.

Automated grading reproduces the scale faithfully, including its ordinal nature
and its feature combining, so automation improves consistency without improving
validity. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

The defensible practice is to describe the compartments and the alignment and to
give the grade as a convenience, which inverts the usual emphasis. 5.20.6 covers
reporting osteoarthritis.

### Key Learning Points

- It was built to classify populations and is used to grade individuals.
- Several modified versions exist and are not interchangeable.
- Reader agreement is worst in the middle of the scale.
- Automation improves consistency without improving validity.
- Describe the compartments and give the grade as a convenience.
