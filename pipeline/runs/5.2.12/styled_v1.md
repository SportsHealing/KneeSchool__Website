# Basic Principles

How a magnetic resonance image is made, kept to what changes the reading of a
knee.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

Hydrogen nuclei in the body behave like small magnets, and a strong static field
aligns a small excess of them. 5.2.13 covers T1 imaging.

A radiofrequency pulse tips that alignment, and the signal is detected as it
returns, which is where the image comes from. 5.2.14 covers T2 imaging.

It matters because the signal depends on the tissue's local environment rather
than on its density, which is why soft tissue contrast is so much better than a
radiograph's. 5.2.2 covers the image formation.

### How It Is Acquired

The static field strength is quoted in tesla, and routine musculoskeletal
imaging runs at 1.5 tesla or 3 tesla. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Gradients are applied so that position can be encoded, which is what makes a
slice a slice. 5.2.18 covers gradient echo imaging.

The sequence parameters decide which tissue property dominates the contrast,
which is the subject of the next several pages. 5.2.15 covers proton density
imaging.

Signal rises with field strength, which is traded for more artefact rather than
taken as free resolution. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

Nothing ionising is involved, so the limits are time, cost, implants and patient
tolerance rather than dose. 5.2.3 covers the radiation dose.

### Key Learning Points

- Hydrogen nuclei align in a strong static field.
- A radiofrequency pulse tips them and the returning signal forms the image.
- The signal depends on local environment rather than density.
- Routine musculoskeletal imaging runs at 1.5 or 3 tesla.
- The limits are time, cost, implants and tolerance rather than dose.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer explains why magnetic resonance imaging separates
soft tissues and a radiograph does not.

The expected points are the static field, the radiofrequency pulse, the
gradients, the sequence parameters, and the absence of ionising radiation.

Say that the contrast is chosen by the sequence rather than fixed by the tissue.
5.2.15 covers proton density imaging.

Say that a higher field gives more signal and more artefact. 5.2.19 covers
artefacts.

Say that metal is the practical limit in a post-operative knee. 5.5.3 covers the
post-operative protocol.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging is the best single modality for the knee's internal
structures and the slowest and most expensive of them.

Its best contribution is that one study answers meniscal, ligamentous,
chondral and marrow questions together. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

It is limited by time, by motion, by metal, and by being supine and unloaded.
5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028. The field
strengths above are the chapter's only measurements, they are registered under
decision 006, and no sequence timing values appear anywhere in the chapter.

### Key Learning Points

- Contrast is chosen by the sequence rather than fixed by the tissue.
- A higher field gives more signal and more artefact.
- Metal is the practical limit in a post-operative knee.
- One study answers meniscal, ligamentous, chondral and marrow questions.
- The study is supine and unloaded.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the physics explains which knee questions a magnetic resonance
study settles and which it only describes.

A structural question about an internal soft tissue is settled here better than
anywhere. 5.6.1 covers the normal meniscus.

A mechanical question is described rather than settled, because the knee is
supine, unloaded and still. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

A question about bone architecture or alignment is answered better by a
radiograph or by computed tomography. 5.3.7 covers the mechanical axis.

A metal bearing knee limits the study, and the protocol rather than the field
strength is what rescues it. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative protocol.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reading a structural abnormality as the cause of a
mechanical complaint. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

The supine unloaded position is forgotten and an appearance is treated as the
standing state. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

A higher field study is assumed to be a better study. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

Signal is read as a tissue property when it is a sequence property. 5.2.15
covers proton density imaging.

An incidental finding is reported with the same weight as a relevant one. 5.20.1
covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- A structural abnormality is read as the cause of a mechanical complaint.
- The supine unloaded position is forgotten.
- A higher field study is assumed to be a better study.
- Signal is read as a tissue property when it is a sequence property.
- Incidental and relevant findings are reported with equal weight.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the gap between what the physics measures and what the
clinician wants is the recurring theme of this whole section.

Every sequence measures a property of water in tissue, and every clinical
question is about mechanics, which means the connection is always an inference.
3.5.1 covers the contact areas.

That inference is strongest for continuity and weakest for function, which is
why this section's pages on grafts, healing and repair all reach the same
conclusion. 5.7.8 covers cruciate healing.

Field strength increases have delivered resolution rather than new answers,
because the limiting factor was never signal. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic
resonance techniques.

Quantitative sequences attempt to measure tissue composition directly, and
composition is still not mechanics. 5.18.1 covers quantitative magnetic
resonance imaging.

The honest framing is that this modality shows structure superbly and infers
function, and a report that distinguishes the two is more useful than one that
does not. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- Every sequence measures a property of water and every question is mechanical.
- The inference is strongest for continuity and weakest for function.
- Field strength has delivered resolution rather than new answers.
- Composition is not mechanics.
- A report that separates structure from inference is more useful.
