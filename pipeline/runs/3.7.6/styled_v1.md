# PL Bundle Function

The bundle that works near extension. Shorter, more horizontal, and the one
that matters most for rotational control in the position injuries happen in.

## For Medical Students

### The Principle

A rope attached further from the axis of a hinge changes length more as the
hinge moves. Its behaviour is therefore more dramatic across the range.

A shorter rope also strains more for the same stretch, because strain is the
stretch divided by the original length.

So a short rope attached far from the axis is tight over a narrow part of the
range and highly strained when it is.

### At the Knee

The posterolateral bundle is named for its tibial attachment, which sits
posteriorly and laterally in the footprint. 2.7.9 covers the anatomy.

Its femoral origin is the more distal and posterior of the two, which puts it
further from the flexion axis in extension.

Conventional teaching has it tightening as the knee extends, with maximal
tension near full extension.

It is therefore the bundle controlling the knee in the position where pivoting
injuries occur, which is near extension with the knee rotating. 3.7.14 covers
mechanisms.

### What Changes It

Flexion angle, which it responds to more sharply than the other bundle.

Rotation, where it contributes most near extension. 3.7.7 covers rotation.

Partial injury affecting this bundle, which leaves rotational laxity with a
near normal test in flexion.

Tunnel position in a reconstruction, since reproducing this behaviour is the
harder half.

### Clinical Relevance

A knee lax on rotational testing and near normal on translation testing in
flexion fits an injury affecting this bundle. Section 4 covers the tests.

Testing at 90 degrees samples the other bundle, so this one can be injured and
missed.

The pivot shift tests this bundle's territory, which is why it is the more
informative test in a pivoting athlete. 3.7.9 covers the pivot shift.

This is the bundle a single graft reproduces less well, which matters for the
symptom patients complain of.

### Key Learning Points

- A rope further from the axis changes length more across the range.
- A shorter rope strains more for the same stretch.
- The posterolateral bundle attaches posteriorly and laterally on the tibia.
- Conventional teaching gives it maximal tension near full extension.
- It controls the knee in the position pivoting injuries occur in.

## MRCS Level

### The Principle

This bundle's length change through the range is larger than the anteromedial
bundle's, so it is further from isometric and more sensitive to attachment
position.

Sensitivity to position cuts both ways. It is harder to reproduce in a
reconstruction and more informative about function when it is intact.

Strain rather than force is the quantity to compare between bundles, because
the bundles differ in length and cross sectional area. 3.7.10 covers strain.

### At the Knee

Conventional teaching gives the posterolateral bundle maximal tension at or
near full extension, slackening progressively with flexion, which is the
reciprocal of the anteromedial pattern.

In rotation it carries a larger share of the restraint near extension, which
is why rotational laxity is assessed near extension. 3.7.7 covers rotation.

Its contribution is why an anterior drawer at 90 degrees can be near normal in
a knee with a demonstrable pivot shift.

It is shorter and its fibres are more obliquely oriented, so for a given
tibial displacement it strains more than the anteromedial bundle.

### What Changes It

Flexion angle, more sharply than for the other bundle.

Rotation and combined loading, where its share is highest near extension.

Attachment position, to which it is more sensitive.

Partial rupture, where its isolated injury produces rotational rather than
translational findings.

### How It Is Measured

Selective sectioning with in situ force measurement apportions the restraint
between bundles at each angle.

Cadaveric strain measurement with the bundle isolated maps its length change
pattern.

Instrumented pivot shift measures the clinical consequence of its loss,
without an agreed threshold. 3.7.9 covers the test.

Isolated posterolateral bundle injury is uncommon, so much of the evidence is
from sectioning rather than from patients.

### Clinical Relevance

Assess rotation near extension, since that is where this bundle's share is
highest.

Expect a near normal drawer at 90 degrees in a knee with a positive pivot
shift.

Compare bundles by strain rather than by force, given their different
dimensions.

Treat isolated injury to this bundle as uncommon and the sectioning evidence
as the main source.

### Key Learning Points

- This bundle is further from isometric and more sensitive to attachment position.
- Conventional teaching: maximal tension near full extension, slackening with flexion.
- Its share of rotational restraint is highest near extension.
- It is shorter and more oblique, so it strains more for a given displacement.
- Compare bundles by strain rather than by force.

## FRCS Level

### At the Knee

Treat this bundle's behaviour as the half a reconstruction most often fails to
restore, and treat residual rotational laxity as the expected sign of it.

That is the mechanical content of the anterolateral augmentation argument. If
the graft does not restore the near extension rotational control, something
else can. 3.11 covers the constructs.

Conventional teaching holds that a more anatomic, lower and more posterior
femoral tunnel restores rotational control better than a high anteromedial
one, which is this bundle's territory being reproduced.

### What Changes It

Femoral tunnel position, to which this bundle's behaviour is most sensitive.

Whether a lateral augmentation was added.

Posterior tibial slope, which increases the demand this control has to meet.
3.13 covers alignment.

Graft diameter and footprint coverage.

### How It Is Measured

Robotic testing in rotation at low flexion angles is the measurement that
distinguishes constructs here.

Instrumented pivot shift measures the clinical result with no agreed
threshold.

Postoperative computed tomography locates the tunnel, which is the actionable
variable.

Reinjury and return to pivoting sport rates measure the consequence.

### Clinical Relevance

Place the femoral tunnel to reproduce near extension rotational control.

Consider lateral augmentation where rotational laxity is high or the knee is a
revision.

Measure slope, which raises the demand on this control.

Expect residual rotational laxity to be the sign that this behaviour was not
restored.

### Key Learning Points

- This is the half a reconstruction most often fails to restore.
- Residual rotational laxity is the expected sign of it.
- A lower, more posterior femoral tunnel reproduces this bundle's territory better.
- Robotic testing in rotation at low flexion distinguishes constructs here.
- Slope raises the demand on near extension rotational control.
