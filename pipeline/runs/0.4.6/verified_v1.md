# Gateway and Graduate Entry Routes

The routes into medicine that do not start with a school leaver applying
straight to a five year course, and who each one is designed for.

## For Young Learners

### What This Is

The standard route into medicine is a school leaver applying to a five or six
year degree. It is the most common route and it is not the only one.

Several alternatives exist, each designed for a different situation. Knowing
they exist at fifteen is more useful than discovering them at twenty five.

People reach medicine from very different starting points. Some miss the
grades. Some decide at twenty two. Some did not take the right subjects. Some
had a school that never mentioned the option.

None of those is the end of it, which is the point of this page. The routes
below all lead to the same registrable qualification.

### How It Works

Gateway or foundation year courses. A six year medical degree with an extra
preparatory year at the start. Usually restricted to applicants who meet
widening participation criteria, and usually with lower entry requirements
than the standard course at the same university.

Graduate entry medicine. A shortened course, often four years, for applicants
who already hold a degree. Some courses accept any degree subject and some
require a science one. Competition is high because the places are few.

Standard entry as a graduate. Applying to the normal five year course with a
degree already held. More places than graduate entry, and many applicants
overlook it.

Access to Higher Education diplomas. A one year qualification for adults
without the usual school qualifications, accepted by some medical schools.

Transfer and intercalation routes exist at a small number of universities and
are specific enough that only the university's own pages are worth reading.

### How to Prepare

Decide which description fits your situation before reading any requirements,
because the routes have very different criteria and mixing them up wastes
time.

For gateway courses, check the widening participation criteria first.
Eligibility is the gate, and grades come second.

For graduate entry, check whether the degree subject matters at each school
and whether the admissions test differs, because for some graduate courses it
does.

Apply across more than one route where you are eligible for more than one.

### Common Mistakes

Believing a foundation year is a lesser degree. The qualification at the end
is the same one.

Applying to graduate entry and ignoring standard entry, where there are far
more places.

Assuming a non science degree rules out graduate entry. At several schools it
does not.

Reading about these routes for the first time in the autumn of the application
year.

### Where to Find Out More

The Medical Schools Council lists which schools offer gateway courses,
graduate entry and Access course acceptance.

UCAS lists the courses and the requirements each university has supplied.

Each university publishes the eligibility criteria for its own gateway course,
and those criteria are the ones that decide it.

### Key Learning Points

- Gateway, graduate entry, standard entry as a graduate and Access routes all lead to the same qualification.
- Gateway courses usually require widening participation eligibility before grades are considered.
- Standard entry has far more places than graduate entry and graduates often overlook it.
- Several schools accept a non science degree for graduate entry.
- Eligibility criteria are published by each university and differ between them.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.
