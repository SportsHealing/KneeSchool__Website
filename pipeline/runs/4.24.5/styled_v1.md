# Functional Testing Literature

The work behind the hop batteries and the return to sport criteria, which
measures well and predicts poorly.

## For Medical Students

### What the Question Was

The question was how to decide when someone can return to sport after a knee
injury, since the ligament examination does not answer it. 4.21.7 covers return
to sport testing.

The first answers were time based, and the work that followed asked whether
performance could do better than a date. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision
making.

The question then became which performance tests, measured how, and against what
threshold. 4.21.2 covers the single leg hop.

### What It Showed

The work established that side to side performance can be measured
reproducibly, which is what made criteria possible.

It established that confidence is a separate and substantial limit on return,
which the physical tests do not capture. 4.22.6 covers the psychological
measure.

It did not establish that passing a battery prevents reinjury, which is the
question everyone wants answered. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

### Key Learning Points

- The ligament examination does not answer the return question.
- The first answers were time based.
- Side to side performance can be measured reproducibly.
- Confidence is a separate and substantial limit.
- Passing a battery has not been shown to prevent reinjury.

## MRCS Level

### What Was Done

Noyes, Barber and Mangine developed the hop tests and the limb symmetry approach
that the batteries are built on, which made a functional deficit a number.
4.21.2 covers the single leg hop.

Daniel and colleagues worked on instrumented laxity and on the relationship
between measured laxity and function, which established how loose that
relationship is. 4.2.2 covers instability.

Landing mechanics were characterised and then scored, which produced the
observational scoring systems. 4.21.6 covers the landing error scoring system.

Webster and Feller developed the psychological readiness measure, which added the
component the physical tests miss. 4.22.6 covers that measure.

Cohort studies followed athletes after reconstruction and related their test
results and their return decisions to reinjury.

### What It Showed

The cohort work established that reinjury rates after cruciate reconstruction in
young athletes are higher than the clinical impression suggested, which is the
finding that changed practice. 4.20.1 covers the reconstruction assessment.

It established that time since surgery and performance criteria both carry
information, and that neither substitutes for the other. 4.21.8 covers return to
play decision making.

It established that psychological readiness predicts actual return
independently, which is why the measure is in the battery. 4.22.6 covers that
measure.

It established that symmetry can be achieved by a bilaterally deconditioned
athlete, which is the systematic weakness of every paired comparison. 4.3.9
covers muscle wasting.

### Key Learning Points

- Hop tests and limb symmetry made a functional deficit a number.
- Measured laxity and function are loosely related.
- Reinjury rates in young athletes are higher than impression suggested.
- Time and performance criteria both carry information.
- Symmetry can be achieved by bilateral deconditioning.

## FRCS Level

### What It Showed

At decision level this literature produced criteria that structure a decision
without resolving it.

A battery identifies the limiting component, which makes advice specific, and
that is its demonstrated contribution. 4.21.7 covers return to sport testing.

Time remains an independent criterion, because graft maturation does not report
through performance. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

The relationship between criterion based clearance and reinjury is contested in
the literature, which means a clinician cannot promise what clearance buys.
4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

Age and activity level dominate reinjury risk in the cohorts, which puts the
largest effects outside anything the examination measures. 4.23.2 covers the
elite athlete.

### What It Changed

It replaced a date with a set of criteria, which is the clearest practical
change. 4.21.7 covers return to sport testing.

It raised the expected rehabilitation duration, because the criteria take longer
to meet than the traditional intervals. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision
making.

It added a psychological measure to an assessment that had been entirely
physical. 4.22.6 covers that measure.

It made the decision explicitly shared, because the residual risk is real and
belongs to the athlete. 4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

### Key Learning Points

- The battery's demonstrated contribution is identifying the limiting component.
- Time remains independent, because graft maturation is silent.
- What clearance buys is contested in the literature.
- Age and activity level dominate the risk.
- The decision became explicitly shared.

## Fellowship Level

### What It Changed

At fellowship level this literature is the most honest in the section about its
own limits, and reading it that way is the point.

Criteria describe a state at a moment and the cohorts describe risk over years,
so a battery result is not a forecast however many components it has. 4.21.8
covers return to play decision making.

Thresholds are conventions drawn from specific cohorts, so applying them to a
different athlete is an extrapolation whose size is unknown. 4.23.2 covers the
elite athlete.

Symmetry measured against a changed limb is the methodological weakness that
every test in the battery shares, and no amount of additional tests fixes it.
4.3.9 covers muscle wasting.

The planned, self initiated tasks in every battery differ from the unplanned
loading that injures knees, which is the gap the field has not closed. 4.21.4
covers the crossover hop.

Psychological readiness predicts return and the intervention for a low score is
less well established than the measurement, which is a familiar asymmetry.
4.22.6 covers that measure.

### What to Read Now

The cohort literature on reinjury after reconstruction is the reading that
changed practice, and it is the most important body of work behind chapter
4.21.

- Criteria describe a moment and cohorts describe years.

- Measurement of readiness is better established than the intervention for it.
