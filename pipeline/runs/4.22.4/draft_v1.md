# Tegner

A single activity level scale rather than a symptom score, used alongside the
others rather than in place of them.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

The Tegner scale places a patient on a graded list of activity levels, from
sedentary through recreational to competitive sport. 4.2.23 covers football.

It records what the patient does rather than how the knee feels, which is why it
is used alongside a symptom score. 4.22.3 covers the Lysholm score.

It matters because activity level is the context a symptom score is read in, and
the same score means different things at different activity levels. 4.22.8
covers the use of these scores.

### How It Is Performed

Ask the patient to identify their current level from the graded list rather than
describing their activity in their own words.

Ask them also to identify their level before the injury, since the drop is the
informative quantity.

Record both values with the date. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Record the sport and the position, because the same level covers different
demands. 4.2.25 covers rugby.

Record it alongside a symptom score rather than on its own. 4.22.1 covers the
IKDC.

Avoid converting the level into a percentage, since it is an ordered list rather
than a measurement.

Record the examination separately. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

### What a Positive Means

A current level and a pre injury level, with the difference between them, is the
result.

A large drop identifies a patient whose activity has changed, which a symptom
score may not show if they have adapted. 4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

No drop with a low symptom score identifies a patient continuing their sport
despite symptoms. 4.2.10 covers severity.

A return to the pre injury level is one of the criteria used in return to sport
work, and it is self reported. 4.21.7 covers return to sport testing.

A low pre injury level means the scale has little room to show change. 4.22.2
covers the KOOS.

### Key Learning Points

- The scale records activity rather than symptoms.
- The drop from the pre injury level is the informative quantity.
- It is used alongside a symptom score rather than instead of one.
- The scale is an ordered list, not a measurement.
- A low pre injury level leaves little room to show change.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva this is the activity scale to name, with the point that it is a
companion measure.

The expected points are the graded list, the current and pre injury levels, the
difference, the sport and position, the date, the pairing with a symptom score
and the separate examination record.

Say that you would record the pre injury level as well as the current one, and
give the reason. 4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

Say that it is an ordinal list and not a measurement. 4.22.8 covers the use of
these scores.

Say that it is used alongside a symptom score. 4.22.1 covers the IKDC.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that symptom scores are reported against the patient's
current activity, so the activity level is needed to interpret them. 4.22.8
covers the use of these scores.

A patient who has reduced their activity reports fewer symptoms, which is
adaptation rather than improvement. 4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

The scale's levels are ordered and unequally spaced, so arithmetic on them is not
meaningful. 4.2.10 covers severity.

Activity level is also a risk factor for the problem, which is why it is recorded
in a return to sport assessment. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

### How Good It Is

The scale is simple, quick and reproducible for a patient placing themselves on a
list.

Its best contribution is supplying the context a symptom score is read in, which
no symptom score provides for itself. 4.22.1 covers the IKDC.

It is coarse, so a meaningful change in activity can happen within one level.

No examination finding corresponds to it. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

### Key Learning Points

- Symptom scores are reported against the patient's current activity.
- Reduced activity with fewer symptoms is adaptation, not improvement.
- The levels are ordered and unequally spaced.
- Activity level is also a risk factor for the problem.
- The scale is coarse, so change can happen within one level.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the scale contributes the activity context and one of the
return to sport criteria.

A symptom score interpreted without an activity level is uninterpretable in an
active patient, and this scale supplies it. 4.22.1 covers the IKDC.

A return to the pre injury level is a criterion in return to sport work and it is
self reported rather than tested. 4.21.7 covers return to sport testing.

A patient who has dropped level and reports few symptoms has adapted, and
identifying that changes the conversation. 4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

Sport and position matter within a level, so they are recorded rather than
assumed. 4.2.25 covers rugby.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is recording the current level without the pre injury one.
4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

The levels are treated as an interval scale and averaged. 4.22.8 covers the use
of these scores.

The scale is used alone, as if it were a symptom measure. 4.22.1 covers the
IKDC.

A self reported return to level is treated as evidence of recovery. 4.21.8
covers return to play decision making.

The sport within a level is not recorded, so different demands are treated as
equal. 4.2.26 covers skiing.

### Key Learning Points

- Recording only the current level is the commonest error.
- The levels are not an interval scale and should not be averaged.
- The scale is not a symptom measure.
- A self reported return to level is not evidence of recovery.
- Different sports within a level carry different demands.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this scale's coarseness is both why it is usable and why it
answers less than it appears to.

A single ordered list compresses sport, frequency, intensity and position into
one value, so two patients at the same level are not doing the same thing.
4.2.23 covers football.

The pre injury level is recalled rather than recorded, and recall of activity
before an injury is systematically generous. 4.2.9 covers the onset.

The scale's levels were written for the sports of a particular time and place,
which affects how a current patient maps onto them. 4.22.8 covers the use of
these scores.

Returning to the same level is not the same as returning to the same performance,
and the scale does not distinguish them. 4.23.2 covers the elite athlete.

Activity level mediates most of the relationship between a knee problem and a
reported score, which makes it a confounder in every comparison that omits it.
4.22.1 covers the IKDC.

### Key Learning Points

- One ordered list compresses sport, frequency, intensity and position.
- The pre injury level is recalled and recall is systematically generous.
- The levels reflect the sports of a particular time and place.
- The same level is not the same performance.
- Activity level is a confounder in every comparison that omits it.
