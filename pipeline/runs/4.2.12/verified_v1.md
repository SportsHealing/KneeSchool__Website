# Relieving Factors

What settles it, how fast, and what the answer says about whether the problem
is mechanical, inflammatory or neither.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

Asking what relieves the pain tests how the problem behaves when the load is
removed, and that behaviour separates the main categories.

Mechanical pain settles with rest, usually within minutes to hours.

Inflammatory pain does not settle with rest and may be worse after it,
improving with gentle movement. 4.2.14 covers the pattern.

Pain that does not settle with anything is a different category and needs a
wider assessment.

### How It Is Performed

Ask what they do when it hurts, rather than offering rest, ice or tablets as
options.

Ask how long it takes to settle, in minutes or hours.

Ask whether analgesia helps, which, and how much, since a complete lack of
response to simple analgesia is informative.

Ask whether a brace, a stick, strapping or particular footwear helps, because
a positive answer points at mechanics and at a possible non-operative route.

Ask whether rest has been tried properly, since a patient who has never rested
the knee cannot say whether rest helps.

### What a Positive Means

Prompt relief with rest fits a mechanical or load-related problem.

Relief with gentle movement after stiffness fits degenerative or inflammatory
disease.

Relief with a brace points to instability or to a load that the brace
redistributes, and it is a useful pointer towards a mechanical cause that may
be correctable.

Relief with a stick or by avoiding one side suggests a load-related
compartment problem.

No relief from anything, particularly with night pain, should raise causes
outside the mechanical differential. 4.2.13 covers night pain.

### Key Learning Points

- Mechanical pain settles with rest; inflammatory pain does not and may worsen after it.
- Ask what they do when it hurts rather than offering the options.
- Relief with a brace points to a mechanical cause that may be correctable.
- A complete lack of response to simple analgesia is informative.
- No relief from anything, with night pain, points outside the mechanical differential.

## MRCS Level

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected here is that the time course of relief reflects the
mechanism.

Immediate relief on unloading suggests a load-dependent problem: contact
pressure, malalignment, or a weight bearing surface.

Relief over hours suggests a reactive component, because synovial fluid and
inflammatory mediators take time to settle. 4.8.1 covers normal fluid
physiology.

Relief over days after an episode suggests a structural event with a reactive
effusion rather than a simple overload.

Relief with anti-inflammatory medication specifically, rather than with
analgesia generally, supports an inflammatory or synovitic component.

### How Good It Is

Relieving factors perform well for category and poorly for structure, which is
the mirror image of aggravating factors.

Paired with aggravating factors they are among the better discriminators in
this chapter between mechanical and inflammatory disease.

They also carry treatment information that no other history question does: a
patient relieved by a brace, by load redistribution or by an anti-inflammatory
has already demonstrated that a corresponding intervention affects their
symptom.

That demonstration is weak evidence but it is the patient's own evidence,
which is worth more than it is usually given credit for.

### Key Learning Points

- The time course of relief reflects the mechanism: immediate for load, hours for a reactive component.
- Relieving factors give category reliably and structure poorly.
- Paired with aggravating factors they discriminate mechanical from inflammatory disease well.
- A patient relieved by a brace has demonstrated that load redistribution affects their symptom.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level a relieving factor is a natural experiment the patient has
already run, and it should be used as one.

A varus knee relieved by a lateral wedge or by an unloader brace has
demonstrated that unloading the medial compartment helps, which supports an
osteotomy more directly than any imaging feature. 4.20.4 covers osteotomy
assessment.

A patellofemoral problem relieved by taping or by a brace has demonstrated
that patellar position affects the symptom.

A knee relieved by nothing, including unloading and immobilisation, has
demonstrated that the symptom is not simply load-dependent, and a
load-changing operation is unlikely to help.

That last inference is the most useful one on this page and is routinely
available before any investigation.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is not asking, because the question sounds like a
formality and carries more information than most of the others.

The second is accepting rest as a relieving factor in a patient who has
stopped doing everything, where rest has not been tested against anything.

The third is over-reading an analgesic response. Simple analgesia improves
most pain to some degree and the response is not diagnostic.

And a knee relieved only by opiates, in the presence of modest findings,
should prompt a broader assessment rather than a mechanical intervention.

### Key Learning Points

- A relieving factor is a natural experiment the patient has already run.
- Relief from an unloader brace supports an osteotomy more directly than any imaging feature.
- Relief from nothing at all suggests a load-changing operation will not help.
- Simple analgesia improves most pain and the response is not diagnostic.
- Relief only by opiates with modest findings calls for a broader assessment.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

After surgery the relieving factors are the clearest guide to whether the
remaining symptom is mechanical.

A post-operative pain relieved by rest and provoked by a defined load is
mechanical and worth pursuing structurally.

A post-operative pain unaffected by rest, position, load or immobilisation is
not mechanical, and further surgery for it is the intervention this chapter
warns about on several pages.

A knee relieved by extension but not by flexion, or the reverse, points at a
position dependent cause such as impingement, a cyclops lesion or a component
issue. 4.20.1 and 4.20.8 cover the two settings.

In a revision assessment the most useful single question is whether anything
at all now relieves it, because the answer frequently decides whether an
operation is on the table.

No figure supports these patterns; they are standard teaching, under decision
023.

### Key Learning Points

- After surgery, relieving factors are the clearest guide to whether the symptom is mechanical.
- Pain unaffected by rest, position, load and immobilisation is not mechanical.
- Position dependent relief points at impingement, a cyclops lesion or a component issue.
- In revision assessment, whether anything relieves it frequently decides whether to operate.
