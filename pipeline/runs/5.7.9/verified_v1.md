# ACL Reconstruction

The reconstructed cruciate ligament, where the graft's appearance and the
graft's function are two different studies.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

A reconstruction replaces the torn ligament with a graft passed through tunnels
in the femur and tibia and fixed at both ends. 3.7.20 covers the reconstruction
biomechanics.

On magnetic resonance imaging the graft is a band running from one tunnel to the
other, with fixation artefact at each end. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

It matters because a graft is expected to change signal as it remodels, so the
appearance that worries a reader is often the expected one. 5.7.10 covers graft
maturation.

### How It Is Acquired

Find out which graft was used and how it was fixed, because the normal
appearance differs between them. 5.17.1 covers post-operative cruciate
reconstruction.

Use the post-operative protocol, since metal or bioabsorbable fixation produces
artefact at the two places the study must assess. 5.5.3 covers the
post-operative protocol.

Assess the graft's continuity and its line against the notch roof, which are the
same two observations the native ligament needed. 2.7.12 covers the surgical
anatomy.

Assess the tunnel positions, which are measurable where graft signal is not.
5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

Assess the rest of the knee, because the menisci and cartilage decide the
outcome the patient notices. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

### What Normal Looks Like

A continuous graft of low signal running from the femoral to the tibial tunnel,
parallel to the notch roof.

Fixation artefact at both ends, which is hardware rather than a lesion. 5.2.19
covers artefacts.

Some increased signal within the graft in the first year or two, which is
remodelling. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

Fluid in the tunnels early after surgery, which is expected rather than a sign of
loosening. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

A discontinuous graft with fluid filling its course, which is a rupture. 5.7.11
covers graft failure.

A graft lying flatter than the notch roof, which indicates it has lengthened.
2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

Tunnels in the wrong position, which is the finding that explains a failure
rather than describing it. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

Anterior tibial translation, which is the mechanical consequence. 3.7.8 covers
anterior stability.

### Key Learning Points

- A reconstruction passes a graft through femoral and tibial tunnels.
- The normal appearance depends on which graft was used.
- Graft signal changes as it remodels, so change is expected.
- Early tunnel fluid is expected.
- Tunnel position is measurable where graft signal is not.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected opening is a question about the operation, because the
appearance cannot be read without it.

The expected points are the graft type, the fixation, the post-operative
protocol, continuity and line, tunnel position, and the rest of the knee.

Say that graft signal change in the first two years is expected. 5.7.10 covers
graft maturation.

Say that the line against the notch roof is the observation that detects a
lengthened graft. 2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

Say that tunnel position is the reliable measurement in this study. 5.7.12
covers tunnel assessment.

### What Normal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that a graft is dead tissue that is repopulated and
remodelled, so it loses and regains its collagen organisation over months, and
its signal follows that process. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

A graft fixed in the correct tunnels is loaded along the native ligament's line,
which is why tunnel position predicts the mechanical result. 3.7.20 covers the
reconstruction biomechanics.

Different grafts carry different amounts of bone and tendon, so their early
appearance and their fixation artefact differ. 3.7.18 covers patellar tendon
graft biomechanics.

Tunnel fluid early reflects an incompletely incorporated graft tendon interface
rather than a failure. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging measures the reconstruction's geometry well and its
biology poorly.

Its best contribution is tunnel position and graft continuity, which are
geometric observations. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

It is limited by graft signal carrying no reliable meaning, by fixation
artefact, and by function being the question. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and the
comparison is the claim: geometry is read reliably and biology is not, which is
a comparison rather than a number.

### Key Learning Points

- A graft is repopulated and remodelled, so its signal follows that process.
- Correct tunnels load the graft along the native line.
- Different grafts have different early appearances and fixation artefacts.
- Early tunnel fluid reflects an incomplete interface rather than failure.
- Geometry is read reliably and biology is not.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the study is read for the geometry, and the function is read
from the patient.

A failing knee with correctly placed tunnels and a continuous graft points away
from the graft and towards the corners, the meniscus or the alignment. 5.10.3
covers posterolateral corner injuries.

A failing knee with a malpositioned tunnel has a technical explanation, which
changes a revision from a repeat to a redesign. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

A stable knee with an abnormal looking graft needs no action, and reporting the
signal without that context invites one. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

Meniscal and cartilage state in the reconstructed knee is the prognosis, and it
is the part of the study most often skimmed. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting expected graft remodelling signal as a partial
tear. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

Fixation artefact is read as a lesion at the fixation site. 5.2.19 covers
artefacts.

Tunnel position is not assessed, so the measurable finding is omitted. 5.7.12
covers tunnel assessment.

The graft's line is not checked, so a lengthened graft is reported as intact.
2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

The operation note is absent and the appearance is read against the wrong normal.
5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

### Key Learning Points

- Expected remodelling signal is reported as a partial tear.
- Fixation artefact is read as a lesion.
- Tunnel position, the measurable finding, is omitted.
- A lengthened graft is reported as intact when the line is not checked.
- Without the operation note the appearance is read against the wrong normal.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the reconstructed knee is where imaging is asked for a
judgement on biology that it has no measurement for.

Graft signal has been correlated with graft strength in several ways and none is
established well enough to act on, so a signal based judgement is a reader's
impression. 5.21.2 covers the cruciate imaging literature.

Tunnel position has an agreed geometric basis and is still reported
inconsistently, which means the study's most reliable content is its most
variably delivered. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

The question in a failing reconstruction is usually rotational, and the study is
read for anterior translation because that is what it can measure. 3.7.7 covers
rotational stability.

Quantitative sequences aimed at graft maturation exist and have not changed
practice, in part because the clinical decision depends on function rather than
composition. 5.18.2 covers T2 mapping.

A report that gave tunnel positions and left the graft signal undescribed would
be more useful than most current reports, which inverts the usual emphasis.
5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- No graft signal to strength relationship is established well enough to act on.
- The study's most reliable content is its most variably reported.
- The failing question is rotational and the measurement is translational.
- Quantitative maturation sequences have not changed practice.
- Tunnel positions without graft signal would be a more useful report.
