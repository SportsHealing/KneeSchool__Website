# End Feel

The quality of the resistance at the limit of a movement, and the finding that
decides which operation a stiff knee needs.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

End feel is what the examiner feels at the end of a passive movement: the
character of the resistance rather than the angle at which it occurs.

It matters because the angle says how restricted a knee is and the end feel says
why, and the why is what determines the treatment. 4.7.2 covers passive motion.

Four end feels are worth recognising: a firm abrupt stop, a springy rebound, a
leathery stretch, and a limit reached because of pain before any resistance.

### How It Is Performed

Move the knee passively to its limit with the patient relaxed and the limb
supported, which is the only condition in which the end feel is interpretable.
4.7.2 covers the technique.

Apply a gentle steady pressure at the limit rather than a sudden force, and feel
how the resistance behaves.

Note whether it stops abruptly, rebounds slightly, gives a little more with
sustained pressure, or whether the patient stops you before resistance is
reached.

Repeat on the other side and compare, since a normal end feel varies between
people.

Record the end feel in words alongside the angle. 4.1.8 covers the record.

### What a Positive Means

A firm abrupt stop indicates bone against bone or something interposed, which in
a reconstructed knee raises an impinging lesion. 4.7.10 covers post-operative
range.

A springy rebound indicates something displaced within the joint, which with a
history of locking indicates a displaced meniscal tear. 4.9.8 covers mechanical
locking.

A leathery stretch that gives slightly with sustained pressure indicates
capsular or soft tissue shortening, which is a contracture. 4.7.7 covers it.

A limit reached because of pain, before resistance, indicates an irritable joint
rather than a restricted one, usually an effusion or inflammation. 4.8.2 covers
it.

An unusually soft end feel at the extremes suggests hypermobility rather than
pathology, and the other knee settles it. 4.23.3 covers those patients.

### Key Learning Points

- End feel is the character of the resistance rather than the angle.
- The angle says how restricted a knee is and the end feel says why.
- A firm abrupt stop indicates bone or something interposed.
- A springy rebound with a history of locking indicates a displaced meniscal tear.
- A leathery stretch indicates capsular shortening, which is a contracture.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva the end feel is where a candidate shows they have examined stiff knees
rather than read about them, and naming the four types is the mark.

The expected points are the relaxed supported limb, the gentle sustained
pressure, the four characters, the comparison, and the record in words.

Say that a springy block with a locked knee is an urgent finding, since that
combination is a displaced tear. 4.9.8 covers it.

Say that you would record the end feel in words rather than as a label, because
the words survive and the label may be disputed. 4.1.8 covers the record.

Say that the end feel is the least reproducible part of the range assessment and
the most informative, and that recording it anyway is the right answer.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that the resistance at a limit reflects the mechanical
properties of whatever is reaching its limit, so bone, displaced tissue,
shortened capsule and muscle guarding each feel different. 4.7.2 covers passive
motion.

Bone and interposed fragments do not deform, which is why they produce an abrupt
stop with no give. 4.9.8 covers one cause.

A displaced meniscal fragment is elastic, which is why it rebounds, and that
elasticity is the physical basis of the springy block. 2.5.2 covers meniscal
histology.

Capsular tissue is viscoelastic, which is why a contracture gives slightly under
sustained pressure and why serial stretching can change it. 4.7.7 covers
contractures.

### How Good It Is

End feel is the most informative and least reproducible element of the range
assessment, which is an uncomfortable combination and the honest position.

Its reproducibility improves with experience, which makes it a finding worth
the same examiner recording across visits where possible.

Its value is that no other part of the examination distinguishes a mechanical
block from a contracture, and that distinction changes the operation. 4.7.7
covers it.

Imaging identifies the structures and does not describe the resistance, so the
two are complementary rather than competing. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

and the comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- The resistance reflects the mechanical properties of whatever reaches its limit.
- Bone and fragments do not deform, so they stop abruptly.
- A meniscal fragment is elastic, which is the physical basis of the springy block.
- Capsular tissue is viscoelastic, so a contracture gives under sustained pressure.
- No other part of the examination distinguishes a block from a contracture.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the end feel decides which operation a stiff knee needs, and
no other finding does.

A firm abrupt block in a reconstructed knee indicates an impinging lesion, and
the operation is an arthroscopic excision with a reliable result. 4.7.10 covers
post-operative range.

A leathery global restriction indicates arthrofibrosis, and the operation is a
release with a less certain result, so the distinction changes what the patient
is told. 4.7.7 covers contractures.

A springy block with locking indicates a displaced tear, and the operation is
urgent rather than elective. 4.9.8 covers mechanical locking.

A pain limited range indicates that the knee is irritable rather than stiff, and
the treatment is the irritability. 4.8.2 covers the effusion.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is recording an angle without an end feel, which leaves the
cause undetermined.

The second is assessing the end feel in a guarding patient, where muscle
guarding mimics every other character.

The third is applying a sudden force, which produces a stop rather than a
character.

The fourth is treating a pain limited range as a contracture, which leads to
releases that were not needed.

The fifth is relying on an end feel recorded by somebody else, since the finding
is examiner dependent and the words are what survive.

### Key Learning Points

- The end feel decides which operation a stiff knee needs, and no other finding does.
- A firm block in a reconstructed knee has a reliable arthroscopic answer.
- A leathery restriction is arthrofibrosis, with a less certain result and a different conversation.
- A springy block with locking is urgent rather than elective.
- A pain limited range treated as a contracture leads to releases that were not needed.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In referred practice the end feel is often the finding that converts a vague
stiffness referral into a specific plan.

A patient referred as stiff after a reconstruction, with a firm abrupt block,
has an impinging lesion and a good prognosis, and the referral is frequently
late because the end feel was never described. 4.7.10 covers the range.

A patient with a leathery global restriction has arthrofibrosis, and the honest
conversation about a less certain outcome begins with this finding. 4.7.7
covers contractures.

A patient whose end feel changes between visits has a changing problem, and in a
reconstructed knee a developing block is more concerning than a stable one.

A patient with a pain limited range and a normal passive range under anaesthesia
has an irritable knee rather than a stiff one, and that is a diagnosis rather
than an inconsistency. 4.14.6 covers that setting.

In medicolegal work an angle recorded without an end feel is a number whose
cause cannot be established retrospectively, which weakens any opinion resting
on it. 4.23.7 covers that setting.

### Key Learning Points

- The end feel converts a vague stiffness referral into a specific plan.
- A firm abrupt block after reconstruction has a good prognosis and is often referred late.
- A leathery restriction begins the honest conversation about arthrofibrosis.
- A developing block between visits is more concerning than a stable one.
- A pain limited range with a normal range under anaesthesia is an irritable knee, not a stiff one.
