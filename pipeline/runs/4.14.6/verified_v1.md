# Examination Under Anaesthesia

The examination that works because the patient cannot help, and the one
rotational instability is actually graded in.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

Examination under anaesthesia is the same set of tests performed on a patient
who is fully relaxed, and relaxation is what most of the rotational tests need.

A guarding patient prevents the subluxation the pivot shift depends on, so the
awake test fails towards a false negative. Under anaesthesia it does not.
4.14.1 covers the pivot shift.

The anterior cruciate chapter describes the general setting. What this page
covers is the rotational examination specifically, which is the part that most
depends on relaxation. 4.10.10 covers the general case.

### How It Is Performed

Examine both knees, always, and examine the uninjured one first so the normal
is established before the abnormal is interpreted.

Perform the pivot shift on both sides and grade it, since the grade rather than
the presence is the finding. 4.14.1 covers the grading.

Perform the dial test at thirty and ninety degrees on both sides. 4.13.5 covers
it.

Perform the Lachman test, the posterior drawer with the tibia reduced, and
valgus and varus stress at two angles each. 4.11.2 covers the drawer.

Record the findings in the operation note as numbers and grades rather than as
positive or negative, because this record is the one that will be read years
later. 4.1.8 covers that argument.

### What a Positive Means

A pivot shift grade that is larger than the awake grade is the usual finding,
and it is the real grade.

An asymmetric dial difference established here is more reliable than an awake
one, because the awake version fails in a guarding patient. 4.13.5 covers it.

A reverse pivot shift found here is less reliable than awake, because the rate
of positive results in normal knees rises with relaxation. 4.11.4 covers that
test.

Findings that appear only under anaesthesia are real laxity. Whether they matter
to the patient is a separate question the examination cannot answer, which is
this section's recurring division. 4.11.5 covers the functional side.

### Key Learning Points

- Relaxation is what most rotational tests need, and anaesthesia supplies it.
- Examine both knees and examine the uninjured one first.
- A pivot shift grade larger than the awake grade is the usual finding and is the real grade.
- The reverse pivot shift becomes less reliable under anaesthesia, not more.
- Record grades and numbers in the operation note, not positive or negative.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva this is the answer to how you would grade a pivot shift reliably, and
saying so directly is better than describing a better awake technique.

The expected points are both knees, the uninjured side first, the graded pivot
shift, the dial test at two angles, the translation tests, and the recorded
grades.

Say that you would examine before the tourniquet is inflated and before any
arthroscopy, because both alter the findings.

Say that you would record the examination in the operation note as the primary
record, since it is more reliable than the clinic examination that preceded it.

Say that a finding present under anaesthesia and absent awake is still a real
finding, and that the decision to act on it is a separate judgement. 4.11.5
covers that judgement.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that muscle activity is a dynamic restraint, so
removing it removes a restraint the awake examination cannot separate from the
ligaments. 3.11.4 covers pivot shift control.

The hamstrings resist anterior translation and the quadriceps resists posterior
translation, so guarding reduces the Lachman test, the pivot shift and the
posterior drawer in the same direction. 4.11.3 covers the test built on the
quadriceps.

The dial test is affected the same way, which is why a normal awake dial test in
a painful knee is an absent test rather than a negative one. 4.13.5 covers it.

The reverse pivot shift moves the other way, because the relaxation that
reveals a true positive also produces false ones. 4.11.4 covers it.

### How Good It Is

Examination under anaesthesia is the most reliable clinical assessment of
rotational laxity available, and it is the reference the awake tests are judged
against.

Its weakness is access rather than accuracy. It requires an anaesthetic, so it
cannot be used to decide whether to operate unless the operation is already
planned.

That constraint shapes practice. It is used to settle a plan at the start of a
listed operation, and to establish laxity in a knee too painful to examine, and
it is not a clinic investigation. 4.11.7 covers the acute setting.

It is also the one examination whose record routinely survives, because it goes
into an operation note rather than a clinic letter. 4.1.8 covers the record.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 023,
and the comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- Muscle activity is a dynamic restraint, and anaesthesia removes it.
- Guarding reduces the Lachman test, the pivot shift and the posterior drawer in the same direction.
- A normal awake dial test in a painful knee is an absent test, not a negative one.
- Its weakness is access rather than accuracy, since it needs an anaesthetic.
- Its record routinely survives, because it goes into an operation note.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level this examination is where the plan for a rotationally unstable
knee is finalised, and in multiligament surgery it is where the plan is made at
all.

A pivot shift graded here is the clinical basis for adding a lateral
extra-articular procedure, and grading it awake is not good enough for that
decision in a borderline case. 4.14.3 covers the pattern.

A dial test established here decides whether a corner is reconstructed alongside
a cruciate, and in the acute knee there is no other way to establish it.
4.13.4 covers the corner assessment.

In the acute multiligament knee it is part of the assessment rather than part of
the operation, and arranging it early is what allows acute repair within its
window. 4.11.7 covers the sequence.

In a revision it is worth arranging deliberately, because the question is
usually whether the graft is incompetent or the knee was never rotationally
controlled, and that is an anaesthetised examination. 4.10.12 covers revision
assessment.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is examining only the injured knee, which removes the
comparison in the one setting where it is easiest to obtain.

The second is examining after arthroscopy or after the tourniquet, both of which
change the findings.

The third is recording positive or negative, which wastes the most reliable
examination the patient will ever have.

The fourth is treating a finding present only under anaesthesia as an indication
by itself, when whether it matters is a functional question. 4.11.5 covers it.

The fifth is relying on a reverse pivot shift found here, which is the one test
anaesthesia makes worse. 4.11.4 covers it.

### Key Learning Points

- This is where the plan for a rotationally unstable knee is finalised.
- A borderline lateral augmentation decision needs a grade from here, not an awake impression.
- In the acute knee there is no other way to establish the dial test.
- Examine before the tourniquet and before any arthroscopy.
- A finding present only here is real laxity, and whether it matters is a functional question.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In referred practice the anaesthetised examination from the first operation is
often the single most useful document in the file, and its absence is the
commonest reason a revision has to start from guesswork.

A recorded grade zero pivot shift at the end of the first reconstruction, with a
high grade shift now, establishes that something changed rather than that the
knee was never controlled. That distinction decides the revision. 4.14.3 covers
the pattern.

No recorded examination means the distinction cannot be made from the notes, and
the present anaesthetised examination has to carry it alone.

An examination performed after the tourniquet or after a long arthroscopy
reads differently, and a grade recorded in those circumstances is worth
discounting rather than trusting.

In a multiply operated knee the examination is constrained by previous surgery
and the comparison with the other knee is no longer a comparison with normal,
so the findings are read against the operation notes. 4.2.30 covers previous
surgery.

In medicolegal work this is the strongest examination evidence that exists about
a knee, and a careless entry in an operation note carries more weight than a
careful clinic letter. That is an argument for recording it properly rather than
for recording less. 4.23.7 covers that setting.

### Key Learning Points

- The anaesthetised examination from the first operation is often the most useful document in the file.
- A recorded grade zero shift then and a high grade now establishes that something changed.
- An examination after the tourniquet or a long arthroscopy is worth discounting.
- In a multiply operated knee the other knee is no longer a comparison with normal.
- This is the strongest examination evidence that exists, which is an argument for recording it properly.
