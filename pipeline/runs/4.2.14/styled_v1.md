# Inflammatory Pain

The pattern that separates an inflamed knee from a worn one, and why getting
it wrong sends a patient to the wrong clinic.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

Asking about the inflammatory pattern tests whether the knee is inflamed
rather than mechanically worn, and the two are managed by different
specialties.

The pattern has four features: stiffness after rest that eases with movement,
prolonged morning stiffness, involvement of other joints, and systemic
symptoms.

A mechanical knee is the opposite on each: better with rest, worse with use,
one joint, no systemic features.

### How It Is Performed

Ask how long the knee is stiff in the morning before it loosens, pressing for
minutes.

Ask whether it is worse after sitting still and better after moving about,
which is the core inflammatory pattern.

Ask about other joints, including the hands, feet and spine, and about
swelling in them rather than only pain.

Ask about systemic features: fatigue, fever, weight loss, rashes, eye
problems, bowel symptoms and psoriasis.

Ask about family history, since several of the relevant conditions cluster.

### What a Positive Means

Prolonged morning stiffness with polyarticular involvement and systemic
features points to an inflammatory arthropathy and needs a rheumatological
assessment rather than a mechanical one.

An acutely hot, swollen, exquisitely painful knee in a single joint is a
crystal arthropathy or an infection and is urgent. 4.8.10 covers crystal
arthropathy.

Episodic swelling with normal periods between episodes, in a single knee, can
be either mechanical or inflammatory and the other features decide. 4.8.8
covers chronic effusions.

Short morning stiffness easing within half an hour, in a single knee, with
activity-related pain, fits degenerative disease. 4.18.1 covers early disease.

### Key Learning Points

- The inflammatory pattern is stiffness after rest, prolonged morning stiffness, other joints, and systemic features.
- A mechanical knee is the opposite of each of those.
- Press for the duration of morning stiffness in minutes.
- Ask whether other joints swell as well as hurt.
- A hot, swollen, exquisitely painful single knee is urgent.

## MRCS Level

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that an inflammatory pattern changes the whole
pathway rather than adding a diagnosis.

A synovitic knee will have an effusion, warmth and a boggy feel on palpation,
which the examination will confirm. 4.8.9 covers synovitis.

Arthroscopy of an inflamed knee for mechanical symptoms frequently
disappoints, because the symptom is synovitis and the findings are incidental.

An inflammatory arthropathy with secondary degenerative change can present
exactly like primary degenerative disease, and the history is usually the only
thing that separates them in clinic.

A monoarthritis in a sexually active young adult, or in anyone
immunosuppressed, has a differential that does not belong in this chapter and
needs excluding first.

### How Good It Is

The inflammatory pattern is one of the better discriminators in the knee
history, because its features are concrete and its opposite is equally
concrete.

The timing features are the most reliable: stiffness after rest, and morning
stiffness duration.

Polyarticular involvement is reliable when present and unreliable when absent,
since several inflammatory conditions begin in one joint.

Systemic features are specific and insensitive: their presence matters a great
deal and their absence excludes little.

### Key Learning Points

- An inflammatory pattern changes the pathway rather than adding a diagnosis.
- Arthroscopy of an inflamed knee for mechanical symptoms frequently disappoints.
- Inflammatory disease with secondary degeneration can look exactly like primary degeneration.
- The timing features are the most reliable part of the pattern.
- Systemic features are specific and insensitive.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the inflammatory pattern is a contraindication question as
much as a diagnostic one.

An operation on a knee with active synovitis of inflammatory origin has a
different risk profile and a different expected result, and the history is
where that is identified.

Arthroplasty in inflammatory arthropathy is a recognised and different
proposition from arthroplasty in degenerative disease, including in the
medication that has to be managed around it.

A patient on immunosuppression or biological therapy has an infection risk
that changes the consent conversation and the perioperative plan, and the
question that identifies them is a history question about other joints and
about medication.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is not asking about other joints, which converts a
systemic problem into a knee problem.

The second is accepting morning stiffness without a duration, which loses the
discrimination entirely.

The third is a knee with both: a patient with an inflammatory arthropathy can
also tear a meniscus, and attributing everything to the arthropathy is the
mirror of attributing everything to the tear.

And a knee that became inflamed after an injection or an operation is an
infection question first, whatever the background diagnosis. 4.2.31 covers
injections.

### Key Learning Points

- The inflammatory pattern is a contraindication question as much as a diagnostic one.
- Arthroplasty in inflammatory disease is a different proposition, including the medication.
- Immunosuppression changes consent and the perioperative plan, and a history question finds it.
- Not asking about other joints converts a systemic problem into a knee problem.
- A patient with an arthropathy can also tear a meniscus.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In referred practice the inflammatory pattern is most often missed in the
patient who has already had mechanical surgery.

A knee that was arthroscoped, improved briefly and returned to a stiff,
swollen, morning-worse state was probably inflamed before the operation, and
the pattern was there to be found.

A knee that developed the pattern after an operation raises low grade
infection, a reactive synovitis, or a metal or cement reaction, and the
sequence of onset separates them. 4.20.9 covers revision assessment.

In a multiply operated knee, persistent synovitis is a recognised and
frequently unexplained end state, and recognising it is more useful than
continuing to look for a mechanical cause.

The honest limit is that distinguishing a reactive from an infective synovitis
cannot be done from the history, and this page is about identifying who needs
that distinction made rather than making it.

### Key Learning Points

- A knee that returned to a stiff, swollen, morning-worse state after arthroscopy was probably inflamed before it.
- A pattern that developed after an operation raises infection, reactive synovitis or a material reaction.
- Persistent synovitis after multiple operations is a recognised and often unexplained end state.
- The history identifies who needs reactive and infective synovitis distinguished; it cannot distinguish them.
