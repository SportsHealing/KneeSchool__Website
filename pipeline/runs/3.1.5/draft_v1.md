# Biomechanics and Injury Prevention

The idea that some mechanics can be changed, and that changing them changes
how often knees get hurt.

## For Young Learners

### The Principle

Some things about your body you cannot change: how tall you are, the shape of
your bones, how old you are.

Other things you can: how strong you are, how you land, how you turn, how
tired you let yourself get.

Injury prevention is the idea of finding the things in the second list that
matter, and changing them.

### At the Knee

When people land badly, their knees tend to fall inwards. That position puts a
lot of load on the ligament inside the knee.

You can practise landing with your knees over your toes instead, and with your
hips and knees bending to absorb the landing.

Teams that practise this as part of their warm up get fewer knee injuries.
That is a real finding, not a guess.

### Key Learning Points

- Some things about your body can be changed and some cannot.
- Prevention means finding the changeable things that matter.
- Knees falling inwards on landing loads the ligament inside the knee.
- Landing with knees over toes, hips and knees bending, spreads the load.
- Teams that practise this in their warm up get fewer knee injuries.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.

## For Patients

### The Principle

Prevention works on the part of the mechanics that can be modified. That is a
smaller list than people expect and it is not empty.

Strength, movement pattern, fatigue management and load progression are
modifiable. Bone shape, joint geometry and past injury are not.

A prevention programme is a set of exercises aimed at the modifiable list,
performed often enough to change a habit.

### At the Knee

The target is usually the position of the knee under load. A knee that
collapses inwards when landing or changing direction is loaded in the
direction its ligaments resist least.

Programmes train landing, cutting, balance and strength together, because the
position is produced by all four.

They are done as a warm up rather than as extra training, which is what makes
them possible to keep doing.

### Clinical Relevance

Prevention programmes reduce knee injury rates in several sports when they are
actually performed. Adherence is the limiting factor rather than the content.

They matter most to people who have already had an injury, because reinjury is
the largest single risk.

Section 8 covers the programmes themselves.

### Key Learning Points

- Prevention works on the modifiable part of the mechanics.
- Strength, movement, fatigue and load progression are modifiable; bone shape is not.
- The usual target is the knee's position under load.
- Programmes are done as a warm up, which is what makes them sustainable.
- Adherence limits the effect more than the content does.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q.** Do prevention programmes actually work?
Several trials show reduced knee injury rates in sports with pivoting demands,
when the programme is performed regularly. The effect is real and it depends
on doing it.

**Q.** How long before they help?
They are warm up programmes, performed at most sessions, and the effect is
measured over a season rather than over weeks.

**Q.** I have already torn a ligament. Is prevention still relevant?
More so. Reinjury and injury to the other knee are both commoner than a first
injury, so the same programmes matter more after an injury than before.

**Q.** Can I do these at home?
Most are designed for a team warm up but the components are simple. A
physiotherapist can set up an individual version.

See a clinician if your knee gives way, locks, swells quickly after an injury,
or will not take your weight.

## For Medical Students

### The Principle

Prevention requires a modifiable variable that is causally related to injury.
Both halves are needed: an unmodifiable risk factor cannot be a target and a
modifiable association that is not causal will not help when changed.

Neuromuscular training targets the position of the joint at contact, which the
mechanics identifies as decisive because the load arrives faster than a
correction can be made.

The outcome measure is injury rate rather than a biomechanical variable, which
is what separates a prevention trial from a mechanism study.

### At the Knee

Dynamic knee valgus during landing and cutting is the usual target. It
combines hip adduction and internal rotation, knee abduction and tibial
internal rotation. 3.12.7 covers it.

Programmes combine plyometrics, balance, strength and technique feedback. No
single component has been shown to carry the effect alone.

Hip strength and trunk control are part of the target, because the knee's
position is partly set above it.

### What Changes It

Programme effect depends on adherence, on the sport's demands and on the
baseline risk of the population.

Fatigue resistance is part of the mechanism, because the protective pattern
degrades within a session.

Age affects trainability of movement patterns, and programmes introduced in
adolescence are reported to have larger effects.

### How It Is Measured

Cluster randomised trials by team are the standard design, with injury rate
per exposure hour as the outcome.

Biomechanical outcome measures such as knee abduction moment are used as
surrogates and are not the same as an injury outcome.

Screening tests for risk have generally performed poorly at the individual
level even where the group association is real, which is a statistical point
with practical consequences.

### Clinical Relevance

Recommend a prevention programme to athletes in pivoting sports, and to any
athlete returning after a knee injury.

Expect the group effect rather than individual prediction, and say so, because
screening an individual does not perform as well as the group data suggests.

Address hip and trunk control alongside the knee, since the position being
targeted is produced above the joint as well as at it.

### Key Learning Points

- Prevention needs a variable that is both modifiable and causal.
- Dynamic knee valgus is the usual target, produced at the hip, knee and trunk together.
- Injury rate is the outcome; a biomechanical measure is a surrogate.
- The protective pattern degrades with fatigue within a session.
- Group association does not give individual prediction, and screening reflects that.

## MRCS Level

### The Principle

State the causal chain being assumed: training changes movement, changed
movement changes joint loading, changed loading changes injury rate. Trials
test the first and last links together and the middle ones are inferred.

That is a defensible position and worth saying out loud, because it explains
why biomechanical surrogates and injury outcomes sometimes disagree.

Prevention and rehabilitation share a mechanism, which is why return to sport
programmes look like prevention programmes.

### At the Knee

Conventional teaching reports relative risk reductions for knee injury in the
range of a third to a half in well conducted programmes with good adherence,
with smaller effects in pragmatic implementation.

The effect is larger for anterior cruciate injury specifically than for knee
injury in general in several analyses.

Hip abductor and external rotator strength, trunk control and landing
technique are the components most often credited, without a clean separation
between them.

### What Changes It

Adherence is the dominant moderator and it falls over a season without
reinforcement.

Coach delivery rather than clinician delivery determines whether a programme
survives in a club setting, which is an implementation variable with a
mechanical consequence.

Baseline risk changes absolute benefit, so the same relative reduction means
different things in different populations.

### How It Is Measured

Cluster randomisation by team avoids contamination and complicates analysis.
Exposure hours rather than player numbers are the denominator.

Injury definition varies between studies, which affects comparability more
than most readers notice.

Surrogate biomechanical endpoints are useful for mechanism and weak for effect
size.

### Clinical Relevance

Prescribe a programme by name and by frequency rather than advising exercise
generally, since adherence is the variable that decides the effect.

Involve the coach where possible, because delivery determines survival of the
programme.

Expect a relative risk reduction rather than elimination, and set that
expectation with the athlete.

Avoid individual screening as a basis for selection or exclusion, since the
tests do not perform at that level.

### Key Learning Points

- The causal chain assumes training changes movement, movement changes loading, loading changes injury.
- Conventional teaching reports a third to a half relative risk reduction with good adherence.
- Adherence is the dominant moderator and falls over a season.
- Injury definitions vary between studies more than readers notice.
- Individual screening does not perform as well as the group association suggests.
