# Posterior Knee

The back of the knee, where ultrasound characterises a cyst definitively and
reaches very little else.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

The posterior knee holds the popliteal fossa, its vessels, the gastrocnemius and
semimembranosus tendons and the bursa between them. 2.9.5 covers the
semimembranosus anatomy.

A popliteal cyst arises from that bursa and communicates with the joint through a
characteristic neck. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

It matters because ultrasound characterises a popliteal cyst better and faster
than any other modality, and reaches almost nothing else back here. 5.2.7 covers
the frequency selection.

### How It Is Acquired

Scan with the patient prone and the knee extended, which opens the fossa. 5.2.6
covers sound waves.

Identify the popliteal artery and vein first, because they are the landmark and
the structure that must not be mistaken. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Find the neck between the semimembranosus and medial gastrocnemius tendons,
which is what identifies a popliteal cyst. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

Use a lower frequency than anteriorly, since the fossa is deeper. 5.2.7 covers
the frequency selection.

Apply Doppler to any cystic structure, to exclude an aneurysm. 5.2.11 covers
Doppler imaging.

### What Normal Looks Like

A patent popliteal artery and vein with normal Doppler flow. 5.2.11 covers
Doppler imaging.

Gastrocnemius and semimembranosus tendons with normal fibrillar pattern. 5.5.10
covers the tendons.

A bursa that is not distended and is barely visible. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

No cystic structure in the fossa.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

An anechoic collection with a neck between the semimembranosus and medial
gastrocnemius, which is a popliteal cyst. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

Internal echoes or loose bodies within the cyst. 5.14.6 covers synovial
chondromatosis.

A pulsatile structure with Doppler flow, which is an aneurysm rather than a cyst.
5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

A solid mass, which takes the lesion out of the cystic category entirely. 5.13.7
covers bone tumours.

### Key Learning Points

- The fossa holds the vessels, the tendons and the bursa between them.
- A popliteal cyst has a characteristic neck between two tendons.
- Ultrasound characterises it better and faster than any other modality.
- The vessels are the landmark and the structure not to mistake.
- Doppler on any cystic structure excludes an aneurysm.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the region to name when asked how to characterise a popliteal
swelling.

The expected points are the prone position, the vessels, the neck, the lower
frequency, and the Doppler.

Say that the neck is what makes it a popliteal cyst rather than any cyst. 5.5.12
covers the bursa.

Say that Doppler excludes an aneurysm, which is the lesion not to aspirate.
5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Say that a cyst implies an intra-articular cause that ultrasound cannot find.
5.6.3 covers horizontal meniscal tears.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the bursa communicates with the joint through a
valve like neck, so a cyst is joint fluid that has been pushed out and cannot
easily return. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

That makes the cyst a sign of an intra-articular process rather than a primary
lesion, and the process is usually a meniscal tear or synovitis the ultrasound
cannot see. 5.14.1 covers synovitis.

Treating the cyst without the cause is therefore a recurrence, which is why the
imaging question continues past the cyst. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct
investigation.

A solid or vascular lesion in the fossa is a different problem and the Doppler
is what separates them at the time of scanning. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

### How Good It Is

Ultrasound characterises a popliteal cyst definitively and finds its cause not at
all.

Its best contribution is confirming a cystic structure with a neck and excluding
a vascular lesion, in one examination with no contrast. 5.2.11 covers Doppler
imaging.

It is limited because the intra-articular cause is out of reach, so a positive
study still needs a magnetic resonance study. 5.6.3 covers horizontal meniscal
tears.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no cyst size
thresholds appear either: those are published quantities and this environment
cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- The bursa communicates with the joint through a valve like neck.
- A cyst is joint fluid pushed out that cannot easily return.
- The cyst is a sign of an intra-articular process, not a primary lesion.
- Treating the cyst without the cause invites recurrence.
- Doppler separates a cyst from a vascular lesion during the examination.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level a posterior ultrasound settles what the swelling is and leaves
the reason open.

A cystic structure with a neck and no Doppler flow is a popliteal cyst, and that
diagnosis is secure enough to act on. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

A pulsatile lesion with flow is a vascular emergency rather than a knee problem,
and recognising it during the scan is the point. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

A solid lesion needs characterising, and ultrasound's role is to establish that it
is solid. 5.13.7 covers bone tumours.

The intra-articular cause needs a magnetic resonance study, because recurrence
after aspiration follows from treating the cyst alone. 5.4.17 covers aspiration.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is aspirating a cystic structure without Doppler. 5.2.11
covers Doppler imaging.

A cyst is treated as the diagnosis and the intra-articular cause is not sought.
5.6.3 covers horizontal meniscal tears.

A deeper structure is reported from a study optimised for a shallow one. 5.2.7
covers the frequency selection.

The neck is not identified, so a different cystic lesion is called a popliteal
cyst. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

A normal posterior ultrasound is taken to exclude posterior knee pathology. 5.1.6
covers imaging pitfalls.

### Key Learning Points

- A cystic structure is aspirated without Doppler.
- The cyst is treated as the diagnosis.
- A deep structure is reported from a shallow study.
- Without the neck, another cystic lesion is called a popliteal cyst.
- A normal study is taken to exclude posterior pathology.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the posterior knee is the clearest case in this chapter of
ultrasound answering one question completely and the surrounding question not at
all.

The cyst is characterised definitively and its cause is intra-articular and
invisible, so the study is complete and insufficient at the same time. 5.6.3
covers horizontal meniscal tears.

That combination invites treating the visible thing, and aspiration without
addressing the joint has a recurrence rate that makes the imaging pathway part of
the outcome. 5.4.17 covers aspiration.

The vascular differential is the one knee ultrasound finding that is urgent, and
it depends on the operator applying Doppler to a structure they have already
decided is a cyst. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Deeper posterior structures, including the posterior cruciate and the posterior
capsule, are beyond useful reach, which bounds the region's role permanently.
5.8.1 covers the normal posterior cruciate.

The honest framing is that this is a focused characterisation study, and calling
it a posterior knee assessment overstates what it covers. 5.1.3 covers choosing
the correct investigation.

### Key Learning Points

- The cyst is characterised definitively and its cause is invisible.
- Treating the visible thing invites recurrence.
- The vascular differential is the one urgent knee ultrasound finding.
- It depends on applying Doppler to a presumed cyst.
- This is a focused characterisation study rather than a regional assessment.
