# Bones, Ligaments, Menisci and Muscles

Four building blocks, and what each one is for.

## For Young Learners

### Structure and Location

Almost everything in your knee belongs to one of four groups.

Bones give the shape. The thigh bone from above, the shin bone from below, the
kneecap at the front, the fibula on the outside. They are hard, they do not
bend, and they set where everything else attaches.

Ligaments are tough cords joining bone to bone. Two sit inside the joint and
cross over each other, which is how they got the name cruciate, from the Latin
for cross. Two more run down the sides. Together they stop the bones going
where they should not.

Menisci are two rubbery pads shaped like the letter C. They sit on top of the
shin bone, between the two bones, and the rounded end of the thigh bone
settles into them.

Muscles pull on bones to make them move. The big group at the front
straightens your knee. The group at the back bends it. They are joined to the
bones by tendons, which are like ligaments except they connect muscle to bone
instead of bone to bone.

### Function

Think of a tent. The poles are the bones. The guy ropes are the ligaments,
holding the poles where they should be. The groundsheet underneath, spreading
the load, is like the menisci. And someone pulling the ropes tighter or looser
as the wind changes, that is the muscles.

No tent stands on poles alone, and no knee works on bones alone.

Each group also fails differently. Bones break. Ligaments tear. Menisci split.
Muscles strain. Knowing which group is involved tells you roughly what to
expect, long before anyone mentions a scan.

### Key Learning Points

- Four groups: bones, ligaments, menisci and muscles with their tendons.
- Ligaments join bone to bone. Tendons join muscle to bone.
- The two cruciate ligaments sit inside the joint and cross each other.
- The menisci are two C shaped pads between the thigh bone and the shin bone.
- Each group fails in its own way, which is why naming the part matters.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.
