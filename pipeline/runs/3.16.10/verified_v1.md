# Future Directions

What would have to change for knee measurement to reach the clinic, and what
this chapter implies about the figures on this site.

## FRCS Level

### The Principle

A field advances when a measurement becomes possible, cheap or continuous.
Which of the three happens decides what changes.

Possible changes what is known. Cheap changes who knows it. Continuous changes
what can be managed rather than described.

Knee biomechanics has had the first for decades and is now getting the third.

### At the Knee

The chapter's recurring finding is a gap: the quantities that predict are
measurable in laboratories and unavailable in clinics. 3.13.7 covers the
clearest example.

Three developments could close it. Wearable estimation of joint level
quantities, markerless capture from ordinary video, and learned models that
turn sparse data into a joint estimate. 3.16.8 and 3.16.6 cover two.

Each is a validation problem rather than a technology problem. The devices
exist and the evidence that their outputs mean something does not.

A fourth development would change more: a non invasive measurement of tissue
material properties in a living person. 3.16.4 covers why that matters.

None of these requires a new principle. They require the unglamorous work of
validating against a reference that is itself imperfect.

### What Changes It

Validation effort, which is the actual bottleneck.

Regulatory frameworks, which determine what can be deployed and monitored.
3.16.6 covers the governance gap.

Cost, since a measurement that is possible and unaffordable does not reach a
clinic.

Clinical questions worth answering, since measurement without a question
produces description.

Data governance, which becomes a clinical responsibility once measurement is
continuous.

### Clinical Relevance

Expect the next decade's change to come from validation rather than from
invention.

Watch wearables and markerless capture as the candidates for leaving the
laboratory.

Treat material property estimation in vivo as the gap that would change
modelling most.

Ask of any new method what reference it was validated against, since that
question survives every technology.

### Key Learning Points

- A field advances when measurement becomes possible, cheap or continuous.
- Continuous changes what can be managed rather than described.
- The gap is that predictive quantities are measurable in laboratories and unavailable in clinics.
- Each candidate for closing it is a validation problem rather than a technology problem.
- Non invasive material property measurement would change modelling most.

## Fellowship Level

### At the Knee

The honest statement about this field is that its measurement capability has
outrun its validation for twenty years, and the gap is widening rather than
closing.

Every method in this chapter can produce a number faster than anyone can
establish what the number means. 3.16.4 covers the clearest case.

That is a structural incentive problem. Producing a new method is publishable
and validating an old one is not.

The specific validations this chapter has named as missing are: external
validity of robotic testing, internal stress in finite element work, native
knee contact force, and any wearable derived clinical threshold.

None of those is impossible. Each is unfunded relative to the next novel
method.

### What Changes It

Funding and publication incentives, which currently favour novelty.

Open data and open models, which make independent validation possible at all.
3.16.5 covers the progress there.

Registry and trial infrastructure willing to carry a biomechanical endpoint.

Regulatory requirements, which can compel validation where publication does
not.

Collaboration between groups, since blind prediction challenges have been
among the field's most useful exercises.

### How It Is Measured

Blind prediction challenges, which have worked and are rare. 3.16.5 covers
one.

Independent replication, which requires shared models and data.

Prospective studies with biomechanical endpoints, which are few and expensive.

Systematic reviews of validation practice, which exist for modelling and
report inconsistency.

There is no accepted metric for how well validated a field is, which is itself
part of the problem.

### Clinical Relevance

Read a new method's arrival as the start of a validation question rather than
the end of one.

Support open data and replication where you have influence, since that is the
rate limiting step.

Expect to wait longer for the validation of an existing method than for the
arrival of a new one.

Name the specific missing validation when a method's output is being relied
on.

### Key Learning Points

- Measurement capability has outrun validation for twenty years and the gap is widening.
- Producing a new method is publishable and validating an old one is not.
- The named gaps are robotic external validity, internal stress, native contact force and wearable thresholds.
- None is impossible; each is unfunded relative to the next novel method.
- There is no accepted metric for how well validated a field is.

## Consultant Perspective

### At the Knee

This chapter has to be turned on the site it appears in, because the argument
applies here as much as anywhere.

Every measurement in Section 3 was written from standard teaching rather than
from a retrieved source, and each is registered with the sentence it supports
and a verification state of unverified. 3.1.7 covers how that came about.

So this site is an example of the thing the chapter describes: numbers that
are plausible, traceable to no measurement, and easier to produce than to
check.

The decision that leaves is whose figures to trust and on what basis, and this
site's answer is to make the list finite and visible rather than to claim it
is checked.

A reader who wants to know which figures here have been verified can be given
the list, and today the honest answer is that none has.

### What Changes It

Whether a source has actually been retrieved for a figure, which is the only
thing that changes its state.

Whether the figure is load bearing for a decision or sets the scale of a
problem. 3.1.7 covers that distinction.

Whether a consultant has reviewed the page the figure sits on.

The register itself, which makes the question answerable rather than
rhetorical.

Time, since verification is slow and generation is fast, which is the
chapter's whole point.

### How It Is Measured

The figure register records every measurement, the sentence it supports and
its verification state, so the unchecked list is generated rather than
estimated.

The position register does the same for clinical recommendations, which are
the other unverified class.

Review packs are generated per chapter from those registers, so a reviewer's
decisions have a route back in.

Nothing in either register has been signed, and the counts are published on
this site's build status page.

That is a measurement of this site's own validation state, which is more than
most sources of knee figures offer and is not the same as being verified.

### Clinical Relevance

Treat every figure on this site as unverified unless its register entry says
otherwise, which today none does.

Ask of any source of knee numbers what its verification state is, and notice
how rarely that question can be answered.

Use the registers rather than the prose when the question is which claims need
checking.

State that a finite visible list of unchecked figures is a weaker claim than a
checked one and a stronger one than silence.

Weigh the patient's age and physiology, comorbidities, knee demand,
psychological state and their own ideas, concerns and expectations before
passing any figure from here into a conversation, because an unverified number
spoken aloud to a patient becomes a fact to them.

### Key Learning Points

- The chapter's argument applies to the site it appears in.
- Every measurement here was written from standard teaching and is registered as unverified.
- Plausible numbers traceable to no measurement are easier to produce than to check.
- A finite visible list of unchecked figures is weaker than a checked one and stronger than silence.
- An unverified number spoken aloud to a patient becomes a fact to them.
