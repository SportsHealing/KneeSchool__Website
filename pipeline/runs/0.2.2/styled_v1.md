# Osgood-Schlatter and Growing Pains

Why a tender lump below the kneecap turns up so often in active teenagers, and
why it settles.

## For Young Learners

### Structure and Location

Run a finger down from your kneecap. A couple of centimetres below it, on the
front of your shin, there is a bony bump. That is where the big tendon from
your thigh muscle attaches to your shin bone.

While you are growing, that attachment point is not solid bone yet. It is a
growing area, and growing areas are softer than the tendon pulling on them.

### Function

The reason it gets better is simple. The problem is a mismatch between a hard
pulling tendon and a soft growing area. When the growing area closes and
becomes ordinary bone, the mismatch disappears.

That is also why it is tied to age rather than to how tough you are.

### Clinical Relevance

Every time your thigh muscle contracts hard, it tugs on that spot. Running,
jumping, kicking, sprinting. During a growth spurt, when you are training a
lot, that tugging can irritate the growing area.

The result is a bump that is bigger and more tender than it was. It hurts
during and after activity, it is sore to kneel on, and it settles with rest.
Both knees are affected in a lot of people. It has a name, Osgood Schlatter,
after the two doctors who described it.

There is a similar one at the bottom tip of the kneecap rather than on the
shin, which behaves much the same way.

Three things are worth knowing. It is extremely common in active teenagers,
particularly during a growth spurt. It is not causing damage that will matter
later. And it resolves when the growing area finishes growing and turns to
solid bone, although the bump itself often stays as a permanent reminder.

The ache people call growing pains is usually something different: a vague
ache in the legs, often in the evening, not tied to one spot and not tied to
activity. Pain in one exact place that is worse after sport is worth
mentioning to an adult rather than filing under growing pains.

### Key Learning Points

- The bump below your kneecap is where the thigh tendon attaches to your shin.
- While you are growing, that attachment point is softer than the tendon pulling on it.
- Repeated hard pulling during a growth spurt irritates it, causing a tender lump.
- It settles when the growing area turns to solid bone, though the bump may remain.
- Pain in one exact spot after sport is different from vague evening growing pains.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.
