# Assessment at 30 Degrees

The angle where the superficial medial ligament stands alone, and the grade
that decides how long the brace stays on.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

Valgus stress applied with the knee flexed to thirty degrees tests the
superficial medial collateral ligament with the least help from anything else.

Flexing the knee relaxes the posterior capsule and the posterior oblique
ligament, which leaves the superficial ligament as the structure taking the
valgus load. 3.9.3 covers its function.

That isolation is the point of the angle. It is the same logic as the Lachman
test, where a particular flexion angle removes the structures that would
otherwise share the work. 4.10.4 covers that test.

### How It Is Performed

Lay the patient supine and flex the knee to about thirty degrees, with the leg
relaxed and the foot resting on the couch or supported by your forearm.

Place one hand along the lateral side of the knee as the fulcrum, with your
fingers over the medial joint line.

Hold the ankle with the other hand and apply a valgus force through it, keeping
the tibia in neutral rotation.

Feel the medial joint line open under your fingers and note the endpoint as the
opening stops.

Grade the opening against the other knee and record both the grade and the
endpoint. 4.1.8 covers the record.

### What a Positive Means

Increased opening at thirty degrees with a firm endpoint in full extension
indicates an injury confined to the superficial medial ligament.

The grade is conventionally described in three degrees. A few millimetres of
extra opening with a firm endpoint is first degree, a moderate increase with a
discernible endpoint is second degree, and a large increase with a soft or
absent endpoint is third degree.

A first or second degree injury heals in a brace in almost every case, which
makes this the one grade in the chapter that reliably predicts the treatment.

A third degree injury at thirty degrees should send you back to full extension,
because the two findings together mean something different from either alone.
4.12.2 covers extension.

Tenderness over the ligament's course with no increase in opening is a sprain
rather than a laxity, and it is treated as pain rather than instability.

### Key Learning Points

- Thirty degrees relaxes the posterior structures and leaves the superficial ligament taking the load.
- The grade at this angle reliably predicts the treatment, which is unusual in this section.
- First and second degree injuries heal in a brace in almost every case.
- A third degree finding here sends you back to the extension test.
- Tenderness with no increased opening is a sprain and is treated as pain.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva the expected points are the flexion angle, the fulcrum, the fingers
on the joint line, neutral rotation, the endpoint, and the grade against the
other side.

Say why thirty degrees, because the reason is the whole content of the question
and the follow up is usually what else resists valgus.

Say that you would keep the tibia in neutral rotation, since external rotation
opens the medial side and internal rotation closes it, and a rotated tibia
turns a grade into a guess.

Say that you would palpate the ligament's course from the femoral epicondyle to
the tibia, because the site of tenderness localises the tear within the
ligament. 4.12.7 covers the discrimination from meniscal pain.

Say that you would grade against the other knee rather than in absolute
millimetres, and that stress radiographs supply the millimetres when a plan
needs them.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that the superficial ligament is the principal valgus
restraint through mid flexion and supplies most of that restraint itself.
3.9.1 covers valgus stability.

Its proximal fibres and distal fibres tighten at different parts of the range,
which is why a partial injury can give a firm endpoint with increased opening
rather than a clean grade. 3.9.9 covers the angles.

The deep ligament lies beneath it and attaches to the meniscus, and its
contribution to valgus restraint is small enough that this test does not
separate it. 4.12.4 covers it.

A grade that improves between two examinations indicates healing in continuity,
which is what this ligament does and what the brace is protecting. 4.12.8
covers the later assessment.

### How Good It Is

The thirty degree test is the most reproducible ligament examination in the
knee, and more reproducible between examiners than any cruciate test.

The reason is that the force has one direction, the fulcrum is fixed, and the
joint line can be felt opening rather than inferred.

It grades well and localises poorly. It cannot tell the superficial ligament
from the deep one and cannot tell a proximal tear from a distal one, both of
which affect healing.

It is reliable acutely, which is why it is the medial examination that is
actually performed in the emergency department.

No figures appear for any of these comparisons, under decision 023, and the
comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- The most reproducible ligament examination in the knee, more so than any cruciate test.
- A rotated tibia turns the grade into a guess, so neutral rotation is part of the technique.
- Proximal and distal fibres tighten at different parts of the range, which blurs partial injuries.
- A grade that improves between examinations indicates healing in continuity.
- It grades well and cannot separate the superficial ligament from the deep one.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the thirty degree grade sets the length of the brace and
little else, which is a modest role performed reliably.

A first degree injury needs support and early movement, a second degree injury
needs a hinged brace for weeks, and a third degree injury needs the extension
test and the corner assessed before anything is decided. 4.12.5 covers the
corner.

Where the grade does matter beyond bracing is in a knee listed for cruciate
reconstruction, because an unhealed medial side changes the timing of that
operation and occasionally its content. 3.9.10 covers the combination.

The site of the tear matters more than the grade for healing. A tear at the
tibial attachment, where the ligament ends can displace, heals less reliably
than one at the femoral end, and that is a palpation finding rather than a
grading one.

Stress radiographs at this angle are the reference for comparison over time and
are worth obtaining where a surgical decision will rest on whether the laxity
changed.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is grading without the extension test, which leaves a
severe injury looking like a moderate one.

The second is grading a rotated knee.

The third is recording a grade with no endpoint, which makes the later
comparison impossible and is the error this whole section keeps naming.

The fourth is treating tenderness as laxity. A painful medial side with no
increased opening is common in meniscal injury and is a different diagnosis.
4.12.7 covers it.

The fifth is examining through a brace or a bulky dressing, where the fulcrum
is wrong and the joint line cannot be felt.

### Key Learning Points

- The grade sets the length of the brace, which is a modest role performed reliably.
- An unhealed medial side changes the timing of a cruciate reconstruction.
- The site of the tear predicts healing better than the grade does, and it is a palpation finding.
- A tear at the tibial attachment heals less reliably than one at the femoral end.
- A painful medial side with no increased opening is often meniscal rather than ligamentous.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In referred practice a residual grade at thirty degrees is usually
asymptomatic, and the work is deciding whether it explains the complaint.

A patient with a second degree residual laxity, a firm endpoint and pain on
twisting is more likely describing the meniscus than the ligament. 4.12.7
covers the separation.

A patient with the same laxity and a sense of the knee opening on cutting is
describing an anteromedial rotatory pattern, which is graded with rotation
added rather than with valgus alone. 4.12.6 covers it.

Chronic medial laxity after a tibial sided tear can be larger than the acute
grade recorded, because the ligament healed in a lengthened position rather
than failing to heal, and the endpoint stays firm throughout. That combination
of a firm endpoint over a lax joint is the chapter's most misleading finding.

In a varus aligned knee a medial laxity is usually irrelevant and in a valgus
aligned knee it is usually not, because alignment decides whether the medial
side is loaded at all. 4.11.5 covers dynamic assessment.

After a medial reconstruction the grade is read against the contralateral side
and against the operation note, and a grade that is better than normal is a
tight reconstruction rather than a success. 4.12.8 covers the assessment.

### Key Learning Points

- A residual grade is usually asymptomatic, and the work is deciding whether it explains the complaint.
- A firm endpoint over a lax joint usually means healing in a lengthened position.
- Pain on twisting with a firm endpoint points at the meniscus rather than the ligament.
- Alignment decides whether the medial side is loaded at all.
- After reconstruction, a grade better than the other side is a tight reconstruction, not a success.
