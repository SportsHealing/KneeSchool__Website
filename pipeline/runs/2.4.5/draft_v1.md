# Surgical Relevance

What the anatomy of the patella constrains in instability surgery and in
arthroplasty, and where each constraint comes from.

## For Medical Students

### Structure and Location

Three features of the patella constrain almost every operation on it.

Its blood supply enters anteriorly and through the lower pole, so the surface
the surgeon works on is the surface the bone is fed through.

It is inside the extensor mechanism, so anything that divides it divides the
mechanism.

Its bone stock is finite and does not come back, so each procedure that
removes some constrains the next.

### Relations

Anteriorly: the quadriceps fibres and the vascular ring on the bone surface.

Medially: the retinaculum in layers with the medial patellofemoral ligament
deepest, attaching at the superomedial border.

Laterally: the retinaculum with deep transverse and superficial oblique
fibres.

Behind: the trochlea and the cartilage surface, the thickest in the body.

### Function

Every operation here is a trade between stability, load and blood supply.

Tightening the medial side improves stability and can increase medial load.
Releasing the lateral side reduces lateral tether and divides part of the
blood supply. Removing bone reduces thickness and the moment arm with it.

### Clinical Relevance

Patellar fracture fixation aims to restore the extensor mechanism first and
the articular surface second, because the mechanism is what the patient uses.

A complete lateral release was once routine and is now used sparingly, and the
reason is the blood supply on this page.

Resurfacing in arthroplasty removes cartilage and replaces it with a
component, and restoring the original overall thickness is part of the
operation.

### Key Learning Points

- The patella is fed through the surface the surgeon works on.
- Anything that divides the patella divides the extensor mechanism.
- Patellar bone stock is finite and non renewable.
- Fixation restores the mechanism first and the surface second.

## MRCS Level

### Structure and Location

For instability surgery the structures named are the superomedial border for
the medial patellofemoral ligament, the lateral retinaculum for release or
lengthening, and the tubercle below for transfer. Only the first two are on
the patella.

For arthroplasty the measures are patellar thickness before and after, the
position of the component on the cut surface, and the amount of lateral facet
left uncovered.

The cut surface in resurfacing is bone whose density is higher laterally, so a
component sits on asymmetrical support.

### Relations

The vascular ring lies on the anterior surface with vessels entering the
middle third and the lower pole. A lateral release divides the superior
lateral and inferior lateral contributions; a medial arthrotomy divides the
medial ones. Doing both divides most of the ring.

The medial patellofemoral ligament attaches at the superomedial border close
to the vastus medialis insertion, and the two are dealt with together.

The fat pad lies behind the tendon and is routinely excised or disturbed in
arthroplasty, which changes tendon excursion and is a source of postoperative
anterior pain.

The infrapatellar branch of the saphenous nerve crosses the medial side of the
approach and is the sensory structure most often sacrificed.

### Blood Supply and Innervation

The anastomotic ring is the limiting structure in combined approaches. The
practical rule is that a medial arthrotomy and a complete lateral release
together leave the patella relying on the remaining anterior soft tissue, and
osteonecrosis is a recognised consequence.

Lateral lengthening rather than complete release exists to reduce that risk
while achieving the same soft tissue effect.

Denervation of the anterior surface by circumpatellar cautery is used to
reduce pain and is a deliberate interruption of the innervation described on
2.4.3. Its effect on blood supply is part of the argument about it.

### Function

In instability surgery the aim is to restore restraint in the first part of
flexion without tethering the patella through the rest of the range, and the
femoral attachment governs that more than the patellar one.

In arthroplasty the aim is a patella that tracks centrally on a component with
the original composite thickness restored. Overstuffing limits flexion;
understuffing shortens the moment arm.

In both, the lateral retinaculum is a variable to be adjusted rather than a
structure to be removed, and the change in thinking is based on the blood
supply.

### Clinical Relevance

Avoid combining a medial arthrotomy with a complete lateral release where the
alternative exists, and document what was divided.

Measure patellar thickness before resection and restore the composite, rather
than resecting to a target depth.

Leave the uncovered lateral facet in mind: a component that does not cover the
whole cut surface leaves bone articulating with the component groove.

In a young patient with instability, prefer the procedure that preserves bone
stock, because the patella cannot afford a second operation that removes more.

### Key Learning Points

- A medial arthrotomy plus a complete lateral release divides most of the vascular ring.
- Lateral lengthening exists to get the soft tissue effect without the vascular cost.
- Restore composite patellar thickness rather than resecting to a depth.
- The femoral attachment governs whether a medial reconstruction tethers through range.
- In a young patient, prefer the procedure that preserves bone stock.

## FRCS Level

### Relations

In revision the relations that matter are the ones previous surgery changed:
how much bone remains, how much of the vascular ring survives, and whether the
retinacula are scarred into a fixed position.

A patella that has been resurfaced, released and transferred has none of its
original relations intact, and planning from normal anatomy will be wrong.

### Function

Patellar anatomy sets a ceiling on what can be achieved. A thin patella cannot
be resurfaced; a devascularised one cannot be osteotomised; a maltracking one
cannot be stabilised by a procedure that does not address the variable at
fault.

Recognising the ceiling is the difference between a staged plan and a sequence
of disappointments.

### Clinical Relevance

In revision arthroplasty with a thin patella, the options are leaving it
unresurfaced, a biconvex or augmenting component, patelloplasty, or resection.
Each is a decision about remaining bone and blood supply. 7.79 owns the
comparison.

In recurrent instability after a failed reconstruction, re-examine all four
tracking variables rather than revising the same procedure.

Patellar osteonecrosis is usually iatrogenic and usually cumulative, and the
anatomy that explains it is the anterior entry of the supply.

Extensor mechanism disruption with a deficient patella is the end of the
salvage ladder, and the anatomical reason is that the mechanism and the bone
are the same structure.

### Key Learning Points

- A revised patella has none of its original relations intact, so planning from normal anatomy is wrong.
- Patellar anatomy sets a ceiling: thin cannot be resurfaced, devascularised cannot be osteotomised.
- Patellar osteonecrosis is usually iatrogenic and cumulative.
- The extensor mechanism and the patella are the same structure, which is why deficiency of one ends the ladder.

## Fellowship Level

### Clinical Relevance

Four situations where the patella is the limiting structure rather than one of
several.

The multiply operated patella with instability. Bone stock, vascularity and
scarred retinacula all constrain, and the decision is often which of the four
tracking variables can still be corrected rather than which should be.
Trochleoplasty enters here, because the trochlea may be the only variable left
untouched.

The thin or deficient patella at revision arthroplasty. Resurfacing needs bone
and perfusion. Where either is absent, leaving the patella unresurfaced is a
decision rather than a failure, and the anatomical point is that the mechanism
survives without a component but not without the bone.

Patellar osteonecrosis after combined approaches. The supply enters anteriorly
through the middle third and the lower pole, and this is the one complication
where the anatomical cause and the surgical cause are the same sentence.

Extensor mechanism reconstruction. Allograft and synthetic options exist and
all of them have to be anchored to or around a patella. Where the patella is
gone or unusable, the reconstruction becomes tibia to quadriceps and the
moment arm is lost permanently.

In all four the anatomy on the first four pages of this chapter is unchanged.
What changes is that there is nothing spare.

### Key Learning Points

- In the multiply operated patella the trochlea may be the only tracking variable left untouched.
- The mechanism survives without a patellar component but not without the bone.
- Patellar osteonecrosis is the complication whose anatomical and surgical causes are one sentence.
- Where the patella is unusable the reconstruction loses the moment arm permanently.
