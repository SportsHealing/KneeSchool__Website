# Graft Assessment

Signal is not failure. Failure is laxity, and the imaging correlate of laxity is
position rather than brightness.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

A graft is not the ligament it replaces, and its appearance changes over the
months after surgery as it remodels. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

So a graft's signal reflects where it is in that process, not whether it is
working. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

It matters because a bright graft is reported as failing, and the graft that is
actually failing is often the one that is continuous and slack. 5.7.11 covers
graft failure.

### How It Is Acquired

Establish the interval since surgery first, because it determines what signal is
expected. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

Read continuity along the whole graft before reading signal anywhere in it. 5.8.5
covers the PCL reconstruction.

Read the graft's path and tension as an appearance, since a slack graft follows a
different line from a taut one. 3.8.8 covers the PCL reconstruction biomechanics.

Read the tibial position relative to the femur, noting how the knee was
positioned. 4.11.1 covers the posterior sag sign.

Read the fixation and the bone around it, where mechanical failure shows before
the graft does. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

### What Normal Looks Like

A continuous graft following a direct path between its fixation points. 5.8.5
covers the PCL reconstruction.

Signal that is raised in the months after surgery and falls later, which is
remodelling. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

Fixation without surrounding lucency or marrow change. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

A tibia sitting in the expected position for how the knee was placed. 4.11.1
covers the posterior sag sign.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

Discontinuity, which is failure of substance. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

A continuous graft on a slack path, which is failure of function. 3.8.2 covers
the posterior stability.

Tunnel widening or lucency at fixation, which precedes graft failure. 5.7.12
covers the tunnel assessment.

Posterior tibial position with the knee extended, which is a sign of residual
laxity rather than a graft finding. 4.11.3 covers the quadriceps active test.

Cartilage change appearing in the medial or patellofemoral compartment after
surgery, which suggests the laxity was not corrected. 5.11.2 covers the chondral
defects.

### Key Learning Points

- A graft is not the ligament it replaces.
- Its signal reflects where it is in remodelling.
- A bright graft is often reported as failing.
- The failing graft is often continuous and slack.
- Tunnel widening precedes graft failure.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer gives the interval before the signal.

The expected points are the interval, the continuity, the path, the fixation, and
the clinical measurement of laxity.

Say that graft signal rises and then falls after surgery. 5.7.10 covers graft
maturation.

Say that a slack continuous graft is a failure the signal does not show. 3.8.2
covers the posterior stability.

Say that laxity is measured on examination and not on the scan. 4.11.2 covers the
posterior drawer test.

### How Good It Is

The modality reports the graft's substance well and its tension badly, and
tension is what the operation was for.

Its best contribution is discontinuity and the state of the fixation, both of
which are unambiguous. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Its second contribution is the compartmental cartilage, which records whether
the laxity was corrected and which accumulates over years. 5.11.2 covers the
chondral defects.

It is limited because tension is assessed from the graft's path on an unloaded
knee, which is an impression rather than a measurement. 3.8.6 covers the
posterior drawer mechanics.

It is limited again because the maturation sequence was described for anterior
cruciate grafts and is applied here, so the expected appearance carries an
assumption this page will not restate as a fact. 5.7.10 covers graft maturation.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no interval,
signal ratio, tunnel diameter, translation distance or graft dimension appears
either: those are published quantities measured differently between studies and
this environment cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- Substance is reported well and tension badly.
- Tension is what the operation was for.
- Discontinuity and fixation state are unambiguous.
- Compartmental cartilage records whether the laxity was corrected.
- The maturation sequence was described for anterior cruciate grafts.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level a revision is a decision about laxity and bone, and this study
supplies the bone.

Tunnel position and widening decide whether a revision can be done in one stage.
5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

A continuous graft in a lax knee moves the question from the graft to the
structures around it, which usually means the corner. 5.10.3 covers PLC injuries.

Alignment is reassessed before a revision, because the reason the first graft
stretched may still be present. 5.3.6 covers the long leg alignment films.

The examination under anaesthesia supplies the laxity, and the combination of
that with this study is what the decision is made on. 4.14.6 covers the
examination under anaesthesia.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting a maturing graft as failing. 5.7.10 covers graft
maturation.

A slack continuous graft is reported as intact. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

Tibial position is read from a knee positioned in flexion. 4.11.1 covers the
posterior sag sign.

Tunnel widening is not measured against the previous study, so a change is not
detected. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

The corner is not reassessed, so a failed graft is attributed to the graft. 5.10.4
covers the popliteofibular ligament.

### Key Learning Points

- A maturing graft is reported as failing.
- A slack continuous graft is reported as intact.
- Tibial position is read from a flexed knee.
- Tunnel widening is not compared with the previous study.
- A failed graft is attributed to the graft rather than to the corner.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this page closes the chapter on the distinction it opened
with, now applied to a graft rather than to a ligament.

Continuity and competence came apart in the native ligament because it heals in
continuity, and they come apart in the graft because a graft can stretch, so the
chapter's rule survives the operation. 5.8.4 covers chronic PCL injury.

Serial imaging is what makes tunnel widening a finding rather than an
appearance, so the baseline study after surgery is the most valuable image in the
series, which is the position the arthroplasty pages reach as well. 5.7.12 covers
the tunnel assessment.

The compartmental cartilage is the slow verdict on the operation, and it is
usually read by somebody who was not told what the operation was for. 5.11.2
covers the chondral defects.

The useful report gives the interval, the graft's continuity and path, each
fixation point with its bone, the compartments, the corner and the alignment, and
states that the laxity is not an imaging measurement. 5.20.1 covers structured
reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- Continuity and competence come apart in the graft as in the ligament.
- A native ligament heals in continuity and a graft can stretch.
- The baseline post-operative study is the most valuable in the series.
- Compartmental cartilage is the slow verdict on the operation.
- The laxity is not an imaging measurement.
