# OCD

Osteochondritis dissecans, where the imaging question is stability and the
patient's skeletal maturity decides what stability means.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

Osteochondritis dissecans is a subchondral lesion with overlying cartilage that
may separate as a fragment, classically on the lateral aspect of the medial
femoral condyle. 5.11.5 covers osteochondral lesions.

The imaging shows the lesion's extent, the state of the overlying cartilage and
whether the fragment is stable. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

It matters because stability decides management, and skeletal maturity changes
both the prognosis and the thresholds. 5.15.3 covers paediatric OCD.

### How It Is Acquired

Identify the lesion at its classic site and assess its size in two planes. 5.3.4
covers the tunnel view.

Assess the overlying cartilage for continuity, since an intact surface over a
lesion is the favourable pattern. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

Look for fluid signal at the interface between fragment and bed, which is the
instability sign. 5.11.5 covers osteochondral lesions.

Assess the physes and record the skeletal maturity, because it changes the
interpretation. 5.15.2 covers the growth plates.

Look for a displaced fragment elsewhere in the joint. 5.14.6 covers synovial
chondromatosis.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

A subchondral lesion at the classic site with a surrounding rim. 5.11.5 covers
osteochondral lesions.

Intact overlying cartilage, which is the stable appearance. 5.11.1 covers normal
cartilage.

Fluid signal fully encircling the fragment interface, which is the instability
sign. 5.11.5 covers osteochondral lesions.

A displaced fragment with an empty donor bed. 5.3.4 covers the tunnel view.

### Key Learning Points

- The lesion has a classic site on the lateral aspect of the medial condyle.
- The imaging shows extent, overlying cartilage and stability.
- Intact overlying cartilage is the favourable pattern.
- Fluid encircling the interface is the instability sign.
- Skeletal maturity changes the prognosis and the thresholds.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer makes stability and skeletal maturity the two
questions.

The expected points are the classic site and size, the overlying cartilage, the
interface fluid, the skeletal maturity, and the displaced fragment.

Say that stability decides management. 4.17.4 covers osteochondritis dissecans.

Say that a skeletally immature patient has a better prognosis. 5.15.3 covers
paediatric OCD.

Say that the interface fluid sign is not fully specific. 5.11.5 covers
osteochondral lesions.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the lesion is a failure at the subchondral bone
rather than in the cartilage, so the cartilage above it can remain intact while
the bone beneath separates. 5.11.5 covers osteochondral lesions.

Fluid completely surrounding the interface means synovial fluid has tracked all
the way round, which is incompatible with bony continuity. 5.11.4 covers
delamination.

An open physis means a vascular supply and a remodelling capacity that a mature
skeleton lacks, which is the mechanism behind the better prognosis. 5.15.2 covers
the growth plates.

The classic site is where it is because of the loading pattern there, so a lesion
at an atypical site should prompt a different differential. 5.13.5 covers
osteonecrosis.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging stages this lesion and assesses its stability better
than anything else, and its stability sign is imperfect.

Its best contribution is the combination of extent, cartilage continuity and the
interface, which together form the staging. 5.11.5 covers osteochondral lesions.

It is limited because the interface fluid sign also appears with healing tissue,
so stability is inferred rather than established. 5.21.3 covers the cartilage
imaging literature.

size or staging thresholds appear either: those are published quantities and this
environment cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- The failure is in the subchondral bone rather than the cartilage.
- Fluid fully surrounding the interface is incompatible with bony continuity.
- An open physis brings a vascular supply and remodelling capacity.
- The classic site reflects the loading pattern there.
- The stability sign also appears with healing tissue.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level this is a staging study whose output is a management category.

A stable lesion with intact cartilage in a skeletally immature patient is managed
non-operatively, and the imaging establishes all three of those. 5.15.3 covers
paediatric OCD.

An unstable lesion needs fixation or removal, and the fragment's bone quality
decides which. 4.17.4 covers osteochondritis dissecans.

A displaced fragment with a locking knee is urgent. 4.17.2 covers mechanical
symptoms.

A large defect after fragment removal is an osteochondral reconstruction problem
rather than a cartilage one. 5.11.11 covers osteochondral grafts.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is calling a lesion unstable on partial interface fluid.
5.11.5 covers osteochondral lesions.

Skeletal maturity is not recorded, so the prognosis cannot be given. 5.15.2
covers the growth plates.

A normal variant of ossification in a child is read as a lesion. 5.15.7 covers
developmental variants.

The overlying cartilage is not assessed. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

A lesion at an atypical site is called this rather than investigated differently.
5.13.5 covers osteonecrosis.

### Key Learning Points

- A lesion is called unstable on partial interface fluid.
- Skeletal maturity goes unrecorded and the prognosis cannot be given.
- A normal ossification variant is read as a lesion.
- The overlying cartilage is not assessed.
- An atypical site is not investigated differently.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this lesion is staged by imaging criteria whose specificity
does not match the weight the staging carries.

The interface fluid sign is the pivot of every staging system in use, and it
appears both where a fragment has separated and where granulation tissue is
healing one, so the same appearance supports opposite conclusions. 5.21.3 covers
the cartilage imaging literature.

Staging systems were derived on particular sequences and are applied across
protocols, so a stage without its protocol is not comparable. 5.20.5 covers
reporting cartilage lesions.

Skeletal maturity changes both the natural history and the thresholds, and a knee
protocol records it only incidentally through the physes. 5.15.2 covers the growth
plates.

Contrast enhancement has been proposed to separate a healing interface from a
separated one and has not become routine, which leaves the central ambiguity
unresolved. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic resonance techniques.

The disciplined report gives the extent, the cartilage continuity, the interface
appearance and the skeletal maturity as four separate observations, and lets the
stage follow from them rather than replacing them. 5.20.5 covers reporting
cartilage lesions.

### Key Learning Points

- The interface fluid sign supports opposite conclusions.
- Staging systems were derived on particular sequences.
- A stage without its protocol is not comparable.
- A knee protocol records skeletal maturity only incidentally.
- Report the four observations and let the stage follow from them.
