# Popliteofibular Ligament

The hardest structure here to see, so its absence from a report means nothing
and the fibular styloid reports it instead.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

The popliteofibular ligament runs from the popliteus tendon to the fibular
styloid. 2.10.3 covers the popliteofibular ligament.

It is small and is visualised inconsistently, even on a dedicated study. 5.5.9
covers the ligaments.

It matters because it is a principal restraint to external rotation, so it has
more functional weight than visibility. 3.10.5 covers the popliteofibular
ligament function.

### How It Is Acquired

Use thin coronal and axial images through the fibular styloid. 5.5.2 covers the
sports knee protocol.

Trace it from the popliteus tendon towards the styloid. 2.10.2 covers the
popliteus tendon.

Accept that it may not be identifiable, and say so rather than omitting it.
5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Assess the fibular styloid for avulsion, which reports the ligament indirectly.
2.3.2 covers the attachments.

Assess the rest of the corner, since this ligament rarely fails alone. 5.10.3
covers PLC injuries.

### What Normal Looks Like

A short band between the popliteus tendon and the fibular styloid where it can
be seen. 2.10.3 covers the popliteofibular ligament.

An intact fibular styloid with no marrow change. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow
oedema.

An intact popliteus tendon, which the ligament depends on. 2.10.2 covers the
popliteus tendon.

The rest of the corner intact. 2.10.4 covers the posterolateral corner.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

Discontinuity where the ligament is visible. 2.10.3 covers the popliteofibular
ligament.

Avulsion of the fibular styloid, which is the reliable indirect sign. 2.3.2
covers the attachments.

Marrow change at the styloid with no visible fragment. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow
oedema.

Injury of the popliteus tendon, which the ligament arises from. 5.10.2 covers
popliteus injuries.

Oedema around the styloid with the ligament not identifiable. 5.5.9 covers the
ligaments.

### Key Learning Points

- The ligament runs from the popliteus tendon to the fibular styloid.
- It is a principal external rotation restraint.
- It is visualised inconsistently.
- The styloid avulsion reports it indirectly.
- It rarely fails alone.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer admits the ligament may not be seen.

The expected points are the thin images, the trace from the tendon, the
admission of non visualisation, the styloid, and the rest of the corner.

Say that not visualised is a legitimate finding and that omission is not. 5.20.8
covers common reporting errors.

Say that the styloid is where this ligament is reported from. 2.3.2 covers the
attachments.

Say that the dial test reports the function this ligament carries. 4.13.5 covers
the dial test.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the ligament connects a tendon to a bone rather
than bone to bone, so its function depends on the popliteus being intact, and
the two are assessed together. 3.10.5 covers the popliteofibular ligament
function.

Its small size and oblique course mean a standard protocol may not resolve it,
which is why the indirect sign carries the weight. 5.5.2 covers the sports knee
protocol.

A styloid avulsion is specific, because the structures attaching there are this
ligament and the capsule rather than the collateral ligament. 2.10.4 covers the
posterolateral corner.

That makes the fibular head and styloid the most informative bone in the lateral
knee. 2.3.3 covers the clinical importance.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging identifies this ligament inconsistently and reports
it reliably through the fibular styloid.

Its best contribution is the styloid avulsion and the state of the popliteus it
arises from. 2.3.2 covers the attachments.

It is limited because the ligament itself is often not resolvable, so the report
depends on an indirect sign. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no
visualisation rates or dimensions appear either: those are published quantities
from particular series and this environment cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- The ligament connects a tendon to bone.
- Its function depends on the popliteus.
- A standard protocol may not resolve it.
- A styloid avulsion is specific.
- The fibular styloid is the most informative bone laterally.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level a styloid avulsion is treated as a corner injury. 5.10.3
covers PLC injuries.

Non visualisation of this ligament does not downgrade the injury. 5.20.8 covers
common reporting errors.

The dial test and the recurvatum test carry the functional question. 4.13.7
covers the external rotation recurvatum test.

A reconstruction of the corner addresses this ligament's function whether or not
the ligament was seen. 3.10.8 covers the PLC reconstruction biomechanics.

A combined posterior cruciate injury changes the operation. 3.10.9 covers the
combined PCL and PLC injury.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is omitting the ligament rather than reporting it as not
visualised. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Non visualisation is read as absence. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

A styloid avulsion is reported as an avulsion of the collateral ligament. 2.10.1
covers the LCL.

Styloid marrow change with no fragment is overlooked. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow
oedema.

The popliteus is reported separately, as though the two were unrelated. 5.10.2
covers popliteus injuries.

### Key Learning Points

- Omission rather than non visualisation is the error.
- Non visualisation is read as absence.
- The styloid avulsion is misattributed.
- Styloid marrow change is overlooked.
- The ligament and the popliteus are one unit.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this ligament is the one most often absent from the report
and most often relevant to the operation. 3.10.5 covers the popliteofibular
ligament function.

A knee with external rotation laxity and a structurally normal looking lateral
side is the common presentation. 4.13.5 covers the dial test.

The original study is worth re-reading for the styloid rather than re-scanning
for the ligament. 2.3.2 covers the attachments.

After a corner reconstruction this ligament's function is carried by a graft
that does not reproduce it, so the post-operative appearance is of a different
structure. 5.10.6 covers the post-operative PLC imaging.

Cadaveric work defines this ligament's role better than any imaging series does,
which is unusual and worth saying. 3.10.6 covers the posterolateral corner
function.

The useful report states whether the ligament was visualised, the styloid, the
popliteus and the rest of the corner. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- Most often absent from the report and most often relevant.
- Laxity with a normal looking lateral side is common.
- Re-read the original study for the styloid.
- A graft carries the function and not the structure.
- Cadaveric work defines its role better than imaging.
