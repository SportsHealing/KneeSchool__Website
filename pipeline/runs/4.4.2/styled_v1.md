# Antalgic Gait

The commonest abnormal gait in any clinic, and the one that says the least about
which structure is at fault.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

An antalgic gait is a pattern that shortens the time spent loading a painful
limb, and it indicates pain rather than any particular cause of it.

It is the most frequently seen gait abnormality and the least specific, which
makes it a finding to recognise and then look past. 4.4.1 covers the normal
pattern it modifies.

Its value is that it is objective. A patient who limps is in pain in a way that
a pain score does not demonstrate. 4.2.10 covers severity in the history.

### How It Is Performed

Watch the patient walk a distance long enough for the pattern to repeat several
times.

Compare the time spent on each foot, which is the defining observation: the
stance time on the painful side is shorter.

Note the step length, which shortens on both sides because the pattern changes
the whole cycle rather than one side of it.

Note whether the trunk moves to reduce the load, which is a compensation rather
than part of the pattern, and whether a walking aid is being used. 4.4.5
covers a related trunk pattern.

Ask where it hurts while they walk, because the location during loading is more
useful than the location at rest. 4.2.8 covers pain location.

### What a Positive Means

A shortened stance time on one side indicates pain on loading that side, and
nothing more specific than that.

An antalgic pattern with an effusion suggests the pain is intra-articular, and
the effusion is the more useful finding. 4.3.8 covers swelling.

An antalgic pattern with a normal knee examination raises the hip, the spine and
the foot, since all three produce it. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

An antalgic pattern that is worse on stairs or on slopes points at the
patellofemoral joint or at a loaded flexion position. 4.2.11 covers aggravating
factors.

An antalgic pattern with a thrust is two findings, and the thrust is the one
that changes the plan. 4.5.7 covers dynamic alignment.

### Key Learning Points

- The pattern shortens the time spent loading the painful limb.
- It indicates pain and not a cause, which makes it a finding to recognise and look past.
- Step length shortens on both sides, because the whole cycle changes.
- An antalgic pattern with a normal knee examination raises the hip, the spine and the foot.
- With a thrust present, the thrust is the finding that changes the plan.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva the antalgic gait is often the first thing a candidate names, and the
useful answer says so and then moves on to what else is in the gait.

The expected points are the shortened stance time, the shortened step length,
the trunk compensation, the aid, and then a deliberate search for a thrust or an
avoidance pattern underneath it. 4.4.3 covers the avoidance pattern.

Say that the pattern is not localising and say what you would do next, because
that is what distinguishes a description from an assessment.

Say that you would examine the hip in any antalgic gait with an unremarkable
knee, since hip pathology referring to the knee is a standard trap. 4.2.15
covers referred pain.

Say that you would ask about night pain and rest pain, because an antalgic gait
with those raises a different differential. 4.2.13 covers night pain.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that loading a joint produces pain proportional to the
load and the duration, so reducing stance time reduces both. 3.14.2 covers the
stance phase.

The pattern is bilateral in its effect because the gait cycle is a loop, so
shortening one stance phase shortens the contralateral step length, which is why
the pattern is sometimes described as symmetrically abnormal.

Pain also inhibits the quadriceps, so a long standing antalgic gait is
accompanied by quadriceps wasting, and the two findings together indicate
duration. 4.3.9 covers wasting.

Where pain and instability coexist, the antalgic pattern usually dominates the
gait, which is why an avoidance pattern can be hidden under it and has to be
looked for once the pain settles. 4.4.3 covers it.

### How Good It Is

The antalgic gait is the most reliably recognised abnormal gait, and
recognising it contributes almost nothing to the diagnosis.

It is sensitive for pain on loading and specific for nothing, so its role is
confirming that the complaint is real and prompting a search for its source.

It is the gait abnormality most affected by the clinic environment, since a
patient walking a short distance on a flat floor in comfortable shoes may not
show it at all.

It masks other patterns, which is the practical reason to re-examine the gait
once pain has been treated. 4.4.7 covers post-operative gait.

and the comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- Reducing stance time reduces both the magnitude and the duration of the load.
- Shortening one stance phase shortens the contralateral step length.
- A long standing antalgic gait comes with quadriceps wasting, and together they indicate duration.
- An avoidance pattern can hide under an antalgic one and has to be looked for later.
- It is the most reliably recognised abnormal gait and contributes least to the diagnosis.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level an antalgic gait establishes that there is something to treat
and contributes nothing to what.

Its most useful role is as an outcome measure. A gait that stops being antalgic
after a treatment has demonstrated that the treatment addressed the pain, and
that is more convincing than a change in a score.

Its second role is as a reason to delay. A knee that is acutely painful enough
to produce a marked antalgic gait is a knee whose ligament examination is
unreliable, and the plan is made later or under anaesthesia. 4.14.6 covers that
setting.

Its third role is to raise the differential. An antalgic gait with rest pain,
night pain or a systemic history is not a mechanical presentation and the
assessment changes. 4.2.14 covers inflammatory pain.

Beyond those, the finding is a prompt rather than a contribution.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is stopping at it, which means recording a limp and not
looking for the pattern underneath.

The second is attributing it to the knee in front of you.

The third is treating it as a severity measure, which it is only loosely.

The fourth is examining a markedly antalgic knee's ligaments and recording the
result as reliable. 4.13.4 covers the assessment that suffers most.

The fifth is using it as an outcome without having recorded it at the start.
4.1.8 covers the record.

### Key Learning Points

- It establishes there is something to treat and contributes nothing to what.
- A gait that stops being antalgic after treatment is persuasive evidence the pain was addressed.
- A markedly antalgic knee's ligament examination is unreliable.
- An antalgic gait with rest or night pain is not a mechanical presentation.
- Using it as an outcome requires having recorded it at the start.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In referred practice a persisting antalgic gait after a technically sound
operation is the commonest presentation of a dissatisfied patient, and the
gait is the objective part of it.

A patient with a competent reconstruction, a normal laxity examination and a
persisting limp has pain rather than instability, and the two need opposite
investigations. 4.14.3 covers the pattern that is instability.

An antalgic gait after an arthroplasty or a reconstruction raises low grade
infection, and in a knee with no other explanation it is worth excluding.
4.3.6 covers the skin.

A patient whose antalgic gait is intermittent and whose examination is normal is
worth observing on more than one occasion, because a single clinic observation
is a small sample of a variable problem.

In the chronically deficient knee the appearance of an antalgic pattern where
there was previously an avoidance pattern indicates compartment change rather
than ligament failure, and it is the gait that sees it first. 4.11.6 covers that
progression.

In medicolegal work the presence or absence of an antalgic gait is one of the
most frequently disputed observations, and recording the distance walked and the
conditions makes the observation defensible. 4.23.7 covers that setting.

### Key Learning Points

- A persisting limp with a normal laxity examination is pain, not instability.
- An antalgic gait after arthroplasty or reconstruction raises low grade infection.
- An intermittent limp with a normal examination deserves more than one observation.
- An antalgic pattern replacing an avoidance pattern indicates compartment change.
- Recording the distance walked and the conditions makes the observation defensible.
