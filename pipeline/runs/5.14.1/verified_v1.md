# Synovitis

Thickened synovium with an effusion, which is where every disease in this
chapter ends up and which names none of them.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

Synovitis is thickening and inflammation of the joint lining, usually with an
effusion. 5.5.11 covers the synovium.

Magnetic resonance imaging shows the thickened lining and the fluid, and
separates them reliably only with contrast. 5.2.13 covers the T1 imaging.

It matters because it is the common end appearance of every condition in this
chapter and of mechanical irritation too, so the finding names nothing by
itself. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

### How It Is Acquired

Use fluid sensitive sequences to show the effusion and its distribution. 5.2.17
covers the STIR imaging.

Add contrast when the question is how much of what you see is thickened lining.
5.2.13 covers the T1 imaging.

Use a gradient echo sequence where a blood product is suspected. 5.2.18 covers
the gradient echo imaging.

Record the distribution, since uniform and nodular behave differently. 5.14.5
covers the PVNS and TGCT.

Record the bone and the cartilage, because erosion separates the inflammatory
causes. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

### What Normal Looks Like

A thin synovial lining not separately visible on most sequences. 5.5.11 covers
the synovium.

A small physiological amount of joint fluid. 4.3.8 covers swelling.

No erosion at the bare areas of bone inside the capsule. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

No signal loss suggesting haemosiderin. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

Uniform thickening of the lining with an effusion. 5.14.2 covers rheumatoid
arthritis.

Nodular thickening, which narrows the differential sharply. 5.14.5 covers the
PVNS and TGCT.

Signal loss on gradient echo images, which points at blood products. 5.2.18
covers the gradient echo imaging.

Bodies within the fluid, which point at cartilage or at loose fragments. 5.14.6
covers synovial chondromatosis.

Erosion at a bare area, which marks an inflammatory arthropathy rather than
irritation. 5.14.3 covers psoriatic arthritis.

### Key Learning Points

- Synovitis is thickening of the lining with an effusion.
- Contrast separates thickened lining from fluid.
- The finding is the end appearance of many conditions.
- Distribution narrows the differential more than severity does.
- Erosion marks an inflammatory cause.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer uses the distribution and the company the finding
keeps.

The expected points are the fluid sensitive sequences, contrast where it helps,
the gradient echo sequence, the distribution, and the bone.

Say that the finding is a sign rather than a diagnosis. 5.20.1 covers structured
reporting.

Say that mechanical irritation produces the same appearance as early
inflammatory disease. 5.1.2 covers clinical examination versus imaging.

Say that aspiration answers questions no sequence can. 5.4.17 covers the
aspiration.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the synovium has a limited repertoire, so a wide
range of insults produce thickening, effusion and hyperaemia, and the
discriminating features are what else is present. 5.5.11 covers the synovium.

Four substances carry their own signal and are therefore diagnostic when seen:
haemosiderin, cartilage, crystal and gas. 5.14.4 covers crystal arthropathy.

Erosion and new bone separate the inflammatory arthropathies from each other on
pattern. 5.14.3 covers psoriatic arthritis.

Infection shares the appearance with all of them and is excluded by aspiration
rather than by imaging. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging shows synovitis better than any other modality and
identifies its cause only when a second feature is present.

Its best contribution is the extent and the distribution, with the state of bone
and cartilage alongside. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

It is limited because thickening is non specific, because separating lining from
fluid needs contrast, and because infection cannot be excluded. 5.14.7 covers
septic arthritis.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no synovial
thickness values or enhancement ratios appear either: those are published
quantities measured differently between studies and this environment cannot
check them.

### Key Learning Points

- The synovium has a limited repertoire of responses.
- Four substances carry their own signal and are diagnostic.
- Erosion and new bone separate the arthropathies.
- Infection shares the appearance with everything here.
- Aspiration rather than imaging excludes infection.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the finding changes a plan once a cause is attached, and until
then it generates investigations rather than decisions. 5.1.3 covers choosing
the correct investigation.

A nodular pattern with signal loss is an operative diagnosis. 5.14.5 covers the
PVNS and TGCT.

Uniform thickening with erosion belongs to rheumatology before surgery. 5.14.2
covers rheumatoid arthritis.

Synovitis with established joint space loss is part of the arthritis rather than
a separate problem. 5.20.6 covers reporting osteoarthritis.

An acute hot knee is aspirated whatever the scan shows. 5.4.17 covers the
aspiration.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting synovitis as a diagnosis. 5.20.8 covers common
reporting errors.

A large effusion is read as severe disease, when volume and activity differ.
4.3.8 covers swelling.

Thickened lining is read as fluid on sequences without contrast. 5.2.13 covers
the T1 imaging.

Post-operative synovial thickening is read as recurrent disease. 5.20.7 covers
reporting the post-operative knee.

A popliteal cyst is reported as a separate finding rather than as a sign of the
joint's pressure. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

### Key Learning Points

- Synovitis is reported as a diagnosis.
- Effusion volume is read as disease activity.
- Thickened lining is read as fluid without contrast.
- A post-operative lining is read as recurrence.
- A popliteal cyst reflects the joint rather than itself.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the scan usually arrives reported as synovitis and the
referral asks for an arthroscopy. 5.1.2 covers clinical examination versus
imaging.

A diagnostic arthroscopy for an undiagnosed synovitis is rarely the right next
step, because the fluid and a biopsy can be obtained with less. 5.4.17 covers
the aspiration.

Treated inflammatory disease looks unlike the textbook, so the appearance is
partly a record of the treatment. 5.14.2 covers rheumatoid arthritis.

Quantifying synovial volume has been proposed as an outcome measure and carries
the standardisation problem of every quantitative measure. 5.18.1 covers the
quantitative MRI.

Automated detection reports thickening reliably and inherits the same
differential. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

The useful report gives the distribution, the second feature if there is one,
the bone and cartilage, and the statement that infection is not excluded. 5.20.1
covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- A diagnostic arthroscopy is rarely the right next step.
- Treated disease is partly a record of the treatment.
- Quantified synovial volume carries the usual problem.
- Automation inherits the differential.
- The report should state that infection is not excluded.
