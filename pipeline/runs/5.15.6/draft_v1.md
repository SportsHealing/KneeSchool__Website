# Patellar Instability

The measurements were derived in adults, and in a child the trochlea being
measured is still forming.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

Patellar instability in a child involves the same structures as in an adult, and
those structures have not finished developing. 2.12.1 covers the trochlea.

The trochlear shape that resists displacement is formed partly by the patella
moving against it, so shape and instability develop together. 5.12.1 covers the
trochlear dysplasia.

It matters because the measurements used to describe adult instability were
derived in mature knees, so applying them unchanged to a child can report
dysplasia where there is immaturity. 4.19.4 covers the paediatric patellar
instability examination.

### How It Is Acquired

Image the trochlea on axial images through its proximal part, where the shape is
assessed. 5.12.1 covers the trochlear dysplasia.

Include the cartilage, because in a child the cartilaginous trochlea differs in
shape from the bony one and the cartilage is what the patella runs on. 5.11.1
covers the normal cartilage.

Read the medial restraints along their course, since their attachments sit near
the physis in a growing knee. 5.12.5 covers the MPFL injury.

Read the physis at the femoral attachment, because an attachment injury there is
a physeal question. 5.15.2 covers the growth plates.

Assess the patella's own ossification, which is irregular at some stages. 2.4.2
covers the ossification.

### What Normal Looks Like

A cartilaginous trochlea deeper than the bony outline suggests, which is the
appearance a bone only assessment misses. 5.11.1 covers the normal cartilage.

A patella with an irregular ossification margin, which is development rather
than a fracture. 2.4.2 covers the ossification.

Medial restraints continuous from the patella to the femur. 5.12.5 covers the
MPFL injury.

An open physis near the femoral attachment with no widening. 5.15.2 covers the
growth plates.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

A flat or convex trochlea assessed on the cartilage rather than on the bone
alone. 5.12.1 covers the trochlear dysplasia.

Disruption of the medial restraints, most often at the patellar side. 5.12.5
covers the MPFL injury.

A bone bruise on the lateral femoral condyle with a medial patellar impaction,
which records the dislocation. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow oedema.

An osteochondral fragment from the patella or the condyle, which changes
management. 5.11.5 covers the osteochondral lesions.

A patella sitting high or tilted, reported as an appearance rather than against
an adult threshold. 5.12.2 covers the patella alta.

### Key Learning Points

- The structures are the same as in an adult and are still developing.
- Trochlear shape and stability develop together.
- The cartilaginous trochlea is deeper than the bony outline suggests.
- Adult measurements can report immaturity as dysplasia.
- A lateral condylar bruise with medial patellar impaction records a dislocation.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer distinguishes the cartilaginous trochlea from the
bony one and says why it matters.

The expected points are the cartilage assessment, the medial restraints, the
impaction pattern, the osteochondral fragment, and the caution about adult
thresholds.

Say that the cartilage is what the patella articulates with. 5.11.1 covers the
normal cartilage.

Say that an osteochondral fragment is the finding that changes the plan acutely.
5.11.5 covers the osteochondral lesions.

Say that the physis constrains reconstruction of the medial restraints. 5.15.2
covers the growth plates.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging is the only modality that shows the cartilaginous
trochlea, which in a growing knee is the structure the measurements are meant to
describe.

Its best contribution is that cartilage assessment, since a bony measurement in
an immature knee describes a shape the patella does not run on. 5.12.1 covers
the trochlear dysplasia.

Its second contribution is the osteochondral fragment, which is easily missed
clinically and changes the operation. 5.11.5 covers the osteochondral lesions.

It is limited because the thresholds in use were derived in mature knees and
this page declines to restate them, so the report describes the shape rather
than grading it. 5.16.4 covers the TT-TG measurement.

It is limited again because instability is a movement and the study is static,
which is the same limit the adult chapter records. 5.12.4 covers the patellar
subluxation.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no
trochlear angle, depth, patellar height ratio, offset distance or age appears
either: those are published quantities derived in mature knees and this
environment cannot check them or their paediatric equivalents.

### Key Learning Points

- Only this modality shows the cartilaginous trochlea.
- A bony measurement in an immature knee describes the wrong surface.
- The osteochondral fragment changes the operation and is missed clinically.
- The thresholds in use were derived in mature knees.
- The report describes the shape rather than grading it.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the physis decides which operations are available, and the
cartilage decides whether the shape is the problem.

A soft tissue reconstruction in a knee with an open plate avoids the plate, so
the report gives the attachment's relationship to it. 5.15.2 covers the growth
plates.

A bony procedure that an adult would be offered may be deferred until maturity,
which makes skeletal stage a finding the report should carry. 5.15.1 covers the
normal paediatric anatomy.

An osteochondral fragment moves the decision to the acute phase regardless of
the shape. 5.11.5 covers the osteochondral lesions.

A first dislocation with normal cartilage, intact restraints and a developing
trochlea is the common case, and saying so plainly prevents an operation.
5.1.4 covers the imaging pathways.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is applying an adult threshold to an immature trochlea.
5.12.1 covers the trochlear dysplasia.

The bony trochlea is measured and the cartilage is not assessed. 5.11.1 covers
the normal cartilage.

Patellar ossification irregularity is reported as a fracture. 2.4.2 covers the
ossification.

An osteochondral fragment is missed because the search is for the ligament.
5.11.5 covers the osteochondral lesions.

The attachment's relationship to the physis is omitted, which is the one thing
the surgeon cannot infer. 5.15.2 covers the growth plates.

### Key Learning Points

- An adult threshold is applied to an immature trochlea.
- The cartilage is not assessed.
- Patellar ossification irregularity is reported as a fracture.
- An osteochondral fragment is missed while the ligament is sought.
- The attachment's relationship to the physis is omitted.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this is the page where the chapter's rule about stage has a
measurable cost, because numbers are involved and the numbers do not transfer.

A measurement reported without the stage it was taken at invites comparison with
an adult norm, and the comparison is the error rather than the measurement.
5.16.4 covers the TT-TG measurement.

Shape develops in response to use, so a trochlea described as dysplastic in a
young child may not be the trochlea that child has at maturity, and a report
that implies a fixed deformity overstates what one study can show. 5.12.1 covers
the trochlear dysplasia.

A child who dislocates and then stops is common, so the study's contribution is
often the exclusion of a fragment rather than an explanation of the instability.
5.1.2 covers clinical examination versus imaging.

The useful report describes the cartilaginous and bony shapes separately, the
restraints, the impaction pattern, any fragment, and the physis, and states the
skeletal stage it read against. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- The numbers do not transfer from the mature knee.
- A measurement without its stage invites the wrong comparison.
- Shape develops in response to use.
- A report implying a fixed deformity overstates one study.
- The contribution is often excluding a fragment rather than explaining the knee.
