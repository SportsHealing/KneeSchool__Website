# Gerdy's Tubercle

A single landmark on the anterolateral tibia, what attaches to it, and why it
is the reference point for the lateral side of the knee.

## For Medical Students

### Structure and Location

Gerdy's tubercle is a smooth prominence on the anterolateral surface of the
proximal tibia, below the level of the lateral plateau and lateral to the
tibial tubercle.

It is palpable in most people, and it is found by following the iliotibial
band distally until it stops.

### Relations

The iliotibial band inserts on it. That is its defining attachment.

Below and behind it, the muscles of the anterior compartment arise from the
lateral surface of the tibia, so the tubercle is the upper limit of that
origin.

The anterolateral capsule attaches above it, between the tubercle and the
plateau margin.

The lateral collateral ligament and the biceps femoris do not attach here.
They pass to the fibula, which lies behind and below.

### Function

The tubercle transmits the pull of the iliotibial band to the tibia. Because
the band runs from the pelvis, that pull contributes to lateral stability of
the knee as well as to extension of the hip's abductor mechanism.

Its position in front of the joint's rotational axis means the band resists
internal rotation of the tibia as well as resisting varus.

### Clinical Relevance

Gerdy's tubercle is the standard landmark for the anterolateral side of the
knee. It locates the iliotibial band's insertion, the upper limit of the
anterior compartment, and the starting point for a lateral approach.

It is also the reference used when describing the anterolateral capsular
structures, which is where current argument about rotational control is
concentrated.

### Key Learning Points

- Gerdy's tubercle is on the anterolateral tibia and carries the iliotibial band insertion.
- It is found by following the band distally until it stops.
- The lateral collateral and biceps femoris go to the fibula, not here.
- The band resists internal rotation as well as varus, because the tubercle lies in front of the rotational axis.

## MRCS Level

### Structure and Location

The tubercle is a flattened, slightly raised area rather than a sharp
prominence, and its upper border blends into the anterolateral capsular
attachment while its lower border gives origin to the anterior compartment.

Its relationship to the three other landmarks of the region is what makes it
useful: it lies lateral to the tibial tubercle, distal to the lateral plateau
margin, and anterior to the fibular head.

### Relations

Iliotibial band insertion on the tubercle itself, with the band's deeper
fibres attaching more proximally to the lateral femoral condyle before the
band reaches the tibia. 2.11.1 owns the band.

The anterolateral capsule and whatever structure is accepted within it attach
between the tubercle and the plateau margin.

Tibialis anterior and the extensor group arise below.

The anterior tibial recurrent artery ascends in front of the tubercle to join
the genicular anastomosis.

The infrapatellar branch of the saphenous nerve crosses medial to it, and the
common peroneal nerve lies posterolaterally at the fibular neck, well away
from it.

### Blood Supply and Innervation

The tubercle and the surrounding anterolateral cortex are supplied by the
inferior lateral genicular artery and by the anterior tibial recurrent artery.

The periosteum is innervated by the recurrent branch of the common peroneal
nerve, which also supplies the anterolateral capsule. A painful Gerdy's
tubercle therefore refers in the territory of that nerve.

### Function

The band acts through this single point, so the whole of its tensile load
passes through a small area of cortex.

Because the insertion lies anterior to the axis of tibial rotation and lateral
to the sagittal plane, the band restrains both internal rotation and varus,
and the relative contribution of each changes with flexion angle. 3.7 owns
that.

### Clinical Relevance

The tubercle is the starting point for the lateral approach to the plateau,
where the band is elevated from it to expose the lateral metaphysis.

It is the tibial fixation point in the lateral tenodesis procedures used for
rotational control, and the anatomical argument about those procedures is
about where the tibial and femoral points should be. Section 7 owns the
procedures.

An avulsion of the anterolateral capsule and bone adjacent to the tubercle is
the Segond fracture, associated with anterior cruciate injury. The fracture is
pathology and belongs to Section 6; the attachment that avulses is here.

Because the band inserts here and arises from the pelvis, a problem at the
tubercle can have its cause above the knee.

### Key Learning Points

- The tubercle lies lateral to the tibial tubercle, distal to the plateau margin and anterior to the fibular head.
- The anterior tibial recurrent artery ascends in front of it to the genicular anastomosis.
- The recurrent branch of the common peroneal nerve supplies the periosteum and the anterolateral capsule.
- The whole tensile load of the iliotibial band passes through a small area of cortex here.
- The band is elevated from the tubercle to begin the lateral approach to the plateau.

## FRCS Level

### Relations

The structure that matters most here is the one whose identity is disputed.
The anterolateral capsule between the tubercle and the plateau margin has been
described as a discrete ligament and as a thickening of capsule, and the
description chosen changes what a reconstruction is trying to reproduce.

What is not disputed is the tubercle itself, which is why reconstructions are
described from it.

### Function

Lateral tenodesis works by changing the moment arm of a structure that already
resists internal rotation, rather than by adding a new restraint. The
tubercle's position is what sets that moment arm.

Moving the tibial fixation point away from the tubercle alters the restraint's
direction and therefore the angle at which it is tight, which is the same
principle as the femoral attachment argument in the previous chapter.

### Clinical Relevance

In anterolateral reconstruction the tibial point is the tubercle or close to
it in almost every described technique, and the variation between techniques
is at the femoral end. That asymmetry of opinion is itself informative.

In plateau fracture work the band's insertion is the structure released to
gain exposure and the structure that must be repaired afterwards.

The anatomical literature on the anterolateral structures is quantitative and
contested at once. None of its measurements appears here, and a reviewer
should treat this page's description as the uncontested part only.

### Key Learning Points

- The identity of the anterolateral capsular structure is disputed; the tubercle is not, which is why reconstructions reference it.
- Lateral tenodesis alters the moment arm of an existing restraint rather than adding one.
- Almost every described technique uses the tubercle tibially and varies at the femoral end.
- This page describes the uncontested anatomy only.
