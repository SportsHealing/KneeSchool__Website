# Surgical Scars

The written history of the knee, read off the skin, and the part that decides
what a revision can actually do.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

Scars record what has been done to a knee, and in a patient whose history is
incomplete they are often the more reliable account. 4.2.30 covers previous
surgery as a history item.

They also constrain what can be done next. The soft tissue envelope has a blood
supply, and an incision crossing an old one can leave skin between them without
enough, so the existing scars decide the available approaches.

Reading them is a matter of pattern recognition: where the scar is, how long it
is, which direction it runs, and how well it has healed.

### How It Is Performed

Expose both knees fully and look at the front, the sides and the back of each.

Note each scar's position, length, orientation and quality, and whether it is
mature, widened, tethered or keloid.

Note arthroscopy portal scars, which are small and in predictable positions at
the front of the joint, and count them.

Note the position of any graft harvest scar, since it says which graft was used
and therefore something about the operation. 4.10.11 covers post-reconstruction
assessment.

Ask the patient what each scar was for, and record which ones they can account
for and which they cannot.

Look at the back of the knee and at the thigh and calf, because posterior and
proximal scars are the ones that get missed.

### What a Positive Means

Two anterior portal scars with a vertical scar over the patellar tendon or the
hamstring origin indicates a cruciate reconstruction, and which one indicates
the graft.

A long medial parapatellar scar suggests an arthroplasty or an open procedure
rather than an arthroscopic one.

A lateral scar running towards the fibular head suggests lateral ligament or
corner surgery, and it raises the question of the common peroneal nerve.
4.13.4 covers the corner.

A widened, tethered or adherent scar indicates a healing problem, which changes
the risk of any further surgery through it.

A scar the patient cannot account for is a gap in the history that has to be
filled from the records rather than guessed at.

### Key Learning Points

- Scars are often a more reliable account of what has been done than the history is.
- The soft tissue envelope's blood supply means existing scars decide the available approaches.
- Graft harvest scars say which graft was used and therefore something about the operation.
- A lateral scar towards the fibular head raises the common peroneal nerve.
- A scar the patient cannot account for is a gap to fill from the records.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva scars are an invitation to reconstruct the history, and a candidate
who names the likely operations from the pattern is answering the real
question.

The expected points are full exposure including the back of the knee, the
position, length, orientation and quality of each scar, the portals, the graft
harvest site, and asking the patient.

Say that you would look at the whole limb, since a hamstring harvest scar sits
distal to the knee and a quadriceps harvest scar proximal to it.

Say that you would ask for the operation notes rather than relying on the
pattern, because the pattern narrows the possibilities and does not settle them.

Say that you would assess the skin's mobility over the joint, since a tethered
scar can restrict movement mechanically. 4.7.7 covers contractures.

Say that you would record the scars in a diagram or in words precise enough to
be matched at the next visit. 4.1.8 covers the record.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that the skin around the knee is supplied by a
network whose branches run in predictable directions, so an incision's
orientation determines what it divides.

Two incisions close together leave the skin bridge between them dependent on a
reduced supply, which is the reason a planned approach may be dictated by an
old one rather than by the pathology.

A tethered scar limits skin excursion, and skin excursion is required for
flexion, so a scar can mechanically restrict movement independently of the
joint. 4.7.4 covers flexion assessment.

Previous surgery also alters the structures the examination reads. Scar can
substitute for ligament and give a firm endpoint over a lax joint, which is a
finding this section has described on several pages. 4.12.3 covers one of them.

### How Good It Is

Reading scars is reliable for establishing that surgery happened and unreliable
for establishing what was done, and that distinction is worth keeping.

An arthroscopic portal pattern is recognisable. The operation performed through
it is not, and a diagnostic arthroscopy and a meniscectomy leave the same
scars. 4.9.10 covers the consequence of the second.

A graft harvest scar narrows the possibilities usefully, which makes it the most
informative single scar in a knee with ligament surgery.

Where the scars and the patient's account disagree, the operation notes settle
it, and the examination's job is to notice the disagreement. 4.2.30 covers the
history.

and the comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- Reliable for establishing that surgery happened, unreliable for establishing what was done.
- A diagnostic arthroscopy and a meniscectomy leave the same scars.
- Two incisions close together leave a skin bridge with a reduced supply.
- A tethered scar can mechanically restrict flexion independently of the joint.
- The examination's job is to notice a disagreement between the scars and the account.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the scars decide feasibility, and feasibility is a harder
constraint than indication.

A knee with multiple incisions, thin or adherent skin, and a history of wound
problems may not tolerate the operation its ligament findings indicate, and the
scars are where that is established.

The orientation of existing scars dictates the approach for any revision, and
in a knee with several, the plan may be built around the skin rather than
around the pathology.

A graft harvest scar also tells you what is no longer available, which
constrains a revision reconstruction directly. 4.10.12 covers revision
assessment.

A lateral scar in a knee with an abnormal gait raises a previous nerve injury,
and establishing that before offering surgery is part of the consent rather
than a discovery afterwards. 4.13.4 covers the corner.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is not looking at the back of the knee.

The second is assuming an operation from a scar, which narrows the field and
does not identify the procedure.

The third is not obtaining the operation notes, which is the only reliable
account.

The fourth is examining a knee with surgical scars against the expectations for
a native knee, where the endpoints and the tracks have been altered.
4.12.8 covers that assessment.

The fifth is recording scars present without describing them, which makes the
record useless for the next comparison.

### Key Learning Points

- Scars decide feasibility, and feasibility is a harder constraint than indication.
- In a multiply operated knee the plan may be built around the skin rather than the pathology.
- A graft harvest scar tells you what is no longer available.
- A lateral scar with an abnormal gait raises a previous nerve injury, which belongs in the consent.
- A native knee's expected findings do not apply to a knee with surgical scars.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In revision practice the scars are frequently the finding that changes the
answer, and they are the finding most often recorded as present and not
described.

A knee with three or more incisions and an adherent anterior scar has a
realistic risk of a wound problem that outweighs the gain from a further
ligament reconstruction, and that calculation belongs in the consultation rather
than in the operating theatre.

A patient with a previous infection and a sinus or a puckered scar has a
possibility that has to be excluded before any revision, and the examination is
where it is raised. 4.3.6 covers the skin.

Scars from an osteotomy change the alignment history, so a knee with a lateral
tibial scar and a varus limb has had a correction that did not hold or was
incomplete, and the pre-operative films are needed to interpret it. 4.5.1
covers the mechanical axis.

A graft harvest scar on the contralateral limb is easy to miss and changes the
reconstruction options, which is a reason to expose both legs in a revision
assessment.

In medicolegal work the scars are the least disputable evidence about what was
done, and a careful description of them at first presentation is worth more
than a summary of the history. 4.23.7 covers that setting.

### Key Learning Points

- Three or more incisions with an adherent anterior scar can outweigh the gain from further surgery.
- A sinus or puckered scar with a previous infection has to be excluded before any revision.
- An osteotomy scar in a varus limb means a correction that did not hold or was incomplete.
- A graft harvest scar on the other limb is easy to miss and changes the options.
- Scars are the least disputable evidence about what was done.
