# Skyline View

The patellofemoral joint seen end on, at one flexion angle out of the range the
joint works through.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

A skyline view projects along the patellofemoral joint with the knee flexed,
showing the patella sitting in the trochlea. 5.2.2 covers the image formation.

It shows the shape of the trochlea, the position of the patella within it and the
patellofemoral joint space. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

It matters because it is the only plain film that shows the patellofemoral
articulation directly, and it shows it at one angle. 5.12.4 covers patellar
subluxation.

### How It Is Acquired

Flex the knee to the protocol's angle and project along the joint, which requires
the beam to clear the thigh. 5.2.5 covers magnification.

Keep the flexion angle consistent between studies, because patellar position
changes through flexion. 5.12.3 covers patellar tilt.

Note that the patella engages the trochlea as flexion increases, so a flexed view
is the view in which it is most likely to look normal. 5.12.4 covers patellar
subluxation.

Assess both knees where instability is the question, since the comparison is
informative. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

Record the flexion angle, because the measurements depend on it. 5.20.8 covers
common reporting errors.

### What Normal Looks Like

A patella centred in a trochlea with a recognisable groove and two facets of
proportionate size.

A congruent joint space on both facets.

A symmetrical appearance between the two knees.

A trochlear sulcus of recognisable depth. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

A patella tilted or displaced laterally within the trochlea. 5.12.3 covers
patellar tilt.

A shallow or flat trochlea. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

Lateral joint space loss with subchondral change. 5.12.7 covers patellofemoral
arthritis.

Patellar osteophyte or a fracture line through a facet. 5.3.12 covers patellar
fractures.

### Key Learning Points

- The view shows the patella sitting in the trochlea, end on.
- It is the only plain film showing the patellofemoral articulation directly.
- It shows one flexion angle out of the working range.
- The patella engages the trochlea as flexion increases.
- The flexion angle must be recorded because the measurements depend on it.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names the flexion angle problem before describing
any finding.

The expected points are the flexion, the consistency between studies, the
engagement with flexion, the bilateral comparison, and recording the angle.

Say that instability happens near extension and this view is taken in flexion.
5.12.4 covers patellar subluxation.

Say that a normal skyline does not exclude instability. 5.12.5 covers medial
patellofemoral ligament injury.

Say that trochlear shape is better assessed on cross sectional imaging. 5.12.1
covers trochlear dysplasia.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the patella is least constrained near extension
and most constrained in flexion, so a flexed view images the joint in its stable
position. 3.12.2 covers the contact areas.

That makes the view good for arthritis, where the question is the articular
surface, and poor for instability, where the question is what happens near
extension. 5.12.7 covers patellofemoral arthritis.

Trochlear dysplasia is a three dimensional shape and the view gives one section
of it. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

Tilt and displacement measured here change with the flexion angle, which is why
the angle is part of the measurement. 5.12.3 covers patellar tilt.

### How Good It Is

The skyline view is a good arthritis view and a poor instability view, and it is
usually requested for instability.

Its best contribution is patellofemoral joint space and articular surface
assessment. 5.12.7 covers patellofemoral arthritis.

It is limited by imaging the joint in its most stable position, and by showing a
three dimensional shape in one plane. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no tilt
angles, congruence angles or flexion angles appear either: those are published
quantities tied to specific conventions and this environment cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- The patella is least constrained near extension and most in flexion.
- The view images the joint in its stable position.
- Good for arthritis, poor for instability.
- Trochlear dysplasia is three dimensional and this is one section.
- Measurements change with the flexion angle.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level this view supports the arthritis decision and should not carry
the instability one.

Isolated patellofemoral arthritis with preserved tibiofemoral compartments is a
specific operative decision, and this is the view it is made on. 5.12.7 covers
patellofemoral arthritis.

A flat trochlea seen here is a real finding and the degree of dysplasia needs
cross sectional assessment. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

Instability assessment needs the measurements this view cannot give, including
the tibial tuberosity position and patellar height. 5.16.4 covers the
tuberosity to groove measurement.

A normal skyline in a patient with recurrent dislocation is a common and
unremarkable combination. 5.12.4 covers patellar subluxation.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is excluding instability on a normal skyline. 5.12.4 covers
patellar subluxation.

The flexion angle differs between studies and the tilt appears to change. 5.12.3
covers patellar tilt.

Trochlear depth is graded from one section. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

Lateral facet joint space loss is read without the other compartments. 5.3.1
covers the anteroposterior view.

The view is requested where cross sectional imaging was needed. 5.1.3 covers
choosing the correct investigation.

### Key Learning Points

- Instability is excluded on a normal skyline.
- Differing flexion makes tilt appear to change.
- Trochlear depth is graded from one section.
- Lateral facet loss is read without the other compartments.
- The view is requested where cross sectional imaging was needed.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the skyline view is a well established film whose
measurements have largely been superseded and are still quoted.

Tilt and congruence angles were defined on this view and their thresholds derive
from cohorts imaged at a particular flexion angle, which is rarely the angle used
now. 5.21.4 covers the patellofemoral imaging literature.

The measurements that drive instability surgery are made on cross sectional
imaging, so the plain film's role has narrowed to arthritis without the
conventions following. 5.16.4 covers the tuberosity to groove measurement.

Dynamic patellar tracking is the question the clinic actually has, and no static
view answers it. 5.18.6 covers dynamic magnetic resonance imaging.

Ultrasound and dynamic magnetic resonance imaging both address that and neither
is routine, so the honest position is that the common question has no routine
answer. 5.4.14 covers patellar tracking.

The useful stance is to read this view for the articular surface and to go
elsewhere for position and tracking. 5.12.7 covers patellofemoral arthritis.

### Key Learning Points

- The angle thresholds derive from a flexion angle rarely used now.
- The measurements driving instability surgery are made cross sectionally.
- The plain film's role has narrowed without the conventions following.
- Dynamic tracking is the clinic's question and no static view answers it.
- Read this view for the articular surface and go elsewhere for position.
