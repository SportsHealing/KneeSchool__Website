# Digital Badges

Small images that record what you have completed, what each one would stand
for, and why none can be issued yet.

## For Young Learners

### What This Is

A digital badge is a small image that records that you finished something.
Each one carries a name, a date and a link to the criteria it was awarded
against.

Badges are a record rather than a reward. The link to the criteria is the part
that makes them worth anything, because it lets anyone check what the badge
actually required.

No badge can be issued yet. Issuing one needs somewhere to keep your progress,
and that depends on the same accounts and the same data protection work set
out on the School Leaderboards page.

### What It Covers

Six badges are planned, each tied to something already on the site.

Knee Explorer. Chapters 0.1 and 0.2 completed and the certificate questions
answered.

Sports Scientist. Chapter 0.2 completed, including warming up and injury
recognition.

Young Investigator. One project carried out using chapter 0.5, with a method
written up.

Paper Reader. One research paper read using the method on page 0.5.3.

Careers Explorer. Chapter 0.3 completed.

Applicant. Chapter 0.4 completed.

Each badge would link to the pages it covers, so the criteria are checkable
rather than a claim.

### How to Earn It

The design is that a badge is issued by the person who confirmed the work. A
teacher, a coach or a parent, in the same way the Knee Explorer Certificate is
awarded.

That keeps an adult in the loop and avoids the site having to decide whether a
child has done something, which it has no way of knowing.

Until badge files exist, the certificate route works for the first badge. The
other five have criteria on this page and nothing stops a teacher recording
them by hand.

### Rules and Fair Play

A badge records completion, not a mark. There is no grade attached and no
ranking between holders.

Criteria are published before any badge is issued, and a badge whose criteria
are not published is worthless.

A badge carries no personal data beyond the name of the holder, and sharing it
is always the holder's choice rather than something that happens
automatically.

A badge is never awarded for spending time on the site, or for visiting pages,
or for anything other than demonstrating that you understood something.
Rewarding time spent would be designing the site to keep children on it, which
the Age Appropriate Design Code treats as a harm.

### Where to Find Out More

first badge.

The School Leaderboards page sets out the data protection work that both

The build status page records what is outstanding.

### Key Learning Points

- A badge is a record of completion with a public link to its criteria.
- Six badges are planned, each tied to a chapter already on the site.
- None can be issued yet, because progress tracking does not exist.
- Badges would be awarded by a teacher, coach or parent rather than by the site.
- No badge is ever awarded for time spent, because rewarding time spent is a recognised harm.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.
