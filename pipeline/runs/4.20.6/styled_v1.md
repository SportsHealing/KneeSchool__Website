# MPFL Reconstruction

Examining a knee after the medial patellar restraint has been rebuilt, where
over tightening and under tightening produce opposite findings.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

After a medial patellofemoral ligament reconstruction the examination is
checking the patellar translation, the tracking, the range and whether the
apprehension has gone. 4.15.7 covers the medial restraint assessment.

The examination is looking for two opposite failures, which are residual
instability and a patella held too tightly. 4.15.4 covers patellar glide.

It matters that the underlying anatomy is usually unchanged, so the trochlear
shape and the alignment still matter. 4.15.8 covers trochlear dysplasia.

### How It Is Performed

Establish what was done, with what graft, whether anything else was corrected
and when. 4.2.30 covers previous surgery.

Look at the scars, which indicate the graft and any concurrent procedure. 4.3.7
covers surgical scars.

Assess the effusion and the range, since stiffness after this operation is a
recognised problem. 4.7.10 covers the post operative range assessment.

Assess the patellar translation laterally, comparing it with the other side and
with the preoperative record. 4.15.4 covers patellar glide.

Assess the medial translation, because a patella that will not move medially has
been tethered. 4.15.4 covers patellar glide.

Assess the patellar tilt, since an over tightened reconstruction tilts the
patella medially. 4.15.5 covers patellar tilt.

Assess apprehension, which should have resolved. 4.15.3 covers the apprehension
test.

Watch the tracking through the range for a residual lateral deviation. 4.15.1
covers patellar tracking.

### What a Positive Means

Reduced lateral translation, no apprehension, a full range and normal tracking
is the expected result.

Persistent lateral translation with apprehension indicates the reconstruction
has not restrained the patella, or the underlying anatomy dominates. 4.15.8
covers trochlear dysplasia.

Reduced medial translation with medial tilt indicates an over tightened
reconstruction, which produces medial pain and a stiff patella. 4.15.5 covers
patellar tilt.

A restricted flexion range after this operation suggests the graft is tight in
flexion. 4.7.4 covers the flexion assessment.

Anterior pain with a stable patella raises the medial facet loading that over
tightening produces. 4.6.5 covers patellar tenderness.

### Key Learning Points

- The examination looks for residual instability and for over tightening.
- Medial translation is assessed as well as lateral translation.
- A medially tilted patella suggests over tightening.
- Restricted flexion suggests the graft is tight in flexion.
- The underlying trochlear shape is usually unchanged.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva this is the post patellar stabilisation station and the expected answer
tests both directions of translation.

The expected points are the operation and the graft, the scars, the effusion, the
range, the lateral and the medial translation, the tilt, the apprehension and
the tracking.

Say that you would assess medial translation, and give the reason. 4.15.4 covers
patellar glide.

Say that you would assess the trochlear shape and the alignment, since the
reconstruction does not change either unless they were addressed. 4.15.8 covers
trochlear dysplasia.

Say that you would record the preoperative findings for comparison. 4.1.8 covers
examination documentation.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that the medial restraint resists lateral translation
near extension, where the trochlea does not yet contain the patella. 3.12.5
covers the function of the medial restraint.

A reconstruction that is too tight raises the medial facet load, which is why
over tightening produces pain rather than instability. 3.12.3 covers contact
pressures.

A reconstruction cannot compensate for a severely dysplastic trochlea, which is
why the shape governs the expected result. 3.12.4 covers trochlear function.

A graft fixed at the wrong length behaves differently through the range, which is
why flexion is tested as well as translation. 3.12.1 covers patellar tracking.

### How Good It Is

The examination establishes the restraint and the tethering reliably, because
both are direct findings.

Its best contribution is the pair of translation tests, which catch both failure
modes in one assessment. 4.15.4 covers patellar glide.

It cannot assess graft position or fixation.

Cross sectional imaging assesses the anatomy and the graft, so the examination
establishes the function. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 023,
and the comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- The medial restraint works near extension, before the trochlea contains the patella.
- An over tightened graft raises the medial facet load and produces pain.
- A reconstruction cannot compensate for a severely dysplastic trochlea.
- A graft fixed at the wrong length behaves differently through the range.
- The pair of translation tests catches both failure modes.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the examination separates residual instability, over
tightening, stiffness and an unaddressed anatomical cause.

Residual instability with a dysplastic trochlea or a torsional abnormality is a
different problem from a failed graft, and the examination raises the
distinction. 4.19.7 covers torsional assessment.

Medial pain with reduced medial translation is over tightening, and the
examination is the only way that is identified. 4.15.5 covers patellar tilt.

Stiffness with a stable patella is a rehabilitation or a graft tension problem,
and the range through flexion separates them. 4.7.4 covers the flexion
assessment.

Anterior pain with a stable patella after this operation raises patellofemoral
cartilage damage from the original dislocations. 4.17.5 covers articular
cartilage defects.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is assessing lateral translation only, so over tightening is
missed. 4.15.4 covers patellar glide.

Translation is compared against the other knee, which is usually abnormal too in
this group. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

Apprehension reported after the operation is attributed to instability when it
can be anxiety. 4.15.3 covers the apprehension test.

Anterior pain is attributed to the graft when the cartilage was damaged at the
first dislocation. 4.2.22 covers the dislocation event.

An unaddressed torsional or trochlear abnormality is attributed to graft
failure. 4.15.8 covers trochlear dysplasia.

### Key Learning Points

- Assessing lateral translation only misses over tightening.
- The opposite knee is usually abnormal too in this group.
- Apprehension after the operation can be anxiety rather than instability.
- Cartilage damage from the first dislocation causes later anterior pain.
- Unaddressed anatomy is attributed to graft failure.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the examination has to decide whether a symptomatic
stabilisation failed, was over tightened, or was the wrong operation.

The three present differently and overlap, since a tight graft in a dysplastic
knee can give both pain and residual instability. 4.15.8 covers trochlear
dysplasia.

Graft tension is assessed through translation at one or two angles, and the
graft's behaviour through the whole range is what matters. 4.15.1 covers
patellar tracking.

Patellar cartilage damage accumulates with each dislocation, so the pain after a
successful stabilisation is often not about the stabilisation. 4.17.5 covers
articular cartilage defects.

Torsional and coronal contributions are not corrected by this operation, and
their persistence is read as graft failure. 4.19.7 covers torsional assessment.

The examination cannot assess the graft's fixation or its tunnel position, which
determine the result more than the tension assessed clinically. 4.15.7 covers
the medial restraint assessment.

### Key Learning Points

- Failure, over tightening and the wrong operation overlap in presentation.
- Translation at one or two angles does not describe the whole range.
- Cartilage damage from previous dislocations explains much of the later pain.
- Uncorrected torsional and coronal contributions are read as graft failure.
- Fixation and tunnel position matter more than clinically assessed tension.
