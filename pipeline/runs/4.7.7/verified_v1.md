# Contractures

A knee that has shortened around its own position, and the reason prevention
beats treatment.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

A contracture is a restriction of the passive range caused by shortening of the
soft tissues around the joint rather than by anything inside it.

The commonest is a flexion contracture, where the posterior capsule shortens
because the knee has been held flexed. 4.5.4 covers flexion deformity.

Assessing it means establishing the passive range, the end feel and the duration,
because a contracture that is weeks old behaves differently from one that is
months old. 4.7.5 covers the end feel.

### How It Is Performed

Measure the passive extension and flexion with the patient relaxed and the limb
supported. 4.7.2 covers passive motion.

Note the end feel, since a leathery resistance that gives slightly under
sustained pressure is the characteristic finding of a contracture. 4.7.5 covers
it.

Distinguish it from a firm abrupt block, which is mechanical, and from a pain
limited range, which is irritability. 4.9.8 covers the mechanical cause.

Establish how long the knee has been in its restricted position, from the
history and from the records.

Assess the hip, since a hip flexion contracture produces an apparent knee
flexion contracture in standing. 4.4.5 covers a related pattern.

### What a Positive Means

A leathery end feel with a passive extension deficit indicates a flexion
contracture.

A contracture of recent onset in a knee that has been immobilised or effused is
usually reversible with movement and time. 4.8.2 covers the effusion.

A contracture of long standing is less reversible, which is why the duration is
part of the assessment rather than a historical detail.

A global contracture limiting both extension and flexion indicates
arthrofibrosis, which has a less certain outcome than an isolated extension
deficit. 4.7.10 covers post-operative range.

A contracture in a knee listed for a ligament reconstruction is a reason to
delay the operation rather than a reason to proceed carefully. 4.7.2 covers the
range.

### Key Learning Points

- A contracture is soft tissue shortening rather than anything inside the joint.
- The leathery end feel giving under sustained pressure is the characteristic finding.
- A recent contracture in an immobilised or effused knee is usually reversible.
- A global contracture limiting both directions is arthrofibrosis.
- A contracture before a reconstruction is a reason to delay the operation.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva the answer separates the three causes of a restricted passive range,
and the end feel is how it does so.

The expected points are the passive measurements, the end feel, the duration,
the distinction from a block and from irritability, and the hip.

Say that prevention is the main treatment, because a contracture that has not
formed does not need releasing, and early movement is what prevents it.

Say that you would delay a reconstruction in a contracted knee, and say why.
4.7.2 covers the range.

Say that you would assess the quadriceps, since a contracted knee has a wasted
quadriceps and the two have to be addressed together. 4.3.9 covers wasting.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that soft tissue adapts its length to the position it
is held in, so a knee held flexed shortens its posterior structures over weeks.
4.7.2 covers passive motion.

That adaptation is viscoelastic at first and becomes structural with time, which
is the basis for both the leathery end feel and the value of early movement.
4.7.5 covers the end feel.

An effusion holds the knee flexed, which is why an untreated effusion produces a
contracture and why addressing the effusion is part of preventing one.
4.8.2 covers it.

Arthrofibrosis is a more generalised process involving the whole capsule and the
fat pad, which is why it limits both directions and why its outcome is less
predictable. 4.7.10 covers the post-operative knee.

### How Good It Is

The diagnosis of a contracture is clinical and reliable when the passive range
and the end feel are both recorded, and unreliable when only the angle is.
4.7.5 covers the end feel.

The distinction from a mechanical block is the part that changes the treatment
and the part that depends most on the examiner.

The duration is obtained from the history and the records rather than from the
examination, which makes the record part of the diagnosis. 4.1.8 covers it.

Imaging contributes little to a contracture and a great deal to a mechanical
block, which is why the clinical distinction has to come first. 4.1.6 covers
that relationship.

and the comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- Soft tissue adapts its length to the position the joint is held in.
- The adaptation is viscoelastic at first and structural with time.
- An untreated effusion holds the knee flexed and produces a contracture.
- Arthrofibrosis involves the whole capsule, which is why it limits both directions.
- The duration comes from the record, which makes the record part of the diagnosis.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level a contracture decides timing, feasibility and occasionally the
operation itself.

A knee with a flexion contracture cannot be reconstructed to a normal ligament
length, so the contracture is treated first and the ligament afterwards.
4.7.2 covers the range.

Early movement after any knee operation is what prevents a contracture, which
makes this a page about prevention rather than treatment.

An established contracture after a reconstruction needs the mechanical causes
excluded before a release, since releasing an impinging lesion is a different
and better operation. 4.7.5 covers the end feel.

A contracture in a knee with a chronically effused joint has a cause that must
be addressed, and releasing without addressing it recurs. 4.8.8 covers chronic
effusions.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is recording an angle without an end feel, which leaves a
contracture and a block indistinguishable.

The second is treating an effusion related held position as a contracture, which
resolves.

The third is releasing a contracture without excluding a mechanical cause.

The fourth is attributing a standing flexion deformity to the knee when the hip
is contracted.

The fifth is operating on a ligament in a contracted knee, which produces a
stiff and tight result.

### Key Learning Points

- A knee with a contracture cannot be reconstructed to a normal ligament length.
- Early movement after any operation is what prevents a contracture.
- An established contracture needs mechanical causes excluded before a release.
- A contracture with a chronic effusion recurs unless the effusion is addressed.
- An angle without an end feel leaves a contracture and a block indistinguishable.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In referred practice the contracture is usually established and the question is
what can still be achieved.

A patient with an isolated extension contracture and a firm end feel may have a
mechanical lesion rather than a contracture, and that is the better diagnosis to
find. 4.7.5 covers it.

A patient with a global leathery restriction has arthrofibrosis, and the
conversation about a partial improvement rather than a normal knee begins with
the examination. 4.7.10 covers the range.

A patient whose contracture has been present for years is unlikely to regain a
normal range, and setting that expectation is more useful than attempting a
release with an implied promise. 4.7.9 covers functional range.

A patient with a contracture and a chronically deficient posterior cruciate has
an abnormal resting position that makes the ligament measurements unreliable,
and that limit has to be stated. 4.11.6 covers that knee.

In medicolegal work the absence of any recorded range in the first weeks after
an injury or an operation is the commonest reason a stiffness claim cannot be
assessed, since the contracture's development cannot be dated. 4.23.7 covers
that setting.

### Key Learning Points

- An isolated extension contracture with a firm end feel may be a mechanical lesion instead.
- A global leathery restriction begins the conversation about partial improvement.
- A contracture present for years is unlikely to regain a normal range.
- A contracture with posterior cruciate deficiency makes the ligament measurements unreliable.
- Without early recorded ranges, the contracture's development cannot be dated.
