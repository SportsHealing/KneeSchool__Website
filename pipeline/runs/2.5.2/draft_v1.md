# Histology

What meniscal tissue is made of, how its collagen is arranged, and why the
arrangement explains the way it tears.

## For Medical Students

### Structure and Location

The meniscus is fibrocartilage: a dense collagen framework with cells
scattered in it and a great deal of water held by the matrix between.

Conventional teaching puts the water content at about 70 per cent of the wet
weight, with collagen making up most of the remaining dry weight and type I
collagen predominating.

The collagen is not arranged randomly. The great majority of the fibres run
circumferentially, following the curve of the C from one horn to the other. A
smaller number run radially, tying the circumferential bundles together.

### Relations

At the surfaces, a thin layer of fibres runs in a mesh rather than in bundles,
which gives a smooth articulating surface.

At the peripheral border the collagen continues into the capsule and into the
coronary ligament, so there is no sharp boundary between meniscus and capsule.

At the horns the circumferential bundles converge and insert into bone through
a transitional zone of fibrocartilage and mineralised fibrocartilage, in the
same way a tendon inserts.

### Function

The circumferential fibres carry the hoop tension that resists the meniscus
being extruded when the wedge is compressed. They are the load bearing
element.

The radial fibres stop the circumferential bundles splitting apart from one
another. They are the reason the meniscus does not simply unravel.

The water held in the matrix makes the tissue stiff under rapid load and
allows it to deform slowly under sustained load, which is why it behaves
differently in a jump and in a long squat.

### Clinical Relevance

The fibre direction predicts the tear pattern. A longitudinal tear runs
between circumferential bundles and leaves the hoop partly intact. A radial
tear cuts across them and breaks the hoop.

That is why a small radial tear can matter more than a large longitudinal one.

Degenerate tissue loses water and the fibre organisation becomes less regular,
which is why an older meniscus tears under loads a young one tolerates.

### Key Learning Points

- The meniscus is about 70 per cent water, with type I collagen predominating in the dry weight.
- Most collagen fibres run circumferentially; a minority run radially and tie them together.
- Circumferential fibres carry hoop tension and are the load bearing element.
- A radial tear cuts the hoop; a longitudinal tear runs between the bundles.
- A small radial tear can matter more than a large longitudinal one.

## MRCS Level

### Structure and Location

Three collagen layers are conventionally described. A superficial mesh at both
surfaces. A lamellar layer beneath it with fibres crossing obliquely. And the
deep central layer, which is the bulk of the tissue and is almost entirely
circumferential.

Radial, or tie, fibres run from the periphery towards the free edge through
the central layer and are more numerous in the posterior horn than elsewhere.

Cells differ by depth. The surface cells are flatter and more fibroblast like.
The deeper cells are rounder, sit in lacunae and resemble chondrocytes, and
are often called fibrochondrocytes.

The matrix between contains proteoglycan, which is present in much smaller
quantity than in articular cartilage but concentrated in the inner part, where
the tissue is loaded in compression rather than tension.

### Relations

The enthesis at each horn has the four zone structure of a direct tendon
insertion: ligament, uncalcified fibrocartilage, calcified fibrocartilage,
bone. That is why a root avulsion is an enthesis failure rather than a tissue
tear.

The peripheral border blends into the capsule, and the perimeniscal plexus
lies in that transitional tissue rather than in the meniscus itself.

The free edge has no vascular or neural supply and no perichondrium, so it has
no mechanism for repair.

### Blood Supply and Innervation

Vessels enter from the perimeniscal plexus and penetrate radially a limited
distance into the peripheral tissue, with a corresponding gradient of
cellularity: the peripheral tissue is more cellular and more vascular, the
inner tissue less of both.

Nerve fibres accompany the vessels. Mechanoreceptors, including Ruffini
endings and Golgi type structures, are concentrated at the horns and in the
peripheral third, which is why the horns contribute to proprioception.

The avascular inner tissue depends entirely on diffusion. That limits its
thickness as much as its healing: nutrition by diffusion only works over a
short distance, which is part of why the free edge is thin.

### Function

The tissue is anisotropic: far stiffer along the circumferential fibres than
across them. Load applied along the fibres is carried; load applied across
them is resisted much less well.

Proteoglycan concentrated inwards means the inner part behaves more like
cartilage and the outer part more like tendon, which matches what each part
does.

Viscoelasticity comes from fluid flow through the matrix. Rapid load gives a
stiff response because the fluid cannot move; sustained load allows creep.

3.6 owns the material properties as numbers.

### Clinical Relevance

Repair relies on the vascular peripheral tissue and on the cellularity that
goes with it. Both fall off towards the free edge, and they fall off together.

Suture holds in circumferentially organised tissue and pulls through tissue
that is degenerate or predominantly radial, which is part of why posterior
horn repairs behave differently from body repairs.

Because the horn insertion is an enthesis, root repair is fixing tendon to
bone and behaves like it: the construct has to survive while the enthesis
reforms.

Allograft and scaffold replacement have to reproduce a circumferentially
organised tissue to transmit load, which is the main reason they are
difficult.

### Key Learning Points

- Three collagen layers: a surface mesh, an oblique lamellar layer, and a deep circumferential bulk.
- Cells grade from fibroblast like at the surface to fibrochondrocytes in the depth.
- Each horn is a four zone enthesis, so a root avulsion is an enthesis failure.
- The tissue is anisotropic: stiff along the circumferential fibres, much less so across them.
- Vascularity and cellularity fall off towards the free edge together.

## FRCS Level

### Relations

The structural relation that governs reconstruction is the continuity between
the meniscus, the capsule and the horn entheses. A meniscus is not a free
body; it is one end of a continuous fibrous system.

A replacement that is not anchored at both horns and not attached peripherally
is not a meniscus, whatever it is made of.

### Function

Because the tissue is anisotropic and the hoop is the mechanism, any repair or
replacement has to restore fibre continuity in the circumferential direction.
Restoring bulk does not restore function.

That is the anatomical argument behind the difficulty of meniscal scaffolds:
growing tissue into a defect is achievable, and growing correctly oriented
load bearing collagen into it is a different problem.

### Clinical Relevance

Tear pattern, location and tissue quality decide repairability, and all three
are histological properties. The decision is made at arthroscopy on what the
tissue looks and feels like.

In allograft transplantation, sizing and both horn fixations matter because
the hoop has to be reconstituted. Section 7 owns the procedure.

Biological augmentation aims at the cellularity gradient on this page rather
than at the mechanics, which is why its effect is on healing rate rather than
on load transmission.

The composition figures on this page are standard teaching values, registered
and unchecked, and they vary with age and with the part of the meniscus
sampled.

### Key Learning Points

- The meniscus is one end of a continuous fibrous system, not a free body.
- Any repair must restore circumferential fibre continuity; restoring bulk does not restore function.
- Repairability is a histological judgement made at arthroscopy.
- Biological augmentation acts on the cellularity gradient, so it affects healing rate rather than mechanics.
