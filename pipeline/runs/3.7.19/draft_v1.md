# Quadriceps Tendon Graft Biomechanics

A thick graft from a large tendon, with or without bone. Its mechanical appeal
is that diameter is a choice rather than a discovery.

## For Medical Students

### The Principle

A larger source tendon allows a graft of chosen size. Where the donor is
small, the graft size is whatever the donor gives.

Thickness matters for strength, and a graft harvested from a thick tendon can
be made as thick as the tunnel requires.

Taking it with or without a bone block is a choice, which changes how it is
fixed.

### At the Knee

The graft is taken from the quadriceps tendon above the patella, optionally
with a bone block from the patella. 2.4.1 covers the patella.

Conventional teaching reports its ultimate failure load as comparable to or
above the other autografts, with a larger cross sectional area available.

Diameter is controllable, which is its main mechanical advantage over a
hamstring graft where the donor tendon may be small. 3.7.17 covers that graft.

It can be harvested with bone at one end, giving bone to bone healing at that
end and soft tissue healing at the other.

### What Changes It

Harvest thickness and width, which set the graft's dimensions.

Whether a bone block is taken, which changes the fixation at that end.

Full thickness or partial thickness harvest, which affects the donor site.

Fixation choice at the soft tissue end.

### Clinical Relevance

Where an adequate diameter cannot be obtained from the hamstrings, this graft
is the usual alternative, which is a mechanical reason rather than a
preference. 3.7.16 covers graft choice.

Its donor site is the extensor mechanism again, with a different distribution
of symptoms from the patellar tendon's. Section 6 covers management.

It has become more common, and the evidence base is smaller than for the other
two autografts.

The choice between the three is a trade, and this one trades a controllable
diameter against a shorter evidence record.

### Key Learning Points

- A larger source tendon allows a graft of chosen size.
- Diameter is controllable, which is the main mechanical advantage.
- Conventional teaching reports ultimate load comparable to or above the other autografts.
- It can be harvested with bone at one end.
- Its donor site is the extensor mechanism, with a different symptom pattern.

## MRCS Level

### The Principle

Cross sectional area is the property this graft offers. Strength scales with
area, so a graft that can be cut to a chosen thickness can be made to a chosen
strength.

A hybrid construct, bone at one end and tendon at the other, has two different
healing interfaces and two different fixation problems in the same graft.

The evidence base is younger than for the other autografts, so mechanical data
is available and long term clinical data is thinner.

### At the Knee

Conventional teaching reports quadriceps tendon graft ultimate load and
stiffness as comparable to bone patellar tendon bone, with greater available
cross sectional area.

Full thickness harvest reaches the suprapatellar pouch and partial thickness
does not, which is a donor site distinction with mechanical and clinical
consequences.

Extensor strength deficit after harvest is reported and conventional teaching
describes it as recovering, with less kneeling pain than bone patellar tendon
bone.

Fixation at the soft tissue end faces the same tendon to bone healing as a
hamstring graft. 3.7.17 covers it.

### What Changes It

Harvest dimensions, which are chosen rather than found.

Full or partial thickness harvest.

Whether a bone block is included and at which end.

Fixation choice at each end.

### How It Is Measured

Time zero tensile testing compares it with the other autografts and with
native tissue.

Construct testing with the intended fixation measures the assembly.

Isokinetic testing measures the extensor deficit after harvest.

Clinical series are shorter and smaller than for the other autografts, which
is the main limitation of the evidence.

### Clinical Relevance

Choose harvest dimensions to the diameter required rather than accepting what
is found.

Distinguish full from partial thickness harvest in the consent and the
rehabilitation.

Expect tendon to bone healing at the soft tissue end.

Read the clinical evidence as shorter and smaller than for the other
autografts.

### Key Learning Points

- Strength scales with cross sectional area, which this graft can supply.
- A hybrid construct has two healing interfaces and two fixation problems.
- Conventional teaching reports load and stiffness comparable to bone patellar tendon bone.
- Full thickness harvest reaches the suprapatellar pouch; partial does not.
- The clinical evidence base is younger and thinner.

## FRCS Level

### At the Knee

Treat this graft as the answer to a diameter problem and as the graft whose
long term evidence is still accumulating. Both are true and they point in
different directions.

Its mechanical case is strong and its clinical case is less mature, which is
an ordinary position for a technique in adoption rather than a criticism of
it.

Conventional teaching reports comparable failure rates to the other autografts
in the series available, with shorter follow up.

### What Changes It

Required diameter and what the hamstrings can supply.

Patient kneeling and extensor demands.

Revision setting, where available graft and tunnel condition dominate.

Surgeon familiarity, which affects harvest quality more than for a simpler
harvest.

### How It Is Measured

Registry data is accumulating and reports comparable early failure rates.

Patient reported outcome captures the donor site difference.

Construct testing establishes the mechanical equivalence at time zero.

Long term comparative data does not yet exist at the volume available for the
other two.

### Clinical Relevance

Use it where diameter cannot be achieved from the hamstrings.

Counsel that its long term comparative evidence is still accumulating.

Account for extensor and kneeling demands in the choice.

Expect harvest quality to depend on familiarity more than for a simpler
harvest.

### Key Learning Points

- It is the answer to a diameter problem with a less mature clinical case.
- Reported failure rates are comparable with shorter follow up.
- A technique in adoption has a strong mechanical and a thinner clinical case.
- Harvest quality depends on familiarity.
- Long term comparative data is not yet at the volume of the other two.

## Fellowship Level

### At the Knee

At fellowship level this graft is often the revision solution. It is an
untouched donor site in a patient whose hamstrings and patellar tendon have
been used, and it supplies the diameter a wide tunnel needs. 3.7.16 covers
graft choice.

It also suits multiligament reconstruction, where graft quantity matters and
several constructs are needed in one sitting.

The constraint is the extensor mechanism, which has a finite amount that can
be taken from it across a patient's surgical history. That is a cumulative
consideration rather than a single operation one.

### What Changes It

Previous harvests and what donor sites remain.

Tunnel width and the diameter required.

Number of grafts needed in one operation.

Extensor mechanism integrity after previous surgery.

### How It Is Measured

Imaging assesses the quadriceps tendon thickness before harvest, which is how
diameter is planned.

Computed tomography measures tunnel width, which sets the diameter required.

Revision series report outcomes with this graft, in small numbers.

No measurement establishes how much extensor tendon a patient can afford to
lose in total.

### Clinical Relevance

Measure tendon thickness on imaging before planning the harvest.

Consider it first in revision where other donor sites are used.

Account for the cumulative extensor cost across a surgical history.

Expect small numbers in the revision evidence for this graft.

### Key Learning Points

- It is often the revision solution: an untouched donor site with controllable diameter.
- It suits multiligament reconstruction, where graft quantity matters.
- The extensor mechanism has a finite amount that can be taken across a surgical history.
- Tendon thickness is measured on imaging to plan the diameter.
- No measurement establishes the total extensor cost a patient can afford.
