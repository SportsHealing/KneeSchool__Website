# When to Tell an Adult

Some knee problems can wait. A few cannot. Here is how to tell the difference.

## For Young Learners

### Function

Who counts as an adult here depends on where you are. A parent or carer. A
teacher. Your coach. A doctor or a physiotherapist. Any of them can help you
work out whether this needs a professional.

What to say is simple, and being specific helps more than being brave. When it
started. What you were doing. Where exactly it hurts. Whether it swelled, and
how quickly. What it stops you doing now.

One thing worth being clear about. Nobody on this site can tell you what is
wrong with your knee, and neither can the internet. This page helps you decide
whether to ask. The asking is the part that matters.

### Clinical Relevance

Young athletes are often reluctant to say anything. You do not want to be
dropped, you do not want to seem soft, and you assume it will settle. Most of
the time it does. Sometimes it does not, and the cost of staying quiet is a
longer time out rather than a shorter one.

Tell someone the same day if any of these happen.

You cannot put weight on the leg after an injury. Not limping, actually
cannot.

The knee swelled up within a few hours of an injury. Fast swelling usually
means bleeding inside the joint.

You heard or felt a pop at the moment of injury, and the knee felt wrong
afterwards.

The knee is stuck and will not straighten, however hard you try.

The knee is hot, red and swollen and you feel unwell or feverish. This one
needs seeing the same day, not tomorrow.

Your foot or lower leg feels numb, cold, or has gone a strange colour after a
knee injury.

Tell someone within a few days if the knee gives way when you are walking or
turning, if pain is stopping you training or sleeping, if it keeps swelling up
after activity, or if something has been sore for more than two or three weeks
and is not improving.

### Key Learning Points

- Cannot bear weight, rapid swelling, a pop, or a locked knee all mean tell someone today.
- A hot red swollen knee with feeling unwell needs seeing the same day.
- Numbness, coldness or colour change in the foot after a knee injury is urgent.
- Giving way, disturbed sleep, or soreness lasting weeks means tell someone soon.
- Say when it started, what you were doing, where it hurts and how fast it swelled.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.
