# Post-Arthroplasty Gait

What a replaced knee does when it walks, and the gap between a satisfied
patient and a normal gait pattern.

## For Medical Students

### The Principle

Replacing the surfaces of a joint removes the painful parts. It does not
replace what was around them.

Ligaments, muscles and years of habit all remain, and they have adapted to the
old joint.

So relieving pain and restoring normal movement are two different outcomes,
and only the first is reliable.

### At the Knee

After knee replacement pain falls and walking speed rises, which is the
operation doing what it is for. 6.80 covers end stage arthritis.

The knee still moves through a smaller range during walking than an uninjured
knee does.

The stance bend is often reduced or absent, and that pattern frequently
persists for years. 3.14.2 covers stance.

Swing flexion also recovers incompletely, which patients describe as the leg
feeling awkward rather than restricted. 3.14.3 covers swing.

Most patients are satisfied despite these differences, which is the honest
shape of the result.

### What Changes It

The pattern of walking before the operation, which largely persists
afterwards.

Muscle strength, particularly the quadriceps, which falls after surgery and
recovers slowly.

The type of implant and how it is aligned. 7.76 covers the principles.

Rehabilitation and how much of it is done. Section 8 covers the programmes.

### Clinical Relevance

A patient can be delighted with a knee that still walks abnormally, and both
facts are true.

Explaining that in advance manages expectations better than promising a normal
knee.

Quadriceps strengthening before and after surgery changes the result. Section
8 covers it.

4.4.7 covers the post-operative gait examination.

### Key Learning Points

- Replacing the surfaces does not replace what is around them.
- Pain falls and walking speed rises after knee replacement.
- Knee excursion during walking stays below normal, often for years.
- Swing flexion recovers incompletely and feels awkward rather than restricted.
- Most patients are satisfied despite these differences.

## MRCS Level

### The Principle

Arthroplasty outcomes are usually reported as pain, function scores and
survivorship. Gait measures something else, and it recovers less.

That divergence is not a failure of the operation; it is a reminder that the
patient's question and the mechanical question differ.

Knowing which question a reported outcome answers is the whole skill of
reading this literature.

### At the Knee

Walking speed, stride length and stance symmetry improve markedly and usually
approach normal within a year.

Sagittal knee excursion during gait remains reduced, and the stance flexion
wave is often flattened or absent years later.

Reduced quadriceps strength persists and correlates with the residual gait
abnormality better than implant factors do.

Stair climbing and descending recover less completely than level walking,
because the quadriceps demand is higher. 3.3.12 covers stair kinematics.

Instrumented implants have measured in vivo tibiofemoral contact force
directly, which is the only such data at the knee and comes only from replaced
joints. 3.14.1 covers the point.

### What Changes It

Preoperative gait and strength, which predict the postoperative pattern
strongly.

Quadriceps recovery, which is the dominant modifiable factor.

Implant design and constraint, which affect kinematics measurably and function
less.

Alignment philosophy, which is debated partly on kinematic grounds. 7.81 and
7.82 cover them.

Rehabilitation intensity and duration.

### How It Is Measured

Three dimensional gait analysis, which shows the residual abnormality and is a
research measurement.

Fluoroscopy, which measures implant kinematics directly and in a few centres.

Instrumented implants, which give in vivo load and exist in a handful of
patients worldwide.

Patient reported outcome measures and ranges of motion, which are what
registries and clinics record.

The measurements that recover well are the ones routinely collected, and the
ones that recover poorly are not.

### Clinical Relevance

Set expectations on function and pain rather than on normal movement, because
the first recovers and the second often does not.

Target quadriceps strength before and after surgery, since it predicts the
residual pattern. Section 8 covers it.

Ask about stairs rather than level walking when assessing recovery.

State which outcome a quoted result refers to, since gait and function diverge
here.

### Key Learning Points

- Speed, stride length and symmetry approach normal; knee excursion does not.
- The stance flexion wave is often flattened or absent years later.
- Residual quadriceps weakness predicts the gait abnormality better than implant factors.
- Stairs recover less completely than level walking.
- The measurements that recover well are the ones routinely collected.

## FRCS Level

### At the Knee

The gait literature's contribution to arthroplasty is an uncomfortable one: it
shows reliably that a successful knee replacement does not restore normal knee
mechanics.

Reduced sagittal excursion, a flattened stance flexion wave and persistent
quadriceps deficit are found years after operations that the patient and the
surgeon both call successful.

Preoperative pattern predicts postoperative pattern strongly, which suggests
much of the residual abnormality is adaptation carried across rather than
created by the implant.

That is the mechanical case for prehabilitation, and the evidence for
prehabilitation changing long term gait is thinner than the mechanical case
is.

Alignment philosophy is argued partly on kinematics, and the kinematic
differences between philosophies are smaller than the differences between
patients. 7.81 and 7.83 cover them. 6.155 covers the controversies.

### What Changes It

Preoperative gait and strength, which are the strongest predictors and are
modifiable before the decision is taken.

Quadriceps recovery, which is slower and less complete than most counselling
implies.

Implant constraint and whether the posterior cruciate is retained, which
changes kinematics measurably. 7.76 covers the principles.

Alignment target, whose kinematic effect is real and small. 7.82 covers
mechanical alignment.

Contralateral limb status, since a bilateral problem has no normal comparator.

### How It Is Measured

Gait analysis, fluoroscopy and instrumented implants, in descending order of
availability and ascending order of directness.

Instrumented implant data is the only direct in vivo knee load measurement in
existence, and the sample is tiny.

Registries measure revision, which is a different and important question.

Patient reported outcome measures have a ceiling effect that conceals the
differences gait analysis finds.

The field's measurements and the field's claims are not well matched, which is
worth saying when quoting either.

### Clinical Relevance

Counsel that the operation reliably relieves pain and does not restore normal
mechanics, which is both honest and usually welcome.

Measure and treat quadriceps strength before surgery, since the preoperative
pattern predicts the postoperative one.

Expect patient reported scores to have a ceiling that conceals residual
abnormality.

Treat kinematic arguments between alignment philosophies as real and smaller
than between patient variation. 7.81 covers one.

Weigh the patient's age, physiology, comorbidities, knee demand, psychological
state and their own ideas, concerns and expectations, because what counts as a
good result here is defined by the patient rather than by the gait laboratory.

### Key Learning Points

- A successful knee replacement does not restore normal knee mechanics.
- Reduced excursion and quadriceps deficit persist after operations everyone calls successful.
- Preoperative pattern predicts postoperative pattern strongly.
- Instrumented implants give the only direct in vivo knee load data, from a tiny sample.
- Patient reported scores have a ceiling that conceals what gait analysis finds.
