# ACL Healing

The cruciate ligament that has healed or is healing, which imaging can describe
and cannot grade.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

Some cruciate tears heal, with fibrous tissue bridging the gap and producing a
continuous ligament of abnormal appearance. 5.7.3 covers the complete tears.

A healed ligament carries increased signal and an irregular outline while being
continuous from femur to tibia. 5.7.1 covers the normal cruciate.

It matters because continuity is not the same as function, and this is the page
where that distinction is the whole subject. 3.7.8 covers anterior stability.

### How It Is Acquired

Establish continuity from attachment to attachment, which is the observation that
distinguishes a healed ligament from a chronic tear. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

Assess the line against the notch roof, because healed tissue can be continuous
and slack. 2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

Measure anterior tibial translation, which is the mechanical observation rather
than the appearance. 5.7.3 covers the complete tears.

Compare with an earlier study where one exists, since the change over time is
more informative than the appearance. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative
knee.

Correlate with the examination, because the question here is function and the
image does not measure it. 4.10.4 covers the Lachman test.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

A continuous ligament with increased signal and an irregular or thickened
outline.

A ligament continuous but lying flatter than the notch roof, which is continuity
with lost length. 2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

Persistent anterior tibial translation despite continuity, which is the
mechanical failure of a structurally healed ligament. 3.7.8 covers anterior
stability.

Resolution of the earlier marrow signal, which dates the injury as no longer
recent. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

### Key Learning Points

- Some cruciate tears heal with fibrous tissue bridging the gap.
- A healed ligament is continuous with increased signal and an irregular outline.
- Continuity is not function.
- A continuous ligament lying flat has healed long.
- Translation is the mechanical observation and the appearance is not.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer separates continuity from function in its first
sentence.

The expected points are continuity on three planes, the line, the translation,
the comparison study, and the examination.

Say that a healed appearance does not establish a stable knee. 3.7.8 covers
anterior stability.

Say that the examination is what grades the result. 4.10.4 covers the Lachman
test.

Say that serial studies carry more than any single appearance. 5.20.7 covers
reporting the post-operative knee.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that healing across a ligament gap produces scar
rather than ligament, and scar has different stiffness, so the restraint is
restored in continuity and not in property. 2.7.3 covers the histology.

A ligament that healed in a lengthened position is continuous and mechanically
useless, which is the appearance that most misleads. 3.7.1 covers the functions
of the cruciate.

The blood supply determines where healing is possible, which is why proximal
tears behave differently from mid substance ones. 2.7.4 covers the blood supply.

Translation measured against the condyles is the closest the study comes to a
functional measurement, and it is made supine and unloaded. 5.18.5 covers weight
bearing imaging.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging shows whether a ligament is continuous and says
little about whether it works.

Its best contribution is continuity, which is a real observation and is
sometimes the question. 5.7.3 covers the complete tears.

It is limited by scar looking similar whatever its mechanical property, and by
the measurement of translation being unloaded. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing
imaging.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and the
comparison is the claim: continuity is read reliably and function is not, which
is a comparison rather than a number.

### Key Learning Points

- Healing produces scar, which restores continuity and not property.
- A ligament healed long is continuous and mechanically useless.
- The blood supply determines where healing is possible.
- Translation is the nearest functional measurement and is made unloaded.
- Continuity is read reliably and function is not.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level a healed cruciate is assessed clinically and the imaging
supports the conversation rather than the decision.

A continuous ligament with a negative pivot shift and a stable knee is a result,
and the imaging confirms what the examination established. 4.10.6 covers the
pivot shift test.

A continuous ligament with a positive pivot shift is a failed heal, and the
examination governs. 3.7.9 covers the pivot shift mechanics.

Non-operative management of a cruciate tear is a decision made on function, and
the follow-up study informs the patient rather than redirecting the plan. 6.1.4
covers the natural history.

A proximal tear with the stump apposed to the femoral footprint is the pattern in
which healing is most discussed, which makes the level of the tear worth
reporting. 2.7.6 covers the femoral footprint.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting a healed ligament as evidence of a stable knee.
3.7.8 covers anterior stability.

Scar signal is reported as a re-tear in a ligament that is continuous. 5.7.2
covers the partial tears.

A ligament healed in a lengthened position is reported as intact without its line
being assessed. 2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

The level of the original tear is not reported, which removes the information
that bears on healing. 2.7.6 covers the femoral footprint.

A single study is read as a trend. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

### Key Learning Points

- A healed ligament is reported as evidence of stability.
- Scar signal is reported as a re-tear.
- A ligament healed long is reported as intact.
- The level of the original tear goes unreported.
- A single study is read as a trend.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level cruciate healing is an active question being asked of
imaging that cannot answer it.

The protocols being tested select patients partly on the appearance of the torn
ligament, and no imaging criterion for a ligament likely to heal is established.
5.21.2 covers the cruciate imaging literature.

Continuity on a follow-up study is used as the outcome measure in several
published series, and continuity is the thing least related to function in this
whole chapter. 3.7.8 covers anterior stability.

A healed ligament's stiffness is the quantity of interest, and no clinical
sequence measures stiffness. 5.18.1 covers quantitative MRI.

Translation measured supine on a relaxed knee will understate the laxity that
matters, so a healed ligament looks better on imaging than on examination. 5.18.5
covers weight bearing imaging.

The honest position is that this field needs a functional measurement and is
using a structural one, and a report should say which it supplied. 5.20.1 covers
structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- No imaging criterion for a ligament likely to heal is established.
- Continuity is used as an outcome and is least related to function.
- Stiffness is the quantity of interest and no clinical sequence measures it.
- Supine translation understates the laxity that matters.
- A report should say whether it supplied a structural or a functional measure.
