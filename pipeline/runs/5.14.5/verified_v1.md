# PVNS/TGCT

A tumour of the synovium that looks like synovitis, and the one condition here
whose deposit gives it a signal signature.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

Pigmented villonodular synovitis, now usually called tenosynovial giant cell
tumour, is a benign proliferative tumour of synovium. 5.5.11 covers the
synovium.

It carries haemosiderin, which produces low signal on every sequence and signal
loss on gradient echo images. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

It matters because that signature separates it from everything else in this
chapter, and because the diffuse form recurs. 5.14.1 covers synovitis.

### How It Is Acquired

Use a gradient echo sequence, because the iron's effect is the diagnostic
finding. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

Use a T1 weighted and a fluid sensitive sequence to show the nodular tissue
against the fluid. 5.2.13 covers the T1 imaging.

Decide whether the disease is localised or diffuse, since the two behave
differently. 5.5.11 covers the synovium.

Map the extent, including the posterior compartment and any extension outside
the capsule. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

Assess the bone for erosion where nodules press on it. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

### What Normal Looks Like

A thin synovial lining with no nodules. 5.5.11 covers the synovium.

No signal loss on gradient echo images. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

No erosion at the capsular attachments. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

A posterior compartment free of soft tissue mass. 5.4.4 covers the posterior
knee.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

Nodular synovial tissue with low signal on T1 weighted and fluid sensitive
sequences alike. 5.2.13 covers the T1 imaging.

Signal loss on gradient echo images, which is the iron. 5.2.18 covers the
gradient echo imaging.

A single localised nodule, often anterior, which is the localised form. 5.5.11
covers the synovium.

Diffuse involvement of the whole lining, the posterior compartment included.
5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

Erosion of bone beside nodules, which is pressure rather than invasion. 5.5.6
covers the bones.

### Key Learning Points

- The tumour carries haemosiderin.
- Low signal on every sequence is the clue.
- A gradient echo sequence shows the iron.
- The localised and diffuse forms behave differently.
- Bone erosion is pressure rather than invasion.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names the signal signature.

The expected points are the gradient echo sequence, the low signal on all
sequences, the localised or diffuse distinction, the extent, and the bone.

Say that this is the only condition here with a specific magnetic resonance
signature. 5.14.1 covers synovitis.

Say that the extent on imaging decides the operation. 5.5.11 covers the
synovium.

Say that the diffuse form recurs, so the follow-up is imaging rather than
discharge. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that repeated small haemorrhages leave iron in the
tissue, and iron disturbs the local magnetic field, which is why the signal is
low everywhere and is lost fastest on a gradient echo sequence. 5.2.18 covers
the gradient echo imaging.

That makes the sequence choice decisive, because a study without a gradient echo
sequence can describe nodular synovitis without naming it. 5.5.1 covers the
standard protocol.

The localised form is excised and usually does not return, while the diffuse form
involves the whole lining including the posterior compartment, which is why
complete excision is difficult. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

Extension outside the capsule changes the approach and is the part most often
under-reported. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging is the best available test for this tumour and is the
only one in this chapter where the appearance names the disease.

Its best contribution is the signature, the distinction between localised and
diffuse, and the extent. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

It is limited because the signature needs the right sequence, because
haemosiderin follows repeated bleeding of any cause, and because the posterior
extent is easy to understate. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no lesion
dimensions or recurrence rates appear either: those are published quantities
from particular series and this environment cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- Iron disturbs the field, so signal is low everywhere.
- A study without a gradient echo sequence can miss the name.
- The localised form is excised and rarely returns.
- The diffuse form involves the whole lining.
- Extension outside the capsule is under-reported.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the imaging decides the operation's extent and its approach.
5.5.11 covers the synovium.

A localised anterior nodule is an arthroscopic excision. 5.14.1 covers
synovitis.

Diffuse disease with posterior involvement needs the posterior compartment
addressed, which the approach has to allow for. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

Extracapsular extension takes the operation beyond the joint. 5.13.7 covers bone
tumours.

Bone erosion and cartilage loss decide whether the joint is salvageable at all.
5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting the tumour as synovitis because no gradient
echo sequence was acquired. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

Haemosiderin from repeated haemarthrosis is read as this tumour. 5.14.1 covers
synovitis.

The posterior extent is understated because the field of view or the attention
stopped anteriorly. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

Pressure erosion is read as malignant invasion. 5.13.7 covers bone tumours.

A localised nodule is reported as a loose body. 5.14.6 covers synovial
chondromatosis.

### Key Learning Points

- Without the right sequence the tumour reads as synovitis.
- Repeated haemarthrosis mimics the signature.
- The posterior extent is understated.
- Pressure erosion is read as invasion.
- A nodule is reported as a loose body.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the disease has usually been excised once and the question
is recurrence. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

Post-operative haemosiderin and scar produce the same low signal as residual
tumour, so the first post-operative scan is a baseline rather than an answer.
5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

That makes the pre-operative extent the single most valuable record, and it is
often not transferred with the patient. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Diffuse disease in a joint with cartilage loss raises whether excision is still
the right operation. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

Metabolic imaging has been used to assess activity in these tumours and is
rarely available. 5.18.7 covers the PET-MRI.

The useful report gives the signature, the form, the full extent with the
posterior compartment named, any extracapsular component, and the state of the
joint. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- Scar and residual tumour share the signal.
- The first post-operative scan is a baseline.
- The pre-operative extent is the valuable record.
- Cartilage loss changes whether excision is right.
- The report should name the posterior compartment.
