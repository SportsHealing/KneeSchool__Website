# Growing Knees

While you are still growing, the weakest part of your knee is not the
ligament. It is the growth plate.

## For Young Learners

### Structure and Location

Your bones are not solid all the way along while you are growing. Near each
end there is a disc of softer cartilage called a growth plate. New bone is
added there, and that is how your bones get longer.

The knee has two of the busiest growth plates in your whole body, one at the
bottom of the thigh bone and one at the top of the shin bone. Between them
they produce roughly two thirds of the length of your leg.

There is another growing spot at the front of your shin, just below the knee,
where the big tendon from your thigh attaches. If you press there and it feels
tender, that is why.

### Function

Growth plates close when you finish growing and turn into ordinary solid bone.
That happens at different ages for different people, usually somewhere in the
mid to late teens, and generally a bit earlier for girls than for boys.

Until then, the growth plate is the softest link in the chain. It is weaker
than the ligaments and the tendons around it.

### Clinical Relevance

That one fact changes everything about young knees.

Take an adult and a fourteen year old, give them exactly the same awkward
landing, and they can end up with completely different injuries. The adult is
more likely to tear a ligament. The fourteen year old is more likely to injure
the growth plate instead, because that is what gives way first.

It is also why growth plate problems do not show up well on an ordinary x-ray.
The growth plate is cartilage, and cartilage does not appear on an x-ray at
all. So a doctor who presses on the bone near your knee and finds it tender
takes that seriously even if the x-ray looks normal.

Growth spurts are worth knowing about too. Bones lengthen before muscles and
tendons catch up, so for a while things are tighter than they were, and knees
often ache during that stretch. It passes.

None of this means you should stop playing sport. It means a knee injury in
someone still growing is checked properly rather than shrugged off.

### Key Learning Points

- Growth plates are discs of cartilage near the ends of growing bones.
- The two at the knee produce about two thirds of the length of your leg.
- They close in the mid to late teens, generally earlier in girls than boys.
- While open, the growth plate is weaker than the ligaments around it.
- Growth plate cartilage does not show on an x-ray, so tenderness is taken seriously.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.
