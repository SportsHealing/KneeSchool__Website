# T1 Imaging

The sequence that shows anatomy and marrow, and the one fluid hides in.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

A T1 weighted image takes its contrast from how quickly tissue recovers its
alignment after the pulse, and fat recovers fastest. 5.2.12 covers the basic
principles.

So fat is bright, fluid is dark, and the result is an anatomical image with
excellent marrow detail. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

It matters because normal marrow is fat and anything replacing marrow darkens
it, which makes this the sequence for marrow replacement. 5.13.7 covers bone
tumours.

### How It Is Acquired

It is acquired with a short repetition time, which is what weights the image
towards the recovery difference. 5.2.12 covers the basic principles.

Fat appears bright, muscle intermediate and fluid dark, which is the pattern to
recognise. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

Cortical bone and ligament are both dark, so they are distinguished by shape
rather than by signal. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

It is the sequence used with contrast, because enhancement is seen as brightening
against a dark background. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic resonance techniques.

It is poor for oedema, which is why it is never used alone. 5.2.14 covers T2
imaging.

### Key Learning Points

- Contrast comes from how quickly tissue recovers after the pulse.
- Fat is bright, fluid is dark.
- Normal marrow is fat, so marrow replacement darkens it.
- Contrast enhancement is assessed on this sequence.
- It is poor for oedema and is never used alone.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names what this sequence is for rather than how it
works.

The expected points are the short repetition time, the bright fat and dark
fluid, the marrow detail, the contrast use, and the oedema blindness.

Say that this is the sequence for marrow replacement and for anatomy. 5.13.7
covers bone tumours.

Say that an effusion can be inconspicuous here. 5.2.14 covers T2 imaging.

Say that it is read alongside a fluid sensitive sequence rather than on its own.
5.2.16 covers fat suppression.

### How Good It Is

T1 weighting gives the best marrow and anatomical detail of the routine
sequences and the worst sensitivity to pathology.

Its best contribution is normal marrow and its replacement, which no fluid
sensitive sequence shows as clearly. 5.13.5 covers osteonecrosis.

It is limited by fluid and oedema being inconspicuous, which covers most acute
pathology. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow oedema.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no
repetition or echo time values appear anywhere in this chapter: those are
protocol quantities that differ by scanner and this environment cannot check
them.

### Key Learning Points

- Best marrow and anatomical detail of the routine sequences.
- Worst sensitivity to pathology of the routine sequences.
- Marrow replacement is shown more clearly here than anywhere.
- Fluid and oedema are inconspicuous.
- No sequence timing values appear in this chapter.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level T1 weighting answers questions about what has replaced normal
tissue.

Marrow replaced by tumour, infection or fibrosis darkens on T1 and that is the
observation the diagnosis rests on. 5.13.7 covers bone tumours.

A fatty or sclerotic change in a chronic process is characterised here better
than on a fluid sensitive sequence. 5.3.19 covers subchondral sclerosis.

Post-contrast assessment of a synovial or soft tissue mass is a T1 question.
5.14.5 covers PVNS and TGCT.

An acute injury question is the wrong question for this sequence. 5.2.16 covers
fat suppression.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is excluding an acute lesion on a T1 sequence. 5.13.1 covers
bone marrow oedema.

An effusion is missed because it is dark. 5.2.14 covers T2 imaging.

Red marrow in a normal patient is read as marrow replacement. 5.15.1 covers the
normal paediatric anatomy.

Ligament and cortical bone are confused because both are dark. 5.5.9 covers the
ligaments.

A post-contrast study is read without the pre-contrast comparison. 5.2.21 covers
advanced magnetic resonance techniques.

### Key Learning Points

- An acute lesion is excluded on the wrong sequence.
- A dark effusion is missed.
- Normal red marrow is read as marrow replacement.
- Ligament and cortex are confused because both are dark.
- A post-contrast study needs its pre-contrast comparison.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the T1 sequence's main hazard is normal marrow variation
being read as disease.

Red marrow distribution changes with age, smoking, anaemia and athletic
training, and all of those darken T1 marrow signal in a pattern that resembles
infiltration. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow oedema.

The discriminating features are symmetry, distribution and the absence of a
fluid sensitive abnormality, and none of those is a single observation. 5.2.16
covers fat suppression.

Quantitative marrow assessment would answer this and is not in routine knee
practice. 5.18.1 covers quantitative magnetic resonance imaging.

A knee study is usually not the right study for a marrow question, since the
question is systemic and the field of view is a joint. 5.13.7 covers bone
tumours.

The practical discipline is to resist a marrow diagnosis from a knee protocol
and to say what further study the question needs. 5.20.1 covers structured
reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- Normal red marrow varies with age, smoking, anaemia and training.
- The discriminating features are symmetry, distribution and the fluid sequence.
- Quantitative marrow assessment is not in routine knee practice.
- A marrow question is systemic and a knee protocol images a joint.
- Resist a marrow diagnosis from a knee protocol.
