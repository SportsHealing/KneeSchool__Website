# Clinical Significance

What an effusion does to the rest of the examination, and why it is a finding
rather than a nuisance.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

The clinical significance of an effusion is in three parts: what caused it, what
it is doing to the knee, and what it is doing to the examination.

The third part is the one most often ignored. An effused knee has an inhibited
quadriceps, a reduced range and unreliable ligament tests, so an effusion
changes how every other finding is read. 4.7.8 covers the lag.

The timing of an effusion is the most useful single fact about it, which is why
the history matters more here than the examination. 4.2.3 covers swelling as a
symptom.

### How It Is Performed

Establish the effusion's presence and size, using the tests in this chapter.
4.8.3 covers the most sensitive.

Establish the timing from the history: immediate, over hours, over days, or
recurrent. 4.2.3 covers it.

Establish whether the knee is warm, since warmth changes the differential
sharply. 4.6.2 covers temperature.

Measure the range and the extension lag, and record that an effusion was present
when they were measured. 4.7.1 covers active motion.

Note which ligament tests were performed and whether the knee was guarding,
since an effused knee's ligament examination is less reliable. 4.14.6 covers the
setting that removes guarding.

### What a Positive Means

An immediate tense effusion after an injury is blood, which narrows the
diagnosis to a short list of structures. 4.8.7 covers it.

An effusion over hours to a day after an injury suggests a meniscal tear or a
capsular injury rather than a cruciate rupture. 4.2.3 covers the timing.

A warm effusion raises infection, inflammatory arthritis or crystal disease, and
that pathway is different. 4.8.9 covers synovitis.

A recurrent effusion with no mechanical findings is an inflammatory, crystal or
synovial problem, and arthroscopy for it is futile. 4.8.8 covers chronic
effusions.

An effusion present at the time of the examination means the quadriceps findings
and the range are provisional, and that should be recorded rather than left
implied. 4.1.8 covers the record.

### Key Learning Points

- An effusion changes how every other finding in the examination is read.
- The timing is the most useful single fact about it.
- An immediate tense effusion after injury is blood.
- A warm effusion moves the assessment to a different pathway.
- An effusion makes the quadriceps findings and the range provisional.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva the effusion is the finding to use as a hinge, because it lets a
candidate talk about timing, differential and the reliability of the rest of the
examination.

The expected points are the size, the timing, the temperature, the range with
the effusion noted, and the caveat on the ligament tests.

Say that an immediate haemarthrosis has a short differential, and name it.
4.8.7 covers it.

Say that you would record that an effusion was present when the range was
measured, because the figure is otherwise uninterpretable later. 4.1.8 covers
the record.

Say that you would not conclude a normal ligament examination in a guarding
effused knee, since the tests fail towards false reassurance. 4.13.4 covers
the assessment that suffers most.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that raised intra-articular pressure inhibits the
quadriceps through a reflex arc, so an effused knee has an active extension
deficit without any muscle or tendon problem. 4.7.8 covers the lag.

The same pressure limits the range, and flexion is lost before extension because
the capsule's volume is greatest in flexion. 4.8.1 covers normal fluid
physiology.

Pain and effusion both increase hamstring activity, which reduces anterior
translation and makes the Lachman test and the pivot shift read smaller than
they are. 4.14.6 covers the remedy.

The rate of accumulation reflects the source: arterial bleeding is fast,
synovial production is slow, which is the physiological basis for the timing
being so informative. 4.8.7 covers it.

### How Good It Is

The presence and size of an effusion are reliably assessed, and the inference
from them to a cause depends almost entirely on the history. 4.2.3 covers it.

The effusion's effect on the rest of the examination is predictable and
consistently underappreciated, which is why this page exists.

The composition cannot be determined clinically, which aspiration answers where
it matters. 4.8.7 covers the acute haemarthrosis.

Recording the effusion alongside the other findings is what makes the
examination interpretable later, and it is a record keeping point rather than a
technique one. 4.1.8 covers it.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 023,
and the comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- Raised pressure inhibits the quadriceps through a reflex arc.
- Flexion is lost before extension because the capsule's volume is greatest in flexion.
- Pain and effusion increase hamstring activity, making the Lachman and pivot shift read smaller.
- The rate of accumulation reflects the source.
- Recording the effusion alongside the other findings is what makes them interpretable later.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level an effusion changes timing, changes the reliability of the
assessment the plan rests on, and occasionally changes the diagnosis entirely.

A reconstruction is timed after the effusion settles, because operating on an
effused knee with an inhibited quadriceps produces a stiffer result and a
slower recovery. 4.7.7 covers contractures.

A ligament assessment in an effused acute knee is unreliable in the direction of
false reassurance, which is the argument for an examination under anaesthesia in
the multiligament knee. 4.11.7 covers that sequence.

A recurrent effusion with no mechanical findings moves the patient out of the
surgical pathway, and that is the most consequential version of this finding.
4.8.8 covers chronic effusions.

A warm effusion after an operation raises infection, which is excluded rather
than observed. 4.6.2 covers temperature.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is treating the effusion as a nuisance rather than a
finding.

The second is reading a limited range in an effused knee as stiffness.
4.7.7 covers it.

The third is reading quadriceps failure as weakness.

The fourth is concluding a normal ligament examination in an effused guarding
knee.

The fifth is recording a range or a laxity grade without noting that an effusion
was present. 4.1.8 covers the record.

### Key Learning Points

- A reconstruction is timed after the effusion settles.
- A ligament assessment in an effused acute knee fails towards false reassurance.
- A recurrent effusion with no mechanical findings moves the patient out of the surgical pathway.
- A warm effusion after an operation is excluded rather than observed.
- A range or grade recorded without noting the effusion is uninterpretable later.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In referred practice the effusion is the finding that most often says the
referral's diagnosis is wrong.

A patient with a persistent effusion, normal ligaments and no mechanical
symptoms has been referred for a mechanical opinion about a non mechanical
problem. 4.8.9 covers synovitis.

A patient whose rehabilitation has stalled with a persistent effusion cannot
progress until the effusion is addressed, which reorders the treatment rather
than adding to it. 4.3.9 covers wasting.

A patient with an effusion and a reconstruction has either infection,
mechanical irritation from a lesion, or compartment change, and the examination
plus the time since surgery narrows it. 4.7.10 covers post-operative range.

A patient whose earlier ligament examination was performed on an effused knee has
findings that may be understated, and repeating them on a dry knee is the first
step rather than ordering more imaging. 4.14.6 covers the alternative.

In medicolegal work the documented timing of the first effusion is often the
single most useful fact in the record, and the examination months later adds
little to it. 4.23.7 covers that setting.

### Key Learning Points

- A persistent effusion with normal ligaments means a mechanical opinion about a non mechanical problem.
- Stalled rehabilitation with an effusion needs the effusion addressed first.
- An effusion with a reconstruction is infection, a mechanical lesion, or compartment change.
- A ligament examination performed on an effused knee may be understated and is worth repeating.
- The documented timing of the first effusion is often the most useful fact in the record.
