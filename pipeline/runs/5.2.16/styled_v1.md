# Fat Suppression

Removing the fat signal so that oedema can be seen, which is what makes marrow
and acute injury visible.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

Fat is bright on most sequences and so is oedema, which means a bright lesion in
fatty tissue is hard to see. 5.2.14 covers T2 imaging.

Suppressing the fat signal leaves the oedema bright against a dark background.
5.13.1 covers bone marrow oedema.

It matters because marrow is fat, so every marrow abnormality in the knee
depends on this. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

### How It Is Acquired

Fat can be suppressed by exploiting its different resonant frequency, which is
efficient and sensitive to field inhomogeneity. 5.2.12 covers the basic
principles.

It can also be suppressed by exploiting its short recovery time, which is more
robust and gives less signal. 5.2.17 covers short tau inversion recovery
imaging.

The frequency based method fails near metal, where the field is distorted, which
is why the post-operative protocol differs. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative
protocol.

Incomplete suppression leaves patchy brightness that mimics oedema. 5.2.19
covers artefacts.

A fat suppressed fluid sensitive sequence in all three planes is the backbone of
any knee protocol. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

### Key Learning Points

- A bright lesion in bright fat is hard to see.
- Suppressing fat leaves oedema bright against a dark background.
- Marrow is fat, so marrow abnormality depends on this.
- The frequency based method fails near metal.
- Incomplete suppression mimics oedema.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the sequence to name when asked how a bone bruise is seen.

The expected points are the two suppression methods, the metal failure, the
patchy incomplete suppression, and the three plane fluid sensitive backbone.

Say that marrow oedema is the finding this sequence exists for. 5.11.7 covers
bone marrow lesions.

Say that near metal the inversion recovery method is preferred. 5.2.17 covers
short tau inversion recovery imaging.

Say that patchy brightness should raise suppression failure before pathology.
5.2.19 covers artefacts.

### How Good It Is

A fat suppressed fluid sensitive sequence is the most sensitive routine sequence
for knee pathology and the least specific.

Its best contribution is detecting marrow oedema, which carries the acute
cruciate injury pattern and much else. 5.7.4 covers the acute cruciate injury.

It is limited by that sensitivity, since oedema accompanies almost everything and
distinguishes little. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow oedema.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no
inversion or frequency offset values appear anywhere in this chapter, because
those are scanner specific protocol quantities.

### Key Learning Points

- The most sensitive routine sequence and the least specific.
- Marrow oedema detection is its defining contribution.
- Oedema accompanies almost everything and distinguishes little.
- Near metal, the inversion recovery method is preferred.
- Patchy brightness suggests suppression failure before pathology.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level this sequence finds the abnormality and rarely names it.

Marrow oedema in the lateral femoral condyle and posterolateral tibia is a
pattern that names a mechanism, which is the exception. 5.7.4 covers the acute
cruciate injury.

Subchondral oedema adjacent to cartilage loss is a marker of the compartment's
state rather than a diagnosis. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Oedema in a stress reaction has a location and a pattern that differ from a
degenerative one, which is the second useful exception. 5.13.2 covers stress
reactions.

An isolated oedema finding with no pattern is a prompt for a clinical question
rather than an answer. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting oedema as a diagnosis. 5.13.1 covers bone
marrow oedema.

Incomplete fat suppression is read as marrow oedema. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

Near metal, suppression failure is read as infection or loosening. 5.5.3 covers
the post-operative protocol.

Normal red marrow is read as oedema on a suppressed sequence. 5.15.1 covers the
normal paediatric anatomy.

Oedema in an asymptomatic compartment is given weight it has not earned. 5.11.7
covers bone marrow lesions.

### Key Learning Points

- Oedema is reported as a diagnosis.
- Incomplete suppression is read as marrow oedema.
- Suppression failure near metal is read as infection or loosening.
- Normal red marrow is read as oedema.
- Asymptomatic oedema is given unearned weight.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level fat suppression is where sensitivity without specificity
does the most damage to a knee pathway.

Marrow oedema is detected in asymptomatic knees and in almost every symptomatic
one, so its presence rarely moves a probability. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow
oedema.

Its prognostic associations in osteoarthritis are real and population level, and
they are routinely applied to an individual patient as though they were
diagnostic. 5.21.5 covers the osteoarthritis imaging literature.

Suppression quality varies with field strength, with the method and with the
presence of metal, and none of that is reported. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative
protocol.

Quantitative approaches to marrow lesion volume exist and are not in routine
practice, so the finding stays qualitative while being used quantitatively.
5.18.1 covers quantitative magnetic resonance imaging.

The disciplined position is to report the pattern rather than the presence, and
to say when the pattern names nothing. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- Marrow oedema is found in asymptomatic and nearly all symptomatic knees.
- Population level prognostic associations are applied to individuals.
- Suppression quality varies and is not reported.
- The finding stays qualitative while being used quantitatively.
- Report the pattern, and say when the pattern names nothing.
