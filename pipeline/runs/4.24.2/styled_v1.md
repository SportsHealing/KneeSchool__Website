# Pivot Shift Literature

The work behind the rotational examination, which produced the most specific test
in the section and the least reproducible one.

## For Medical Students

### What the Question Was

The question was how to detect the rotational instability a cruciate deficient
knee produces, since the anterior tests measure translation in one direction
only. 4.10.4 covers the Lachman test.

The answer had to reproduce the event the patient describes, which is the knee
giving way during a turn rather than sliding forwards. 4.2.6 covers giving way.

Several manoeuvres were described to do that, and they share a mechanism while
differing in how the knee is held and loaded. 4.10.6 covers the pivot shift
test.

### What It Showed

The work established that the rotational event can be reproduced by hand, and
that reproducing it is strong evidence of a cruciate deficient knee.

It also established that the test needs a relaxed patient, which is why it is
often uninterpretable in the acute knee. 4.23.1 covers the examination under
anaesthesia.

It did not produce agreement on grading, so the result is usually recorded as
present or absent rather than graded. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

### Key Learning Points

- The anterior tests measure translation in one direction only.
- The rotational test reproduces the event the patient describes.
- Reproducing it is strong evidence of a deficient cruciate.
- The test needs a relaxed patient.
- Grading never reached agreement.

## MRCS Level

### What Was Done

Galway and MacIntosh described the manoeuvre that gave the phenomenon its
clinical name, applying valgus and rotation to a knee moving from extension into
flexion. 4.10.6 covers the technique.

Losee and Jakob described variants that load the knee differently, and the
existence of several variants is itself informative about how hard the finding
is to elicit. 4.14.1 covers the rotational examination.

Comparative accuracy work set the rotational tests against the anterior tests
and against operative findings.

Instrumented and image based measurement of the shift followed, attempting to
replace a graded hand finding with a measured translation.

Examination under anaesthesia established how much of the test's failure rate is
guarding rather than an absent lesion. 4.23.1 covers that examination.

### What It Showed

The comparative work established that the pivot shift is specific and
insensitive, which is the opposite profile from the Lachman and the reason both
are performed. 4.10.4 covers the Lachman test.

It established that the test's sensitivity rises substantially under anaesthesia,
which identifies guarding as the limiting factor rather than the lesion. 4.23.1
covers that examination.

It established that interobserver agreement on grading is poor, which is why the
grade travels badly between clinics. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

No sensitivity, specificity or likelihood ratio appears on this page, under
decision 023 and decision 022 together. The ranking is what is recalled
reliably; a specific percentage written from memory would look exactly like a
correct one.

### Key Learning Points

- The named manoeuvre loads the knee with valgus and rotation through flexion.
- Several variants exist, which says how hard the finding is to elicit.
- The test is specific and insensitive, the opposite profile from the Lachman.
- Sensitivity rises substantially under anaesthesia.
- Interobserver agreement on grading is poor.

## FRCS Level

### What It Showed

At decision level the test's profile is what makes it useful, because a positive
result is close to decisive and a negative one is close to uninformative.

A positive pivot shift in an awake patient is strong enough to act on, which is
the practical consequence of high specificity. 4.10.6 covers the test.

A negative pivot shift in an awake patient says little, which is why it never
excludes a cruciate injury. 4.2.6 covers giving way.

The rotational examination's relationship to the anterolateral structures was
established by the same body of work, which is why a high grade shift raises
them. 4.14.3 covers anterolateral rotatory instability.

Instrumented measurement improved reproducibility and has not displaced the hand
test, because the quantity it measures is not the quantity being judged. 4.2.2
covers instability.

### What It Changed

It made the rotational examination a standard part of the cruciate assessment
rather than an advanced extra. 4.10.6 covers the test.

It made a positive result in an awake patient actionable, which changes the
imaging threshold rather than replacing the imaging. 4.1.6 covers that
relationship.

It made examination under anaesthesia the accepted route to the finding when the
awake test fails. 4.23.1 covers that examination.

It raised the anterolateral structures as a clinical question, which changed the
operation in some patients. 4.14.3 covers anterolateral rotatory instability.

### Key Learning Points

- A positive result is close to decisive and a negative one close to uninformative.
- A negative test never excludes a cruciate injury.
- A high grade shift raises the anterolateral structures.
- Instrumented measurement has not displaced the hand test.
- The finding changed the operation in some patients.

## Fellowship Level

### What It Changed

At fellowship level this literature's legacy is a test everyone performs and
nobody grades the same way.

The grade is used in decisions about adding an anterolateral procedure, and the
grade is the least reproducible quantity in the section, which is an
uncomfortable combination. 4.14.3 covers anterolateral rotatory instability.

The post reconstruction pivot shift is the finding used to judge rotational
control, and it inherits the same reproducibility problem. 4.20.1 covers the
reconstruction assessment.

The relationship between a residual shift and a patient's reported instability
remains loose, so the test and the symptom are recorded separately. 4.2.2 covers
instability.

Measurement under anaesthesia is reproducible and measures the knee the patient
does not use, which is the trade this literature never resolved. 4.23.1 covers
that examination.
