# Alignment Changes

Alignment as a consequence of wear rather than a cause of it, and why one film
cannot tell the two apart.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

As a compartment wears, the limb's alignment shifts towards that compartment.
5.3.7 covers the mechanical axis.

So alignment on a radiograph is both a cause of the wear and a result of it.
3.13.8 covers alignment and osteoarthritis.

It matters because a single film shows the current state and says nothing about
which came first, and the answer changes the operation. 5.16.5 covers osteotomy
planning.

### How It Is Acquired

Measure alignment on a standing long leg film, which is the only film that can.
5.3.6 covers long leg alignment films.

Assess whether the deformity is intra-articular or bony, since wear driven
deformity sits in the joint. 5.3.9 covers the joint line obliquity.

Assess the other limb, because a symmetrical pattern suggests a constitutional
component. 3.13.5 covers constitutional varus.

Obtain a stress view where the correctability matters, which separates bone from
soft tissue. 4.5.2 covers varus alignment.

Compare with older films where they exist, which is the only direct evidence of
progression. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

### What Normal Looks Like

A mechanical axis close to the knee centre with a perpendicular joint line.
5.3.7 covers the mechanical axis.

Preserved and symmetrical compartment heights. 5.3.17 covers joint space
narrowing.

No marginal osteophyte or sclerosis. 5.3.18 covers osteophytes.

Symmetry with the other limb. 3.13.5 covers constitutional varus.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

An axis deviated towards a worn compartment, with an oblique joint line. 5.3.9
covers the joint line obliquity.

Compartment narrowing, sclerosis and osteophyte on the loaded side. 5.3.19 covers
subchondral sclerosis.

Opening of the opposite side under load, indicating soft tissue laxity. 4.5.3
covers valgus alignment.

A progressive deviation across serial films, which is the only direct evidence of
the sequence. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

### Key Learning Points

- Wear shifts the alignment towards the worn compartment.
- Alignment is both a cause and a result of wear.
- One film shows the state and not the sequence.
- Wear driven deformity sits in the joint rather than the bone.
- A stress view separates bone from soft tissue.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the page behind the answer that alignment and arthritis cause
each other.

The expected points are the long film, the intra-articular against bony
distinction, the contralateral comparison, the stress view, and the older films.

Say that the direction of causation cannot be read off one film. 3.13.8 covers
alignment and osteoarthritis.

Say that an intra-articular deformity is not corrected by an osteotomy. 5.3.9
covers the joint line obliquity.

Say that correctability decides what the operation can achieve. 4.5.2 covers
varus alignment.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that deviation increases a compartment's load, the load
accelerates wear, and the wear increases the deviation, which is a positive
feedback loop. 3.13.8 covers alignment and osteoarthritis.

That loop means late presentation shows a deformity whose origin is no longer
recoverable from the imaging. 5.3.7 covers the mechanical axis.

A constitutional deviation starts the loop without any pathology, which is why
the contralateral limb matters. 3.13.5 covers constitutional varus.

Soft tissue laxity on the unloaded side adds to the standing deformity without
being structural, which is what a stress view removes. 4.5.3 covers valgus
alignment.

### How Good It Is

A standing film measures the current alignment reliably and cannot date it.

Its best contribution is the correctability assessment when paired with a stress
view, because that is what determines what surgery can achieve. 4.5.2 covers
varus alignment.

It is limited by being a single moment in a feedback loop, so the sequence has to
come from history or from older films. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no deformity
or correctability thresholds appear either: those are published quantities and
this environment cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- Deviation, load and wear form a positive feedback loop.
- Late presentation shows a deformity whose origin cannot be recovered.
- A constitutional deviation starts the loop without pathology.
- Soft tissue laxity adds to standing deformity without being structural.
- The film measures the alignment and cannot date it.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the question is what the alignment allows rather than what
caused it.

A correctable deformity with preserved opposite compartments supports realignment
or unicompartmental replacement. 5.16.5 covers osteotomy planning.

A fixed intra-articular deformity with multi-compartment wear supports total
replacement. 5.16.6 covers arthroplasty planning.

A constitutional deviation sets the alignment target, which is now a patient
specific rather than a neutral goal. 3.13.10 covers the alignment correction
principles.

A young patient with deformity and early wear is the one in whom correcting the
alignment might change the course, which makes the assessment most valuable
earliest. 3.13.9 covers the osteotomy biomechanics.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is inferring the sequence from one film. 3.13.8 covers
alignment and osteoarthritis.

An intra-articular deformity is planned as a bony correction. 5.3.9 covers the
joint line obliquity.

A constitutional deviation is corrected to neutral. 3.13.5 covers constitutional
varus.

Correctability is assumed rather than tested with a stress view. 4.5.2 covers
varus alignment.

Alignment is estimated from a knee film. 5.3.6 covers long leg alignment films.

### Key Learning Points

- The sequence is inferred from one film.
- An intra-articular deformity is planned as a bony correction.
- A constitutional deviation is corrected to neutral.
- Correctability is assumed rather than tested.
- Alignment is estimated from a knee film.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this page closes the chapter on the same point it opened
with, that a radiograph measures a relationship and not a state.

Alignment, wear and laxity are three contributors to one standing measurement,
and separating them needs a stress view and a history, neither of which is in the
image. 4.5.2 covers varus alignment.

The shift from neutral alignment targets to patient specific ones makes knowing
the pre-arthritic alignment valuable, and nothing in the imaging recovers it
except an old film. 3.13.5 covers constitutional varus.

Whether correcting alignment changes the natural history is supported for the
right patient and remains uncertain for most, which makes the measurement's
precision less decisive than the patient selection. 3.13.8 covers alignment and
osteoarthritis.

Weight bearing computed tomography and gait analysis each measure part of what
matters and neither is routine, so the clinical decision rests on the oldest
imaging technology in this section. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

The chapter's conclusion is that plain radiography answers questions about load
and alignment that nothing else answers, and answers them only when the position
is controlled and stated. 5.2.5 covers magnification.

### Key Learning Points

- Alignment, wear and laxity are three contributors to one measurement.
- Patient specific targets make the pre-arthritic alignment valuable and unrecoverable.
- Whether correction changes the natural history is uncertain for most patients.
- The decision rests on the oldest imaging technology in this section.
- Radiography answers load and alignment questions only when position is controlled.
