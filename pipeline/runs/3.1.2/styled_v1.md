# Why Biomechanics Matters

The link from force to injury and from injury back to treatment. Why a subject
that looks like physics decides what happens in a clinic.

## For Young Learners

### The Principle

Things break when the force on them is bigger than they can take. That is true
of a bridge, a bicycle and a knee ligament.

So if you want to know why something got injured, you need to know two things:
how strong it was, and how much force went through it.

Biomechanics is how you find out the second one.

### At the Knee

Most knee injuries in sport do not involve anyone else. The player lands,
turns, and the knee gives way. No tackle, no collision.

That tells you the force came from the player's own body and the ground.
Working out how it added up to enough to tear a ligament is a mechanics
problem.

Once you know how the force builds up, you can try to change it. That is where
warm ups and landing drills come from, and 3.1.5 covers them.

### Key Learning Points

- Things break when the force is bigger than they can take.
- To explain an injury you need the strength of the tissue and the force on it.
- Most sporting knee injuries happen without anyone touching the player.
- The force comes from the player's own body and the ground.
- Understanding how the force builds up is how you try to change it.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.

## For Patients

### The Principle

Knee problems are mechanical problems. Tissue fails when load exceeds what it
can carry, and that can happen in one moment or over many years.

A single large load tears something. Many small loads on a structure that
cannot repair fast enough wears it. Both are mechanics and the difference is
time.

That is why two patients with the same diagnosis can have arrived there by
different routes, and why their treatment may differ.

### At the Knee

The knee is loaded every step, and the load is several times body weight
because muscles pull as well as gravity pushing.

The load does not spread evenly. Alignment, the shape of the joint, and the
state of the menisci all decide which part carries most.

When one part is overloaded for long enough, the cartilage there wears first.
That is the mechanical account of why arthritis often affects one side of the
knee more than the other.

### Clinical Relevance

Almost every treatment offered for a knee changes a force. Losing weight
reduces load, strengthening redistributes it, a brace redirects it, an insole
shifts it, and surgery changes the structure carrying it.

This is also why treatment can work without anything being repaired. A painful
knee that is loaded differently can stop hurting while the joint itself is
unchanged.

It explains why rest alone often fails. Rest removes the load and the problem
returns when the load does.

### Key Learning Points

- Tissue fails when load exceeds what it can carry.
- One large load tears; many small loads wear. The difference is time.
- The knee carries several times body weight each step.
- Load does not spread evenly, which is why arthritis often affects one side first.
- Almost every knee treatment works by changing a force.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q.** If my knee hurts, does that mean something is damaged?
Not necessarily. Pain and damage are related but not the same. A knee can be
painful with little structural change, and a knee with visible change on a
scan can be painless.

**Q.** Will losing weight really help my knee?
Mechanically, yes. Joint load rises faster than body weight because the muscle
forces balancing it rise too, so a given weight loss removes more load than
its own amount.

**Q.** Why do I get told to strengthen a knee that already hurts?
Because stronger muscles change how load is shared. The aim is not to make the
knee carry more, it is to change which parts carry what.

**Q.** Is my knee wearing out faster because of exercise?
Regular loading is what keeps cartilage healthy. Sudden large increases in
load are the problem rather than activity itself.

See a clinician if your knee gives way, locks, swells quickly after an injury,
or will not take your weight.

## For Medical Students

### The Principle

Tissue injury is a mechanical event. Failure occurs when stress exceeds the
material's ultimate strength, and the mode of failure depends on the rate at
which the load was applied.

Two mechanisms produce injury. Acute overload exceeds the ultimate strength in
one event. Fatigue failure accumulates damage below that threshold faster than
repair can keep up.

Both are described by the same stress and strain relationship, read at
different points on the curve and over different timescales.

### At the Knee

Non-contact anterior cruciate injury is the clearest worked example.
Deceleration with the knee near extension, valgus and internal rotation
together put the ligament in the position where it carries most load, and the
failure is mechanical rather than incidental. 3.11.4 covers the mechanics.

Medial compartment osteoarthritis is the fatigue example. A varus knee loads
the medial compartment at every step, and the accumulated load exceeds what
the cartilage can maintain. 3.9 covers alignment.

Patellofemoral pain sits between the two. The joint is loaded most in flexion
under load, which is why stairs and squatting provoke it. 3.12.9 covers it.

### What Changes It

Alignment, muscle strength, movement pattern, body mass and activity history
are the modifiable variables. Joint shape and previous surgery are not.

Loading rate changes both the injury produced and the tissue that fails. A
fast load fails ligament, a slow one fails bone at the attachment.

Neuromuscular control changes the position the joint is in when a load
arrives, which changes which structure carries it.

### How It Is Measured

Injury mechanism is studied from video analysis, laboratory simulation and
cadaveric testing, and the three disagree often enough to be worth naming
separately.

Fatigue failure is studied by cyclic loading in the laboratory and by
population studies in people, which measure different things and are
frequently quoted as one.

Intervention studies measure whether changing a mechanical variable changes
the injury rate, which is the only design that connects the mechanics to the
outcome.

### Clinical Relevance

A mechanical account of an injury predicts which structures to examine. A
pivoting injury implicates the anterior cruciate, the anterolateral complex
and the lateral meniscus together because one mechanism loads all three.

It also predicts what will not help. A structural repair in a limb whose
alignment caused the failure will be loaded the same way again.

And it is how prevention is argued at all. Only a modifiable mechanical
variable can be the target of a prevention programme.

### Key Learning Points

- Acute overload exceeds ultimate strength once; fatigue failure accumulates below it.
- Non-contact cruciate injury is the acute worked example, medial arthritis the fatigue one.
- Loading rate changes which tissue fails as well as whether it fails.
- A mechanical account predicts which structures to examine together.
- Only a modifiable mechanical variable can be a prevention target.

## MRCS Level

### The Principle

Put the argument in order for a viva: a mechanism applies a load, the load
produces stress in a tissue, the tissue fails if stress exceeds its strength,
and the pattern of failure identifies the mechanism. Every step is testable.

Note where the argument is weakest. Tissue strength varies between people and
within one person over time, so a given load does not predict failure in an
individual.

The same framework covers treatment. An intervention is a change to one term
in that chain, and naming which term is how two treatments are compared.

### At the Knee

Conventional teaching gives anterior cruciate ultimate tensile load as of the
order of 2,000 newtons, and estimates of the load during a pivoting manoeuvre
approach that figure. The margin is small, which is the mechanical reason the
injury is common.

Meniscal loss raises contact pressure substantially because contact area
falls, which 3.6 covers. That is a mechanical explanation for an outcome
observed clinically.

Alignment correction is the clearest demonstration that mechanics decides
outcome: moving the load line changes symptoms without touching the painful
tissue. Section 7 covers osteotomy.

### What Changes It

Group the variables by whether an intervention can reach them. Mass, strength,
control and activity are reachable without an operation; alignment and
structure need one; age and joint shape are not reachable at all.

A prevention programme targets the reachable ones, which is why those
programmes are built around landing, cutting and strength rather than around
ligaments.

Surgery moves a variable from the unreachable column to the reachable one,
which is a useful way to frame when it is indicated.

### How It Is Measured

Distinguish mechanism studies, which describe how an injury happens, from
intervention studies, which test whether changing something helps. The first
does not imply the second.

Cadaveric ultimate load figures are measured at a loading rate that is not the
rate of injury, which is a limit worth stating when the figure is quoted.

Registry and cohort data give injury rates but not mechanics, so the two
literatures answer different questions and are often cited together as though
they answered one.

### Clinical Relevance

Identify the mechanism before planning a repair, because the mechanism
predicts whether the repair will be loaded the same way again.

Explain treatment to a patient in mechanical terms where possible. It is the
account that makes rehabilitation make sense to them.

Assess alignment in any knee with compartment specific symptoms, since
alignment is the mechanical variable with the largest effect per step.

Avoid quoting an ultimate load figure as though it predicted an individual
injury. Tissue strength varies and the figure is a population property.

### Key Learning Points

- Mechanism applies load, load produces stress, stress exceeds strength, pattern identifies mechanism.
- Conventional teaching gives anterior cruciate ultimate load of the order of 2,000 newtons.
- The margin between pivoting load and ultimate load is small, which is why the injury is common.
- Mechanism studies and intervention studies answer different questions.
- Surgery moves a variable from unreachable to reachable.
