# Synovium

The joint lining, which is invisible when normal and is the one knee tissue that
needs contrast.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

The synovium lines the joint and produces its fluid, and when normal it is too
thin to see. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

It becomes visible when it thickens, which is what synovitis is. 5.14.1 covers
synovitis.

It matters because it is the one knee tissue whose assessment regularly needs
intravenous contrast, and the routine protocol does not include it. 5.2.21 covers
advanced magnetic resonance techniques.

### How It Is Acquired

Assess for synovial thickening on the fluid sensitive sequences, where thickened
synovium is distinguishable from fluid with difficulty. 5.2.16 covers fat
suppression.

Give intravenous contrast where the distinction matters, because synovium
enhances and fluid does not. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic resonance
techniques.

Assess the recesses, since synovial proliferation collects in them. 5.5.12 covers
the bursa.

Look for a mass like synovial process, which is a different diagnosis from
diffuse thickening. 5.14.5 covers PVNS and TGCT.

Assess for loose bodies, which synovial processes generate. 5.14.6 covers synovial
chondromatosis.

### What Normal Looks Like

No visible synovium, because a normal lining is too thin to resolve.

A small amount of physiological joint fluid without surrounding thickening. 5.5.12
covers the bursa.

Clean recesses without proliferative tissue. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

No enhancement where contrast was given. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic
resonance techniques.

### Key Learning Points

- A normal synovium is too thin to see.
- It becomes visible when it thickens, which is synovitis.
- Thickened synovium and fluid are hard to separate without contrast.
- Synovium enhances and fluid does not.
- Synovial processes collect in the recesses and generate loose bodies.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the tissue to name when asked when a knee study needs contrast.

The expected points are the fluid sensitive sequences, the contrast, the recesses,
the mass like process, and the loose bodies.

Say that contrast separates synovium from fluid. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic
resonance techniques.

Say that diffuse thickening and a mass like process are different diagnoses.
5.14.5 covers PVNS and TGCT.

Say that the routine protocol does not include contrast. 5.5.1 covers the standard
protocol.

### What Normal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that synovium is a thin vascular membrane, so it is
below the resolution of a routine study when normal and becomes resolvable only
when it proliferates. 5.14.1 covers synovitis.

Being vascular is what makes it enhance, and fluid being avascular is what makes
contrast the discriminator. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic resonance techniques.

Proliferative synovium collects where the capsule is redundant, which is why the
recesses are where it is found. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

A haemosiderin containing synovial process has its own signal characteristics,
which is why a mass like lesion is approached differently from diffuse thickening.
5.14.5 covers PVNS and TGCT.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging detects synovial thickening and characterises it only
with contrast.

Its best contribution is detecting proliferative synovium and loose bodies, which
changes the diagnostic direction. 5.14.6 covers synovial chondromatosis.

It is limited without contrast, because thickened synovium and joint fluid have
similar signal on the routine sequences. 5.2.16 covers fat suppression.

### Key Learning Points

- Synovium is a thin vascular membrane, below resolution when normal.
- Being vascular is what makes it enhance.
- Fluid is avascular, which makes contrast the discriminator.
- Proliferative synovium collects where the capsule is redundant.
- A haemosiderin containing process has its own signal characteristics.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level synovial assessment redirects a knee from a mechanical pathway
to an inflammatory or neoplastic one.

Diffuse synovial thickening with an effusion suggests an inflammatory process
rather than a mechanical one, which is a referral. 5.14.2 covers rheumatoid
arthritis.

A mass like synovial process is a surgical diagnosis with its own operation.
5.14.5 covers PVNS and TGCT.

Loose bodies from a synovial process are a mechanical finding within a synovial
diagnosis. 5.14.6 covers synovial chondromatosis.

A contrast study is the one to request when the distinction matters, and it has to
be requested. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic resonance techniques.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reading an effusion as synovial thickening without
contrast. 5.2.21 covers advanced magnetic resonance techniques.

Normal recess fluid is reported as synovitis. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

A mass like process is reported as diffuse thickening. 5.14.5 covers PVNS and
TGCT.

A synovial process is found and the loose bodies are not sought. 5.14.6 covers
synovial chondromatosis.

Contrast is not requested where the question needed it. 5.5.1 covers the standard
protocol.

### Key Learning Points

- An effusion is read as synovial thickening without contrast.
- Normal recess fluid is reported as synovitis.
- A mass like process is reported as diffuse thickening.
- The loose bodies are not sought.
- Contrast is not requested where the question needed it.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the synovium is the knee tissue whose assessment most often
fails for a reason outside the imaging.

The study that would answer the question needs contrast, the routine protocol does
not include it, and the request rarely says that a synovial question is being
asked, so the wrong study is performed. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Synovitis is also found in mechanically driven arthritis, so its presence does not
by itself indicate an inflammatory arthropathy, and the report often implies that
it does. 5.14.1 covers synovitis.

Quantitative synovial volume measurement exists in research and is not routine, so
a diffuse process is graded by impression. 5.18.1 covers quantitative magnetic
resonance imaging.

Ultrasound with Doppler assesses synovial activity directly and is rarely used
alongside, which leaves two partial answers rather than one complete one. 5.2.11
covers Doppler imaging.

The useful practice is to name the synovial question on the request so that
contrast is given, and to report thickening without implying its cause. 5.1.3
covers choosing the correct investigation.

### Key Learning Points

- The answering study needs contrast the routine protocol omits.
- The request rarely says a synovial question is being asked.
- Synovitis occurs in mechanically driven arthritis too.
- Quantitative synovial measurement is not routine.
- Ultrasound with Doppler assesses activity and is rarely used alongside.
