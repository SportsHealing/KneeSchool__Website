# Pes Anserinus Pathology

A tender spot an inch below the medial joint line, and the commonest
extra-articular cause of medial knee pain.

## For Medical Students

### What It Tests

The pes anserinus is the conjoined insertion of the medial hamstring tendons on
the upper medial tibia, with a bursa beneath it. 4.16.6 covers the tendons.

Examining it tests for tenderness at that insertion, which presents as medial
knee pain and is frequently attributed to the joint or the meniscus.
4.6.4 covers joint line tenderness.

It matters because the site is a short distance below the medial joint line, and
that distance is the whole diagnosis. 4.12.7 covers a related separation.

### How It Is Performed

Lay the patient supine with the knee slightly flexed and the medial side
exposed.

Find the medial joint line with a finger and then move distally and slightly
posteriorly onto the upper medial tibia, which is where the insertion sits.
4.6.1 covers systematic palpation.

Palpate for focal tenderness and for a boggy swelling, which indicates a
bursitis rather than an insertional problem alone.

Compare the tenderness there with the tenderness at the joint line itself, and
record which is greater, because that comparison is the finding. 4.6.4 covers
the joint line.

Ask the patient to flex the knee against resistance and note whether that
reproduces the pain, which implicates the hamstring insertion. 4.16.6 covers
that test.

Perform the medial ligament and meniscal assessments, since the alternatives
have to be excluded rather than assumed away. 4.12.1 covers valgus stress
testing.

### What a Positive Means

Focal tenderness below the medial joint line with a normal joint line, a normal
valgus examination and a normal meniscal examination indicates a pes problem.

A boggy swelling at that site indicates a bursitis, which is the version that
responds to local treatment.

Pain reproduced by resisted flexion supports the hamstring insertion as the
source. 4.16.6 covers it.

Tenderness at both the pes and the joint line means two possible sources, which
is common in a degenerative knee and should be recorded as both. 4.18.3 covers
the varus arthritic knee.

A normal pes with medial joint line tenderness points at the meniscus or the
deep medial ligament instead. 4.12.7 covers that separation.

### Key Learning Points

- The pes is the conjoined medial hamstring insertion, with a bursa beneath it.
- It sits a short distance below and behind the medial joint line, and that distance is the diagnosis.
- A boggy swelling there indicates a bursitis.
- Pain reproduced by resisted flexion supports the hamstring insertion.
- Tenderness at both sites means two possible sources, which is common in a degenerative knee.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Performed

In a viva this is the answer when asked for an extra-articular cause of medial
knee pain, and the anatomical localisation is the mark.

The expected points are finding the joint line first, moving distally and
posteriorly, the comparison of the two tenderness sites, the resisted flexion,
and the exclusion of the joint causes.

Say that you would find the joint line before palpating the pes, since the
diagnosis depends on knowing where the joint line is. 4.6.4 covers it.

Say that the condition coexists with medial compartment arthritis often enough
that finding it does not exclude the joint. 4.18.3 covers that knee.

Say that you would examine the hamstrings, since the pes is their insertion and
the problem may be proximal to it. 4.16.6 covers them.

### What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that the medial hamstring tendons converge on a small
area of the upper medial tibia, so the stress there is concentrated and the
bursa beneath reduces friction. 2.9.5 covers the semimembranosus.

Overload or a degenerate knee's altered mechanics increases that stress, which
is why the condition appears both in athletes and in older patients with medial
compartment change. 3.5.3 covers load distribution.

The bursa inflames in response, which is why a boggy swelling is part of the
picture in some patients and absent in others.

The site is below the joint line because the insertion is, and that is why the
examination's localisation to within a short distance is diagnostic. 4.6.4
covers the joint line.

### How Good It Is

The examination is specific when the tenderness is focal, below the joint line
and reproduced by resisted flexion, which makes this one of the better
localising diagnoses in the section.

It is non specific when the whole medial side is tender, which is common in a
degenerative knee. 4.18.3 covers that knee.

It cannot separate an insertional tendinopathy from a bursitis reliably, and
ultrasound does, although the distinction changes the treatment modestly.
4.1.6 covers that relationship.

Its main value is excluding the joint as the source, which prevents a
pointless arthroscopy in a patient whose problem is on the surface. 4.9.11
covers that error's commonest version.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 023,
and the comparison is the claim being made.

### Key Learning Points

- The medial hamstring tendons converge on a small area, so the stress there is concentrated.
- A degenerate knee's altered mechanics increases that stress, which is why older patients get it.
- The bursa inflames in response, which is why a boggy swelling appears in some patients.
- It is specific when focal and below the joint line, and non specific when the whole side is tender.
- Its main value is excluding the joint as the source.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level this is a non surgical diagnosis whose value is preventing an
operation on the joint.

A patient with focal pes tenderness, a normal joint line and normal ligament and
meniscal examinations has an extra-articular problem managed locally and by
load. 4.6.4 covers the joint line.

A patient with both pes tenderness and medial compartment change has two
sources, and treating the compartment leaves part of the complaint, which is
worth saying before an operation rather than after. 4.18.3 covers that knee.

A patient with pes tenderness after a hamstring graft harvest has donor site
symptoms at the insertion, and the history separates them. 4.20.1 covers that
assessment.

A patient with tenderness at several insertions and systemic features has an
enthesopathy, which moves the specialty. 4.2.14 covers inflammatory pain.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is attributing the tenderness to the joint line without
finding the joint line first.

The second is treating the pes finding as an exclusion of medial compartment
disease, when the two coexist.

The third is omitting the resisted flexion test, which is the localising step.

The fourth is missing a hamstring problem proximal to the insertion. 4.16.6
covers it.

The fifth is injecting or treating locally without excluding the joint causes.

### Key Learning Points

- The value of this diagnosis is preventing an operation on the joint.
- Pes tenderness with medial compartment change means two sources and a partial result.
- Donor site symptoms after a hamstring harvest sit at this insertion.
- Several tender insertions with systemic features is an enthesopathy.
- Finding the joint line first is what makes the localisation mean anything.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

In referred practice the pes is the finding that explains medial knee pain in a
patient whose joint has been investigated and found acceptable.

A patient with a normal arthroscopy and persisting medial pain, tender below the
joint line, had an extra-articular problem available at the first examination.
4.6.4 covers the joint line.

A patient with a hamstring graft harvest and medial pain at the insertion has
donor site symptoms, which are common and under recognised. 4.20.1 covers that
assessment.

A patient with medial compartment arthritis and a prominent pes component has a
complaint that an osteotomy or an arthroplasty will only partly address, and
saying so before the operation protects the expectation. 4.18.3 covers that
knee.

A patient with a previous medial reconstruction and tenderness at the pes may
have a problem at a fixation point rather than at the insertion, and the
operation note separates them. 4.12.8 covers that assessment.

In medicolegal work this is an overload or degenerative condition and is
difficult to attribute to a single event. 4.23.7 covers that setting.

### Key Learning Points

- The pes explains medial pain in a patient whose joint has been found acceptable.
- Donor site symptoms after a hamstring harvest sit here and are under recognised.
- With medial compartment arthritis, an operation on the joint only partly addresses the complaint.
- After a medial reconstruction the tenderness may be at a fixation point.
- As an overload condition it is hard to attribute to a single event.
