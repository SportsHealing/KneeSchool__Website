# Fissuring

A surface split in cartilage of preserved thickness, which is the earliest
morphological change the modality can show.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

A fissure is a cleft in the cartilage surface with the layer's overall thickness
preserved. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

It appears as a line of fluid signal extending from the surface into the
substance. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

It matters because it is the earliest morphological abnormality visible, and it is
at the limit of what the resolution supports. 5.2.5 covers magnification.

### How It Is Acquired

Use a cartilage sensitive sequence with a sharp cartilage to fluid interface.
5.2.18 covers gradient echo imaging.

Use the thinnest slices available, because a fissure is narrower than most slice
thicknesses. 5.2.5 covers magnification.

Look at the surface contour as well as the signal, since a surface irregularity
may be the only sign. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

Confirm in a second plane, because partial volume averaging produces a line in
one plane. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Assess whether the thickness is preserved, which is what separates this from a
defect. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

A linear fluid signal cleft running from the surface into the cartilage with
preserved thickness around it. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

Surface irregularity without a visible cleft, which is a softer sign.

Fissuring in a compartment whose cartilage is otherwise of normal thickness.
5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

No subchondral marrow change, or a small amount beneath it. 5.11.7 covers bone
marrow lesions.

### Key Learning Points

- A fissure is a surface cleft with thickness preserved.
- It is the earliest morphological change the modality shows.
- It is narrower than most slice thicknesses.
- Surface contour may be the only sign.
- Preserved thickness separates it from a defect.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the finding to name when asked what the earliest visible
cartilage change is.

The expected points are the cartilage sequence, the thinnest slices, the surface
contour, the second plane, and the preserved thickness.

Say that it is at the limit of the resolution. 5.2.5 covers magnification.

Say that a single plane line may be partial volume averaging. 5.5.1 covers the
standard protocol.

Say that matrix change precedes it and is invisible on a routine study. 5.18.2
covers T2 mapping.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that a fissure is narrower than the voxel it sits in, so
it is detected by the fluid filling it rather than by its own dimensions, which
makes detection dependent on the fluid contrast. 5.2.18 covers gradient echo
imaging.

That dependence is why a cartilage sensitive sequence matters more here than for
any other cartilage finding. 5.5.7 covers the cartilage.

Fissuring follows matrix degeneration, so the tissue was already abnormal before
the fissure appeared, and the compositional sequences detect that earlier phase.
5.18.3 covers T1 rho imaging.

Preserved thickness is what makes it a different lesion from a defect, because
there is still a full layer bearing load. 3.5.2 covers the contact pressures.

### How Good It Is

Fissuring is the cartilage finding at the limit of the modality's resolution, and
its detection depends almost entirely on the sequence.

Its best contribution is identifying a compartment with early change in a knee
that is otherwise normal. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

It is limited by being narrower than the voxel, by partial volume artefacts
imitating it, and by its clinical significance being uncertain. 5.21.3 covers the
cartilage imaging literature.

width values appear either: those are published quantities and this environment

### Key Learning Points

- A fissure is narrower than the voxel and is detected by the fluid in it.
- Detection depends on the fluid contrast more than for any other cartilage finding.
- Matrix degeneration precedes the fissure.
- Compositional sequences detect the earlier phase.
- Preserved thickness means a full layer is still bearing load.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level fissuring changes the conversation rather than the operation.

Fissuring in a symptomatic compartment establishes that the cartilage is involved,
which supports a loading and activity discussion. 4.17.5 covers articular
cartilage defects.

Fissuring is not itself a repair indication, because there is no defect to fill.
5.11.8 covers cartilage repair procedures.

Fissuring adjacent to a planned meniscal or cruciate operation is prognostic
information for that operation. 5.6.13 covers meniscal repair imaging.

Fissuring in an asymptomatic compartment is a finding of uncertain significance
and should be reported as such. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting a partial volume artefact as a fissure. 5.2.5
covers magnification.

A single plane appearance is accepted. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Fissuring is treated as a repair indication. 5.11.8 covers cartilage repair
procedures.

A normal zonal signal boundary is read as a fissure. 5.11.1 covers normal
cartilage.

Symptoms are attributed to it without the clinical picture matching. 4.17.1
covers the cartilage clinical presentation.

### Key Learning Points

- A partial volume artefact is reported as a fissure.
- A single plane appearance is accepted.
- Fissuring is treated as a repair indication.
- A normal zonal boundary is read as a fissure.
- Symptoms are attributed without the clinical picture matching.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level fissuring sits in the awkward space between a finding the
modality can barely see and a change that matters before it is visible.

Detection is at the resolution limit, so reported fissuring prevalence depends on
the protocol more than on the population, which makes cross study comparison
weak. 5.21.3 covers the cartilage imaging literature.

Matrix degeneration precedes fissuring and is detectable by compositional
sequences, so the clinically interesting window is earlier than the
morphologically visible one and is not routinely imaged. 5.18.3 covers T1 rho
imaging.

The clinical significance of isolated fissuring is not established, so a finding
at the edge of detectability carries an uncertain consequence, which is the worst
combination for a report. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

Three dimensional isotropic acquisition improves detection and changes the
contrast, so improved sensitivity arrives with a changed reference. 5.2.18 covers
gradient echo imaging.

The useful report states that the finding is at the limit of resolution and that
its significance in isolation is uncertain. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage
lesions.

### Key Learning Points

- Reported prevalence depends on the protocol more than on the population.
- The clinically interesting window precedes the visible one.
- The significance of isolated fissuring is not established.
- Improved sensitivity arrives with a changed reference.
- Say that the finding is at the resolution limit.
