# School Leaderboards

A planned competition between classes and schools, the rules it would run
under, and the data protection reasons it is not open.

## For Young Learners

### What This Is

The plan is a competition between classes and between schools, based on how
much of the Junior Academy a group has worked through together.

It is between groups, never between named individuals. That is a design
decision rather than a detail, and the reasons are in the next sections.

Nothing is open. A leaderboard needs accounts, progress tracking, a moderation
process and a lawful basis for handling data about children, and this site has
none of those. This page sets out what would have to be true first.

### How to Earn It

Entry would be through a teacher or coach, who registers the group. Pupils
would not register themselves, which keeps the responsibility and the consent
with an adult.

Four things have to exist before that is possible.

A way of recording progress. The site is currently a set of pages with no
accounts and no memory of who has visited.

A lawful basis for processing data about children under the United Kingdom
General Data Protection Regulation, with a data protection impact assessment
completed first.

Compliance with the Information Commissioner's Age Appropriate Design Code,
which sets fifteen standards for any online service likely to be used by
children.

A named safeguarding lead and a pre moderation process, both of which the
publishing plan requires for anything on the site that a user can contribute
to.

### What It Covers

The unit entered would be a class, a year group, a team or a school. Never a
pupil.

What would count is chapters completed by the group and challenge rungs
cleared by the group, not speed and not individual scores.

A table would show school or class names and their totals. It would show no
pupil names, no ages, no photographs, no locations beyond the school, and
nothing that could identify a child.

### Rules and Fair Play

These rules are fixed in the design, before any of it is built.

No individual pupil appears on any public table, under any circumstances.

No personal data is collected from a pupil. Not a name, not an age, not an
email address, not a photograph.

A teacher can withdraw a group at any time and the entry is deleted rather
than hidden.

Entries are moderated before they appear. Nothing goes live automatically.

Progress counts completion, not speed, so the competition cannot be won by
rushing.

No award, prize or anything of value attaches to a position.

### Where to Find Out More

The Information Commissioner's Office publishes the Age Appropriate Design
Code in full, and it is readable.

The editorial standards page on this site sets out the governance rules that
apply across the Junior Academy.

The build status page records what remains outstanding before this feature
could open.

### Key Learning Points

- The leaderboard would rank classes and schools, never named individuals.
- It is not open, because the site has no accounts and no lawful basis for children's data.
- A data protection impact assessment and compliance with the ICO code would come first.
- No personal data would be collected from any pupil at any point.
- Progress would count completion rather than speed, and no prize would attach to a position.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.
