# Cartilage Procedures

Repair tissue that never matches native cartilage, so a scan read against native
cartilage reports failure in a success.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

Post-operative imaging of a cartilage procedure shows the repair tissue, its
surface, the bone beneath it and the surrounding cartilage. 5.11.8 covers the
cartilage repair procedures.

The repair tissue differs from native cartilage in thickness, signal and surface
contour. 5.11.9 covers the ACI imaging.

It matters because those differences are expected, so the reference appearance
is not native cartilage. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

### How It Is Acquired

State the procedure and the interval before describing anything. 5.5.3 covers the
post-operative protocol.

Use a cartilage sensitive sequence rather than a fluid sensitive one alone. 5.5.7
covers the cartilage.

Assess the fill of the defect, the surface and the integration at the edges.
5.11.8 covers the cartilage repair procedures.

Assess the bone beneath, since marrow change and overgrowth are both recognised.
5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Assess the opposing surface and the rest of the compartment. 5.12.6 covers
cartilage injury.

### What Normal Looks Like

A defect filled to approximately the surrounding level. 5.11.8 covers the
cartilage repair procedures.

Signal in the repair tissue unlike native cartilage, which is expected. 5.11.10
covers the MACI imaging.

Integration at the edges with no fluid filled cleft. 5.11.4 covers delamination.

Marrow change under the repair appropriate to the interval. 5.11.7 covers bone
marrow lesions.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

A defect that has not filled, or has lost its fill. 5.11.2 covers chondral
defects.

A fluid filled cleft at the interface with native cartilage. 5.11.4 covers
delamination.

Overgrowth of the repair above the surrounding surface. 5.11.8 covers the
cartilage repair procedures.

Bone overgrowth beneath, which is recognised after some procedures. 5.11.11
covers the osteochondral grafts.

Marrow change persisting long after the interval at which it should have
settled. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

### Key Learning Points

- Repair tissue differs from native cartilage by design.
- The reference appearance is not native cartilage.
- Fill, surface and integration are the three features.
- The bone beneath is part of the assessment.
- Overgrowth occurs as well as deficiency.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names the procedure and the interval first.

The expected points are the procedure, the interval, the cartilage sensitive
sequence, the fill and integration, and the bone.

Say that repair tissue reads abnormal on every sequence and that this is
expected. 5.11.9 covers the ACI imaging.

Say that the scoring systems for repair tissue differ. 5.20.5 covers reporting
cartilage lesions.

Say that symptoms rather than appearance decide the outcome. 4.20.5 covers the
cartilage surgery examination.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that no procedure reproduces hyaline cartilage, so
each produces a tissue with its own composition and its own appearance, and the
appearance has to be read against that procedure. 5.11.8 covers the cartilage
repair procedures.

Marrow stimulation, cell based repair and osteochondral grafting therefore give
three different expected appearances at the same interval. 5.11.11 covers the
osteochondral grafts.

Marrow change under a repair is part of the course and becomes abnormal only by
persisting. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Quantitative sequences have been used to follow maturation and carry the
standardisation problem of every quantitative measure. 5.18.2 covers the T2
mapping.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging is the best available way to follow a cartilage
repair and reports a tissue with no agreed normal appearance.

Its best contribution is the fill, the integration and the bone beneath. 5.11.8
covers the cartilage repair procedures.

It is limited because the expected appearance differs by procedure and interval,
because scoring systems differ, and because appearance and symptoms correspond
loosely. 4.20.5 covers the cartilage surgery examination.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no fill
percentages or repair scores appear either: those are published quantities from
particular systems and this environment cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- No procedure reproduces hyaline cartilage.
- Each procedure has its own expected appearance.
- Marrow change becomes abnormal by persisting.
- Quantitative follow-up carries the usual problem.
- Appearance and symptoms correspond loosely.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the imaging answers whether the repair is structurally present
and whether the joint has moved on. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

A delaminated or lost repair is a mechanical finding and an operative one.
5.11.4 covers delamination.

Overgrowth can be symptomatic and is treated differently from deficiency. 5.11.8
covers the cartilage repair procedures.

Alignment and the meniscus decide whether a repeat procedure has a chance.
5.16.5 covers osteotomy planning.

Progression elsewhere in the compartment moves the conversation to arthritis.
5.20.6 covers reporting osteoarthritis.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting a working repair as failed because it does not
look like cartilage. 5.11.9 covers the ACI imaging.

Expected marrow change is read as graft failure. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow
lesions.

Overgrowth is reported as a loose body. 5.14.6 covers synovial chondromatosis.

A graded report is given with no procedure and no interval attached. 5.20.5
covers reporting cartilage lesions.

Quantitative values from one site are compared with a published range. 5.18.1
covers the quantitative MRI.

### Key Learning Points

- A working repair is reported as failed.
- Expected marrow change is read as failure.
- Overgrowth is reported as a loose body.
- A grade with no procedure or interval is unusable.
- Quantitative values do not travel.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the procedure was done elsewhere and the records do not say
which one. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

The expected appearance depends on the procedure, so without it the report
describes a tissue it cannot classify. 5.11.8 covers the cartilage repair
procedures.

A second procedure over a failed one has a different expected appearance again.
5.11.10 covers the MACI imaging.

The index imaging and the operative detail are worth more than any new sequence.
5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Automated grading is trained on native cartilage and applied to repair tissue.
5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

The useful report gives the procedure if known, the interval, the fill, the
integration, the surface, the bone and the rest of the compartment. 5.20.1
covers structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- Without the procedure the tissue cannot be classified.
- A second procedure changes the expectation again.
- Index imaging beats any new sequence.
- Automated grading is applied outside its training.
- The report names the procedure and the interval.
