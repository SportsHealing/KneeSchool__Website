# Dynamic Alignment

Alignment while the limb is loaded and moving. The adduction moment during
walking predicts progression where the static angle does not.

## For Medical Students

### The Principle

A measurement taken standing still describes one instant. Most of what a joint
does happens in movement.

Standing on two legs halves the load on each. Standing on one puts all of it
through that leg, and more than all of it when moving.

So the number that matters is taken during the activity, and the number
usually available is taken in a radiology department.

### At the Knee

During walking the body passes over one leg at a time, and the load on that
knee rises above body weight. 3.14.2 covers stance phase.

How far the body's weight passes to one side of that knee changes through the
step.

Some knees also shift suddenly outwards as weight comes onto them, which is
called a varus thrust. 3.13.3 covers varus.

A thrust means the knee is moving into deformity under load, which a standing
film cannot show.

### What Changes It

Walking speed, which changes the forces.

Trunk position, because leaning over the loaded leg reduces the sideways
lever. 3.14.1 covers normal gait.

Muscle strength and control around the hip and knee.

Ligament laxity, which decides whether the knee shifts under load.

### Clinical Relevance

Watching a patient walk shows things a radiograph cannot. 4.3.2 covers gait
observation.

A thrust is a bad sign and it is free to look for.

Teaching someone to lean slightly over the loaded leg reduces the load on that
side, which is a real intervention.

Section 8 covers the rehabilitation.

### Key Learning Points

- A standing measurement describes one instant; movement is where load happens.
- Single leg stance puts more than body weight through one knee.
- A varus thrust is the knee shifting into deformity under load.
- Trunk lean over the loaded leg reduces the sideways lever.
- Watching a patient walk shows what a radiograph cannot.

## MRCS Level

### The Principle

The mechanically correct quantity is the external knee adduction moment during
stance, which is the product of the ground reaction force and its moment arm.
3.4.6 covers external moments.

Static alignment contributes the moment arm and nothing else. Force magnitude,
direction and timing all come from the movement.

That is why a static angle and a dynamic moment correlate moderately rather
than closely.

### At the Knee

The adduction moment peaks early in stance and again late, and its first peak
is the value usually reported.

It predicts medial compartment progression more strongly than the static hip
knee ankle angle does. 3.13.8 covers progression.

Varus thrust is a visible dynamic increase in varus during loading, and it is
an independent predictor of progression.

Trunk lean towards the stance limb reduces the moment arm and measurably
reduces the moment, which is the basis of gait retraining.

Toe out walking and a lateral wedge also alter the moment, with smaller and
less consistent effects.

### What Changes It

Static alignment, which sets the baseline moment arm. 3.13.1 covers it.

Walking speed, which scales the ground reaction force. 3.4.2 covers it.

Trunk and pelvic kinematics, which move the force's line of action.

Soft tissue laxity, which permits thrust. 3.10.1 covers the lateral
restraints.

Body mass, which scales the force directly. 3.5.7 covers obesity.

### How It Is Measured

Three dimensional gait analysis with force plates, which is the reference and
is confined to laboratories. 3.16.7 covers motion capture.

Clinical observation of thrust during walking, which is free and has moderate
reproducibility. 4.3.2 covers it.

Wearable sensors and instrumented insoles, which estimate the moment in the
field with less accuracy. 3.16.8 covers them.

Musculoskeletal modelling, which converts measured kinematics into estimated
moments and inherits its assumptions. 3.16.4 covers it.

No routine clinical pathway measures the adduction moment, which is the
chapter's central gap.

### Clinical Relevance

Observe gait in every patient in whom alignment is being considered, because
thrust predicts independently of the film. 4.3.2 covers it.

Explain gait retraining and trunk lean as a genuine mechanical intervention
rather than as advice. Section 8 covers the programmes.

Expect walking speed and body mass to change the moment without changing the
angle.

State that the predictive quantity is not the one being measured when quoting
an alignment angle. 3.13.1 covers the angle.

### Key Learning Points

- The relevant quantity is the external knee adduction moment during stance.
- Static alignment contributes only the moment arm.
- The adduction moment predicts medial progression better than the static angle.
- Varus thrust is an independent predictor and a free clinical observation.
- Trunk lean towards the stance limb measurably reduces the moment.

## FRCS Level

### At the Knee

This page is where the chapter's argument lands. The variable that predicts is
not the variable that is measured, and it is not the variable that surgery
corrects.

Correcting the static angle changes the moment arm and leaves force magnitude,
direction and timing untouched, so the moment changes by less than the
geometry suggests.

That partly explains why osteotomy outcomes vary between patients with
identical radiographic corrections. 3.13.9 covers the operation.

It also explains why gait retraining, lateral wedges and weight loss have
measurable mechanical effects without changing a single degree on a film.

Conventional teaching plans from the static angle because it is reproducible
and correctable, and the honest position is that it is the available surrogate
rather than the right variable. 6.154 covers the controversies.

### What Changes It

Geometry, which surgery changes and which is only the moment arm.

Force magnitude, which body mass and walking speed change. 3.5.7 covers
obesity.

Force direction and timing, which gait retraining changes.

Laxity and thrust, which decide whether the loaded alignment matches the
standing one.

Neuromuscular control, which is modifiable and is the least measured of these
in clinic.

### How It Is Measured

Laboratory gait analysis, which is the reference and measures a laboratory
walk rather than a life. 3.16.7 covers it.

Instrumented implants, which give direct in vivo load and only in replaced
knees.

Wearables, which trade accuracy for the number of steps measured, and which
are the most likely route to a clinical measure. 3.16.8 covers them.

Clinical thrust assessment, which is the only part of this available today at
no cost. 4.3.2 covers it.

The absence of a routine dynamic measurement is a limitation of practice
rather than of knowledge: the quantity is known, it is simply not measured.

### Clinical Relevance

Assess thrust clinically in every alignment decision, since it is the only
dynamic information routinely obtainable. 4.3.2 covers it.

Expect a radiographic correction to change the moment by less than the
geometry implies, and counsel on that basis.

Offer gait retraining and weight reduction as mechanical interventions in
their own right rather than as adjuncts. Section 8 covers them.

State clearly that the static angle is a surrogate for the adduction moment
when explaining the rationale for surgery.

Weigh the patient's age, physiology, comorbidities, knee demand, psychological
state and their own ideas, concerns and expectations, since the non operative
levers here depend entirely on what the patient will do.

### Key Learning Points

- The variable that predicts is neither the one measured nor the one surgery corrects.
- Correcting geometry changes the moment arm and leaves force magnitude and timing untouched.
- The moment changes by less than the radiographic correction suggests.
- Gait retraining, wedges and weight loss change mechanics without changing a degree on a film.
- The dynamic quantity is known and simply not measured in practice.
