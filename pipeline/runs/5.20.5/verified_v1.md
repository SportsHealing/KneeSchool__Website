# Reporting Cartilage Lesions

A grade with no system attached, which is the commonest uninterpretable sentence
in a knee report.

## For Medical Students

### What It Shows

A cartilage report states the depth of the lesion, its area, its location and
the state of the bone beneath it. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

It is built from a sequence with cartilage contrast rather than from a fluid
sensitive sequence alone. 5.5.7 covers the cartilage.

It matters because the depth and the location decide whether a cartilage
procedure is possible at all. 5.17.5 covers the cartilage procedures.

### How It Is Acquired

Name the grading system used, because several are in use and their grades are
not interchangeable. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

State the depth as partial or full thickness rather than by grade alone. 5.11.3
covers fissuring.

State the area and the compartment, since a contained lesion differs from
diffuse loss. 5.11.1 covers the normal cartilage.

Report the bone beneath, because marrow change often marks a lesion the surface
does not. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Report whether the cartilage is separated from the bone with the surface intact.
5.11.4 covers delamination.

### What Normal Looks Like

The grading system named in the technique or the findings. 5.11.2 covers
chondral defects.

Depth, area, compartment and surface each stated. 5.11.1 covers the normal
cartilage.

The subchondral bone reported alongside each lesion. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow
lesions.

Opposing surfaces described separately rather than as one joint. 5.12.6 covers
the patellofemoral cartilage injury.

A statement of which sequences the assessment rested on. 5.5.7 covers the
cartilage.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

A grade quoted with no system named. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

Chondral thinning reported with neither depth nor area. 5.11.2 covers chondral
defects.

Signal change with an intact surface reported as a defect. 5.11.3 covers
fissuring.

A lesion reported with no mention of the bone under it. 5.11.7 covers bone
marrow lesions.

Loss on one surface reported and the opposing surface left out. 5.12.6 covers
the patellofemoral cartilage injury.

### Key Learning Points

- Depth, area, location and bone are the four facts.
- Several grading systems are in use and are not interchangeable.
- A grade with no system is uninterpretable.
- Signal change under an intact surface is not a defect.
- Opposing surfaces are reported separately.

## MRCS Level

### How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names the system before the grade.

The expected points are the system, the depth, the area and compartment, the
subchondral bone, and the opposing surface.

Say that the report should state which sequences the assessment rests on. 5.5.7
covers the cartilage.

Say that a grade from an arthroscopic system applied to a scan is an inference.
5.17.5 covers the cartilage procedures.

Say that the patellofemoral joint is the hardest compartment to grade. 5.12.6
covers the patellofemoral cartilage injury.

### What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the systems in use were written for different
purposes, some for arthroscopy and some for imaging, so a grade carries its
system's assumptions with it. 5.21.3 covers the cartilage imaging literature.

Depth decides whether the subchondral plate is breached, which is the division
that matters surgically. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

Area decides whether a lesion is containable, and a depth alone cannot answer
that. 5.11.8 covers the cartilage repair procedures.

Marrow change under an intact surface is a finding rather than a grade and needs
reporting as one. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

### How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance reporting of cartilage detects a full thickness lesion well
and grades the intermediate ones poorly.

Its best contribution is a full thickness defect with its location and its bone.
5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

It is limited by grading variation between readers and between systems, by
curved thin surfaces, and by separation hiding under an intact surface. 5.11.4
covers delamination.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no grade
boundaries or thickness values appear either: those are published quantities
defined differently between systems and this environment cannot check them.

### Key Learning Points

- Grading systems carry their own assumptions.
- Depth decides whether the subchondral plate is breached.
- Area decides whether a lesion is containable.
- Marrow change under an intact surface is a finding.
- The intermediate grades are the unreliable ones.

## FRCS Level

### How Good It Is

At decision level the report either supports a cartilage procedure discussion or
it does not. 5.17.5 covers the cartilage procedures.

A contained full thickness lesion with a stated area supports one. 5.11.8 covers
the cartilage repair procedures.

Diffuse loss across a compartment does not, and the report should say so rather
than grade it. 5.20.6 covers reporting osteoarthritis.

Loss on both opposing surfaces changes the options and belongs in the
conclusion. 5.12.6 covers the patellofemoral cartilage injury.

The alignment and the meniscal state decide whether the lesion has a cause worth
correcting. 5.3.20 covers the alignment changes.

### Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reading a cartilage lesion as the explanation for the
pain. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

An incidental lesion in an older knee is reported in the vocabulary of injury.
5.20.6 covers reporting osteoarthritis.

A partial volume effect at a curved surface is reported as loss. 5.5.7 covers
the cartilage.

A repair site is reported as a defect, because repair tissue never looks normal.
5.11.8 covers the cartilage repair procedures.

Separation from the bone is absent from the report because the surface above it
was intact. 5.11.4 covers delamination.

### Key Learning Points

- A lesion is reported as the cause of the pain more often than it is.
- An incidental lesion is described as an injury.
- Partial volume at a curved surface mimics loss.
- Repair tissue is reported as a defect.
- Separation hides under an intact surface.

## Fellowship Level

### Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the grade in the report has already shaped the patient's
expectation of a cartilage operation. 5.17.5 covers the cartilage procedures.

The grade on the scan and the grade at arthroscopy disagree, so the operative
plan should carry both and the consent should say so. 5.11.8 covers the
cartilage repair procedures.

Quantitative measures are reported as objective while being acquired differently
in every department. 5.18.2 covers T2 mapping.

After a repair no agreed reference appearance exists, so a report of failure may
be a report of the graft. 5.11.9 covers the ACI imaging.

Automated grading is trained on labels that readers disagree about and returns a
grade with no interval of uncertainty. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence
literature.

The useful report gives the system, the depth, the area, the compartment, the
opposing surface, the bone, and the sequences relied on. 5.20.1 covers
structured reporting.

### Key Learning Points

- Imaging and arthroscopic grades disagree.
- Quantitative measures vary between departments.
- A repair has no agreed reference appearance.
- Automated grading returns no uncertainty.
- The report should name the sequences relied on.
