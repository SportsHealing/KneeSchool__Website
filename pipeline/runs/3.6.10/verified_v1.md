# Meniscal Extrusion

A meniscus that has slid out of the joint. The tissue is all there and it has
stopped working, which is the clearest case of function parting from
appearance.

## For Medical Students

### The Principle

A structure only works where it is. A load spreader that has moved out of the
load path spreads nothing, however intact it is.

Being pushed outwards is what the meniscus is loaded to resist. If the
resistance fails, it goes.

A washer that has slipped off a bolt is the comparison. Nothing is broken and
nothing is working.

### At the Knee

Extrusion is displacement of the meniscus beyond the edge of the tibial
plateau, measured on imaging. 5.6 covers imaging.

Conventional teaching treats displacement of more than 3 millimetres as
significant, which is a threshold on a continuous measurement rather than a
biological boundary.

It happens when the ring can no longer resist the outward push. That means a
root tear, a radial tear, or failure of the peripheral attachments. 3.6.2
covers hoop stress.

An extruded meniscus is outside the contact, so the compartment behaves as if
the meniscus had been removed. 3.5.5 covers meniscal loss.

### What Changes It

Root integrity, which is the commonest structural cause. 3.6.11 covers root
tears.

Radial tears, which release the hoop and let the meniscus spread outwards.

Degeneration and loss of the peripheral attachments.

Load and alignment, which push harder on a weakened ring.

### Clinical Relevance

Extrusion is a functional finding rather than an incidental measurement, which
is worth stating when it appears in a report.

It explains how a compartment can degenerate with a meniscus that looks
present.

It is associated with medial compartment osteoarthritis, and the mechanical
account runs through lost load distribution. 6.73 covers the condition.

Treating it means restoring the ring and its anchors rather than addressing
the displacement itself. Section 6 covers management.

### Key Learning Points

- A load spreader outside the load path spreads nothing.
- Extrusion is displacement beyond the tibial plateau edge.
- Conventional teaching treats more than 3 millimetres as significant.
- It follows root tears, radial tears or loss of peripheral attachment.
- An extruded meniscus leaves the compartment behaving as if it had been removed.

## MRCS Level

### The Principle

Extrusion is the mechanical endpoint of a failed hoop. Compression pushes the
wedge outwards and circumferential tension resists it, so losing the tension
lets the displacement happen. 3.6.2 covers the mechanism.

That makes extrusion a sign of hoop failure rather than a lesion in itself,
which changes how it is read and what is done about it.

It is load dependent, so a supine measurement underestimates it. Weight
bearing imaging shows more extrusion than unloaded imaging in the same knee.

### At the Knee

Conventional teaching gives 3 millimetres as the commonly used threshold for
significant medial extrusion, measured from the tibial plateau margin on a
coronal image.

Posterior root tears are the structural lesion most strongly associated with
extrusion, which follows from the roots carrying the hoop tension. 3.6.11
covers them.

Extrusion is also described in degenerative knees without a discrete tear,
where the tissue has lost its tensile capacity rather than its continuity.

The lateral meniscus extrudes less, partly because of its meniscofemoral
attachments. 2.6.3 covers them.

### What Changes It

Root integrity, which is the dominant structural factor.

Weight bearing at the time of imaging, which changes the measurement.

Degeneration, which reduces tensile capacity without a discrete tear.

Alignment and body mass, which increase the outward push.

### How It Is Measured

Coronal magnetic resonance imaging measures the displacement from the plateau
margin and is the standard, supine.

Weight bearing magnetic resonance or ultrasound measures it loaded and finds
more.

Cadaveric work demonstrates the relationship between root sectioning and
extrusion directly.

The 3 millimetre threshold is a convention applied to a continuous
measurement, and it should be treated as such.

### Clinical Relevance

Read extrusion as a sign of hoop failure and look for the cause.

Expect supine imaging to underestimate it, and consider loaded imaging where
it matters.

Treat the 3 millimetre figure as a convention rather than a boundary.

Expect less extrusion laterally, given the meniscofemoral attachments.

### Key Learning Points

- Extrusion is the mechanical endpoint of a failed hoop, not a lesion in itself.
- It is load dependent, so supine imaging underestimates it.
- Conventional teaching uses 3 millimetres as the threshold for significance.
- Posterior root tears are the lesion most strongly associated with it.
- Degenerative knees extrude without a discrete tear, having lost tensile capacity.

## FRCS Level

### At the Knee

Treat extrusion as the outcome measure for whether a meniscus is working, in a
field with no direct measurement of function. It is the closest available
proxy.

That applies after surgery too. Conventional teaching holds that extrusion
persisting after root repair indicates the hoop was not restored, and that it
predicts a poorer result.

A transplant that extrudes is a transplant that is not in the load path, which
is one of the recognised modes of failure. 6.43 covers transplantation
problems.

### What Changes It

Whether the hoop and its anchors were restored, which is the question
extrusion answers.

Graft sizing and fixation in transplantation.

Alignment, which pushes a restored meniscus outwards if uncorrected. 3.13
covers it.

Tissue quality, which decides whether tension can be held at all.

### How It Is Measured

Serial coronal imaging before and after surgery measures the change, supine by
convention.

Weight bearing imaging measures it under the condition that matters and is
less widely available.

Clinical cohorts relate postoperative extrusion to outcome, which is the
evidence that it is a useful proxy.

No measurement of meniscal function itself exists, which is why extrusion
carries this weight.

### Clinical Relevance

Measure extrusion before and after meniscal surgery as the available proxy for
function.

Read persisting extrusion after root repair as the hoop not having been
restored.

Correct alignment, or a restored meniscus will extrude again.

Prefer loaded imaging where the measurement will change a decision.

### Key Learning Points

- Extrusion is the closest available proxy for whether a meniscus is working.
- Persisting extrusion after root repair indicates the hoop was not restored.
- A transplant that extrudes is not in the load path.
- An uncorrected malalignment will push a restored meniscus out again.
- No measurement of meniscal function itself exists, which is why extrusion carries weight.
