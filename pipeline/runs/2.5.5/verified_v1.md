# MRI Anatomy

What a normal medial meniscus looks like on magnetic resonance imaging, plane
by plane, and which normal appearances are mistaken for tears.

## For Medical Students

### Structure and Location

On magnetic resonance imaging a normal meniscus is uniformly dark on every
sequence. It contains little free water, so it returns almost no signal.

On a sagittal image through the body of the meniscus the anterior and
posterior horns appear as two dark triangles, one at each end of the plateau,
with their apices pointing towards the centre of the joint.

On a sagittal image through the periphery the meniscus appears instead as a
continuous dark band, the bow tie, because the slice passes through the body
rather than the horns.

On a coronal image the meniscus appears as a dark wedge at the margin of the
joint on each side, thick peripherally and tapering centrally.

### Relations

The dark meniscus sits between the bright cartilage of the femoral condyle
above and the plateau below, which is what makes it easy to identify.

Peripherally it is continuous with the capsule, and the deep fibres of the
medial collateral ligament attach to it. On a coronal image the superficial
collateral is seen as a separate dark band further out.

The posterior horn of the medial meniscus is larger than the anterior horn,
which is a normal asymmetry and the first thing to check when deciding which
meniscus a slice shows.

### Function

Imaging exists here to answer whether the meniscus is intact, where a tear is
and whether it reaches the surface. Knowing the normal appearance is what
makes any of that possible.

The sagittal and coronal planes answer different questions. Sagittal shows the
horns and the anteroposterior extent; coronal shows the radial width, the
peripheral attachment and whether the meniscus has extruded.

### Clinical Relevance

A uniformly dark meniscus of normal shape with a normal peripheral attachment
is normal, and that is most of what this page is for.

Tear signal grading and the criteria for calling a tear belong to 5.6.2. This
page stops at normal.

### Key Learning Points

- A normal meniscus is uniformly dark on every sequence, because it holds little free water.
- Peripheral sagittal slices show a continuous bow tie; central slices show two triangles.
- The posterior horn of the medial meniscus is larger than its anterior horn.
- Sagittal shows the horns and extent; coronal shows width, attachment and extrusion.

## MRCS Level

### Structure and Location

Count the bow ties. Because the meniscus is a continuous ring at its
periphery, consecutive sagittal slices through the outer part of the joint
show a continuous band, and the number of such slices reflects the radial
width of the meniscus at that point.

Moving centrally, the band becomes two triangles as the slice passes inside
the free edge of the body and catches only the horns.

On the coronal images, follow the meniscus from front to back: the anterior
horn, the body at the mid coronal slice, then the posterior horn, with the
peripheral attachment visible at each level.

The horn attachments are seen on the sagittal images closest to the midline
and on the coronal images through the intercondylar area. 2.5.7 covers the
roots.

### Relations

The deep medial collateral fibres blend with the meniscus on coronal images,
so there is no clear line between them. The superficial collateral is a
discrete band further peripherally with a bursa between the two layers.

The transverse intermeniscal ligament crosses in front of the joint and is
seen on sagittal images as a small dark structure just anterior to the
anterior horns. Where it inserts, it can mimic a tear of the anterior horn,
which is a recognised pitfall.

The meniscal periphery and the tibial margin should be close. A gap between
them on a mid coronal image is meniscal extrusion.

Posteriorly the semimembranosus and the posterior oblique fibres lie outside
the capsule next to the posterior horn.

### Blood Supply and Innervation

Vascularity is not directly visible in the meniscus on standard sequences. The
perimeniscal plexus lies in tissue that appears as capsule rather than as
vessels.

Contrast enhancement at the periphery after injury reflects the vascular zone
and is one of the few ways the zones can be inferred radiologically, but the
zone is still assessed at arthroscopy.

Signal change within the meniscus reflects water content rather than
perfusion, which is why intrasubstance signal in a middle aged meniscus is a
tissue change rather than a vascular one.

### Function

The two planes together answer the anatomical questions. Sagittal defines
which part of the ring is abnormal; coronal defines how far across the width
and whether the peripheral attachment is intact.

Thin slices and good positioning matter more here than field strength, because
the structures are small and the pitfalls are partial volume effects.

Normal variants have to be known before abnormality can be called, and the
three that matter on the medial side are the intermeniscal ligament insertion,
the oblique meniscomeniscal ligament where present, and a prominent peripheral
vascular cleft.

### Clinical Relevance

Meniscal extrusion is assessed on the mid coronal image and is a functional
finding: it implies the hoop has failed, usually at the root.

Partial volume effects at the free edge and at the horns produce apparent
signal that is not a tear, which is why a finding on one slice in one plane is
treated with suspicion.

The intermeniscal ligament pitfall is worth knowing by name, because it
produces an apparent anterior horn tear in a normal knee.

Tear criteria, signal grading and the accuracy of each sign are owned by 5.6
and 5.6.2. This page is the normal appearance only, which is what the
architecture assigns it.

### Key Learning Points

- The number of consecutive bow tie slices reflects the radial width of the meniscus.
- The deep collateral blends with the meniscus on coronal images; the superficial is a discrete band further out.
- The transverse intermeniscal ligament insertion mimics an anterior horn tear.
- Extrusion is assessed on the mid coronal image and implies hoop failure, usually at the root.
- Intrasubstance signal reflects water content rather than perfusion.

## FRCS Level

### Relations

The relation worth checking before operating is the peripheral one. A tear
whose peripheral attachment is intact and which sits close to the margin is
probably repairable, and the coronal images answer that better than the
arthroscopy report of the previous surgeon.

In a previously operated meniscus, imaging cannot reliably distinguish a
healed repair from a recurrent tear, because both show signal reaching a
surface. That limit is anatomical rather than technical.

### Function

Imaging sets the plan and arthroscopy sets the operation. Planning repair from
imaging and finding unrepairable tissue is a common and manageable sequence;
planning resection and finding repairable tissue without the instruments is
not.

That asymmetry is why the imaging question before a meniscal operation is
where and how peripheral rather than whether torn.

### Clinical Relevance

Assess extrusion, root integrity, rim width and tear location before
arthroscopy, and plan for repair wherever those four favour it.

In the degenerate knee, imaging findings in the meniscus are common and
frequently incidental. The anatomical point is that intrasubstance change is a
property of an ageing meniscus rather than a lesion.

Where imaging suggests root pathology, 2.5.7 is the anatomy and 7.14 the
procedure.

No measurement appears on this page. Rim width, extrusion distance and every
signal criterion are measured values owned by Section 5, and this page
describes appearances rather than thresholds.

### Key Learning Points

- The imaging question before meniscal surgery is where and how peripheral, not whether torn.
- Imaging cannot reliably separate a healed repair from a recurrent tear.
- Assess extrusion, root integrity, rim width and tear location before arthroscopy.
- Intrasubstance change is a property of an ageing meniscus rather than a lesion.
