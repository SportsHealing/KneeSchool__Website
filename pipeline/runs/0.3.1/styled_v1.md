# The Road to Knee Surgeon

The route from school to knee surgeon is a fixed sequence of stages. This page
sets them out in order and names who publishes the rules for each one.

## For Young Learners

### The People Who Do It

A knee surgeon is an orthopaedic surgeon who went on to specialise in the
knee. Orthopaedic surgery covers bones, joints, muscles, tendons and
ligaments. The knee is one of several areas a surgeon can take as a sub
speciality, alongside the hip, the shoulder, the hand, the spine, and the foot
and ankle.

Nobody arrives at that job in one step. The route is built from stages. Each
stage has an entry point, a list of things you have to show you can do, and an
assessment or an examination at the end of it.

A consultant knee surgeon runs operating lists and clinics, and usually
teaches and researches as well. They are one person in a team. A knee service
also needs physiotherapists, radiographers, radiologists, anaesthetists,
theatre nurses, sports scientists and often a researcher.

Most people who work in knee care are not surgeons. If the operating theatre
does not appeal to you, that closes one door and leaves several others open.

### The Route

The stages run in this order.

1. School. Science subjects at GCSE or equivalent, then science at A level or
equivalent. Chapter 0.4 covers subject choice properly.

2. Medical school. A degree in medicine. You qualify as a doctor at the end of
it. You do not qualify as a surgeon.

3. Foundation training. Working as a newly qualified doctor across several
specialities. This is where most people first spend time on an orthopaedic
ward.

4. Core surgical training. The first post that is surgical rather than
general. The MRCS examination sits at this stage.

5. Specialty training in trauma and orthopaedic surgery. Years of operating
and clinics under supervision, with a logbook that records every case. The
FRCS examination sits near the end.

6. Fellowship. Optional on paper and usual in practice. A year or so
concentrated on one area. This is the stage at which most knee surgeons become
knee surgeons.

7. Consultant post.

How many years each stage takes is set nationally and it changes. The General
Medical Council and the Royal College of Surgeons publish the current figures.
Check them there rather than here.

### What It Takes

Three things come up repeatedly from people who have done it.

Stamina. The route is long, and the examinations sit on top of a full working
week.

Hands and eyes that work together. Surgery is a practical craft, and like any
craft it is learned by repetition rather than born with.

Liking the problem. Knee surgery is mostly about how a joint moves and why it
has stopped moving well. If that question is interesting to you at fourteen it
will probably still be interesting at forty.

### Where to Find Out More

Three organisations publish the rules and the current requirements.

The General Medical Council keeps the register of doctors and sets the
standards for medical training.

The Royal College of Surgeons sets and runs the surgical examinations.

The British Orthopaedic Association is the specialty body for trauma and
orthopaedic surgery.

A school careers adviser can also arrange for you to speak to a doctor,
usually through a local hospital or a university outreach scheme.

### Key Learning Points

- Qualifying as a doctor and qualifying as a surgeon are two different things, several years apart.
- The route is a fixed sequence of stages, each with its own entry point and its own assessment.
- MRCS sits early in surgical training and FRCS sits near the end of it.
- Fellowship is the stage where a general orthopaedic surgeon becomes a knee surgeon.
- Most jobs in knee care are not surgical jobs.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.
