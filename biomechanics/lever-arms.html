Lever Arms

Page 3.4.4 · Knee Kinetics · biomechanics page

A force's effect on a joint depends on how far its line passes from the axis. The patella exists to lengthen one of those distances.

Lever Arms

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

The Principle

A lever arm, or moment arm, is the perpendicular distance from a force's line of action to the axis it turns about.

Turning effect is the force multiplied by that distance. Doubling the distance doubles the effect without changing the force.

A spanner is the everyday version. A longer spanner turns a tight nut with the same hand strength, because the distance has grown.

At the Knee

The quadriceps moment arm is the distance from the patellar tendon's line to the knee's axis of rotation. The patella holds that line away from the joint and lengthens it.

A longer moment arm means less muscle force is needed for the same extension moment, and less muscle force means less joint compression. 3.4.3 covers joint force.

The arm is not constant. It changes through flexion because both the tendon's line and the axis move, and the axis moves because the knee is polycentric. 3.3.7 covers the instant centre.

Conventional teaching puts the quadriceps moment arm at its greatest in the mid range of flexion and smaller at both extremes, which is part of why terminal extension is the hardest part of the arc to work through.

What Changes It

Patellar height. A high patella changes the tendon's line and the contact position.

Patellectomy, which removes the spacer and shortens the arm substantially.

Flexion angle, through the moving axis and the changing tendon line.

Tibial tubercle position, which is why moving it is a mechanical operation rather than a cosmetic one. Section 7 covers technique.

Clinical Relevance

Removing the patella weakens extension by shortening a lever arm rather than by damaging a muscle, which is why the loss persists after rehabilitation.

A patient's extension strength through range follows the lever arm as well as the muscle, so weakness can be angle specific.

Operations that move the tubercle change the lever arm, and that change is part of their effect rather than a side effect.

The same logic applies to every muscle around the knee, and the quadriceps is the one with a bone dedicated to the problem.

Key Learning Points

  • A lever arm is the perpendicular distance from a force's line to the axis.
  • Turning effect is force multiplied by lever arm.
  • The patella lengthens the quadriceps lever arm, so less force does the same work.
  • Less muscle force means less joint compression.
  • The arm changes through flexion, because the axis itself moves.

MRCS Level

The Principle

Define the axis before the arm. A moment arm is meaningless without the axis it is measured to, and at the knee that axis moves through the arc.

The effective moment arm is therefore measured rather than derived from anatomy, usually as the ratio of tendon excursion to joint rotation.

Agonist and antagonist arms matter together. The hamstrings' arm decides what a given hamstring force does to the joint, which is why co-contraction has a net effect that depends on both.

At the Knee

The quadriceps effective moment arm rises from extension into mid flexion and falls again in deep flexion, by conventional teaching, with the peak commonly placed somewhere between 20 and 60 degrees.

The patellar tendon angle relative to the tibia changes through the arc and reverses sign, which is the mechanical basis of the shear the tendon applies to the tibia.

That shear is the reason open chain extension in early flexion loads the anterior cruciate: the tendon pulls the tibia forwards when the knee is near extension. 3.7 covers it.

Hamstring moment arms change less dramatically and their line is closer to the joint, which is why they are a weaker antagonist to anterior translation than their strength suggests.

What Changes It

Patellar height and tendon length, which set the tendon's line through the arc.

Trochlear geometry, which positions the patella and therefore the tendon.

Tubercle position in all three planes, which is why anteromedialisation changes load as well as tracking.

Component position in arthroplasty, which moves the axis and so changes every moment arm at once.

How It Is Measured

The tendon excursion method measures the arm as excursion per unit rotation, in cadavers and in imaging studies.

Magnetic resonance imaging through flexion measures the geometric arm directly, without load.

Modelling computes arms from anatomy and inherits the error in the assumed axis.

Published values differ by method, so the shape of the curve through the arc is more reliable than any single figure.

Clinical Relevance

Expect extension weakness after patellectomy to be mechanical and permanent rather than a rehabilitation failure.

Account for the changing tendon angle when choosing open or closed chain exercise after cruciate reconstruction.

Treat tubercle transfer as a load changing operation and plan it as such.

Read the shape of a moment arm curve rather than a single published value.

Key Learning Points

  • A moment arm needs its axis named, and at the knee the axis moves.
  • The effective arm is measured as tendon excursion per unit joint rotation.
  • The quadriceps arm peaks in mid flexion, by conventional teaching.
  • The patellar tendon angle reverses through the arc, which creates tibial shear.
  • That shear is why open chain extension near full extension loads the anterior cruciate.

FRCS Level

At the Knee

Treat the extensor lever arm as a surgical variable in every operation that touches the extensor mechanism, the joint line or the tubercle.

Joint line elevation in arthroplasty shortens the extensor arm and raises patellofemoral load, which is the mechanical account of anterior knee pain after a raised joint line. 3.12 covers the patellofemoral joint.

Anteromedialisation of the tubercle is an explicit lever arm and load distribution operation, and its indication is mechanical rather than symptomatic alone.

What Changes It

Joint line position, which moves the axis and the arm together.

Patellar thickness after resurfacing, which changes the tendon line.

Tubercle position after transfer, in all three planes.

Component sizing and posterior offset, which change where the axis sits through flexion.

How It Is Measured

Lateral radiography measures joint line position and patellar height, which are the surgical proxies for the arm.

Modelling and cadaveric work quantify the arm change a given joint line shift produces.

Fluoroscopic kinematics measures the axis in a replaced knee and therefore the arm indirectly, in prescribed tasks.

No intraoperative measurement of the extensor moment arm exists, so it is managed through its proxies.

Clinical Relevance

Restore the joint line in arthroplasty, since raising it shortens the extensor arm and raises patellofemoral load.

Measure patellar height before and after any procedure that alters the extensor mechanism.

Plan tubercle transfer by the load change intended rather than by tracking alone.

Investigate anterior knee pain after replacement for a raised joint line before attributing it to the implant.

Key Learning Points

  • The extensor lever arm is a surgical variable in joint line, patella and tubercle surgery.
  • Joint line elevation shortens the extensor arm and raises patellofemoral load.
  • Anteromedialisation is an explicit lever arm and load distribution operation.
  • The arm is managed through proxies, since no intraoperative measurement exists.
  • Patellar height should be measured before and after extensor mechanism surgery.
