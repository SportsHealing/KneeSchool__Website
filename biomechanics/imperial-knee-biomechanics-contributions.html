Imperial Knee Biomechanics Contributions

Imperial Knee Biomechanics Contributions

Choose a depth

FRCS Level

What the Question Was

Seven of the nine other pages in this chapter cite work from the Imperial knee biomechanics group, which is the reason the architecture gives that group a page.

The question this page answers is narrow: what runs through that body of work, as opposed to what each individual study found.

One theme does run through it. Most of the group's published knee work asks what a specific structure contributes across the flexion range, rather than whether a structure matters, and it answers by loading a cadaveric knee in a controlled rig and removing or reconstructing one thing at a time.

This page also has to say what it cannot answer, which is set out in the fellowship block and is a limit on the page rather than a limit on the group.

What Was Done

Amis and Dawkins described the anterior cruciate's bundle arrangement and the way bundle tension changes through flexion, which is part of the anatomical basis for the tunnel position argument on 3.17.1.

Race and Amis tested posterior cruciate bundle function and reconstruction constructs, which is where the double bundle case on 3.17.2 comes from.

Robinson and Amis separated the medial structures and measured their contributions to valgus and to rotation, which is the rotational half of the medial argument on 3.17.4.

Farahmand and Amis measured lateral patellar stability across the flexion range and quantified the soft tissue contribution, and Amis and colleagues examined the medial patellofemoral ligament, both of which sit behind 3.17.6.

Bull and Amis worked on knee kinematics measurement and on the registration problem that navigation depends on, which connects to 3.16.3.

Swanson and Freeman designed and reported an early total knee replacement, which 3.17.8 treats as one of the designs modern practice descends from.

Taken together those are studies of the cruciates, the medial side, the patellofemoral joint, measurement method and implant design, which is most of Section 3's subject matter.

What It Showed

The recurring finding is that a structure's contribution is angle dependent, and that a contribution measured at one flexion angle cannot be assumed at another.

That is a methodological result as much as an anatomical one, and it is why so much of this chapter insists on a flexion angle beside every laxity figure.

The reconstruction work repeatedly shows that a construct can restore a measurement at one angle and fail to restore it across the range, which is a more demanding standard than most reconstruction studies apply.

The patellofemoral work establishes that soft tissue restraint dominates early flexion and bony geometry dominates later, which is the shape of current instability practice.

What It Changed

Double bundle posterior cruciate reconstruction, medial reconstruction at measured positions and medial patellofemoral ligament reconstruction all trace part of their rationale to this body of work.

The convention of reporting laxity across a flexion range rather than at a single angle is now general, and this group's work is part of why.

Section 3 of this site inherits that convention. Every page here that quotes a laxity figure attaches a flexion angle to it, and that is a direct consequence.

Key Learning Points

  • Most of this group's published knee work asks what a structure contributes across the flexion range, not whether it matters.
  • The recurring result is that contribution is angle dependent, which is methodological as much as anatomical.
  • A construct can restore a measurement at one angle and fail across the range, which is the standard this work applies.
  • Soft tissue restraint dominates early patellofemoral flexion and geometry dominates later.
  • Seven of the nine other pages in this chapter rest partly on this work.

Fellowship Level

What Was Done

What this page cannot do needs saying plainly, because the architecture asked for something broader than what was written.

The architecture asks for the platform's own research programme summarised. A research programme includes current projects, unpublished findings, positions the group holds and directions it intends to take, and none of those is standard teaching.

This page was written by a process with no source access and no contact with the group. Decision 022 permits writing a finding that is standard teaching, because the finding and the author it is taught under check each other. It does not permit describing what a named group is currently doing, because nothing checks that at all.

So the published work above is written on the chapter's ordinary terms and the programme is not written. The gap is deliberate and it is the only page in Section 3 where the architecture's scope has been knowingly under-filled.

What It Showed

What the finished page needs, in order: confirmation or correction of every attribution above, since all of them are written from memory and some will be wrong.

A list of the group's published knee work that the group itself considers representative, which is a different list from the one memory produces.

A statement of the current programme in the group's own words, with the claims that require review marked as such.

A decision about whether this page should name individuals at all. The architecture names an institution and this page names people, because that is how the work is taught, and the people concerned have not been asked.

What to Read Now

Treat this page as the least reliable in the chapter and read the other nine first.

Read the attribution register for this page before using anything on it, since the register is where a correction will appear first.

Do not quote this page as a description of the group's research programme, because it is not one and does not claim to be.

Key Learning Points

  • The architecture asked for a programme summary and this page delivers a published work summary, which is less.
  • Describing a named group's current work from memory is a different act from writing standard teaching, and decision 022 permits only the second.
  • The finished page needs four things from the group, listed above.
  • This is the only page in Section 3 where the architecture's scope is knowingly under-filled.

Consultant Perspective

What It Changed

The decision this page puts to a reviewer is not clinical, and the balance is about attribution rather than about a patient.

Arguing for publishing the page as it stands: the published work is genuinely standard teaching, it is already cited across seven other pages, and gathering it in one place is useful to a trainee whatever the programme section eventually says.

Arguing for holding it: a page bearing an institution's name invites the reader to treat its contents as that institution's account of itself, and the register note correcting that impression sits at the bottom of the page where fewer people read.

The site's own safeguard is what makes publishing defensible for now. The discoverable flag is false, so no page here is indexed or crawled, and this page is reachable only by someone given the link. That is a weaker protection than review and a real one.

The balance falls towards publishing with the limit stated on the page rather than only in the register, which is why the fellowship block above says it in the body text. A reviewer from the group may reasonably take the other view, and that is their call rather than this process's.

What to Read Now

The review this page needs is different from the review the rest of the chapter needs. Elsewhere a reviewer checks whether a finding is right. Here they also decide whether the page should exist in this form.

That question should go to the group before the page is made discoverable, and it is listed as an open item in the build status rather than left to be noticed.

Key Learning Points

  • The decision here is about attribution rather than about a patient, and it is still a balance.
  • Publishing gathers useful standard teaching; holding avoids implying an institutional account of itself.
  • The discoverable flag being false is what makes publishing defensible, and it is weaker than review.
  • The limit is stated in the page body rather than only in the register, deliberately.
  • Whether the page should exist in this form is the group's call, and it is logged as open.
