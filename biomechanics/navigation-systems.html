Navigation Systems

Page 3.16.3 · Advanced Biomechanics · biomechanics page

A measurement device that works in theatre on a living patient. The only method in this chapter whose output changes the operation while it is happening.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Navigation Systems

Choose a depth

FRCS Level

The Principle

A measurement is only useful at the moment a decision is made. Most knee measurement happens before or after an operation, not during it.

Navigation tracks bones in real time and reports angles and gaps while the surgeon can still act on them.

That changes the measurement from evidence into feedback, which is a different kind of thing.

At the Knee

Rigid trackers are fixed to the femur and tibia, and their positions are followed optically or electromagnetically.

Registration maps those trackers to the patient's anatomy, usually by touching landmarks and by moving the hip to find its centre.

The system then reports mechanical alignment, flexion and extension gaps, and component position during arthroplasty. 7.76 covers the principles.

It can also record laxity through range, which is a kinematic measurement made on a living knee under anaesthesia.

The accuracy of everything it reports depends on the registration, not on the tracking, which is the point most often missed.

What Changes It

Registration technique and the care taken with it, which is the dominant error source.

Landmark identification, which is surgeon dependent and varies most at the ankle and the femoral epicondyles.

Tracker fixation, since a loosened pin invalidates everything after it.

Soft tissue state under anaesthesia, which differs from the awake knee.

The algorithm used to compute the hip centre, which differs between systems.

Clinical Relevance

Read a navigation figure as the consequence of a registration, since that is where the error enters.

Expect navigated alignment to be more consistent than conventional instrumentation, and the clinical consequence of that consistency to be smaller than expected. 3.13.10 covers the targets.

Treat intraoperative laxity figures as measurements of the anaesthetised knee.

Check tracker fixation when a reading changes unexpectedly rather than believing the change.

Key Learning Points

  • A measurement is useful at the moment a decision is made.
  • Navigation reports while the surgeon can still act, which makes it feedback rather than evidence.
  • Registration maps trackers to anatomy and is where the error enters.
  • Accuracy depends on registration, not on tracking.
  • Navigated alignment is more consistent, and the clinical consequence is smaller than expected.

Fellowship Level

At the Knee

Navigation improves the precision of achieving a chosen alignment target without saying anything about whether the target is right. 3.13.10 covers that question.

That distinction explains the literature: outliers fall, mean alignment improves, and patient reported outcomes move very little.

Its second use is measurement rather than guidance. A navigated knee generates intraoperative kinematic data that is otherwise unobtainable.

That data has supported the kinematic alignment argument by showing how much native alignment varies. 7.81 covers the philosophy.

Robotic assistance adds execution to navigation's measurement, and the two are often conflated in discussion. 7.84 covers robotic arthroplasty.

What Changes It

The target chosen, which navigation hits more precisely and does not select.

Registration accuracy, which bounds everything downstream.

Whether the system is imageless or image based, which changes the registration error profile.

Surgeon experience, which affects registration more than execution.

Soft tissue assessment method, since gap measurement under tension is operator dependent.

How It Is Measured

Postoperative computed tomography against the intraoperative reading, which is how navigation accuracy is established.

Reported alignment accuracy within one to two degrees, which is the system's figure under good registration.

Registry outcomes by navigated and conventional technique, which is the clinical question. 6.155 covers the controversies.

Intraoperative kinematic recording, which is navigation used as a research tool rather than a guide.

Accuracy studies compare navigation against imaging, and both are measurements, so neither is ground truth.

Clinical Relevance

Separate hitting a target from choosing one, since navigation does only the first.

Expect reduced outliers and a small outcome effect, and say so when discussing it with a patient.

Use navigation's kinematic recording as data where a question is being asked, rather than as a guide alone.

Do not conflate navigation with robotics, since one measures and the other also executes.

Key Learning Points

  • Navigation improves precision of hitting a target and says nothing about the target.
  • Outliers fall, mean alignment improves, patient reported outcomes move very little.
  • Its second use is as a measurement tool generating otherwise unobtainable data.
  • That data supported the kinematic alignment argument.
  • Accuracy studies compare two measurements, so neither is ground truth.

Consultant Perspective

At the Knee

The decision here is whether to adopt a technology that reliably improves a surrogate and has not reliably improved an outcome.

On one side: fewer alignment outliers, a measurable reduction in a known failure mechanism, and a dataset generated at no extra risk. 3.13.8 covers alignment and degeneration.

On the other: added operative time, capital and per case cost, a learning curve whose errors are registration errors, and registry data that has not shown the survivorship benefit the mechanism predicts. 6.155 covers the controversies.

What moves the weight is case mix. A unit doing complex deformity has more outliers to prevent than a unit doing routine primaries.

What does not settle it is the precision figure. A system accurate to a degree is not an argument for adoption if the degree does not change what happens to the patient.

What Changes It

Case complexity and the baseline outlier rate, which set how much there is to gain.

Unit volume, which spreads the capital cost and shortens the learning curve.

Whether the unit intends to use the kinematic data, which is a benefit most adopters do not take.

The alignment philosophy in use, since a patient specific target needs measurement more than a fixed one does. 7.81 covers it.

Operative time, which has its own cost in infection risk and theatre throughput.

How It Is Measured

Radiographic outlier rate, which navigation reliably improves and which is a surrogate.

Registry survivorship, which is the outcome and has not followed the surrogate.

Patient reported outcome measures, which have a ceiling that would conceal a small benefit. 3.14.12 covers that problem.

Operative time and cost, which are measured and rarely reported alongside the benefit.

No trial has powered survivorship as its endpoint, which is the study the decision needs and which would take decades.

Clinical Relevance

Decide on case mix rather than on the precision specification, since precision is not the quantity in question.

State that outlier reduction is a surrogate and that survivorship has not followed it.

Use the kinematic data if adopting, because otherwise half the benefit is unclaimed.

Price operative time into the comparison rather than treating it as free.

Weigh the patient's age and physiology, comorbidities, knee demand, psychological state and their own ideas, concerns and expectations, since a longer operation is not neutral for every patient and a younger one has more years for a surrogate to matter in.

Key Learning Points

  • The decision is whether to adopt a technology that improves a surrogate and not an outcome.
  • Fewer outliers and a dataset at no extra risk sit against time, cost and a learning curve.
  • Case mix moves the weight: complex deformity has more outliers to prevent.
  • A precision figure is not an argument for adoption.
  • No trial has powered survivorship as its endpoint, and it would take decades.
