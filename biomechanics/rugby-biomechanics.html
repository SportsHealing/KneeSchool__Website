Rugby

Page 3.15.3 · Sports-Specific Knee Biomechanics · biomechanics page

The sport that adds a second player to every mechanism in football, and the one where the force can come from any direction at any time.

Rugby

Choose a depth

For Patients

The Principle

If something can be pushed from any direction, it has to be strong in every direction.

A knee is not. It is built to bend one way and to resist the others with ligaments.

Adding another person's weight and speed to the force is what makes contact sport different.

At the Knee

Rugby has all the turning and landing injuries of football, with contact added. 3.15.1 covers those.

The characteristic rugby mechanism is a tackle to the side of a planted leg, which bends the knee in a direction it does not bend.

A blow to the front of a bent knee, as in a tackle or a fall onto it, loads the ligament at the back of the joint.

Scrummaging and rucking load the knee while the foot is fixed and the body is being pushed, which is the same problem held for longer.

Clinical Relevance

The direction of the tackle is useful information, because it predicts which structures were loaded.

A knee injured by a blow to the outside and one injured by a twist are different problems with different recoveries.

Several structures are often injured together in contact sport, which lengthens rehabilitation. 6.4 covers associated injuries.

No injury rates are given on this page, because no source was available to check them against.

Key Learning Points

  • A knee bends one way and resists the others with ligaments.
  • Rugby adds another person's weight and speed to every mechanism.
  • The characteristic mechanism is a tackle to the side of a planted leg.
  • A blow to the front of a bent knee loads the ligament at the back.
  • Several structures are often injured together in contact sport.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is rugby worse for the knees than football? It adds contact mechanisms to the same non contact ones, and contact injuries more often involve several structures. That makes the injuries more complex rather than simply more frequent.

Q. Does strapping or a brace help? A brace can limit sideways movement and some players find it reassuring after an injury. It does not stop a large enough force, and it is not a substitute for strength and control.

Q. Why does my knee hurt at the front after scrummaging? Scrummaging holds the knee bent and loaded for long periods, which presses the kneecap against the thigh bone. Discomfort there is usually a loading problem.

Q. Can I play on after a knee injury? Not until it has been assessed. A knee that gives way or swells quickly needs looking at before any return, and playing on can turn one injured structure into several.

See a clinician if your knee swells quickly after a tackle, gives way, locks, or will not take your weight.

For Medical Students

The Principle

Contact sport adds an externally applied force of a magnitude and direction the athlete does not control.

That removes the protective value of anticipation, which is the mechanism most prevention training relies on. 3.14.6 covers anticipation.

So contact injuries are less preventable by training and more influenced by law and by technique.

At the Knee

Lateral blow to a planted limb produces valgus with or without rotation, injuring the medial collateral complex and, at higher force, the cruciate. 3.9.10 covers the combination.

A blow to the anterior tibia in a flexed knee, from a tackle or a fall, is the classical posterior cruciate mechanism. 3.8 covers the ligament.

A medially directed blow produces varus and loads the lateral structures and the posterolateral corner. 3.10.6 covers the corner.

Non contact mechanisms persist and account for a substantial share, particularly in backs. 3.7.14 covers them.

Multiligament injury is more common in contact sport than in non contact sport, which changes the assessment. 6.4 covers associated injuries.

What Changes It

Position, since forwards and backs have different exposures.

Laws of the game, particularly those governing tackle height and the ruck.

Playing surface and footwear traction.

Fatigue and match time, which affect non contact mechanisms more than contact ones.

Previous injury and the quality of rehabilitation.

Clinical Relevance

Take the direction of force in the history, since it predicts the structures involved.

Examine for multiligament injury rather than assuming a single structure. 6.4 covers them.

Assess the posterolateral corner when the mechanism was a varus or hyperextension force. 3.10.6 covers it.

Describe mechanism rather than quoting rates, as this page does, because no source was available.

Key Learning Points

  • Contact adds a force whose magnitude and direction the athlete does not control.
  • That removes the protective value of anticipation.
  • A lateral blow produces valgus and injures the medial complex, then the cruciate.
  • A blow to the anterior tibia in flexion is the classical posterior cruciate mechanism.
  • Multiligament injury is commoner in contact sport.

MRCS Level

The Principle

In contact sport the clinically important question shifts from which ligament to how many, because the energy available exceeds a single structure's capacity.

A multiligament knee is a different clinical entity from a single ligament knee, with different urgency and different consequences.

That reframing is the main thing rugby adds to the chapter.

At the Knee

Valgus with external rotation loads the medial collateral and posteromedial corner, and then the anterior cruciate. 3.9.10 covers the sequence.

Varus with internal rotation or hyperextension loads the lateral and posterolateral structures, and the common peroneal nerve is at risk. 3.10.6 covers the corner.

Hyperextension loads the posterior cruciate and the posterior capsule, and at high energy threatens the popliteal vessels.

Knee dislocation in sport is rare and is a limb threatening injury whose vascular assessment cannot wait. Section 6 covers the condition.

Anterolateral structures are commonly involved in the rotational component and are underdiagnosed. 3.11.6 covers the Segond lesion.

What Changes It

Energy of the collision, which is a function of player mass and closing speed.

Direction of the applied force, which determines the structures sequentially loaded.

Foot fixation at the moment of contact, which determines whether the force is absorbed or transmitted.

Law changes and their enforcement, which are the sport's main population level lever.

Protective equipment, which has limited evidence at the knee.

How It Is Measured

Video analysis of match injuries, which is the main evidence for contact mechanisms. 3.7.14 covers the approach.

Instrumented mouthguards and wearables, which measure collision load and were developed for the head rather than the knee. 3.16.8 covers wearables.

Cadaveric and robotic sectioning studies, which establish which structures resist which direction. 3.16.2 covers robotic testing.

Injury surveillance in professional competitions, which gives rates this page does not quote, because no source was retrieved.

There is no measurement of knee load during a tackle, so the mechanisms are inferred from video and from sectioning studies.

Clinical Relevance

Assess for multiligament injury and for vascular and nerve compromise after a high energy mechanism, since those cannot wait.

Record the direction of force, because it maps onto the structures loaded.

Look for anterolateral involvement in rotational mechanisms, which is underdiagnosed. 3.11.6 covers it.

State that contact mechanisms are inferred from video and sectioning rather than measured.

Weigh the player's age, physiology, comorbidities, knee demand, psychological state and their own ideas, concerns and expectations, since return to contact is a decision about accepting a repeat exposure.

Key Learning Points

  • The question shifts from which ligament to how many.
  • A multiligament knee is a different clinical entity with different urgency.
  • Varus and hyperextension mechanisms put the peroneal nerve and popliteal vessels at risk.
  • Knee dislocation is rare and limb threatening, and its vascular assessment cannot wait.
  • Contact mechanisms are inferred from video and sectioning, not measured.
