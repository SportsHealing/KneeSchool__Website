Joint Lubrication

Page 3.6.7 · Meniscal Biomechanics · biomechanics page

The meniscus helps keep a film of fluid between the surfaces. A smaller function, a real mechanism, and a thin evidence base.

Joint Lubrication

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

The Principle

Two solid surfaces sliding on each other wear out. A film of fluid between them stops them touching, and the wear stops with it.

Keeping a film in place needs the fluid to be held where it is wanted and the surfaces to be shaped so it does not escape.

A hydroplaning tyre is the unwanted version of the same physics. The film lifts the solid surfaces apart.

At the Knee

Synovial fluid is the lubricant. Cartilage is the bearing surface, and it releases fluid under load, which contributes to the film.

The menisci help in two ways. They spread synovial fluid across the surfaces as they move, and their shape helps contain the fluid in the contact.

Reducing contact stress also helps lubrication, because a heavily loaded contact squeezes the film out. 3.5.2 covers contact pressure.

This is the fourth of the five meniscal functions and it is clearly subordinate to load transmission. 3.6.1 covers the five.

What Changes It

The presence and movement of the menisci, since the spreading depends on both.

Synovial fluid quality, which changes with inflammation and with age.

Contact stress, which affects whether a film can be maintained.

Cartilage surface integrity, which affects the bearing surface itself.

Clinical Relevance

A meniscectomised joint is worse lubricated as well as more highly loaded, and both contribute to wear.

This function is often stated without qualification, and the honest position is that the mechanism is sound and the magnitude is not established.

It is not a reason to preserve a meniscus on its own, and it is part of why preservation is preferred.

A patient asking how a joint lasts decades is asking a lubrication question as much as a load one.

Key Learning Points

  • A fluid film between surfaces prevents the wear that solid contact causes.
  • Synovial fluid is the lubricant and cartilage releases fluid under load.
  • The menisci spread fluid across the surfaces and help contain it in the contact.
  • A heavily loaded contact squeezes the film out.
  • This is the fourth of the five functions and clearly subordinate.

MRCS Level

The Principle

Name the regime. Boundary lubrication is molecules bound to the surface; fluid film lubrication is a pressurised layer keeping the surfaces apart; mixed lubrication is both at once, and the knee operates mixed.

Weeping and interstitial fluid pressurisation describe cartilage contributing to the film from within, which is a consequence of its biphasic nature. 3.6.6 covers that.

The meniscus participates by distributing fluid and by lowering the contact stress that would otherwise collapse the film, which are both indirect contributions.

At the Knee

Conventional teaching describes the knee as operating in a mixed lubrication regime, shifting between boundary and fluid film with activity and load.

Meniscectomy raises contact stress, which shifts the regime towards boundary lubrication and increases wear, which is a mechanistic chain rather than a measured one.

Lubricin and hyaluronic acid are the molecular contributors to boundary lubrication, and their relationship to meniscal status is not established.

The lubricating role is supported by in vitro friction measurements rather than by in vivo data.

What Changes It

Contact stress, which determines whether a fluid film survives.

Synovial fluid composition, altered by inflammation and by injury.

Cartilage surface and matrix state, which affect interstitial pressurisation.

Meniscal presence and mobility, which affect fluid distribution.

How It Is Measured

Friction testing of cartilage on cartilage and cartilage on meniscus in a tribometer measures coefficients of friction, in excised tissue.

Synovial fluid analysis measures composition rather than lubrication performance.

No in vivo measurement of joint lubrication exists, so the clinical claim is mechanistic.

The evidence here is thinner than for any other meniscal function, which is worth saying when the function is cited.

Clinical Relevance

Cite lubrication as a mechanism rather than as a quantified function.

Expect raised contact stress to shift the lubrication regime unfavourably.

Treat synovial fluid changes after injury as part of the picture rather than a separate problem.

Say that the evidence for this function is thin when it is being used in an argument.

Key Learning Points

  • The knee operates in a mixed lubrication regime, between boundary and fluid film.
  • Cartilage contributes to the film from within, through interstitial pressurisation.
  • The meniscus contributes indirectly, by distributing fluid and lowering contact stress.
  • Meniscectomy shifts the regime towards boundary lubrication, by a mechanistic chain.
  • No in vivo measurement of joint lubrication exists.

FRCS Level

At the Knee

Treat lubrication as a mechanism to understand rather than a function to restore. No operation targets it, and no measurement would confirm it had been affected.

Its practical value is explanatory. It is part of why a knee with raised contact stress degenerates faster than the stress figure alone predicts, and it belongs in that account rather than in a treatment argument.

Intra-articular injectables are the one intervention aimed at lubrication, and their mechanism is molecular rather than meniscal. Section 6 covers management.

What Changes It

Contact stress, which the surgical options do change.

Synovial environment, which injury and inflammation alter.

Cartilage state, which changes the bearing surface.

Nothing surgical that targets lubrication directly.

How It Is Measured

Tribological testing in vitro is the only direct measurement and uses excised tissue.

Synovial fluid biomarker work measures composition and is not a performance measure.

Clinical trials of injectables measure symptoms rather than lubrication.

No measurement links a patient's meniscal status to their joint's lubrication.

Clinical Relevance

Use lubrication to explain progression rather than to justify an operation.

Lower contact stress where it can be lowered, which helps lubrication indirectly.

Describe injectable mechanisms as molecular rather than meniscal.

State the absence of any confirming measurement when this function is raised.

Key Learning Points

  • No operation targets lubrication and no measurement would confirm an effect.
  • Its value is explanatory: why raised stress degenerates faster than stress alone predicts.
  • Injectables target lubrication molecularly, not meniscally.
  • Tribological testing in vitro is the only direct measurement.
  • No measurement links a patient's meniscal status to their joint's lubrication.
