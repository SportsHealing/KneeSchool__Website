Surgical Anatomy

The approaches to the proximal tibia and the safe zones in each: which interval, what lies in it, and what limits how far it can be extended.

Surgical Anatomy

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

Structure and Location

The proximal tibia is approached from four directions, and each one exposes a different part of it.

Anterior and anteromedial, over the subcutaneous surface, for the tubercle and the medial metaphysis. Anterolateral, elevating the iliotibial band from Gerdy's tubercle, for the lateral plateau. Posteromedial, behind the pes anserinus, for the posteromedial plateau. Posterolateral, which is the most constrained of the four.

Relations

Anteromedial: skin, then the subcutaneous surface of the tibia almost immediately. The infrapatellar branch of the saphenous nerve crosses the upper part and the long saphenous vein runs behind the medial border.

Anterolateral: the iliotibial band, then the capsule, then the lateral plateau. The anterior compartment muscles arise below and are elevated with the periosteum.

Posteromedial: the pes anserinus, then the interval in front of the medial head of gastrocnemius, which protects the popliteal contents.

Posterolateral: the common peroneal nerve around the fibular neck is the limiting structure, and the proximal tibiofibular joint is in the way.

Function

The subcutaneous medial surface makes the anteromedial approach simple and makes the soft tissue envelope there thin and unforgiving.

The posteromedial interval exists because gastrocnemius arises above the joint line and can be held back, giving a plane between the bone and the neurovascular structures.

Clinical Relevance

The thin anteromedial envelope is why swelling and incision placement matter more here than almost anywhere else around the knee.

The common peroneal nerve is the structure to identify and protect in any approach that goes posterolaterally, and it is closer to the fibula than to the tibia.

Key Learning Points

  • Four approaches: anteromedial, anterolateral, posteromedial and posterolateral.
  • The medial surface is subcutaneous, so the soft tissue envelope there is thin.
  • Medial gastrocnemius arising above the joint line creates the posteromedial safe plane.
  • The common peroneal nerve limits the posterolateral approach and lies at the fibular neck.

MRCS Level

Structure and Location

Name each interval and its landmark. Anteromedial: longitudinal incision a finger's breadth medial to the tibial crest, straight onto bone. Anterolateral: curved incision based on Gerdy's tubercle, elevating the band and the anterior compartment subperiosteally. Posteromedial: incision along the posteromedial border, developing the interval between the pes anserinus and the medial head of gastrocnemius. Posterolateral: either through the fibular neck region, accepting the nerve, or via a posterior midline approach to the popliteal fossa.

The dual incision approach to bicondylar injuries exists because the anterior skin bridge between an anterolateral and a posteromedial incision has to be wide enough to survive.

Relations

Anteromedial: infrapatellar branch of the saphenous nerve crossing obliquely, the saphenous nerve and long saphenous vein posteromedially, and the pes anserinus insertion at the upper end.

Anterolateral: iliotibial band, the anterolateral capsule, the inferior lateral genicular artery at the joint line, the anterior tibial recurrent artery lateral to the tubercle, and the lateral meniscus immediately inside the capsule.

Posteromedial: pes anserinus, the superficial medial collateral insertion deep to it, the posterior oblique fibres, the semimembranosus, and the medial head of gastrocnemius behind. The plane in front of gastrocnemius is the safe one.

Posterolateral and posterior: popliteal artery, vein and tibial nerve in that order from deep to superficial, the fibrous arch of soleus tethering the artery, the anterior tibial artery passing forward through the interosseous membrane, and the common peroneal nerve passing round the fibular neck under the peroneus longus origin.

Blood Supply and Innervation

The anterior skin over the proximal tibia is supplied by perforators that are sparse compared with the thigh, which is why wound problems are commoner here.

The periosteal supply to the metaphysis arrives circumferentially, so subperiosteal elevation on one side leaves the other intact, and circumferential stripping does not.

The nutrient artery enters the posterior surface below the soleal line.

Sensory territories to warn about: the infrapatellar branch of the saphenous nerve for the anterior approaches, and the superficial peroneal nerve further distally for extended lateral exposures.

Function

Each approach is defined by what it protects rather than by what it reaches. The posteromedial approach protects the neurovascular bundle behind gastrocnemius. The anterolateral approach protects nothing posteriorly, which is why it cannot be extended round the back.

Timing is part of the anatomy here. A thin envelope over a swollen metaphysis will not tolerate an incision, and the decision to wait is a decision about the soft tissues rather than about the bone.

Clinical Relevance

For bicondylar plateau fractures, the anterolateral and posteromedial combination is the standard pair, and the skin bridge between them is the limiting factor.

Subperiosteal elevation should be on one surface rather than circumferential, because the metaphyseal supply is circumferential.

An external fixator applied to the proximal tibia goes through the subcutaneous medial surface for the same reason bone graft is taken from there.

The anterior tibial artery passing through the interosseous membrane is the structure at risk in proximal tibial osteotomy, and it is distal to the usual osteotomy level but not far distal.

Key Learning Points

  • The plane in front of the medial head of gastrocnemius is the posteromedial safe plane.
  • The anterolateral approach cannot be extended posteriorly, because nothing protects the bundle there.
  • Anterior skin over the proximal tibia has sparse perforators, so wound problems are commoner here.
  • Elevate periosteum on one surface, not circumferentially, because metaphyseal supply is circumferential.
  • The skin bridge between an anterolateral and a posteromedial incision is the limiting factor in dual approaches.

FRCS Level

Relations

In a knee with a thin or previously operated envelope, the relations that decide the plan are the perforators and the previous scars rather than the deep structures. The deep structures have not moved; the covering has.

Posteriorly, capsular and muscular scar reduces the posteromedial interval, and the plane in front of gastrocnemius can be obliterated by previous posterior surgery.

Function

Approach selection at the proximal tibia is a soft tissue decision disguised as a bony one. The bone can be reached from several directions; the skin usually cannot.

Staging exists for that reason. The interval between temporary stabilisation and definitive fixation is time bought for the envelope, and the anatomy of the envelope is what sets how much is needed.

Clinical Relevance

Where a plateau fracture needs both columns addressed, the sequence and the patient's position are determined by the approaches chosen, and the approaches are determined by the envelope.

In revision arthroplasty, tubercle osteotomy for exposure is a proximal tibial procedure with its own anatomy: the fragment needs enough length and enough muscle attachment laterally to retain a blood supply.

For posterolateral access, taking the fibular head or working around the proximal tibiofibular joint both put the common peroneal nerve at risk, and the nerve should be identified rather than avoided by estimation. 2.10.6 owns the nerve.

Every published safe zone around this bone is expressed as a distance from a landmark. None of those distances appears here. Decision 005 records why, and on a surgical anatomy page it is the most visible cost of the policy.

Key Learning Points

  • Approach selection here is a soft tissue decision disguised as a bony one.
  • Previous posterior surgery can obliterate the plane in front of gastrocnemius.
  • A tubercle osteotomy fragment needs length and lateral muscle attachment to keep a blood supply.
  • For posterolateral access the common peroneal nerve should be identified, not estimated.

Fellowship Level

Clinical Relevance

Four situations where the standard four approaches are not enough, each defined by anatomy.

The bicondylar fracture with a compromised anterior envelope. The question is whether any anterior incision is survivable, and the alternatives are a posterior based approach, external fixation as definitive treatment, or waiting. The perforator anatomy decides it, not the fracture pattern.

The posterior plateau fragment. A posteromedial approach reaches the medial part and a direct posterior approach reaches the rest, and the limiting structure moves from gastrocnemius to the neurovascular bundle itself as the exposure extends laterally.

The periprosthetic proximal tibia. The implant occupies the metaphysis, so fixation has to find bone elsewhere, and the subcutaneous medial surface is both the thinnest envelope and the only accessible cortex.

Resection for tumour. The constraint is the neurovascular bundle and the anterior tibial artery's passage through the interosseous membrane. Resection proximal to that point and resection distal to it are different operations, in the same way that resection either side of the adductor hiatus is on the femoral side.

In all four, the bone's anatomy is unchanged from the medical student page. What changes is that the soft tissue envelope has become the operative problem.

Key Learning Points

  • With a compromised anterior envelope the perforator anatomy decides the approach, not the fracture pattern.
  • As a posterior exposure extends laterally the limiting structure changes from gastrocnemius to the bundle itself.
  • In the periprosthetic tibia the subcutaneous medial surface is the thinnest envelope and the only accessible cortex.
  • Resection proximal and distal to the anterior tibial artery's passage are different operations.
