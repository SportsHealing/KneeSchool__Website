Histology

Page 2.7.3 · ACL · anatomy page

What the ligament is made of, how its fibres are organised, and the four zone insertion that explains how it fails.

Histology

For Medical Students

Structure and Location

The anterior cruciate ligament is dense regular connective tissue: parallel collagen fibres with fibroblasts between them and a small amount of ground substance.

Type I collagen predominates, as it does in tendon. Fibrils are grouped into fibres, fibres into fascicles, and fascicles into the bundles described on 2.7.8 and 2.7.9.

The fibres have a wavy, crimped appearance when unloaded. The crimp straightens as the ligament is loaded, which is why the first part of its load response is compliant.

At each end the ligament inserts through four zones: ligament, uncalcified fibrocartilage, calcified fibrocartilage, bone. That is a direct enthesis, the same arrangement as a meniscal root.

Relations

Outside the collagen is a synovial covering, continuous with the synovium of the notch, carrying the vessels and nerves.

Inside, the fascicles are separated by loose connective tissue through which the vessels run longitudinally.

At the insertions the collagen interdigitates with bone rather than sitting on it, which is why a healthy enthesis is stronger than the ligament above it.

Function

Parallel collagen carries tension along the fibre direction and very little across it. The ligament is built for one direction of load.

The crimp gives a toe region in the load response: small loads straighten fibres, larger loads stretch them. That is why a ligament feels lax at first and then stiff.

The four zone enthesis spreads load from a flexible structure into a rigid one over a gradient, which is how a tissue interface survives repeated loading.

Clinical Relevance

In an adult, the ligament usually fails in its substance rather than avulsing bone, because the mature enthesis is stronger than the mid substance. In a child the reverse is true.

The avascular, hypocellular mid substance does not heal reliably, which is covered on 2.7.4.

A graft is a different tissue from the ligament it replaces, and the process by which it becomes ligament like is called ligamentisation. Section 7 owns it.

Key Learning Points

  • Type I collagen predominates, in fibrils, fibres, fascicles and bundles.
  • Crimped fibres give a compliant toe region before the ligament stiffens.
  • Each insertion is a four zone enthesis: ligament, uncalcified fibrocartilage, calcified fibrocartilage, bone.
  • In an adult the mid substance fails first; in a child the bone does.
  • The tissue carries tension along the fibres and almost nothing across them.

MRCS Level

Structure and Location

Describe the hierarchy and the insertion separately, because examiners ask for both.

Hierarchy: collagen molecules into fibrils, fibrils into fibres, fibres into fascicles within a loose endoligament, fascicles into the two bundles. Fibroblasts lie in rows between the fibres and are more fusiform in the mid substance and more rounded, resembling fibrochondrocytes, near the insertions.

Composition: type I collagen dominant with a smaller proportion of type III, elastin in small quantity, and proteoglycan concentrated near the insertions where the tissue is loaded in compression as well as tension.

Insertion: the four zone direct enthesis, with the transition from uncalcified to calcified fibrocartilage marked by a tidemark. The gradient in stiffness across those zones is what prevents stress concentration at the interface.

Relations

The synovial sheath is the vascular and neural route and is continuous with the notch synovium. A ligament that has torn within an intact sheath is in a different biological position from one whose sheath has gone.

The endoligament carries the longitudinal vessels between fascicles, so the blood supply runs along the ligament rather than across it.

Near the femoral insertion the fibres fan and the tissue becomes more fibrocartilaginous, which is relevant to where a proximal tear occurs.

Blood Supply and Innervation

Vessels run longitudinally in the endoligament from the synovial sheath, which is supplied by the middle genicular artery. The mid substance is relatively hypovascular and the tissue near the insertions better supplied.

Cellularity follows vascularity. The mid substance is the least cellular part, which is the combination that makes mid substance healing unreliable.

Neural elements accompany the vessels. Mechanoreceptors sit in the subsynovial tissue near both insertions rather than within the collagen, which 2.7.5 covers.

Function

Material behaviour follows structure. Crimp gives the toe region, parallel collagen gives high tensile stiffness along the fibre axis, and viscoelasticity comes from fluid movement through the matrix.

The ligament therefore behaves differently under fast and slow loading, and stress relaxes under sustained load. 3.7 owns the numbers.

The enthesis gradient is the reason an intact insertion is not the weak link, and the reason a graft fixed to bone without that gradient has a different failure pattern.

Clinical Relevance

Age changes the tissue. Collagen cross linking increases, cellularity falls and the mid substance becomes relatively weaker, which is part of why failure patterns differ with age.

A graft begins as a different tissue and undergoes necrosis, revascularisation, repopulation and remodelling. It is weakest some weeks after implantation, which is an anatomical reason rehabilitation is staged rather than a conservative preference.

Fixing a graft to bone creates an interface that is not a four zone enthesis, and tunnel healing is the process by which something like one forms.

In a proximal avulsion with an intact synovial sheath, the biology is more favourable than in a mid substance tear, which is the histological basis of the repair argument.

Key Learning Points

  • Cells are fusiform in the mid substance and rounder near the insertions.
  • Proteoglycan concentrates near the insertions, where the tissue is compressed as well as tensioned.
  • Vessels run longitudinally in the endoligament, so the supply runs along the ligament.
  • Cellularity follows vascularity, and the mid substance is lowest in both.
  • A graft is weakest some weeks after implantation, which is why rehabilitation is staged.

FRCS Level

Relations

The synovial sheath is the relation that decides whether repair is biologically plausible. A proximal tear with the sheath intact retains a vascular and cellular route that a mid substance rupture does not.

At the femoral insertion the fanning fibres and the fibrocartilaginous transition mean the tissue available for suture differs along the footprint.

Function

Ligamentisation is the central biological fact of reconstruction. A graft does not stay the tissue it was, and it does not become the ligament it replaced. It becomes something in between, over months.

That process sets the timeline rather than the fixation doing so, which is why graft choice, fixation method and rehabilitation are discussed together.

The enthesis cannot be reproduced surgically. Tunnel healing produces an indirect insertion rather than the four zone gradient, and that is a permanent difference between a reconstructed and a native knee.

Clinical Relevance

Graft remodelling explains the timing of failures: early failures are fixation or technique, intermediate failures are biological, and late failures are usually a new injury.

In repair, select on tissue quality and sheath integrity rather than on tear level alone. The histology is what is being selected on even when the description is arthroscopic.

Biological augmentation acts on the vascularity and cellularity gradients described here. Its ceiling is the same histology.

No material property figure appears on this page. Tensile strength, modulus and stiffness are all owned by 3.7 and none could be verified here.

Key Learning Points

  • An intact synovial sheath is what makes repair biologically plausible.
  • Ligamentisation sets the rehabilitation timeline, not the fixation.
  • Tunnel healing gives an indirect insertion, so the four zone enthesis is never reproduced.
  • Failure timing separates technique, biology and new injury.
