Anteromedial Bundle

Page 2.7.8 · ACL · anatomy page

The anteromedial bundle as anatomy: where its fibres run, how they are arranged, and what distinguishes them. Behaviour through range is Section 3.

Anteromedial Bundle

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

Structure and Location

The anteromedial bundle is the group of fibres that attach anteromedially on the tibial footprint and proximally on the femoral wall.

It is named for its tibial attachment, not its femoral one. Both bundles are.

It is the longer of the two bundles and the more vertically oriented when the knee is extended. It lies anterior to the posterolateral bundle at the tibia and superior to it at the femur.

The division between the bundles is a convention rather than a plane. There is no septum, and dissecting a clean separation requires choosing where to cut.

Relations

At the femur: proximal to the bifurcate ridge on the lateral wall of the notch, with the posterolateral bundle distal to it.

At the tibia: anteromedial within the footprint, closest to the anterior horn of the medial meniscus and the intermeniscal ligament.

Through its course: anterior and medial to the posterolateral fibres, with the two twisting around each other as the knee flexes.

The notch roof lies above, and these are the fibres closest to it.

Function

Orientation only, because bundle function through range is owned by 3.7.5. The anteromedial fibres are the more vertical of the two in extension and become more horizontal with flexion.

They are the fibres a Lachman test loads at thirty degrees, which is why the test is described as assessing this bundle preferentially.

What each bundle contributes at each angle, and how their contributions trade, belongs to 3.7.5 and 3.7.6.

Clinical Relevance

Isolated anteromedial bundle injury exists and is uncommon. It presents with a positive Lachman test and a relatively less marked pivot shift.

In a partial tear, which bundle remains determines which test is abnormal, and that is the practical use of knowing the bundles apart.

Reconstruction of a single bundle in a knee with a surviving posterolateral bundle is an augmentation rather than a replacement, and Section 7 owns it.

Key Learning Points

  • The bundle is named for its anteromedial tibial attachment, not its femoral one.
  • It attaches proximal to the bifurcate ridge at the femur.
  • It is the longer bundle and the more vertical in extension.
  • The division between bundles is a convention with no septum.
  • It is the bundle a Lachman test loads preferentially.

MRCS Level

Structure and Location

Define the bundle by its two attachments and accept the convention. Tibially: the anteromedial part of the footprint. Femorally: the proximal part of the footprint on the lateral wall, above the bifurcate ridge with the knee extended.

It is conventionally described as the longer bundle, and its fibres are longer on average than the posterolateral fibres because the distance between its attachments is greater in flexion.

Fibre arrangement within it is parallel but the bundle as a whole twists, because the femoral attachment is proximal and the tibial attachment anterior. That twist increases with flexion.

Histologically it is the same tissue as the rest of the ligament. There is no structural feature that identifies a fibre as belonging to one bundle rather than the other.

Relations

The bifurcate ridge separates the femoral attachments where it is present, and it is variably prominent.

The notch roof is the relation that matters anteriorly. These are the fibres that impinge first if the ligament or a graft sits too far forward.

The posterolateral bundle lies posterior and distal at the femur and posterolateral at the tibia, and the two cross as the knee flexes.

The synovial sheath covers both bundles as one structure, so they share a blood supply and a nerve supply.

Blood Supply and Innervation

Shared with the whole ligament: the middle genicular artery through the synovial sheath, with a tibial contribution from the inferior geniculars and the fat pad.

There is no bundle specific supply. A tear that spares one bundle may spare its sheath and therefore its supply, which is the biological basis for a surviving bundle remaining viable.

Mechanoreceptors are distributed near both insertions rather than by bundle.

Function

Orientation only on this page. The anteromedial fibres are more vertical in extension and more horizontal in flexion, and the bundle's attachment positions mean its length changes less than the posterolateral bundle's across the range.

That relative isometry is why the anteromedial bundle is the one a single bundle reconstruction most often aims to reproduce.

Everything quantitative about length change, in situ force and the reciprocal behaviour of the two bundles belongs to 3.7.5.

Clinical Relevance

Partial tears are classified by which bundle is intact, and the examination findings follow: an isolated anteromedial tear gives a positive Lachman with a relatively normal pivot shift.

Selective augmentation preserves the intact bundle's vascularity and proprioception, and its difficulty is that the remnant obscures the footprint.

A single bundle graft placed to reproduce the anteromedial fibres controls anterior translation well and rotation less well, which is the anatomical origin of the double bundle and lateral augmentation debates. Section 7 owns them.

No measurement appears on this page. Bundle lengths and cross sectional areas are published and vary, and the ones on 2.7.1 are the only dimensions registered for this ligament.

Key Learning Points

  • Define the bundle by its two attachments and accept that the division is conventional.
  • No structural feature identifies a fibre as belonging to one bundle.
  • There is no bundle specific blood supply; a spared sheath keeps a spared bundle viable.
  • Relative isometry is why single bundle reconstruction aims at this bundle.
  • An isolated anteromedial tear gives a positive Lachman with a relatively normal pivot shift.

FRCS Level

Relations

In a partial tear the surviving bundle is both the thing being preserved and the thing obscuring the footprint. That tension is the practical content of selective augmentation.

The notch roof relation is the one that punishes anterior placement, and these fibres are the first to meet it.

Function

The bundle description is useful for examination and for describing partial injury, and it is a convention rather than a dissectable anatomy. Treating it as two cords predicts that reproducing both restores native behaviour, and the evidence has not borne that out as cleanly as the anatomy suggested it might.

The honest position is that the ligament is a continuum, the bundles are a useful simplification, and 3.7.5 owns what the simplification costs.

Clinical Relevance

Where a bundle survives and is functional, augmentation is defensible and its cost is visualisation. Where it survives and is not functional, it is an obstacle.

Judging that difference is arthroscopic and is a judgement about tissue, not about tear pattern.

In chronic partial injury the surviving bundle may have adapted, and restoring the other does not necessarily restore normal behaviour.

Nothing on this page is quantitative, and bundle function through range is excluded from it by the architecture rather than by the figure policy.

Key Learning Points

  • A surviving bundle is both the thing preserved and the thing obscuring the footprint.
  • Two cords predicts that reproducing both restores native behaviour, and that has not borne out cleanly.
  • The useful judgement is whether a surviving bundle is functional, and it is made arthroscopically.
  • In chronic partial injury the surviving bundle may have adapted.
