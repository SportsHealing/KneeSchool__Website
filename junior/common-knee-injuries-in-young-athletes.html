Common Knee Injuries in Young Athletes

The injuries that turn up most often in school and club sport, and what they tend to feel like.

Common Knee Injuries in Young Athletes

For Young Learners

Function

A few signs matter more than others, whatever the cause.

Swelling within a few hours of an injury suggests bleeding inside the joint, which usually means something significant.

A knee that locks and will not straighten suggests something is physically caught.

A knee that gives way suggests it is not being held steady properly.

Not being able to put weight on it at all needs checking the same day.

Clinical Relevance

This page is for recognising, not for diagnosing. Working out what is actually wrong with a knee needs someone who can examine it. What you can do is notice when something deserves attention.

Sudden twisting injuries. A foot stays planted, the body turns, and something inside gives way. Often there is a pop that the person hears or feels, and the knee swells up within a few hours. Afterwards it can feel untrustworthy on turns. This group includes ligament injuries and tears of the rubbery pads inside.

Kneecap problems. Pain right at the front, worse going downstairs, worse after sitting still for a long time with the knee bent. Very common in teenagers, and most of it settles with the right strength work. Sometimes a kneecap slips out of its groove entirely, which is dramatic and usually goes back by itself.

Growing area problems. Aching and tenderness at specific spots, most often just below the kneecap at the top of the shin, or at the bottom tip of the kneecap. These follow activity rather than a single accident, and they are tied to growing.

Overuse pain. No single moment. The knee just starts aching after training and gradually gets worse. Almost always it follows a change: more sessions, a new sport, a different surface, new boots.

Direct blows. A knock or a collision. Usually bruising, occasionally something more, and worth checking if the knee will not bear weight afterwards.

Key Learning Points

  • This is for recognising a problem, not for diagnosing one.
  • A pop and swelling within hours usually means something significant.
  • Front of knee pain, worse on stairs and after sitting, is very common in teenagers.
  • Overuse pain almost always follows a change in training, surface or footwear.
  • Locking, giving way, and not bearing weight are the signs that need checking.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent, teacher, coach or doctor.
