Beyond Surgery

Page 0.3.3 · Careers in Knee Medicine · careers page

Six careers in knee health that do not involve operating, what each one does, and how people get into them.

What the Work Involves

These roles divide roughly into three kinds of work.

Hands on with patients, week after week. Physiotherapy, nursing and orthotics sit here.

Measurement. Imaging and sports science sit here. The question is always whether the measurement answers something useful.

Design. Engineering sits here, and it is the furthest from the patient and often the furthest reaching.

The People Who Do It

Surgery is the career most people have heard of, which makes it look like the only one. It is not even the largest.

For every knee operation there are many more appointments, scans, rehabilitation sessions and strength programmes, and those are run by people who never pick up a scalpel.

Physiotherapist. Assesses how a knee moves and builds a programme to change it. Entry is through a physiotherapy degree.

Sports and exercise scientist. Measures movement, strength and load, usually in sport rather than in hospital. Entry is through a sport and exercise science degree.

Radiographer. Produces the images, and in some roles reports them. Entry is through a diagnostic radiography degree.

Orthotist or prosthetist. Designs braces and supports that change how force passes through a joint. A small profession with a specific degree route.

Nurse specialist. Runs clinics, often with more continuity of contact than any doctor in the team. Entry is through nursing, then specialist training.

Biomedical engineer. Designs the implants and the instruments. Entry is through engineering rather than through a health degree.

What It Takes

Every one of these routes needs science at school, and most need maths beyond GCSE or its equivalent. Engineering needs the most maths and physics of the group.

None of them is a consolation prize for not getting into medicine. Several are harder to get into than some medical schools, and all of them are full careers in their own right.

Where to Find Out More

Each profession has a body that publishes its entry requirements, and those requirements change, so read them there rather than relying on a summary.

The Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, the Society of Radiographers, the British Association of Sport and Exercise Sciences, the Royal College of Nursing and the Institution of Mechanical Engineers all publish careers information.

The NHS publishes a careers site covering every clinical role it employs, including the ones most school pupils have never heard of.

Key Learning Points

  • Most of the work in knee health is done by people who do not operate.
  • These roles divide into hands on care, measurement and design.
  • Several of the non medical routes are as competitive as medicine.
  • Entry requirements are published by each profession's own body and change regularly.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent, teacher, coach or doctor.
