How the Knee Moves

Not quite a hinge. It rolls, it glides, and it twists a little while it does both.

For Young Learners

Function

Most people say the knee is a hinge, like a door. That is close, and it is not the whole story.

A door hinge does exactly one thing. It swings around a fixed pin, and the pin never moves. Your knee does something more interesting.

As your knee bends, the rounded end of your thigh bone rolls backwards on the flat top of your shin bone, the way a ball rolls along the floor. If it only rolled, it would roll straight off the back after a short distance. So at the same time it also glides forwards, sliding against the direction it is rolling. Rolling and gliding together let the knee bend a long way while staying on the shin bone.

There is a third movement that surprises people. Your knee twists. Not much, and only a few degrees, but it is real and it is necessary. The twist is largest when your knee is bent and almost disappears when it is straight.

At the very end of straightening, something neat happens. Your shin bone rotates slightly outwards and the joint tightens into its most stable position. People call it the screw home mechanism, because it works a little like turning a screw the last half turn. Once it is locked, you can stand for ages without your thigh muscles doing much at all. Try standing still and feel how relaxed the front of your thigh is.

Clinical Relevance

That twist is also where trouble comes from. Rolling and gliding are fine. Twisting too far, especially with your foot stuck to the ground, is how a lot of sports injuries happen.

It is why the dangerous moment in football or netball is not usually the tackle. It is the turn.

Key Learning Points

  • The knee is more than a hinge: it rolls and glides at the same time.
  • Rolling alone would carry the thigh bone off the back of the shin bone.
  • The knee also twists a few degrees, most when bent and least when straight.
  • At full straightening the joint locks, so standing costs little muscle effort.
  • Twisting on a planted foot is how many sports injuries happen.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent, teacher, coach or doctor.
