What This Is

Ask a Surgeon is an archive of questions sent in by school age readers, with answers written by a consultant and checked before they appear.

Every question is read by a person before it is published. Nothing goes straight onto the site. That is deliberate, and it is the reason answers take time to appear.

The archive is not open yet. The question route and the moderation process are still being built, and no questions have been answered, so there is nothing to read here today. This page describes how it will work rather than pretending it already does.

The People Who Do It

Answers are written by a consultant surgeon and reviewed before publication. The reviewer checks two separate things.

First, whether the answer is correct.

Second, whether it stays on the right side of the line between teaching and treating. A question about how a ligament heals can be answered. A question about whether a particular knee needs an operation cannot, by anyone who has not examined it.

What It Takes

A good question for this archive is about how something works, why something is done, or what a career involves. Those can be answered usefully for everyone reading.

Questions that cannot be answered here fall into three groups.

Anything about your own knee, or a friend's. Nobody can assess a joint through a form. Take that to a doctor or physiotherapist who can examine it.

Anything that needs personal details. Do not send your full name, your school, your address or anything identifying. Questions are published without them.

Anything asking which clinic or which surgeon to choose. This site does not make those comparisons.

Where to Find Out More

Until the archive opens, the eleven pages in chapters 0.1 and 0.2 answer the questions that come up most often about how knees work and what goes wrong with them.

For a question about getting into medicine, chapter 0.4 covers subject choice, work experience, admissions tests and interviews.

For a question about your own knee, speak to a parent, a teacher, a coach, a physiotherapist or a doctor. That is the right route and it is faster than this one.

Key Learning Points

  • Every question is read and checked by a person before any answer is published.
  • The archive is not open yet, and this page says so rather than implying otherwise.
  • Questions about how something works can be answered; questions about your own knee cannot.
  • Never include identifying personal details in a question.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent, teacher, coach or doctor.

References

No reference list is attached to this draft. Evidence verification has not run, because source retrieval was unavailable at generation time. Under the Operations Handbook a missing reference is acceptable and an invented one is a critical failure, so nothing has been cited. This page carries no entry requirement, test score, fee, deadline or statistic, because those change every year and none could be checked against an official source. Where a figure would normally go, the page points to the body that publishes it instead. Every claim is listed for verification in the accompanying handoff block.
