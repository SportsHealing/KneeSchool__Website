Anterior cruciate ligament

Choose a depth

The knee's strongest brake

The ACL is a short, tough band deep inside the knee. It stops the shin bone sliding forward when you turn, land or stop quickly. An athlete who tears it often hears a pop, and the knee swells the same day. Warm up programmes that train landing and turning lower the risk. If a knee gives way, swells or hurts to stand on, tell a parent, a teacher or a coach, and have it looked at by a health professional.

What it is and why it matters

A strong cord inside the knee that stops the shin sliding forward. People often describe a pop, swelling within a few hours, and a knee that gives way on turning. Not every tear needs surgery. The decision depends on your symptoms and what you need the knee to do.

Structure and function

An intracapsular, extrasynovial ligament running from the medial wall of the lateral femoral condyle to the anterior intercondylar area of the tibia. Two functional bundles, anteromedial and posterolateral. It is the primary restraint to anterior tibial translation and a secondary restraint to rotation.

Examination and imaging

Lachman is the most sensitive clinical test. The pivot shift demonstrates rotatory instability and correlates with function. A Segond fracture on plain radiograph implies a rupture until proven otherwise. MRI confirms the tear and grades meniscal, ramp and chondral injury.

Decision and evidence

Reconstruct on instability and demand rather than on the scan alone. Graft choice trades re-rupture risk against donor site morbidity and is age dependent. Missed posterolateral corner injury, ramp lesion or excessive tibial slope are recognised causes of early graft failure.

Revision and complex cases

Revision starts with tunnel position and tunnel width on CT. Stage with bone graft when tunnels are confluent or wide. Address meniscal deficiency, coronal malalignment and slope. Add lateral extra-articular tenodesis in high grade pivot, young pivoting athletes and revision.

Open questions

Repair versus reconstruction in proximal avulsion. The slope threshold that justifies anterior closing wedge osteotomy. Whether criterion based return to sport testing changes re-rupture rates in adolescents. How registry data should shape graft choice in routine practice.
