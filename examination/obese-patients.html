Obese Patients

Page 4.23.4 · Special Situations · examination page

Examining a knee where the soft tissue envelope limits most of what the examination depends on.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Obese Patients

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Tests

The examination is the same and several of its steps become harder, because palpation, alignment assessment and ligament testing all depend on reaching or seeing the structures. 4.6.1 covers systematic palpation.

The examination is deciding what it can still establish and recording what it could not, rather than reporting an incomplete examination as a normal one. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

It matters because an unreported limitation becomes a false negative in the record. 4.1.5 covers common examination pitfalls.

How It Is Performed

Position the patient so the knee is accessible, which may need a different couch height or a supported leg. 4.3.5 covers the supine assessment.

Assess the alignment standing, accepting that the soft tissue obscures the contour and that the stance may be wider. 4.3.3 covers standing alignment.

Watch the gait over a usable distance, since the pattern is still visible when the contour is not. 4.4.2 covers the antalgic gait.

Palpate the joint lines with the knee flexed, which brings them closer to the surface. 4.6.4 covers joint line tenderness.

Assess the effusion, accepting that the bulge sign is harder and the tap is more reliable here. 4.6.3 covers the effusion assessment.

Perform the ligament tests with the limb supported, since the weight of the limb otherwise defeats the examiner's control. 4.12.1 covers valgus stress testing.

Record which parts of the examination were limited and why. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

What a Positive Means

A positive finding means what it usually means, and a negative one means less than usual.

A limited examination with a negative result is recorded as limited rather than as negative. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Joint line tenderness is still localising when it is obtainable. 4.6.4 covers joint line tenderness.

An effusion detected by tap rather than by bulge is the same finding. 4.6.3 covers the effusion assessment.

An alignment estimated rather than measured is recorded as an estimate, and the film carries the measurement. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

Key Learning Points

  • The examination is the same and several steps are harder.
  • The job is recording what could not be established.
  • Flexing the knee brings the joint lines closer to the surface.
  • The patellar tap outperforms the bulge sign here.
  • A limited negative is recorded as limited.

MRCS Level

How It Is Performed

In a viva this is the practical examination question and the expected answer adapts the technique and records the limits.

The expected points are the positioning, the standing alignment, the gait, the flexed joint line palpation, the effusion assessment, the supported ligament tests and the explicit record of what was limited.

Say that you would record which parts of the examination were limited. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Say that you would support the limb for the ligament tests, and give the reason. 4.12.1 covers valgus stress testing.

Say that imaging carries more of the assessment here. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that every palpation and stress test depends on transmitting force through soft tissue, and more tissue means less transmitted and less felt. 4.6.1 covers systematic palpation.

Flexion brings the joint line anteriorly and reduces the tissue between the examiner and the bone, which is why the position changes the yield. 4.6.4 covers joint line tenderness.

The limb's weight reduces the examiner's control during a stress test, which is why support rather than strength is the answer. 4.12.1 covers valgus stress testing.

Load through the knee is higher, so the compartment findings and the patellofemoral findings are more likely to be present as well as harder to elicit. 3.5.2 covers contact pressures.

How Good It Is

The examination's specificity is preserved and its sensitivity falls.

Its best contribution is unchanged where it can be performed, which is why technique and positioning carry the difference here. 4.3.5 covers the supine assessment.

It produces false negatives rather than false positives, which is the characteristic failure.

Imaging's relative value rises, so the threshold for it is lower in this group. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 023, and the comparison is the claim being made.

Key Learning Points

  • Palpation and stress testing depend on force transmitted through soft tissue.
  • Flexion reduces the tissue between examiner and joint line.
  • The limb's weight is answered by support rather than strength.
  • Knee load is higher, so findings are more likely and harder to elicit.
  • The characteristic failure is a false negative.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the examination still establishes the compartment, the alignment pattern, the range and the function, which is most of what an arthritic decision needs. 4.18.2 covers established disease.

A recorded limitation changes the weight the imaging carries, which is a documented reasoning step rather than a gap. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Function and walking distance are unaffected by the examination's difficulty and remain fully available. 4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

A ligament assessment that cannot be completed is a specific indication for an examination under anaesthesia or for imaging. 4.23.1 covers the examination under anaesthesia.

The soft tissue envelope is itself a finding for an operative decision, and it is recorded as one. 4.3.6 covers skin changes.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is recording an incomplete examination as a normal one. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

The alignment is estimated and reported as measured. 4.3.3 covers standing alignment.

An effusion is missed because only the bulge sign was attempted. 4.6.3 covers the effusion assessment.

A ligament test is performed without support and its negative result is trusted. 4.12.1 covers valgus stress testing.

Findings are attributed to weight rather than examined for, which is a substitution of explanation for assessment. 4.2.10 covers severity.

Key Learning Points

  • Recording an incomplete examination as normal is the commonest error.
  • An estimated alignment is reported as an estimate.
  • Attempting only the bulge sign misses an effusion.
  • An unsupported ligament test's negative result is not trustworthy.
  • Attributing findings to weight substitutes explanation for assessment.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the problem is that the examination's reduced sensitivity is rarely quantified and therefore rarely weighed.

A negative finding carries less information here and the record usually does not say how much less, which leaves the next clinician to assume a full examination. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Alignment assessed clinically is least reliable in exactly the group whose alignment matters most to the decision. 4.3.3 covers standing alignment.

The functional record is the part of the assessment least affected, which makes it proportionally more important here than elsewhere. 4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

Comorbidity travels with the finding, so the knee's contribution to a reduced walking distance is harder to isolate. 4.2.15 covers referred pain.

The soft tissue envelope affects the operative options and the complication profile, which makes it part of the assessment rather than a comment on it. 4.3.6 covers skin changes.

Key Learning Points

  • A negative finding carries less information and the record rarely says so.
  • Clinical alignment is least reliable where it matters most.
  • The functional record is proportionally more important here.
  • The knee's contribution to reduced walking is harder to isolate.
  • The soft tissue envelope is part of the assessment.
