Return to Sport Testing

Page 4.21.7 · Functional Testing · examination page

The battery as a whole, where the examination's job is to assemble the tests rather than to perform any one of them.

Return to Sport Testing

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Tests

Return to sport testing combines strength, hop performance, control, landing mechanics and a psychological measure, because no single test covers readiness. 4.21.2 covers the single leg hop.

The examination is assembling the results into a picture and identifying which component is limiting. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

It matters that the battery is a set of criteria rather than a test with a result, so a patient passes or fails each component. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

How It Is Performed

Establish the sport, the position and the level, since the demands differ and the criteria are read against them. 4.2.23 covers football.

Establish the time since surgery or injury, because time is a criterion in its own right. 4.2.9 covers the onset.

Assess quadriceps and hamstring strength, as the foundation the rest rests on. 4.3.9 covers muscle wasting.

Perform the hop battery, recording distance and landing quality for each test. 4.21.3 covers the triple hop.

Assess control with a slow single limb test. 4.21.5 covers the Y balance test.

Assess landing mechanics with a scored observation. 4.21.6 covers the landing error scoring system.

Record a psychological readiness measure, since confidence limits return independently of physical capacity. 4.22.6 covers the return to sport psychological measure.

Examine the knee itself, because the functional battery does not replace the structural examination. 4.20.1 covers the reconstruction assessment.

What a Positive Means

Passing every component, with a structurally sound knee and sufficient time elapsed, is the position from which return is discussed.

A strength deficit with good hop performance indicates compensation, and the strength is the criterion. 4.3.9 covers muscle wasting.

Good physical results with a low psychological readiness score identifies confidence as the limiting factor. 4.22.6 covers the return to sport psychological measure.

An asymmetrical hop result with passing strength identifies a control or a confidence deficit. 4.21.2 covers the single leg hop.

Any structural finding on the knee examination overrides the battery, since the battery assumes a sound knee. 4.8.3 covers the bulge sign.

Key Learning Points

  • No single test covers readiness, so the battery is a set of criteria.
  • The sport, position and level change what the criteria mean.
  • Time since surgery is a criterion in its own right.
  • Confidence limits return independently of physical capacity.
  • A structural finding overrides the battery.

MRCS Level

How It Is Performed

In a viva this is the return to sport question and the expected answer lists the components and says what each adds.

The expected points are the sport and level, the time elapsed, the strength assessment, the hop battery, the control test, the landing score, the psychological measure and the structural examination.

Say that time since surgery is a criterion and not simply a context. 4.2.9 covers the onset.

Say that you would include a psychological measure, and give the reason. 4.22.6 covers the return to sport psychological measure.

Say that the knee examination is part of the battery rather than superseded by it. 4.20.1 covers the reconstruction assessment.

What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that readiness has a structural, a strength, a control and a psychological component, and a deficit in any one returns an athlete who is not ready. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

Graft maturation takes time regardless of function, which is why time is a criterion that performance cannot substitute for. 3.7.16 covers graft biomechanics.

Compensation lets a strong limb pass a functional test while the knee's own contribution remains deficient, which is why strength is measured separately. 4.3.9 covers muscle wasting.

Fear of reinjury predicts return independently of the physical measures, which is why it is measured rather than inferred. 4.22.6 covers the return to sport psychological measure.

How Good It Is

The battery is more informative than any component and still an incomplete description of readiness.

Its best contribution is identifying which component is limiting, which directs the next phase of rehabilitation. 4.2.34 covers previous rehabilitation.

It does not predict reinjury in an individual, and the criteria themselves are conventions.

Nothing in imaging substitutes for the battery, and nothing in the battery substitutes for graft maturation. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 023, and the comparison is the claim being made. The battery's thresholds are literature conventions and this repository does not state them.

Key Learning Points

  • Readiness has structural, strength, control and psychological components.
  • Graft maturation takes time that performance cannot substitute for.
  • Compensation lets a limb pass a functional test with a deficient knee.
  • Fear of reinjury predicts return independently of physical measures.
  • The battery identifies the limiting component.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the battery structures a decision that would otherwise be made on time alone.

A full battery identifies the limiting component, which makes the advice specific rather than a date. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

A passing battery with a short time since surgery is a conflict between criteria, and the time criterion is the one that is not negotiable on current understanding. 4.2.9 covers the onset.

A failing single component with everything else passing directs rehabilitation precisely. 4.2.34 covers previous rehabilitation.

Sport specific demands change which components matter most, so the battery is weighted by the sport rather than applied uniformly. 4.2.25 covers rugby.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is using time alone, which the battery exists to improve on. 4.2.9 covers the onset.

The battery is used as a pass or fail whole, so the limiting component is not identified. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

The psychological measure is omitted, and it is often the limiting one. 4.22.6 covers the return to sport psychological measure.

Strength is inferred from hop performance, which compensation makes unreliable. 4.3.9 covers muscle wasting.

Passing the battery is treated as permission, when it is one input into a decision that involves the athlete. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

Key Learning Points

  • Using time alone is what the battery exists to improve on.
  • A pass or fail whole hides the limiting component.
  • The psychological measure is often the limiting one and is often omitted.
  • Strength inferred from hop performance is unreliable.
  • Passing the battery is an input rather than permission.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the battery's limits are about what passing it establishes, which is less than it is usually taken to mean.

The components are each imperfect and their combination does not become a prediction, so the battery describes a state rather than forecasting an outcome. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

The thresholds are conventions drawn from specific populations, and applying them to a different athlete is an extrapolation. 4.23.2 covers the elite athlete.

Symmetry criteria are satisfied by a bilaterally deconditioned athlete, which is the systematic weakness of every paired comparison here. 4.3.9 covers muscle wasting.

Graft maturation, control and confidence recover on different timescales, so the battery's components become limiting in turn rather than together. 3.7.16 covers graft biomechanics.

The decision involves risk that belongs to the athlete, so the battery informs a conversation rather than settling it. 4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

Key Learning Points

  • The battery describes a state rather than forecasting an outcome.
  • Thresholds are conventions from specific populations.
  • Symmetry criteria are satisfied by bilateral deconditioning.
  • The components become limiting in turn rather than together.
  • The decision involves risk that belongs to the athlete.
