Crystal Arthropathy

Page 4.8.10 · Effusion Assessment · examination page

A hot swollen knee that is not infected and not injured, and the differential that has to be held alongside the one that is.

Crystal Arthropathy

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Tests

Crystal arthropathy produces an acutely hot, swollen, painful knee, and its importance in a mechanical section is that it mimics both infection and injury. 4.8.9 covers synovitis.

The examination cannot distinguish it from a septic joint, which is the single most important thing on this page, so the two are held together until the investigation separates them. 4.6.2 covers temperature.

It also produces recurrent effusions between attacks, which is why it appears in the chronic effusion differential as well as the acute one. 4.8.8 covers chronic effusions.

How It Is Performed

Assess the effusion's size and tension. 4.8.6 covers ballottement.

Assess the temperature of the knee and of the limb, and take the patient's temperature. 4.6.2 covers temperature.

Look at the skin for erythema, which is often marked and extends beyond the joint. 4.3.6 covers skin changes.

Assess the range, which is usually severely limited by pain rather than by any structural restriction. 4.7.2 covers passive motion.

Examine the other joints, since crystal disease is often polyarticular and a history of a similar attack elsewhere is strongly suggestive. 4.2.14 covers inflammatory pain.

Ask about previous attacks, diet, alcohol, diuretics and renal function, since the history carries the diagnosis more than the examination does. 4.2.9 covers onset.

What a Positive Means

A hot, red, swollen, exquisitely painful knee with a severely limited range is a presentation shared by crystal arthropathy and septic arthritis, and the examination cannot separate them.

A history of identical previous attacks that resolved makes crystal disease more likely and does not exclude infection.

Involvement of other joints, particularly a first metatarsophalangeal joint, is strongly suggestive of crystal disease. 4.2.14 covers inflammatory pain.

A recurrent effusion between attacks, with a boggy synovium, indicates a chronic crystal arthropathy. 4.8.9 covers synovitis.

An afebrile patient with a hot knee is not reassuring, since both diagnoses can present without systemic features. 4.6.2 covers temperature.

Key Learning Points

  • Crystal arthropathy mimics both infection and injury.
  • The examination cannot distinguish it from a septic joint.
  • A history of identical previous attacks makes it likelier and does not exclude infection.
  • Involvement of other joints, particularly the first metatarsophalangeal, is suggestive.
  • An afebrile patient with a hot knee is not reassuring.

MRCS Level

How It Is Performed

In a viva this is a safety station, and the expected answer treats the knee as potentially septic until the investigation says otherwise.

The expected points are the effusion, the temperature of the knee and the patient, the erythema, the severely limited range, the other joints, and the history of previous attacks.

Say that you cannot distinguish it from a septic joint clinically, and say what you would do about that.

Say that the joint is aspirated and the fluid examined, which is the investigation that separates the two, and that it is done before treatment is assumed. 4.8.7 covers the acute presentation.

Say that a history of gout does not exclude infection, which is the trap the question is usually set to probe.

What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that crystals within the joint provoke an intense inflammatory response, which produces the same clinical picture as a bacterial infection because the final pathway is the same. 4.8.9 covers synovitis.

That shared pathway is why the examination cannot separate them, and it is an argument about mechanism rather than about examiner skill.

The inflammation also damages cartilage, which is why repeated attacks have a structural consequence and why the diagnosis matters beyond the attack. 4.9.11 covers one consequence.

Between attacks a low grade synovitis persists in chronic disease, which is why a recurrent effusion in a patient with crystal disease is part of the same process rather than a separate problem. 4.8.8 covers chronic effusions.

How Good It Is

The clinical examination establishes that a knee is acutely inflamed and cannot establish why, which is a clear and important limitation.

The history carries more diagnostic weight than the examination, particularly the pattern of previous attacks and their resolution. 4.2.9 covers onset.

Aspiration and fluid examination are the investigation that separates the diagnoses, and the examination's job is to make sure that happens rather than to pre-empt it.

Recording the findings precisely matters, because a knee treated as crystal disease that turns out to be septic is a serious and recognised event. 4.1.8 covers the record.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 023, and the comparison is the claim being made.

Key Learning Points

  • Crystals provoke an inflammatory response with the same final pathway as infection.
  • That shared pathway is why the examination cannot separate them.
  • Repeated attacks damage cartilage, so the diagnosis matters beyond the attack.
  • A low grade synovitis persists between attacks in chronic disease.
  • The examination's job is to ensure the aspiration happens rather than to pre-empt it.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level this diagnosis changes the pathway completely and the examination's contribution is to make sure the possibility is considered.

A hot swollen knee is aspirated before it is treated, and in a surgical clinic that is the decision this page exists to support. 4.8.7 covers the acute presentation.

A knee with crystal disease should not be arthroscoped for its effusion, and the recognisable futility in this area is a mechanical operation on a crystal knee. 4.8.8 covers chronic effusions.

A knee with crystal disease and genuine mechanical findings presents a judgement, since surgery in an acutely inflamed joint carries added risk and the timing matters.

A post-operative hot swollen knee is infection until excluded, and a known history of crystal disease makes that assessment harder rather than easier. 4.6.2 covers temperature.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is diagnosing crystal disease clinically and treating it without aspiration, which is how a septic joint is missed.

The second is reading a history of gout as reassurance.

The third is reading an afebrile patient as not septic.

The fourth is arthroscoping a knee whose recurrent effusion is crystal related.

The fifth is attributing a post-operative hot knee to crystal disease on the basis of the history. 4.6.2 covers temperature.

Key Learning Points

  • A hot swollen knee is aspirated before it is treated.
  • A mechanical operation on a crystal knee is the recognisable futility here.
  • Surgery in an acutely inflamed joint carries added risk, so timing matters.
  • A known history of crystal disease makes a post-operative assessment harder, not easier.
  • Diagnosing it clinically without aspiration is how a septic joint is missed.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

In referred practice crystal disease is the explanation for the knee whose effusions have never fitted a mechanical pattern.

A patient with recurrent hot effusions, normal mechanics and several previous arthroscopies has had a medical problem treated surgically repeatedly, and the pattern of the attacks is the clue the examinations missed. 4.8.8 covers chronic effusions.

A patient with chronic crystal arthropathy has a knee whose cartilage has been damaged by the inflammation, and a mechanical complaint on top of that is a compound problem rather than a simple one. 4.9.11 covers meniscal examination in osteoarthritis.

A patient with crystal disease and a prosthesis presents a difficult assessment, because every hot swollen episode has to be treated as possible infection and the history offers an alternative that is sometimes right. 4.6.2 covers temperature.

A patient with a chondrocalcinosis seen on imaging and a mechanical complaint has two findings, and attributing the whole complaint to either is the error. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

In medicolegal work a knee treated as crystal disease that was septic is a recognised and serious failure, and the absent aspiration is almost always the point of criticism. 4.23.7 covers that setting.

Key Learning Points

  • It explains the knee whose effusions never fitted a mechanical pattern.
  • Chronic crystal disease damages cartilage, so a mechanical complaint on top is compound.
  • With a prosthesis every hot episode is possible infection and the history offers a tempting alternative.
  • Chondrocalcinosis with a mechanical complaint is two findings, not one.
  • A septic knee treated as crystal disease is criticised on the absent aspiration.
