Revision Surgery Patients

Page 4.23.5 · Special Situations · examination page

The patient group whose knees have been operated on repeatedly, where the examination has lost every reference it normally uses.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Revision Surgery Patients

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Tests

A patient with several previous operations has a knee whose normal findings are whatever the operations left, so the examination is read against the surgical history. 4.20.9 covers planning the revision itself.

The examination is establishing what has been done, what the current state is, and which part of the state is new. 4.2.30 covers previous surgery.

It matters because the contralateral knee is often operated too, which removes the comparison the examination depends on. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

How It Is Performed

Build the operative history in order, with dates, and accept that the patient's account and the records may differ. 4.2.30 covers previous surgery.

Map the scars on both knees, since they are the most reliable record available. 4.3.7 covers surgical scars.

Assess the soft tissue envelope, because it limits what any further operation can do. 4.3.6 covers skin changes.

Record the range on both sides, since both are likely to be abnormal. 4.7.10 covers the post operative range assessment.

Record the alignment on both sides. 4.3.3 covers standing alignment.

Perform the ligament examination and record each finding as a state rather than as a diagnosis. 4.12.1 covers valgus stress testing.

Record the function and what has changed recently, because the change is the only thing in the examination that is new. 4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

Record the patient's expectation and their account of previous results. 4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

What a Positive Means

An abnormal finding in this group may be the expected state of that knee rather than a problem.

A recent change in a long standing abnormal state is the finding, and the history is where it comes from. 4.2.9 covers the onset.

Symmetrical abnormality in a patient with bilateral surgery is uninformative rather than reassuring. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

A compromised soft tissue envelope is a constraint on treatment rather than a diagnosis. 4.3.6 covers skin changes.

A function that has deteriorated with an unchanged examination points somewhere the examination is not looking. 4.18.6 covers functional limitation.

Key Learning Points

  • An abnormal finding may be that knee's expected state.
  • The recent change, not the abnormal state, is the finding.
  • The contralateral comparison usually fails in this group.
  • A compromised envelope is a constraint rather than a diagnosis.
  • Scars are the most reliable record available.

MRCS Level

How It Is Performed

In a viva this is the complex history station and the expected answer reconstructs the history before examining.

The expected points are the ordered operative history, the scar map on both knees, the envelope, the bilateral range and alignment, the ligament state, the function and its recent change, and the expectation.

Say that you would record findings as a state rather than as a diagnosis. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Say that the contralateral comparison may be unavailable, and say what you would use instead. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

Say that you would establish what has changed recently. 4.2.9 covers the onset.

What a Positive Means

The reasoning expected is that each operation changes the knee's anatomy and its restraints, so the examination's normal references no longer describe it. 4.20.9 covers planning the revision.

Laxity after several procedures reflects those procedures as much as any injury, which is why a finding is a state rather than a cause. 4.12.1 covers valgus stress testing.

Scar tissue changes the end feel and the range, so the mechanical findings have a surgical as well as a pathological explanation. 4.7.5 covers the end feel.

The soft tissue envelope limits the approach, so it is part of the assessment rather than a note on it. 4.3.6 covers skin changes.

How Good It Is

The examination is reliable for the current state and poor at attributing any part of it.

Its best contribution is the serial record, since a change over time is the only interpretable finding in a knee with no valid reference. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

It cannot separate the effects of previous surgery from a new problem on a single assessment.

Imaging and operative records carry the structural history, so the examination contributes the current state and the change. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 023, and the comparison is the claim being made.

Key Learning Points

  • Each operation changes the anatomy the examination's references assume.
  • Laxity reflects the procedures as much as any injury.
  • Scar tissue gives the mechanical findings a surgical explanation.
  • The envelope is part of the assessment.
  • A change over time is the only interpretable finding without a reference.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level this group's examination establishes feasibility and the current state, and the decision rests on both.

A soft tissue envelope that will not tolerate another approach is a finding that can stop a plan, and only the examination supplies it. 4.3.6 covers skin changes.

An extensor mechanism that is deficient changes what any revision can achieve, and it is a clinical finding. 4.3.9 covers muscle wasting.

A recent deterioration against a stable abnormal baseline is the clinical case for intervening, and a stable abnormal state is not. 4.2.9 covers the onset.

Expectation in this group is shaped by previous disappointment, which changes the conversation rather than the findings. 4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reading an abnormal finding as new. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

The contralateral knee is used as a control when it has also been operated. 4.1.4 covers the structured approach.

Laxity is attributed to a specific structure in a knee where several have been altered. 4.12.1 covers valgus stress testing.

The envelope is not assessed until the operation is planned. 4.3.6 covers skin changes.

Infection is excluded clinically, which cannot be done in any operated knee. 4.6.2 covers temperature assessment.

Key Learning Points

  • Reading an abnormal finding as new is the commonest error.
  • An operated contralateral knee is not a control.
  • Laxity cannot be attributed to one structure in a multiply altered knee.
  • The envelope is assessed before the plan, not after.
  • Infection cannot be excluded clinically in an operated knee.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this group is where the examination's dependence on references becomes the limiting factor.

There is no valid normal for the knee and often none for the other side, so the examination's only interpretable product is a change in a series it may not have. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Pain has more candidate sources than any examination can separate, and attributing it confidently is the error the setting most invites. 4.2.8 covers the location of pain.

Previous records are frequently incomplete, so the history the examination is read against is itself reconstructed. 4.2.30 covers previous surgery.

Expectation after repeated disappointment predicts the reported result as strongly as anything the examination finds. 4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

The decision is often whether to stop operating, and the examination contributes to that without being able to settle it. 4.2.10 covers severity.

Key Learning Points

  • There is no valid normal for either knee, so only a change is interpretable.
  • Pain has more candidate sources than the examination can separate.
  • The history the findings are read against is itself reconstructed.
  • Expectation after repeated disappointment predicts the reported result.
  • The decision is often whether to stop operating.
