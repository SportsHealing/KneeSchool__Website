For Medical Students

What the Question Was

The question this chapter closes on is what the examination is still bad at, now that imaging answers much of what it used to be asked. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

The honest list is short and consistent across the chapters: grading is unreliable, the worn knee defeats the provocation tests, and function is measured better than it is predicted. 4.9.11 covers the worn knee.

The question is which of those is a problem with the tests and which is a problem with what is being asked of them. 4.1.5 covers common examination pitfalls.

What It Showed

The section's own pages show that the reproducible findings are the gross ones and the decisive findings are the fine ones, which is the central difficulty. 4.7.5 covers the end feel.

They show that the examination's strongest remaining contributions are localisation, function and the serial record. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

They show that nothing in the examination dates a finding, which limits it in every setting where causation matters. 4.23.7 covers the medico legal setting.

Key Learning Points

  • Grading is unreliable across the section.
  • The worn knee defeats the provocation tests.
  • Function is measured better than it is predicted.
  • The reproducible findings are gross and the decisive ones are fine.
  • Nothing in the examination dates a finding.

MRCS Level

What Was Done

Instrumented measurement was applied to anterior laxity, to the pivot shift and to functional performance, replacing a hand grade with a number in each case. 4.2.2 covers instability.

Observational scoring systems were developed for movement quality, which converted an impression into a count. 4.21.6 covers the landing error scoring system.

Patient reported instruments were developed and validated, which made the patient's account measurable alongside the examination. 4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

Wearable and video based measurement moved assessment out of the clinic, which is the most recent direction and the least settled. 4.4.8 covers running gait assessment.

What It Showed

Each of these moves improved reproducibility and none replaced the examination, because each measures a narrower quantity than the clinical question. 4.2.2 covers instability.

Instrumented laxity measures displacement and the question is restraint, which is why the hand test survived. 4.10.4 covers the Lachman test.

Scoring a movement improves agreement between examiners without establishing that the scored feature predicts anything in an individual. 4.21.6 covers the landing error scoring system.

Patient reported measures capture a different quantity, so they sit beside the examination rather than displacing it. 4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

No sensitivity, specificity or likelihood ratio appears on this page, under decision 023 and decision 022 together. The ranking is what is recalled reliably; a specific percentage written from memory would look exactly like a correct one.

Key Learning Points

  • Each measurement move improved reproducibility and replaced nothing.
  • Instrumented laxity measures displacement and the question is restraint.
  • Scoring improves agreement without establishing prediction.
  • Patient reported measures capture a different quantity.
  • Out of clinic measurement is the newest and least settled direction.

FRCS Level

What It Showed

At decision level the open questions are specific enough to state, which is more useful than a prediction.

Whether a reproducible measurement of rotational laxity would change an operative decision is open, and the grade currently used is the section's least reproducible quantity. 4.14.3 covers anterolateral rotatory instability.

Whether any functional battery predicts reinjury in an individual is open, and the honest current answer is that it describes a state. 4.21.8 covers return to play decision making.

Whether the examination can be made useful in the worn knee is open, and the current position is that the symptoms and the imaging carry that decision. 4.9.11 covers the worn knee.

Whether out of clinic measurement adds anything a clinic assessment does not is open, and the volume of data it produces is not itself an answer. 4.4.8 covers running gait assessment.

What It Changed

The section's practical conclusion is that the examination's role has narrowed and sharpened rather than declined. 4.1.6 covers that relationship.

Localisation, function, the serial record and the decision to image are what it now carries, and each of those it carries better than any alternative. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Grading, dating a finding and predicting an outcome are what it does not carry, and saying so is the section's most useful output. 4.23.7 covers the medico legal setting.

The registers this repository keeps are an attempt to make that distinction auditable rather than asserted, which is the same argument applied to the site itself. 4.22.8 covers the use of these scores.

Key Learning Points

  • The role has narrowed and sharpened rather than declined.
  • Localisation, function, the serial record and the imaging decision are what it carries.
  • Grading, dating and prediction are what it does not.
  • Four open questions can be stated specifically.
  • Saying what it does not carry is the section's most useful output.

Fellowship Level

What It Changed

At fellowship level the section ends on a position rather than a prediction, and the position has consequences for how an examination is recorded.

A finding that is reproducible is recorded as a finding and one that is a judgement is recorded as a judgement, which is a change in documentation rather than in technique. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

A decision that rests on an unreproducible grade is identified as such, which is uncomfortable and is the state of the evidence. 4.14.3 covers anterolateral rotatory instability.

The examination's value in the worn knee is largely negative and recording that honestly is better than a positive finding nobody can act on. 4.9.11 covers the worn knee.

The serial record is the examination's strongest product and the one least often kept, which is a service problem rather than a knowledge problem. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Key Learning Points

  • Findings and judgements are recorded differently.
  • A decision resting on an unreproducible grade is identified as such.
  • A negative conclusion honestly recorded beats an unusable positive one.
  • The serial record is the strongest product and the least often kept.
  • This repository's registers are the honest reading list for its own pages.
