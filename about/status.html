W-18 Section 3 Biomechanics Academy, chapter 3.13All 10 pages written, style gate passed, awaiting reviewAlignment and Biomechanics. The chapter is about one number nobody can measure in the state that matters: the static angle is taken standing still, the load it governs is applied during movement, and corrections are planned to a target no trial has tested. 3.13.5 and 3.13.7 are where that lands, with constitutional varus saying neutral is not normal for everyone and dynamic alignment saying the adduction moment predicts where the angle does not. W-19 Section 3 Biomechanics Academy, chapter 3.14All 12 pages written, style gate passed, awaiting reviewGait Analysis. The organising idea is that gait is the one place the whole knee is measured doing its actual job, and what it measures best is adaptation: every disease page here is about a compensation the patient makes without being told to and usually without knowing. The recurring honest point is that this is the most mechanically complete measurement available and it is almost never available in a clinic. Two overlaps are handled on the page rather than left to the reader: chapter 3.3 owns kinematics during walking, running, landing and cutting, and 3.8.7 owns the PCL deficient knee from the ligament's side. 3.14.1 carries a patient tier, which brings the FAQ block and the when to seek help message.Consultant review by someone who reads gait literature. The statements carrying most weight are 3.14.9's, that quadriceps avoidance is the best known finding and not the commonest, and that altered kinematics persist after reconstruction; 3.14.11's, that the adduction moment predicts progression better than the static angle surgery is planned from; and 3.14.12's, that a successful knee replacement does not restore normal knee mechanics. 3.14.8's claim that the landing screen has underperformed while the training has held up is the fourth.12 published pages, 15,507 body words as the style gate counts them
