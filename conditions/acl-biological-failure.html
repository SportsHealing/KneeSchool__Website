Biological Failure

Page 6.8.3 · Revision ACL Problems · condition page

The graft that never became a ligament, and the category that stops the enquiry rather than explaining it.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Biological Failure

Choose a depth

For Young Learners

Definition

A graft starts as a piece of tendon. Over months the body rebuilds it into something that behaves like a ligament. 6.1.2 covers the functions of the ACL.

During that rebuilding the graft is weaker than it was on the day it was put in, and weaker than it will be at the end. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

How It Presents

Biological failure means the rebuilding did not work properly, so the graft never became strong even though it was in the right place and nothing new happened to it. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

The knee then gives way again without any new injury to explain it. 6.5.1 covers instability.

Key Learning Points

  • A graft starts as tendon and is rebuilt by the body.
  • It is weaker during the rebuilding than at either end of it.
  • Biological failure means the rebuilding did not work.
  • The position was right and nothing new happened.
  • The knee gives way again with no new injury to explain it.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent, teacher, coach or doctor.

For Patients

Definition

A graft is not a finished ligament on the day it is implanted. It loses its own blood supply, is repopulated by the body's cells, and is gradually remodelled, a process usually called ligamentisation. 6.1.2 covers the functions of the ACL.

It passes through a weaker phase during that process, which is one reason rehabilitation is staged rather than quick. 6.6.4 covers prevention.

Biological failure is the term used when a graft in a sound position, in a knee with no new injury, fails anyway. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Causes and Risk Factors

The candidate contributors described include the type of graft used and how it was processed, loading the knee hard during the weak phase, smoking, general metabolic and nutritional factors, and widening of the bone tunnels. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

None of them has been established as the cause in any individual case, and that is the honest position rather than a gap in this page. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

Management Overview

Because the label is reached by excluding the other causes, the first step is confirming that the other causes really were excluded. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Where a revision is planned, the factors that can be changed are addressed: graft choice, smoking, loading during the weak phase and the pace of return. 6.6.4 covers prevention.

Where nothing modifiable is found, the conversation has to include the possibility that a second graft behaves like the first, and that is the part most often left out. 4.23.5 covers revision surgery patients.

Key Learning Points

  • A graft is remodelled by the body over months.
  • It passes through a weaker phase during that process.
  • Biological failure is a sound position and no new injury.
  • Graft type, loading, smoking and tunnel widening are candidates.
  • A second graft may behave like the first, and that belongs in the conversation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Is this my fault? No. Most of the candidate factors are not under a patient's control. Smoking and the pace of return are the two that partly are, and they are discussed because they can be changed rather than to assign blame.

Q. How do you know it was biological? By excluding the alternatives: the tunnels measured correctly, the other restraints were intact, the alignment was normal and no new injury happened. That is a diagnosis by exclusion and it is weaker than a positive finding.

Q. Would a different graft work better? Graft choice is one of the modifiable factors and the evidence comparing types in this situation is limited. A change is often made, and it is a reasonable move rather than a solved question.

Q. Will the second one fail too? It may. That is the uncomfortable part of this category, and a surgeon who does not raise it has left the patient to discover it.

If your knee cannot take your weight after an injury, if it is hot and swollen with a fever, or if you develop new numbness or coldness in the foot, arrange urgent assessment.

For Medical Students

Definition

Ligamentisation describes the sequence a free tendon graft passes through: avascular necrosis, revascularisation, cellular repopulation and collagen remodelling towards ligament-like organisation. 6.1.2 covers the functions of the ACL.

Mechanical strength falls before it rises, so the graft is at its weakest some weeks after implantation rather than at implantation. 6.6.4 covers prevention.

Biological failure is recurrent instability attributed to inadequate incorporation or remodelling, with technical and traumatic causes excluded. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Causes and Risk Factors

Graft related candidates include allograft processing and irradiation, graft diameter, and the choice between tendon sources. 3.7.17 covers hamstring graft biomechanics.

Patient related candidates include smoking, metabolic and nutritional status, and generalised laxity. 6.6.1 covers the epidemiology.

Mechanical candidates include loading during the weak phase and tunnel widening, which blurs the boundary with the technical category. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

The category is therefore defined by what it is not, which is its central weakness as an explanation. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

Investigations and Imaging

MRI may show a graft that is thin, poorly defined or absent, and signal change within a graft is difficult to interpret during remodelling. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

Computed tomography excludes the technical causes by measuring the tunnels, and that exclusion is what licenses the label. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

No test confirms biological failure positively, which is worth stating plainly rather than implying. 5.7.14 covers revision ACL imaging.

Key Learning Points

  • Ligamentisation runs through necrosis, revascularisation, repopulation and remodelling.
  • The graft is weakest some weeks after implantation.
  • Graft, patient and mechanical factors are all candidates.
  • The category is defined by what it is not.
  • No test confirms it positively.

MRCS Level

Causes and Risk Factors

In a viva the expected answer gives the ligamentisation sequence and then the candidate factors in three groups.

Say necrosis, revascularisation, repopulation, remodelling, and name the weak window, because the rehabilitation question follows from it. 6.6.4 covers prevention.

Say graft factors, patient factors and loading factors, and concede that the category is a diagnosis of exclusion. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Investigations and Imaging

Say that the investigations are aimed at excluding the technical causes rather than at confirming this one. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Say that graft signal on MRI during remodelling is not a measure of strength, which is the trap in the question. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

Management Overview

State that the modifiable factors are addressed before a revision rather than alongside it: smoking, nutrition, graft choice and the loading plan. 6.6.4 covers prevention.

State that the technical exclusion is repeated rather than assumed, because the commonest reason a knee is labelled biological is an unmeasured tunnel. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

Key Learning Points

  • Give the sequence, then the three groups of factor.
  • Name the weak window, because rehabilitation follows from it.
  • The investigations exclude rather than confirm.
  • Graft signal during remodelling is not a measure of strength.
  • The commonest reason for this label is an unmeasured tunnel.

FRCS Level

Management Overview

At examination level the candidate is given a failure with correct tunnels and no trauma and asked what to do, which is the hardest version of the revision question.

The answer that holds up re-examines the exclusions first, in detail, because the label carries no treatment of its own and an unmeasured secondary restraint or an unrecognised varus is more likely than a biological anomaly. 3.13.8 covers alignment and OA.

It then addresses the modifiable factors explicitly and states them to the patient as conditions of the second operation rather than as advice beside it. 6.6.4 covers prevention.

It then states the uncertainty: a second graft in the same biology may do the same thing, and the consent conversation has to contain that sentence. 4.23.5 covers revision surgery patients.

Complications and Prognosis

The specific risk of this label is that it ends enquiry. A knee called a biological failure is a knee nobody will measure again, and some of those knees have a technical cause nobody found. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

The second risk is that it transfers responsibility to the patient's body, which is comfortable for everyone involved and unfalsifiable. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Controversies and Evidence

The first open question is whether biological failure is a distinct entity or a residual category holding undiagnosed technical causes. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

The second is whether allograft processing explains the higher failure rates reported in young athletes with allografts. 3.7.17 covers hamstring graft biomechanics.

The third is whether the weak window can be identified in an individual well enough to time return to sport by biology rather than by criteria. 6.6.3 covers neuromuscular factors.

This page states the three and settles none, because no source has been checked in this environment. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Key Learning Points

  • Re-examine the exclusions in detail before accepting the label.
  • State the modifiable factors as conditions rather than as advice.
  • The consent conversation has to contain the recurrence sentence.
  • The label ends enquiry, and some of those knees have a technical cause.
  • It transfers responsibility to the patient's body and is unfalsifiable.

Fellowship Level

Complications and Prognosis

At fellowship level the referral letter often arrives with this label already applied, and the first task is to find out who applied it and on what.

A letter saying biological failure with no tunnel measurement in the notes is an opinion rather than an exclusion, and accepting it inherits somebody else's unfinished assessment. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

The habit worth keeping is to record the exclusions individually: tunnel positions measured and by what method, secondary restraints examined, alignment measured, and the mechanism of failure as the patient describes it. Four items, each with its finding rather than a summary word. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

The second habit is to record the graft and its processing in full, including the allograft source and whether it was irradiated, since that is the factor most plausibly carrying the biology and the one most often missing. 3.7.17 covers hamstring graft biomechanics.

The third is to record the time from surgery to failure and whether any trauma preceded it, because early failure without trauma is the only positive signal this category has. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Key Learning Points

  • The label often arrives already applied and without its basis.
  • Biological failure without a tunnel measurement is an opinion.
  • Record each exclusion with its finding rather than a summary word.
  • Record the graft and its processing in full.
  • Early failure without trauma is this category's only positive signal.

Consultant Perspective

Controversies and Evidence

The consultant difficulty is that this is the explanation that blames nobody, which makes it the most comfortable one in the chapter and the one most likely to be reached too early. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

A technical cause implicates a surgeon, a traumatic cause implicates a return to sport decision, and a biological cause implicates nothing, so the incentives all point one way and the evidence does not. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

The five things frame it. The patient's age and physiology, which here carry real weight, since metabolic and healing factors are the proposed mechanism. Their medical comorbidities, including smoking, which is the one modifiable factor with a plausible mechanism and an awkward conversation attached. The demands they put on the knee, which determine whether the weak window was respected. Their psychological state, because a patient told their biology failed hears something about themselves rather than about an operation. And their own ideas, concerns and expectations, which after a second failure usually include a question about whether to stop.

Take all five explicitly rather than by impression, and answer the question about stopping honestly rather than deferring it. 4.23.5 covers revision surgery patients.

Key Learning Points

  • This is the explanation that blames nobody.
  • The incentives point one way and the evidence does not.
  • Smoking is the modifiable factor with a plausible mechanism.
  • A patient told their biology failed hears something about themselves.
  • Answer the question about stopping honestly rather than deferring it.
