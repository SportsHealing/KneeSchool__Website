Infection

Uncommon, time critical, and the one page in the chapter where a delay of days changes the joint rather than the ligament.

Infection

Choose a depth

For Young Learners

Definition

Infection after a knee operation means germs have got into the joint and are growing there. 6.2.5 covers acute haemarthrosis.

It is uncommon, and it is treated urgently when it happens, because an infected joint damages its own smooth surfaces quickly. 6.5.3 covers cartilage degeneration.

How It Presents

The knee becomes more painful rather than less, it swells, it feels hot, and the person can feel unwell or feverish. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

The important pattern is getting worse rather than better after an operation, and that is always a reason to be seen. 6.5.1 covers instability.

Key Learning Points

  • Infection means germs growing inside the joint.
  • It is uncommon after this operation.
  • The knee gets more painful, swollen and hot.
  • The person can feel unwell or feverish.
  • Getting worse rather than better is always a reason to be seen.

If any of this sounds like something happening to you, speak to a parent, teacher, coach or doctor.

For Patients

Definition

Septic arthritis after a reconstruction is a bacterial infection inside the joint itself, rather than a wound infection of the skin. The two are different problems with different urgency. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

It is uncommon. The reason it is given a page is that the consequences depend almost entirely on how quickly it is recognised. 6.5.3 covers cartilage degeneration.

How It Presents

Pain increasing after the first few days rather than settling, a knee that swells again having begun to improve, warmth, difficulty moving it, and feeling systemically unwell. 6.2.5 covers acute haemarthrosis.

A knee that is improving and then deteriorates is the pattern that matters, and it is worth seeking assessment the same day rather than waiting for a scheduled appointment. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Management Overview

Assessment involves blood tests and, usually, drawing fluid from the joint so it can be examined and cultured. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

Treatment is described rather than advised here, and it generally involves washing out the joint in theatre and antibiotics directed by what grows. 4.20.9 covers revision knee surgery.

Keeping the graft is attempted where the infection is controlled, and the graft is removed where it is not. The joint takes priority over the ligament, which is the principle worth understanding. 6.5.3 covers cartilage degeneration.

Key Learning Points

  • Septic arthritis is inside the joint, unlike a wound infection.
  • It is uncommon and its consequences depend on speed of recognition.
  • Improving and then deteriorating is the pattern that matters.
  • Joint fluid is usually examined and cultured.
  • The joint takes priority over the ligament.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. How soon after the operation does it happen? Most reported cases present within the first weeks. A later presentation is described and is less common.

Q. Will I lose the graft? Not necessarily. Retention is attempted when the infection is controlled, and removal becomes necessary when it is not. The decision is made on how the joint responds rather than at the outset.

Q. What is the lasting damage? The concern is the cartilage surface and stiffness, rather than the ligament. That is why speed matters, and it is also why the page exists.

Q. How do I tell this from normal post-operative swelling? By the direction of travel. Normal recovery improves with occasional setbacks. This gets worse, often with warmth and feeling unwell, and that combination is worth assessing the same day.

If your knee cannot take your weight after an injury, if it is hot and swollen with a fever, or if you develop new numbness or coldness in the foot, arrange urgent assessment.

For Medical Students

Definition

Post-operative septic arthritis after ACL reconstruction is an intra-articular infection, distinct from superficial wound infection and from a sterile inflammatory effusion. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

Coagulase negative staphylococci and Staphylococcus aureus are the organisms most often reported, and graft contamination and skin flora are the proposed routes. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

How It Presents

Increasing pain, a recurrent or increasing effusion, warmth, restricted movement and systemic symptoms, typically within the first weeks. 6.2.5 covers acute haemarthrosis.

The differential includes a sterile reactive effusion, a haemarthrosis and mechanical pain from the graft or hardware, and the distinguishing feature is the trajectory rather than any single sign. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

A low grade presentation with pain and stiffness but little systemic upset is described and is the one that is missed. 6.8.3 covers biological failure.

Investigations and Imaging

Inflammatory markers are obtained and their trend matters more than a single value. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

Joint aspiration for cell count, Gram stain and culture is the diagnostic step, and culture before antibiotics is the principle that protects the diagnosis. 6.2.5 covers acute haemarthrosis.

Imaging is not the diagnostic test. MRI may show synovitis and bone changes and cannot exclude the diagnosis. 5.7.14 covers revision ACL imaging.

Management Overview

Arthroscopic lavage and debridement with organism directed antibiotics is the described approach, with repeat lavage where the response is inadequate. 4.20.9 covers revision knee surgery.

Graft retention is attempted where the infection responds, and graft removal follows persistent infection, hardware involvement or a destructive course. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Key Learning Points

  • It is intra-articular, unlike a wound infection.
  • Staphylococcal organisms predominate in the reported series.
  • Trajectory distinguishes it rather than any single sign.
  • Aspirate and culture before antibiotics.
  • Imaging cannot exclude the diagnosis.

MRCS Level

Investigations and Imaging

In a viva the expected answer aspirates before treating and says why.

Say inflammatory markers with their trend, aspiration for cell count, Gram stain and culture, and antibiotics after the sample rather than before it. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

Say that imaging does not make or exclude this diagnosis, which is the trap when a scan is offered in the stem. 5.7.14 covers revision ACL imaging.

Management Overview

State urgent arthroscopic lavage and debridement, organism directed antibiotics with microbiology involvement, and a low threshold for repeat lavage. 4.20.9 covers revision knee surgery.

State the graft retention principle and its limit: retain while the infection is controlled, remove when it is not. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

State that the priority is the articular surface rather than the ligament, since the ligament can be reconstructed later and the surface cannot be replaced. 6.5.3 covers cartilage degeneration.

Complications and Prognosis

The complications are cartilage damage, arthrofibrosis and loss of the graft, roughly in that order of lasting consequence. 6.5.4 covers osteoarthritis.

Delay is the variable that drives all three, which is why this page's whole content is about recognition. 6.8.3 covers biological failure.

Key Learning Points

  • Aspirate and culture before antibiotics.
  • Imaging neither makes nor excludes the diagnosis.
  • Urgent lavage, directed antibiotics, low threshold for repeating.
  • Retain the graft while infection is controlled, remove it when not.
  • Cartilage damage and stiffness outlast the loss of a graft.

FRCS Level

Management Overview

At examination level the candidate is given a swollen, painful knee three weeks after reconstruction and is marked on urgency and on sequence.

The defensible sequence is examine, bloods, aspirate, then theatre, with microbiology engaged early and antibiotics held until the sample is taken unless the patient is septic. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

The candidate who starts antibiotics before the aspirate has made the next four weeks harder for everyone, and the examination is testing exactly that. 6.2.5 covers acute haemarthrosis.

The graft decision is then made on response rather than at the first operation, and saying so is better than committing early in either direction. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Complications and Prognosis

The prognosis is driven by time to lavage, and the surface damage that occurs before it is irreversible. 6.5.3 covers cartilage degeneration.

The honest counselling separates three outcomes: the infection, which usually resolves; the stiffness, which often needs its own treatment; and the joint surface, which does not recover. 6.5.4 covers osteoarthritis.

Controversies and Evidence

The first open question is when graft retention stops being appropriate, which is decided clinically and supported by small series. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

The second is whether graft soaking or other intra-operative measures reduce the incidence enough to adopt routinely. 6.8.3 covers biological failure.

The third is whether the low grade presentation is under-diagnosed and contributing to cases labelled arthrofibrosis or biological failure. 6.8.2 covers tunnel malposition.

This page states the three and settles none, because no source has been checked in this environment. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

Key Learning Points

  • Examine, bloods, aspirate, theatre, with microbiology engaged early.
  • Starting antibiotics before the aspirate makes everything harder.
  • Decide the graft on response rather than at the first operation.
  • Prognosis is driven by time to lavage.
  • The infection resolves, the stiffness needs treating, the surface does not recover.

Fellowship Level

Complications and Prognosis

At fellowship level the referrals are the late ones: a stiff, sore knee months after a reconstruction with an unremarkable record and nobody certain whether it was ever infected.

Without a cultured aspirate from the acute episode the question cannot be settled afterwards, because markers normalise and imaging shows a stiff joint rather than a cause. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

The habit worth keeping is to record the acute episode fully when it happens: the day of onset, the day of aspiration, the cell count, the organism and its sensitivities, the day of lavage, the number of lavages, and whether the graft was retained. 4.20.9 covers revision knee surgery.

The second habit is to record range of movement at each review afterwards, since arthrofibrosis is the complication that develops quietly and is the one most amenable to early action. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

The third is to record the articular surface at every subsequent arthroscopy, because the lasting consequence of this page is cartilage and nobody tracks it. 6.5.3 covers cartilage degeneration.

Key Learning Points

  • The late referral cannot be settled without the acute aspirate.
  • Markers normalise and imaging shows the result rather than the cause.
  • Record onset, aspiration, organism, lavages and graft retention.
  • Record range of movement at every review afterwards.
  • Record the articular surface at every subsequent arthroscopy.

Consultant Perspective

Controversies and Evidence

The consultant difficulty is that this diagnosis is uncommon enough to be unfamiliar and urgent enough that unfamiliarity costs the joint. 6.5.3 covers cartilage degeneration.

The practical consequence is that the threshold for aspirating a deteriorating post-operative knee has to be set low in advance, as a standing position rather than a judgement made at the time by whoever is on call. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

The five things frame it. The patient's age and physiology, which bear on immunity and on how much cartilage they can afford to lose. Their medical comorbidities, diabetes and immunosuppression among them, which change both risk and presentation. The demands they put on the knee, which are irrelevant this week and decisive afterwards. Their psychological state, since a patient who has been reassured once is slower to return a second time. And their own ideas, concerns and expectations, which at this point include a reasonable question about whether the operation was worth having.

Take all five explicitly rather than by impression, and make the aspiration threshold a policy rather than a decision. 6.8.1 covers graft failure.

Key Learning Points

  • Uncommon enough to be unfamiliar and urgent enough that unfamiliarity costs.
  • Set the aspiration threshold low in advance, as a standing position.
  • Comorbidities change both the risk and the presentation.
  • A patient reassured once is slower to return a second time.
  • Make the aspiration threshold a policy rather than a decision.
