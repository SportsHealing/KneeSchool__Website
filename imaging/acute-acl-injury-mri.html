Acute ACL Injury

Page 5.7.4 · ACL MRI · imaging page

The acutely injured knee, where the study is hardest to read and asked for soonest.

Acute ACL Injury

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

An acute cruciate injury is imaged within days to weeks of the event, when the knee is full of blood and the tissues are oedematous. 6.2.5 covers the acute haemarthrosis.

The study shows the ligament, the impaction marks of the mechanism, and the injuries that happened at the same moment. 3.7.14 covers the injury mechanisms.

It matters because this is the one timepoint at which the secondary signs are at their most informative and the ligament at its least legible. 5.7.3 covers the complete tears.

How It Is Acquired

Use a fluid sensitive sequence in all three planes, because oedema is the signal that carries most of this study. 5.2.16 covers fat suppression.

Read the marrow signal first in an equivocal study, since it is present early and resolves later. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Search the posteromedial meniscocapsular junction, the meniscal roots, the collaterals and the anterolateral structures deliberately. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

Assess the cartilage surfaces, because an impaction injury damages them at the same instant. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

Note that a large effusion displaces structures and changes their apparent position, which affects the translation measurement. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

What Abnormal Looks Like

A discontinuous or absent cruciate ligament with surrounding oedema that blurs its margins.

Marrow signal in the lateral femoral condyle and the posterolateral tibia, the pair that records the pivoting impaction. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

A tense effusion with layering, which is blood rather than a simple effusion.

Oedema tracking through the posteromedial or anterolateral soft tissues, pointing at the structures injured alongside. 5.9.2 covers medial collateral sprains.

Key Learning Points

  • The acute study is read while the knee is full of blood.
  • Oedema carries most of the information and obscures the ligament.
  • Marrow signal is present early and resolves later.
  • The associated injuries are searched for rather than noticed.
  • A tense effusion changes the apparent position of structures.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the study where the expected answer names the timing before anything else.

The expected points are the fluid sensitive sequences, the marrow signal, the deliberate search, the cartilage, and the effect of the effusion.

Say that the marrow signal is the sign you would use when the ligament is equivocal. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Say that the bone bruise resolves, so an early study carries information a late one does not. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow oedema.

Say that you would report the associated injuries as the part that changes the operation. 5.6.9 covers root tears.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the injury is a single mechanical event, so the findings are a pattern rather than a list and each one points at the others. 3.7.14 covers the injury mechanisms.

The lateral femoral condyle impacting the posterolateral tibia produces the marrow signal pair, and the same translation stresses the posteromedial structures. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

Blood in the joint raises the intra-articular pressure and distends the capsule, which is why the apparent translation can exceed the ligamentous laxity. 3.7.8 covers anterior stability.

Cartilage injured at the moment of impaction is the finding with the longest consequence and the least conspicuous appearance. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

How Good It Is

The acute study is the most informative cruciate study and the hardest to read.

Its best contribution is the marrow signal, which is present only early and supports the diagnosis when the ligament does not. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

It is limited by oedema obscuring the ligament margins, by the effusion distorting positions, and by the associated injuries being hardest to see in exactly this setting. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and the comparison is the claim: the acute study carries more information and is read with less certainty than a later one.

Key Learning Points

  • The findings are a mechanical pattern rather than a list.
  • The marrow signal pair records the impaction that stressed the corners.
  • Blood in the joint can make the apparent translation exceed the laxity.
  • Cartilage injury is the least conspicuous finding with the longest consequence.
  • The study carries most information and is read with least certainty.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the acute study is read for the things that will not be visible later and for the things that change the operation.

A ramp lesion or a root tear found acutely is repairable, and the same lesion found late often is not, which makes the timing of the study a decision in itself. 5.6.13 covers meniscal repair imaging.

Anterolateral injury found acutely contributes to the argument for an extra-articular augmentation. 2.11.3 covers the anterolateral ligament.

A displaced meniscal fragment makes the operation urgent rather than elective. 5.6.6 covers bucket handle tears.

Cartilage injury found acutely changes the prognosis given to the patient more than it changes the operation. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is calling the ligament indeterminate because of oedema and not using the secondary signs. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

The posteromedial corner is not inspected in the one study where the lesion is repairable. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

Oedema around a collateral is overcalled as a high grade injury. 5.9.2 covers medial collateral sprains.

Apparent translation measured in a tense effusion is reported as laxity. 3.7.8 covers anterior stability.

The study is delayed until the swelling settles, which loses the marrow signal that would have supported the diagnosis. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow oedema.

Key Learning Points

  • Oedema is used as a reason to call the ligament indeterminate.
  • The posteromedial corner goes uninspected while the lesion is repairable.
  • Collateral oedema is overcalled as a high grade injury.
  • Translation in a tense effusion is reported as laxity.
  • Delaying the study loses the marrow signal.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the acute study is where the imaging pathway and the surgical decision are least aligned.

The findings that are repairable only acutely are the findings detected worst acutely, which is the central problem of this page and is not solved by reading harder. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

Requesting the study early gives the marrow signal and the repairable window, and requesting it late gives a clearer ligament nobody needed, so the request timing matters more than the protocol. 5.1.4 covers imaging pathways.

The bone bruise's relationship to later cartilage loss is an association without an established mechanism of action, and it is reported as though it carried a prognosis. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Translation measured on a supine study in a distended joint is not the quantity the examination measures, and the two are compared as though they were. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

A structured acute report would name the corners, the roots and the anterolateral structures as mandatory fields, and most are free text that omits them. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Key Learning Points

  • The findings repairable only acutely are detected worst acutely.
  • Request timing matters more than the protocol here.
  • The bone bruise is reported as carrying a prognosis it has not established.
  • Supine translation and examined laxity are different quantities.
  • A structured report would make the corners and roots mandatory fields.
