Graft Maturation

Page 5.7.10 · ACL MRI · imaging page

The graft's signal over time, which is a biological process read with a tool that measures water.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Graft Maturation

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

A graft is transplanted tissue that dies, is repopulated by host cells, and is remodelled into something resembling a ligament. 2.7.3 covers the histology.

That process changes the graft's water content, so its signal rises and then falls over months to years. 5.2.14 covers T2 imaging.

It matters because a rising graft signal in the first year is the expected course and is routinely reported as a tear. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

How It Is Acquired

Establish the time since surgery, because the appearance is only interpretable against it. 5.17.1 covers post-operative cruciate reconstruction.

Assess continuity first, since a continuous graft with high signal is a different thing from a discontinuous one. 5.7.9 covers cruciate reconstruction.

Compare with the earliest post-operative study, which turns the appearance into a trend. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

Use consistent sequences between studies, because graft signal cannot be compared across different contrast. 5.2.15 covers proton density imaging.

Note the graft type, since a bone containing graft incorporates differently from a soft tissue one. 3.7.18 covers patellar tendon graft biomechanics.

What Normal Looks Like

A low signal graft in the first weeks, before the revascularisation has progressed.

Rising signal through the graft over the following months, which is the remodelling phase.

Falling signal towards a low signal band over the following years, as the collagen reorganises.

Continuity and a normal line throughout, which is what makes the signal change benign. 2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

Key Learning Points

  • A graft dies, is repopulated and is remodelled.
  • The process changes water content, so signal rises and then falls.
  • Rising signal in the first year is expected.
  • The appearance is only interpretable against the time since surgery.
  • Continuity and line are what make the signal change benign.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names the time since surgery before describing any appearance.

The expected points are the date of surgery, continuity, the comparison study, consistent sequences, and the graft type.

Say that high graft signal in the first year is expected rather than abnormal. 5.7.9 covers cruciate reconstruction.

Say that continuity and line are what you would report. 2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

Say that signal cannot be compared across different sequences. 5.2.15 covers proton density imaging.

What Normal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the graft is avascular at implantation and is revascularised from the surrounding tissue, so the water content peaks when the cellular activity does. 2.7.4 covers the blood supply.

Collagen that is being remodelled is less organised than collagen that is finished, and less organised collagen holds more water and so more signal. 2.7.3 covers the histology.

That gives a predictable sequence rather than a predictable appearance at any one time, which is why the trend carries the information. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

A bone containing graft heals bone to bone at the tunnels and so incorporates faster at the ends, which changes where the early signal sits. 3.7.18 covers patellar tendon graft biomechanics.

How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging tracks graft signal reliably and interprets it unreliably.

Its best contribution is the serial trend, because a direction of change is interpretable where a single value is not. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

It is limited by signal depending on sequence and on time, and by the absence of any established relationship between signal and graft strength. 5.21.2 covers the cruciate imaging literature.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no signal intensity ratio appears either: those ratios are published quantities tied to specific sequences and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • The graft is avascular at implantation and revascularised from around it.
  • Remodelling collagen is less organised, holds more water and carries more signal.
  • The sequence of change is predictable where any single appearance is not.
  • A bone containing graft incorporates faster at the ends.
  • No signal to strength relationship is established.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level graft signal rarely changes a decision, and the main clinical act is declining to over-read it.

A continuous graft with high signal in a stable knee at nine months is a normal reconstruction, and saying so prevents an unnecessary investigation. 4.10.11 covers the post-reconstruction assessment.

A graft whose signal is rising at three years rather than falling is outside the expected course and is worth following. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

Return to sport decisions are made on function and time, and graft signal has been proposed as a contributor without being established as one. 5.21.2 covers the cruciate imaging literature.

A discontinuous graft is a different page, and separating the two questions is the useful discipline here. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting expected remodelling signal as a partial graft tear. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

The date of surgery is absent, so the appearance is read against no timeline. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

Signal is compared between studies acquired on different sequences. 5.2.15 covers proton density imaging.

Graft signal is used to clear a patient for sport, which it has not been shown to support. 4.10.11 covers the post-reconstruction assessment.

Fixation artefact at the ends is read as part of the graft's signal. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

Key Learning Points

  • Expected remodelling signal is reported as a partial graft tear.
  • Without the date of surgery there is no timeline to read against.
  • Signal compared across different sequences is not a comparison.
  • Graft signal has not been shown to support a return to sport decision.
  • Fixation artefact is read as graft signal.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level graft maturation is a well described biological process attached to a poorly performing imaging surrogate.

Signal intensity ratios are sequence dependent, scanner dependent and inconsistently defined, so figures from one centre do not transfer to another. 5.21.2 covers the cruciate imaging literature.

The quantity of interest is mechanical strength, and water content is at best indirectly related to it, which is the structural weakness of the whole approach. 3.7.16 covers the graft biomechanics.

Quantitative methods aimed at the composition have not displaced the problem, because a stiffer graft and a wetter graft are not the same measurement. 5.18.2 covers T2 mapping.

Ultra short echo time sequences image the organised collagen more directly and are not in routine knee practice. 5.18.4 covers ultra short echo time imaging.

Return to sport is where the pressure to use this comes from, and using an unvalidated surrogate for that decision risks the one outcome the patient came for. 5.7.11 covers graft failure.

Key Learning Points

  • Signal intensity ratios do not transfer between centres.
  • Water content is at best indirectly related to mechanical strength.
  • Quantitative composition methods do not measure stiffness.
  • Ultra short echo time imaging is more direct and is not routine.
  • An unvalidated surrogate is being pushed towards a return to sport decision.
