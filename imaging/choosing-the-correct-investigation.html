Choosing the Correct Investigation

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Each modality shows a different thing well, so the choice is made from what the question is rather than from what is available. 5.1.1 covers why imaging matters.

Radiographs show bone, alignment and joint space. Magnetic resonance imaging shows soft tissue, marrow and cartilage. Ultrasound shows tendon and moving structures. Computed tomography shows bone detail and metalwork. 5.3.1 covers the AP view.

The modality that shows everything does not exist, which is why a scan chosen without a question answers none. 5.1.5 covers cost effectiveness.

How It Is Acquired

Start with the clinical question in one sentence, naming the structure and what the answer would change. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Choose radiographs first in a knee where alignment, joint space or a fracture is the question, and weightbearing where alignment matters. 5.3.6 covers long leg alignment films.

Choose magnetic resonance imaging where a soft tissue structure is the question, and say which one, because the protocol changes. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Choose ultrasound where a tendon, a bursa or a dynamic event is the question, and where an intervention may follow in the same visit. 5.4.15 covers dynamic instability.

Choose computed tomography where bone detail, metalwork or a three dimensional plan is the question. 5.16.1 covers fracture assessment.

Record what you would do if the result were normal, because that answer decides whether to request at all.

Key Learning Points

  • Each modality shows a different thing well.
  • The modality that shows everything does not exist.
  • The clinical question comes before the choice.
  • Weightbearing matters when alignment is the question.
  • What you would do with a normal result decides whether to request.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the request station and the expected answer gives a question, a modality and a reason.

The expected points are the clinical question, the first line modality, the protocol modifiers, the timing, and what a normal result would change.

Say that a radiograph precedes cross sectional imaging in almost every knee, because it is quick and it answers the bone and alignment questions. 5.3.1 covers the AP view.

Say that you would state the suspected structure on a magnetic resonance request so the protocol can be chosen. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Say that previous surgery and metalwork change both the modality and the protocol. 5.5.3 covers the post operative protocol.

How Good It Is

The choice matters more than the quality of the study, because the right question on an adequate scan beats the wrong question on an excellent one.

Its best contribution is excluding the modality that cannot answer the question, which is a negative decision and the one most often skipped. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

It is undermined by availability, since the scan that can be obtained this week is often requested instead of the scan that answers the question.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 028, and the comparison is the claim being made.

Key Learning Points

  • The right question on an adequate scan beats the wrong question on a good one.
  • A radiograph precedes cross sectional imaging in almost every knee.
  • The suspected structure belongs on the request so the protocol can follow.
  • Previous surgery changes both the modality and the protocol.
  • Availability competes with correctness and often wins.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the investigation is chosen against the decision it serves, not against the diagnosis.

A knee heading for alignment correction needs a weightbearing long leg film whatever its magnetic resonance findings show, because the geometry is the decision. 5.3.6 covers long leg alignment films.

A knee heading for a soft tissue reconstruction needs the structure imaged in the plane that shows it, which is a protocol question rather than a modality one. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

A knee with metalwork needs computed tomography for the bone and may need nothing else, because magnetic resonance will be degraded where it matters. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

A knee where the decision is whether to operate at all may need no imaging, which is the hardest version of this choice. 5.1.5 covers cost effectiveness.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is requesting magnetic resonance imaging as a default rather than as an answer to a question. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

A radiograph is omitted because cross sectional imaging feels more thorough, which loses the alignment and the joint space. 5.3.1 covers the AP view.

Weightbearing is not specified, so the alignment question is answered on a supine film that cannot answer it. 5.3.6 covers long leg alignment films.

The protocol is left to the department, which is reasonable when the question is stated and harmful when it is not. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Ultrasound is omitted for a dynamic question that only ultrasound answers. 5.4.15 covers dynamic instability.

Key Learning Points

  • Magnetic resonance imaging as a default is the commonest error.
  • Omitting the radiograph loses the alignment and the joint space.
  • An unspecified weightbearing status answers the alignment question wrongly.
  • Leaving the protocol to the department is safe only with a stated question.
  • A dynamic question has one modality that answers it.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the choice is usually constrained before it is made, and the constraints are not clinical.

The study already exists, so the real choice is whether to repeat it, and that is decided against what it could not answer rather than against its age. 5.1.5 covers cost effectiveness.

Protocols differ between departments, so a study that answers the question in one centre does not in another, and the difference is invisible on the report. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

The modality that answers the question may not be available, and substituting the available one silently changes what the plan is built on.

Advanced techniques answer questions the standard protocols cannot and are unavailable in most places, which makes the literature's answers unreachable in practice. 5.18.1 covers quantitative imaging.

The choice is made by the requester and the quality by the department, so responsibility for the answer is split and neither half sees the whole. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Key Learning Points

  • The real choice is usually whether to repeat an existing study.
  • Protocol differences between departments are invisible on the report.
  • Substituting an available modality silently changes the plan's basis.
  • Advanced techniques answer questions that are unreachable in most places.
  • Responsibility for the answer is split between requester and department.
