Osteophytes

Page 5.3.18 · Plain Radiography · imaging page

New bone at the joint margin, which is the earliest radiographic sign and the least mechanical one.

Osteophytes

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

An osteophyte is new bone formed at the margin of a joint, visible as a bony projection or lipping. 5.3.1 covers the anteroposterior view.

It is the earliest feature of radiographic osteoarthritis and the one the grading system weights first. 5.3.16 covers Kellgren-Lawrence grading.

It matters because osteophyte presence drives the radiographic diagnosis while joint space loss drives the mechanical consequence, and the two do not arrive together. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

How It Is Acquired

Look at the compartment margins on the standing anteroposterior view. 5.3.1 covers the anteroposterior view.

Look at the patellar margins on the lateral and skyline views, since patellofemoral osteophyte is easily missed. 5.3.3 covers the skyline view.

Look in the intercondylar notch, where osteophyte limits extension and is foreshortened on the standard views. 5.3.4 covers the tunnel view.

Distinguish an osteophyte from an enthesophyte at a tendon attachment, which has a different location and meaning. 5.5.10 covers the tendons.

Note that osteophyte is the feature most reliably agreed between readers. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

What Abnormal Looks Like

Marginal lipping at the compartment edges, which is the early appearance.

Discrete bony projections at the margins in established disease.

Notch osteophyte narrowing the intercondylar space. 5.3.4 covers the tunnel view.

Patellar osteophyte at the superior and inferior poles. 5.12.7 covers patellofemoral arthritis.

Key Learning Points

  • An osteophyte is new bone at the joint margin.
  • It is the earliest radiographic feature and is weighted first in grading.
  • Osteophyte and joint space loss do not arrive together.
  • Notch osteophyte is foreshortened on the standard views.
  • Osteophyte is the feature readers agree on best.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer separates the diagnostic value of osteophyte from its mechanical value.

The expected points are the compartment margins, the patellar margins, the notch, the enthesophyte distinction, and the reader agreement.

Say that osteophyte makes the radiographic diagnosis and does not explain the symptoms. 5.1.2 covers clinical examination versus imaging.

Say that notch osteophyte can mechanically limit extension. 4.7.8 covers the extension lag.

Say that osteophyte corrupts the tibial landmark for extrusion measurement. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that osteophyte is a bone response to altered joint mechanics, so it indicates that the joint has changed rather than how much cartilage is left. 3.13.8 covers alignment and osteoarthritis.

That is why it appears early and why its size does not track the compartment's state. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

A notch osteophyte is a mechanical block, so it is the one osteophyte whose size has a direct functional consequence. 4.7.5 covers the end feel.

Osteophyte at the tibial margin also changes the landmark meniscal extrusion is measured from, which makes it a measurement problem elsewhere in this section. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

How Good It Is

Osteophyte is the most reliably detected radiographic feature of osteoarthritis and the least informative about the compartment.

Its best contribution is diagnostic: its presence establishes that the joint has osteoarthritic change. 5.3.16 covers Kellgren-Lawrence grading.

It is limited as a severity measure, since size and compartment state are only loosely related. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no osteophyte size criteria appear either: the grading thresholds are published quantities and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Osteophyte is a bone response to altered joint mechanics.
  • It indicates that the joint has changed, not how much cartilage is left.
  • Its size does not track the compartment's state.
  • A notch osteophyte is the one with a direct functional consequence.
  • Tibial osteophyte corrupts the extrusion landmark.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level osteophyte matters in three specific ways and not as a severity measure.

A notch osteophyte limiting extension has a surgical answer that is independent of the compartment's state. 4.7.8 covers the extension lag.

Osteophyte at the compartment margin is part of the radiographic diagnosis a patient is given, which affects the conversation. 5.20.6 covers reporting osteoarthritis.

Tibial osteophyte makes extrusion measurement unreliable in exactly the knees where extrusion matters. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

Osteophyte in a knee with preserved joint space is not an indication for anything, which is worth saying to the patient explicitly. 5.1.2 covers clinical examination versus imaging.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is grading severity by osteophyte size. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

Notch osteophyte is missed because no tunnel view was obtained. 5.3.4 covers the tunnel view.

An enthesophyte is reported as an osteophyte. 5.5.10 covers the tendons.

Osteophyte is included in the tibial margin when measuring extrusion. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

Osteophyte with preserved space is reported in a way that suggests a mechanical problem. 5.20.6 covers reporting osteoarthritis.

Key Learning Points

  • Severity is graded by osteophyte size.
  • Notch osteophyte is missed without a tunnel view.
  • An enthesophyte is reported as an osteophyte.
  • Osteophyte is included in the extrusion landmark.
  • Osteophyte with preserved space is reported as a mechanical problem.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level osteophyte is the feature that makes the grading system diagnose osteoarthritis earliest and predict its course worst.

Because the scale weights osteophyte early, a knee enters the diagnosis on a feature with no mechanical consequence, which is part of why radiographic grade and symptoms separate. 5.3.16 covers Kellgren-Lawrence grading.

Osteophyte formation may be an adaptive response that stabilises a joint, which would make its presence a mixed signal rather than a measure of damage. 3.13.8 covers alignment and osteoarthritis.

Magnetic resonance imaging detects osteophyte earlier and more often than radiography, which lowers the threshold for the diagnosis without changing what it means. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Its interference with the extrusion landmark is a concrete measurement problem that has no agreed solution, and it affects the one mechanical marker this section argues for. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

The useful stance is to report osteophyte as a diagnostic feature and to carry the severity in the compartment description. 5.20.6 covers reporting osteoarthritis.

Key Learning Points

  • The scale weights the feature with no mechanical consequence earliest.
  • Osteophyte may be adaptive, making its presence a mixed signal.
  • Magnetic resonance imaging detects it earlier without changing its meaning.
  • Its interference with the extrusion landmark has no agreed solution.
  • Report it as diagnostic and carry severity in the compartment description.
