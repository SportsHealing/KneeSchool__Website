Dynamic MRI

Page 5.18.6 · Advanced Imaging · imaging page

Imaging through movement, which records a slow deliberate motion rather than the fast one that fails.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

The same topic at 4 depths

Each tier deepens the one below it and never repeats it. Choose the level you want.

Dynamic MRI

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Dynamic magnetic resonance imaging acquires a series of images while the knee moves. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

The movement is slow and often repeated, and the images are built across it. 5.2.12 covers the basic principles.

It matters because the failures that bring patients to clinic happen during movement, and a static study records a posture. 3.12.1 covers patellar tracking.

How It Is Acquired

Use a fast sequence and accept the resolution that the speed costs. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

Ask for a slow controlled movement through the available range. 3.3.8 covers the changes throughout flexion.

Repeat the movement where the acquisition is built across cycles. 5.2.19 covers the artefacts.

Record whether the movement was loaded or unloaded. 5.18.5 covers weight- bearing MRI.

Record the range covered, because it is narrower than the clinical one. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

What Normal Looks Like

A patella engaging in the groove early in flexion. 3.12.1 covers patellar tracking.

Smooth tracking with no lateral deviation. 4.15.6 covers dynamic tracking.

Menisci moving with the tibia through the range. 3.6.1 covers the functions of the meniscus.

Tibiofemoral contact moving posteriorly with flexion. 3.3.2 covers the roll and glide mechanisms.

What Abnormal Looks Like

A lateral deviation of the patella early in flexion. 5.12.3 covers patellar tilt.

Delayed engagement in a dysplastic groove. 5.12.1 covers trochlear dysplasia.

Abnormal meniscal movement alongside a root tear. 5.6.9 covers root tears.

A movement so slow that the abnormality never appears. 4.15.2 covers the J sign.

Key Learning Points

  • Images are acquired through a slow repeated movement.
  • Speed costs resolution.
  • The range covered is narrower than the clinical one.
  • The clinical failure is faster than the acquisition.
  • Whether the movement was loaded has to be recorded.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names the trade between speed and resolution.

The expected points are the fast sequence, the slow movement, the repeated cycles, the loading, and the range covered.

Say that resolution and speed trade directly. 5.2.18 covers the gradient echo imaging.

Say that the imaged movement is not the symptomatic one. 4.15.6 covers dynamic tracking.

Say that the examination observes the real movement and the scan does not. 4.15.2 covers the J sign.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that patellar instability occurs rapidly near extension under load, while a dynamic study is slow, often unloaded and limited in range, so the mechanism it images is a different one. 5.12.4 covers patellar subluxation.

Ultrasound observes the same movement in real time through a limited view, so the two techniques fail in opposite ways. 5.4.14 covers patellar tracking.

Building an acquisition across repeated cycles assumes the cycles are identical, which a symptomatic knee does not guarantee. 5.2.19 covers the artefacts.

A normal dynamic study therefore excludes very little, which is the conclusion the static patellofemoral pages reach as well. 5.12.4 covers patellar subluxation.

How Good It Is

Dynamic magnetic resonance imaging is the only cross sectional technique that images the knee moving, and it images a movement the patient does not complain about.

Its best contribution is tracking through early flexion, where the groove engages. 3.12.1 covers patellar tracking.

It is limited by resolution, by the slow movement, by the narrow range, by the usual absence of load, and by availability. 5.19.9 covers the clinical translation.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no frame rates, resolutions or angular ranges appear either: those are protocol quantities tied to particular implementations and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Resolution and speed trade directly.
  • The imaged mechanism is not the symptomatic one.
  • Ultrasound and this technique fail in opposite ways.
  • Repeated cycle acquisition assumes identical cycles.
  • A normal study excludes very little.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level this rarely changes a plan and occasionally settles a disagreement between examination and static imaging. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

A patient with instability and normal static measurements is the case for it. 5.16.4 covers the TT-TG measurement.

The apprehension test and the observed J sign still carry more information about the behaviour. 4.15.3 covers the patellar apprehension test.

Combining it with load would address both mismatches at once and is not routinely available. 5.18.5 covers weight-bearing MRI.

A normal study should be reported as not excluding the diagnosis. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reading a normal dynamic study as excluding instability. 5.12.4 covers patellar subluxation.

Low resolution is read as a normal appearance. 5.2.19 covers the artefacts.

An unloaded movement is reported as tracking. 5.18.5 covers weight-bearing MRI.

A narrow range is reported as the range. 3.3.8 covers the changes throughout flexion.

A movement the patient could perform is reported as the movement that fails. 4.15.6 covers dynamic tracking.

Key Learning Points

  • A normal study does not exclude instability.
  • Low resolution is read as normal.
  • Unloaded movement is reported as tracking.
  • A narrow range is reported as the range.
  • The performed movement is not the failing one.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the study arrives having been requested to settle an argument it cannot settle. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

Implementations differ in speed, range and loading, so studies from two centres are not comparable. 5.18.1 covers the quantitative MRI.

A patient who guards during the acquisition produces a normal study for that reason alone. 4.15.3 covers the patellar apprehension test.

The alternative is cheaper and already available: observe the knee and record what you see. 4.1.8 covers the examination documentation.

Automated analysis of these series is being developed on movements that are already unrepresentative. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

The honest position is that this technique addresses a real gap and that the examination still addresses it better. 4.15.2 covers the J sign.

Key Learning Points

  • The study is asked to settle what it cannot.
  • Implementations are not comparable.
  • Guarding during the acquisition produces a normal study.
  • The examination is cheaper and already available.
  • Automation is built on unrepresentative movements.
