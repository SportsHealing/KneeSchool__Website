Radiation Dose

Page 5.2.24 · Imaging Physics for Knee Specialists · imaging page

The dose a computed tomography study carries, which is the one that dominates a knee patient's total.

Radiation Dose

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Computed tomography deposits far more energy in tissue than a radiograph of the same region, because it measures transmission from many directions. 5.2.22 covers the computed tomography acquisition.

The chapter carries two pages with this title, one for radiography and this one for computed tomography, because the source document separates the physics that way. 5.2.3 covers the radiographic dose.

It matters because almost all the ionising dose in a knee pathway comes from computed tomography rather than from radiographs. 5.1.4 covers imaging pathways.

How It Is Acquired

Dose rises with tube current and falls with faster rotation and higher pitch, and all three are protocol settings. 5.2.22 covers the computed tomography acquisition.

Dose rises with the scanned length, so collimating to the joint rather than the limb matters. 5.16.1 covers the fracture assessment.

Iterative reconstruction allows a lower dose acquisition for the same noise, which is where most modern reduction has come from. 5.2.23 covers the reconstruction.

Automatic exposure modulation adjusts the current through the scan, which helps in a limb of changing thickness. 5.2.22 covers the computed tomography acquisition.

A knee is peripheral and away from radiosensitive organs, which lowers the consequence without lowering the deposited energy. 5.2.3 covers the radiographic dose.

Key Learning Points

  • Computed tomography deposits far more energy than a radiograph.
  • Nearly all ionising dose in a knee pathway comes from computed tomography.
  • Dose rises with current and scanned length.
  • Iterative reconstruction has delivered most modern dose reduction.
  • A peripheral joint has lower consequence for the same deposited energy.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer gives the relative scale between the modalities and names what can be adjusted.

The expected points are the current and pitch, the scanned length, iterative reconstruction, exposure modulation, and the peripheral site.

Say that this is the dominant dose in a knee pathway. 5.1.4 covers imaging pathways.

Say that a magnetic resonance study is the zero dose alternative where it answers the question. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Say that repeated studies in a young patient accumulate. 5.15.1 covers the normal paediatric anatomy.

How Good It Is

Computed tomography buys bone geometry with dose, and the exchange is favourable for a geometric question and poor for anything else.

Its best contribution is that the questions it answers are answered quickly and definitively, so one study replaces a pathway. 5.16.2 covers tibial plateau fractures.

It is limited by the dose being real and cumulative, and by it being requested for questions magnetic resonance imaging answers without dose. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no dose values appear either, in millisieverts or in milligray centimetres: those are published quantities that differ by scanner and protocol and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Bone geometry is bought with dose.
  • The exchange is favourable for a geometric question only.
  • One definitive study can replace a pathway.
  • The dose is real and cumulative.
  • It is requested for questions magnetic resonance imaging answers without dose.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level this page is about which patients accumulate dose and who is counting.

A young patient through a staged revision may have several computed tomography studies, and nobody adds them up. 5.7.14 covers revision cruciate imaging.

A deformity correction with pre-operative and post-operative computed tomography plus serial radiographs is a real total in a growing patient. 5.16.5 covers osteotomy planning.

A repeat study for a question answerable by reprocessing the stored volume is avoidable dose. 5.2.23 covers the reconstruction.

Weight bearing computed tomography is becoming available, and it competes with a radiograph rather than with a magnetic resonance study, which changes the dose comparison. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is requesting computed tomography for soft tissue because it is quicker to obtain. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Cumulative dose is not tracked across a pathway. 5.1.4 covers imaging pathways.

A repeat is ordered rather than a reprocessing. 5.2.23 covers the reconstruction.

A paediatric study is requested on adult reasoning. 5.15.1 covers the normal paediatric anatomy.

Dose figures are quoted from memory, which is why none appears here. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

Key Learning Points

  • Computed tomography is requested for soft tissue because it is quicker.
  • Cumulative dose is not tracked across a pathway.
  • A repeat is ordered where reprocessing would do.
  • A paediatric study is requested on adult reasoning.
  • A remembered dose figure is not a measurement.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level dose is the clearest example of a cost that nobody in a knee pathway owns.

The surgeon requests, the radiology department delivers, and no part of the pathway holds a running total for the patient. 5.1.4 covers imaging pathways.

Dose reduction has improved substantially and unevenly, so the same request at two hospitals delivers different exposure for the same answer, invisibly. 5.2.23 covers the reconstruction.

Weight bearing computed tomography will increase the number of knee studies because it answers a question nothing else does, which makes the dose question more pressing rather than less. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

The risk estimates at these exposures rest on a contested model, which argues for treating dose as a reason to prefer an alternative rather than as a quantity to compute. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

The practical position is that the right response is protocol and modality choice, and the wrong response is declining a study that would change an operation. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Key Learning Points

  • No part of a knee pathway owns the cumulative dose.
  • Dose reduction has improved unevenly and invisibly between hospitals.
  • Weight bearing computed tomography will increase study numbers.
  • The risk estimates at these exposures rest on a contested model.
  • Respond with protocol and modality choice, not by declining a useful study.
