For Medical Students

What It Shows

Research on this effect divides into two efforts: removing it from reports, and turning it into a measurement. 5.19.1 covers the physics of magic angle imaging.

The first effort is finished in principle, because the confirming sequence is standard and the failure is one of practice. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

The second is open, and the obstacle is the one this chapter returns to, which is that routine imaging does not record the angle. 5.19.9 covers the clinical translation.

This page names no study, no investigator, no institution's result and no date. Evidence verification has not run in this environment, and a reference written from memory is the failure the Operations Handbook treats most seriously. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

How It Is Acquired

The questions a reader should expect this literature to address are listed here in place of its findings.

How much of the signal graded as meniscal or tendon degeneration is orientation rather than tissue change. 5.6.2 covers meniscal signal grading.

Whether deliberate repositioning within one examination yields a usable measure of fibre order. 5.18.5 covers weight-bearing MRI.

Whether the quantitative sequences already in use can be corrected for orientation rather than merely acquired at a fixed one. 5.18.2 covers T2 mapping.

Whether sequences that image ordered collagen directly make the orientation route unnecessary. 5.18.4 covers UTE imaging.

Key Learning Points

  • The effort to remove the effect from reports is finished in principle.
  • The effort to turn it into a measurement is open.
  • The obstacle is that routine imaging does not record the angle.
  • This page names no study, investigator, result or date.
  • A reference written from memory is a critical failure, not a shortcut.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva a candidate asked about research on this effect should describe the question rather than cite a paper, which is also what this page does.

The expected points are the separation of artefact from measurement, the unrecorded angle, the entanglement with quantitative imaging, and the direct collagen sequences.

Say that the artefact is solved and the measurement is not. 5.2.20 covers the magic angle effect.

Say that an orientation correction and an orientation measurement are the same problem seen from opposite ends. 5.18.1 covers quantitative MRI.

Say that a claim about this literature needs a reference, and that this page carries none. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

How Good It Is

As a research summary this page is incomplete by declaration rather than by oversight, and the distinction is the point.

Its best contribution is the shape of the field: two efforts, one closed and one open, with a single shared obstacle. 5.19.1 covers the physics of magic angle imaging.

It is limited by the absence of source access, which means no finding here is attributed, quantified or dated. 5.19.8 covers the Imperial magic angle programme.

It is limited again because a reader cannot tell from this page how active the field is, and the page does not guess. 5.1.8 covers the future of knee imaging.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no study count, publication date, effect size or angle appears either: every one of those would be an invented quantity in this environment.

Key Learning Points

  • The page is incomplete by declaration rather than by oversight.
  • The field divides into one closed effort and one open one.
  • Both share the obstacle of the unrecorded angle.
  • No finding here is attributed, quantified or dated.
  • The page does not guess at how active the field is.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level nothing on this page changes a management decision, and stating that is more useful than implying the opposite.

A clinician reading a report today uses the confirming sequence, which needs no research. 5.2.14 covers T2 imaging.

A clinician reading a quantitative map today should know that the map carries an orientation term, which also needs no research. 5.18.2 covers T2 mapping.

The open questions would change practice by adding a measurement rather than by correcting an error. 5.19.6 covers the cartilage applications.

Until a reviewer with the literature works through this chapter, every claim in it stands on standard teaching and none on a cited source. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

Where It Misleads

The commonest way a page like this misleads is by sounding current while citing nothing. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

A named effect with a long history reads as settled, and the measurement side of it is not. 5.2.20 covers the magic angle effect.

A reader takes the absence of references as an editorial style rather than as a missing step. 5.19.8 covers the Imperial magic angle programme.

A research question listed clearly is mistaken for a research finding. 5.19.10 covers the future applications.

Enthusiasm for a physical mechanism outruns the evidence that it measures anything useful in a patient. 5.18.1 covers quantitative MRI.

Key Learning Points

  • A page can sound current while citing nothing.
  • A long history makes an effect read as settled.
  • Missing references are a missing step, not a style.
  • A clearly listed question is mistaken for a finding.
  • Enthusiasm for a mechanism outruns the evidence for its use.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this page is the chapter's statement of its own standing, and the chapter is better served by saying what it does not know.

The whole section was written without source retrieval, so this page differs from the others in degree rather than in kind: its subject is the literature, and the literature is the thing missing. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

A reviewer with access can fill this page without restructuring it, because the questions are stated in the order the findings would answer them. 5.19.9 covers the clinical translation.

The risk the chapter runs is advocacy: a physical mechanism that is real, elegant and clinically unproven invites more confidence than it has earned. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

The chapter's rule, read as a rule about itself, says that an unrecorded variable makes a measurement incomparable, and an uncited claim makes a page unverifiable. 5.19.1 covers the physics of magic angle imaging.

Key Learning Points

  • The page's subject is the literature and the literature is what is missing.
  • It differs from its siblings in degree rather than in kind.
  • A reviewer with access can fill it without restructuring it.
  • The risk the chapter runs is advocacy for an unproven mechanism.
  • An uncited claim makes a page unverifiable.
