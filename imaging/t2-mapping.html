T2 Mapping

Page 5.18.2 · Advanced Imaging · imaging page

A relaxation time that changes with cartilage water and collagen, which detects degeneration before the surface breaks.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

T2 Mapping

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

T2 mapping measures the T2 relaxation time at each point in a tissue and displays it as a map. 5.2.14 covers the T2 imaging.

In cartilage the value rises with water content and falls with collagen organisation. 5.19.2 covers the collagen architecture.

It matters because both change early in degeneration, before any defect appears on a conventional sequence. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

How It Is Acquired

Acquire several images at different echo times and fit the signal decay at each point. 5.2.14 covers the T2 imaging.

Segment the cartilage carefully, since the value at the edges is contaminated. 5.5.7 covers the cartilage.

Measure by zone through the cartilage depth rather than as one value. 5.11.1 covers the normal cartilage.

Standardise the time since the patient last loaded the knee, because the value changes with loading. 5.18.5 covers weight-bearing MRI.

Keep the scanner and the sequence fixed across any comparison. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

What Normal Looks Like

A gradient through the cartilage depth, lower deep and higher superficially. 5.11.1 covers the normal cartilage.

A smooth map without the discontinuities of a segmentation error. 5.5.7 covers the cartilage.

Values inside the site's own range for that compartment. 5.21.3 covers the cartilage imaging literature.

Symmetry between compartments where the joint is normal. 5.18.1 covers the quantitative MRI.

What Abnormal Looks Like

A regional rise in value with an intact surface. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

Loss of the normal depth gradient, which reflects collagen disorganisation. 5.19.2 covers the collagen architecture.

A focal rise over a bone marrow lesion. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Raised values in repair tissue, which is expected rather than a failure. 5.11.8 covers the cartilage repair procedures.

Key Learning Points

  • The value rises with water and falls with collagen order.
  • Both change before a defect appears.
  • Cartilage is measured by depth zone.
  • Loading before the scan changes the value.
  • Repair tissue reads high and that is expected.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer names what the value is sensitive to.

The expected points are the multiple echo times, the segmentation, the depth zones, the loading history, and the fixed hardware.

Say that the depth gradient matters more than the mean. 5.11.1 covers the normal cartilage.

Say that the measure is sensitive and not specific. 5.18.1 covers the quantitative MRI.

Say that values are not transferable between sites. 5.21.3 covers the cartilage imaging literature.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that collagen fibre orientation changes through the cartilage depth, so the relaxation time does too, and the gradient rather than the mean carries the structural information. 5.19.2 covers the collagen architecture.

Early degeneration raises water content and disrupts the collagen network, which raises the value and flattens the gradient together. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

Fibre orientation also sets the magic angle behaviour, which is why one tissue property turns up in two different techniques. 5.2.20 covers the magic angle effect.

Loading expresses water from the matrix, so a value taken after walking differs from one taken after rest. 5.18.5 covers weight-bearing MRI.

How Good It Is

T2 mapping is the most studied quantitative cartilage measure and the least specific one.

Its best contribution is detecting matrix change before a surface defect, within one study. 5.21.3 covers the cartilage imaging literature.

It is limited by scanner dependence, by loading sensitivity, by segmentation error, and by responding to anything that changes water content. 5.5.7 covers the cartilage.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no relaxation values or reference ranges appear either: those are published quantities tied to particular scanners and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Fibre orientation sets the depth gradient.
  • Early degeneration raises the value and flattens the gradient.
  • The same property drives the magic angle behaviour.
  • A value after walking differs from one after rest.
  • The measure is sensitive and not specific.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the map does not decide an operation and does inform a research question. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

After a cartilage procedure the map describes the repair tissue, which is a maturation question rather than a failure one. 5.11.8 covers the cartilage repair procedures.

In a trial it shortens the follow-up needed to show a difference. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

A value quoted in a letter with no site and no sequence should be set aside rather than interpreted. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

The conventional study still answers the operative questions. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reading a raised value as a cartilage defect. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

A segmentation error at the subchondral edge produces a band of high values. 5.5.7 covers the cartilage.

A knee scanned after activity is compared with one scanned after rest. 5.18.5 covers weight-bearing MRI.

Repair tissue is compared with native cartilage and reported as failing. 5.11.8 covers the cartilage repair procedures.

The patellofemoral compartment is mapped where its curved thin cartilage makes the value least reliable. 5.12.6 covers the patellofemoral cartilage injury.

Key Learning Points

  • A raised value is not a defect.
  • A segmentation error produces a false high band.
  • Activity before the scan changes the comparison.
  • Repair tissue is not native cartilage.
  • The patellofemoral compartment is the least reliable.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level these maps arrive from trials and from centres with their own normal ranges. 5.21.3 covers the cartilage imaging literature.

A multicentre study has to harmonise the sequence before it can pool the values, and the harmonisation is itself a result. 5.18.1 covers the quantitative MRI.

A patient scanned on one scanner before surgery and another after has no comparable pair. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

The relationship between the value and the symptoms is weak, so an abnormal map in a comfortable knee is common. 5.21.5 covers the osteoarthritis imaging literature.

Automated segmentation makes the maps reproducible and inherits the same edge problem at the deep zone. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

The useful position is that T2 mapping has earned its place as an outcome measure and not as a diagnostic test. 5.19.9 covers the clinical translation.

Key Learning Points

  • Harmonisation across sites is itself a study result.
  • Two scanners make an uncomparable pair.
  • An abnormal map in a comfortable knee is common.
  • Automation inherits the deep zone edge problem.
  • It is an outcome measure rather than a diagnostic test.
