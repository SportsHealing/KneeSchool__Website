Meniscal Repair

Page 5.17.2 · Post-operative Imaging · imaging page

A repaired meniscus never looks normal, so imaging cannot declare it healed and should not be asked to.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Meniscal Repair

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Post-operative imaging of a meniscal repair shows the meniscus, the repair implants and the rim. 5.6.13 covers the meniscal repair imaging.

Signal persists at the repair site for a long time, whether or not the tear has healed. 5.6.12 covers the post-operative meniscus.

It matters because the appearance of a healed repair overlaps the appearance of a failed one. 5.20.2 covers reporting meniscal tears.

How It Is Acquired

State the interval since surgery and the repair technique if it is known. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative protocol.

Assess whether the meniscus is in position rather than whether the signal has resolved. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

Assess for a displaced fragment, which is a positive finding. 5.6.6 covers bucket handle tears.

Assess the implants and whether any has migrated. 5.5.8 covers the menisci.

Assess the cartilage of the same compartment. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

What Normal Looks Like

A meniscus in position with its rim intact. 5.5.8 covers the menisci.

Persisting signal at the repair site, which is expected. 5.6.13 covers the meniscal repair imaging.

Implants in position with no migration. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

No displaced fragment and no extrusion. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

What Abnormal Looks Like

A displaced fragment, which is the finding that changes management. 5.6.6 covers bucket handle tears.

Fluid signal tracking across the full width of the repair with the meniscus displaced. 5.6.13 covers the meniscal repair imaging.

Extrusion that was not there before. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

A migrated implant, which can abrade cartilage. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

New cartilage loss in the compartment. 5.12.6 covers cartilage injury.

Key Learning Points

  • Signal persists whether or not the repair healed.
  • Position matters more than signal.
  • A displaced fragment is the finding that changes management.
  • Implants can migrate and abrade cartilage.
  • The healed and failed appearances overlap.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer refuses to call the repair healed from a scan.

The expected points are the interval, the position, the displaced fragment, the implants, and the cartilage.

Say that no agreed appearance of a healed repair exists. 5.6.13 covers the meniscal repair imaging.

Say that persisting signal is expected rather than failure. 5.6.12 covers the post-operative meniscus.

Say that the symptoms and the examination decide the outcome. 4.20.2 covers the meniscal repair examination.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that healing produces fibrovascular tissue rather than normal meniscus, so the repair site retains signal indefinitely and the signal cannot be the test. 5.6.13 covers the meniscal repair imaging.

Displacement is the finding that is unambiguous, because a healed repair does not displace. 5.6.6 covers bucket handle tears.

Extrusion appearing after a repair means the meniscus is not restraining load even where it looks attached. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

A contrast study has been proposed to settle healing and is not routine. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging is a good test of the meniscus's position and a poor test of whether a repair has healed.

Its best contribution is displacement, extrusion and implant position. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

It is limited because persisting signal is expected, because no healed reference exists, and because the clinical outcome is not an imaging finding. 4.20.2 covers the meniscal repair examination.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no healing rates or signal grade boundaries appear either: those are published quantities from particular series and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Healing produces fibrovascular tissue, not normal meniscus.
  • Displacement is unambiguous and signal is not.
  • New extrusion means the meniscus is not restraining load.
  • No healed reference appearance exists.
  • The outcome is clinical.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the imaging answers whether there is a mechanical cause for the symptoms. 5.6.6 covers bucket handle tears.

A displaced fragment is an operative finding. 5.6.13 covers the meniscal repair imaging.

Persisting signal with no displacement in a comfortable knee is a reason to do nothing. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

A failed repair in a knee with malalignment or an untreated root raises the mechanical cause. 5.6.9 covers root tears.

Re-repair against resection turns on tissue quality, which imaging reports poorly. 5.6.14 covers the meniscal transplantation imaging.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting a repair as failed because signal persists. 5.6.12 covers the post-operative meniscus.

A repair implant is read as a loose body. 5.14.6 covers synovial chondromatosis.

Expected post-operative extrusion is read as new. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

A knee is re-scoped for an appearance rather than for a symptom. 5.1.2 covers clinical examination versus imaging.

The compartment's cartilage is omitted from the report. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

Key Learning Points

  • Persisting signal is reported as failure.
  • An implant is read as a loose body.
  • Expected extrusion is read as new.
  • A knee is re-scoped for an appearance.
  • The cartilage belongs in the report.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the repair has failed once and the question is whether to repair again or to resect. 5.6.13 covers the meniscal repair imaging.

Imaging cannot judge tissue quality, which is the deciding factor, so the decision is made at operation. 5.6.14 covers the meniscal transplantation imaging.

A root tear left untreated at the index repair is the commonest mechanical reason for failure and is visible on the original study. 5.6.9 covers root tears.

Alignment belongs in the assessment of any failed meniscal surgery. 5.3.6 covers the long leg alignment films.

Serial scans across departments compare protocols as much as healing. 5.18.1 covers the quantitative MRI.

The useful report gives the interval, the position, any displacement, the implants, the extrusion and the cartilage, and states that healing cannot be assessed. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Key Learning Points

  • Tissue quality decides and imaging cannot judge it.
  • An untreated root tear is the commonest mechanical cause.
  • Alignment belongs in every failed meniscal assessment.
  • Serial scans compare protocols as much as healing.
  • The report states that healing cannot be assessed.
