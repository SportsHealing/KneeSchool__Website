Gradient Echo Imaging

Page 5.2.18 · Imaging Physics for Knee Specialists · imaging page

The fast family of sequences, which the knee uses for cartilage and for three dimensional acquisition.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Gradient Echo Imaging

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

A gradient echo sequence refocuses the signal with a magnetic field gradient rather than with a second radiofrequency pulse. 5.2.12 covers the basic principles.

That is faster, which allows a thin slice three dimensional volume in a usable scan time. 5.5.7 covers the cartilage.

It matters because cartilage surface assessment and three dimensional reconstruction both depend on thin isotropic slices. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

How It Is Acquired

Omitting the refocusing pulse shortens the sequence, which buys speed or resolution. 5.2.12 covers the basic principles.

It makes the sequence sensitive to field inhomogeneity, so metal and air interfaces distort it more. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

That same sensitivity makes it good at detecting anything that disturbs the field, such as blood products or calcification. 5.14.4 covers crystal arthropathy.

A three dimensional isotropic acquisition can be reformatted in any plane after the fact. 5.16.8 covers three dimensional reconstructions.

Cartilage sensitive variants give high contrast between cartilage and fluid. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

Key Learning Points

  • The signal is refocused with a gradient rather than a second pulse.
  • That is faster, allowing thin slice three dimensional volumes.
  • It is more sensitive to field inhomogeneity.
  • That sensitivity detects blood products and calcification.
  • An isotropic volume can be reformatted in any plane.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the sequence family to name for cartilage and for three dimensional work.

The expected points are the gradient refocusing, the speed, the inhomogeneity sensitivity, the isotropic volume, and the cartilage contrast.

Say that it is worse near metal than a spin echo sequence. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

Say that the isotropic volume is what allows reformatting. 5.16.8 covers three dimensional reconstructions.

Say that it detects calcification and blood products well. 5.14.4 covers crystal arthropathy.

How Good It Is

Gradient echo imaging gives the best cartilage surface detail and the worst performance near metal.

Its best contribution is the cartilage surface and the reformattable volume, neither of which the conventional sequences deliver. 5.11.2 covers chondral defects.

It is limited near metal and at air interfaces, and it is less sensitive to marrow oedema than a fat suppressed spin echo sequence. 5.2.16 covers fat suppression.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no flip angle or slice thickness values appear either: those are protocol quantities that differ by scanner and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Best cartilage surface detail of the routine sequences.
  • Worst performance near metal.
  • Less sensitive to marrow oedema than fat suppressed spin echo.
  • The isotropic volume allows reformatting in any plane.
  • Good at detecting calcification and blood products.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level this sequence family answers the cartilage question and the geometry question.

A chondral surface lesion being considered for a repair procedure needs this detail. 5.11.8 covers cartilage repair procedures.

A three dimensional volume supports measurement and planning that separate plane acquisitions do not. 5.16.8 covers three dimensional reconstructions.

Loose bodies and calcification are detected well, which is a specific strength. 5.14.6 covers synovial chondromatosis.

In a metal bearing knee this is the wrong family and a spin echo based protocol is needed. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative protocol.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is assessing marrow on a gradient echo sequence, which understates oedema. 5.2.16 covers fat suppression.

Field distortion near metal is read as a lesion. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

A reformatted plane is treated as an acquired one despite its thinner data. 5.16.8 covers three dimensional reconstructions.

Cartilage thickness is measured on a sequence whose contrast was not designed for it. 5.11.1 covers normal cartilage.

Air at a surgical site mimics calcification. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative protocol.

Key Learning Points

  • Marrow oedema is understated on a gradient echo sequence.
  • Field distortion near metal is read as a lesion.
  • A reformatted plane is treated as an acquired one.
  • Cartilage thickness is measured on the wrong contrast.
  • Surgical air mimics calcification.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the move to three dimensional isotropic acquisition is changing knee imaging faster than the conventions that interpret it.

An isotropic volume replaces three separate acquisitions with one, which changes the contrast each plane is read on, and the grading systems were written for the old contrast. 5.2.15 covers proton density imaging.

Reformatted images look like acquired ones and carry different noise and partial volume behaviour, which is invisible to the reader. 5.16.8 covers three dimensional reconstructions.

Cartilage morphology measured on these sequences is the basis of several osteoarthritis progression measures, and the sequences differ between the cohorts those measures came from. 5.21.3 covers the cartilage imaging literature.

The same volumes feed automated segmentation, so a change in acquisition propagates into a quantitative output with no visible warning. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

The useful caution is that a thinner, prettier image is not automatically a more interpretable one, which is the chapter's point in a different form. 5.2.12 covers the basic principles.

Key Learning Points

  • An isotropic volume changes the contrast each plane is read on.
  • The grading systems were written for the older contrast.
  • Reformatted images carry different noise and partial volume behaviour.
  • Progression measures came from cohorts with different sequences.
  • A thinner image is not automatically a more interpretable one.
