Aspiration

Page 5.4.17 · Ultrasound of the Knee · imaging page

Taking fluid out under vision, where the imaging also decides whether the collection should be aspirated at all.

Aspiration

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

An ultrasound guided aspiration images the collection, the needle and the decreasing volume as fluid is withdrawn. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

The imaging also characterises the collection first, which determines whether aspirating it is appropriate. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

It matters because a pulsatile vascular lesion looks like a cyst on a still image, and Doppler before the needle is what separates them. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

How It Is Acquired

Characterise the collection before planning anything, including its echogenicity and whether it has a neck. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

Apply Doppler to exclude a vascular lesion, which is the step that must not be skipped. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Choose the approach that avoids vessels and nerves and reaches the deepest part of the collection. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

Keep the needle in plane and watch the collection decrease, which confirms the tip. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

Note the fluid's appearance, since internal echoes and loose bodies change the interpretation. 5.14.6 covers synovial chondromatosis.

What Normal Looks Like

An anechoic collection that decreases as fluid is withdrawn.

A needle visible in plane within the collection. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

No Doppler flow within the structure. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

A collapsing cavity at the end of the procedure. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

What Abnormal Looks Like

Doppler flow within the structure, which makes it a vascular lesion and not a target. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Internal echoes or septations, which suggest a complex collection. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

Loose bodies within the fluid. 5.14.6 covers synovial chondromatosis.

A collection that does not decrease, which means the tip is not in it. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

Key Learning Points

  • The imaging characterises the collection before the needle is planned.
  • Doppler before the needle separates a cyst from a vascular lesion.
  • The approach avoids vessels and nerves and reaches the deepest part.
  • Watching the collection decrease confirms the tip position.
  • Internal echoes and loose bodies change the interpretation.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the procedure where the expected answer begins with Doppler.

The expected points are the characterisation, the Doppler, the approach, the in plane needle, and the fluid's appearance.

Say that Doppler excludes an aneurysm before the needle. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Say that a popliteal cyst implies an intra-articular cause. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

Say that a suspected septic joint is a different urgency with a different pathway. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that fluid and a patent vessel both appear anechoic on a still grey scale image, so the only distinguishing feature available in real time is flow. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

That makes Doppler a safety step rather than a characterisation nicety, and the one part of this procedure with a serious consequence if omitted. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

A collection with internal echoes may be blood, pus or a complex cyst, and ultrasound narrows that without settling it, which is why the fluid itself is sent. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

A popliteal cyst's neck means the fluid came from the joint, so aspirating the cyst leaves the source producing more. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

How Good It Is

Ultrasound guided aspiration reaches a collection reliably and characterises it only partly.

Its best contribution is the combination of safe access and real time confirmation of emptying. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

It is limited in characterising the fluid, so the laboratory rather than the imaging settles what it is. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no volume or recurrence figures appear either: those are published quantities and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Fluid and a patent vessel both appear anechoic on grey scale.
  • Flow is the only real time distinguishing feature.
  • Doppler is a safety step with a serious consequence if omitted.
  • Ultrasound narrows what the fluid is without settling it.
  • A cyst's neck means the source keeps producing.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the imaging contributes access and characterisation, and the decision to aspirate is clinical.

A suspected septic joint needs fluid for the laboratory, and guidance helps where the effusion is small. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

A large simple effusion is reached without guidance, so guidance adds least where the target is largest. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

A popliteal cyst aspirated without addressing the intra-articular cause will usually recur, which makes the imaging pathway part of the outcome. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

A vascular lesion must be excluded before any posterior aspiration, and that is a procedural rule rather than a preference. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is aspirating a posterior collection without Doppler. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

An anechoic appearance is taken as simple fluid. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

A cyst is aspirated and the intra-articular cause is not sought. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

The collection is not watched during aspiration, so incomplete emptying is missed. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

Imaging appearance is used to exclude infection. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

Key Learning Points

  • A posterior collection is aspirated without Doppler.
  • An anechoic appearance is taken as simple fluid.
  • The cyst is aspirated and the cause is not sought.
  • Incomplete emptying is missed by not watching.
  • Imaging appearance is used to exclude infection.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level aspiration is the knee procedure where ultrasound's diagnostic and procedural roles are hardest to separate, and the separation matters.

The scan that plans the procedure also decides whether the procedure should happen, and a Doppler finding changes it from a knee problem to a vascular referral, which is the only urgent change ultrasound makes in this chapter. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Nothing in the imaging distinguishes infected from inflammatory fluid, so a confident imaging appearance in a hot knee is a hazard rather than a help. 5.14.7 covers septic arthritis.

Guidance's measurable benefit is greatest for small and loculated collections and smallest for the large effusions it is most often used on, which means its use is inversely matched to its value. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

Recurrence after cyst aspiration is driven by the intra-articular source, so the procedure's outcome depends on a study ultrasound cannot perform. 5.6.3 covers horizontal meniscal tears.

The consistent recommendation is Doppler first, laboratory for the fluid, and a magnetic resonance study for the cause. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Key Learning Points

  • The planning scan also decides whether the procedure should happen.
  • A Doppler finding is the only urgent change ultrasound makes here.
  • Imaging cannot distinguish infected from inflammatory fluid.
  • Guidance's value is inversely matched to its common use.
  • Doppler first, laboratory for the fluid, magnetic resonance for the cause.
