Hydrodissection

Page 5.4.18 · Ultrasound of the Knee · imaging page

Separating tissue planes with fluid under vision, which is a technique ultrasound made possible.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Hydrodissection

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Hydrodissection injects fluid into a tissue plane to separate structures that have become adherent, most often around a nerve. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

Ultrasound shows the plane opening as the fluid enters, which is the confirmation that the right plane was entered. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

It matters because the technique depends entirely on seeing the plane, so it exists because of the imaging rather than alongside it. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

How It Is Acquired

Identify the structure to be freed and the plane around it before starting. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

Place the needle tip in the plane rather than in the structure, which is the critical distinction. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

Inject a small volume and watch the plane open, which confirms the position before continuing. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

Advance along the plane as it opens, using the fluid to create the space ahead. 5.2.9 covers refraction.

Avoid injecting into the nerve itself, which is the hazard the imaging exists to prevent. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

What Normal Looks Like

A thin anechoic layer appearing around the structure as fluid enters.

The structure becoming surrounded by fluid and separating from adjacent tissue. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

A needle tip visible in the plane rather than in the structure. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

A progressively opening plane as injection continues.

What Abnormal Looks Like

Fluid entering the structure itself, which swells rather than separates. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

No plane opening despite injection, which means the tip is in the wrong place. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

High resistance with no visible spread, which should stop the injection. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

Fluid tracking away from the target along a different plane. 5.2.9 covers refraction.

Key Learning Points

  • Fluid is injected into a plane to separate adherent structures.
  • Ultrasound shows the plane opening, which confirms the position.
  • The technique exists because of the imaging.
  • The tip goes in the plane, not in the structure.
  • Injecting into a nerve is the hazard the imaging prevents.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the technique to name when asked what ultrasound enables that was not previously possible.

The expected points are the plane identification, the tip placement, the test volume, the advance along the plane, and the nerve hazard.

Say that the plane opening is the confirmation. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

Say that injecting into the nerve is the complication the imaging prevents. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

Say that the indication belongs to the treatment literature rather than to imaging. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that fluid follows the path of least resistance, so a tip correctly placed in a plane opens that plane and a tip in a structure distends the structure. 5.2.9 covers refraction.

That gives an immediate and unambiguous visual check, which is why the technique is safe only under vision. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

The nerve is the usual target because adhesion around a nerve produces symptoms that freeing it may relieve, and the nerve is also the structure that must not be injected. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

High resistance means the needle is in tissue rather than a plane, so resistance and the image agree and either alone is weaker. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

How Good It Is

Ultrasound makes hydrodissection possible and says nothing about whether it helps.

Its best contribution is the real time confirmation that the correct plane has been entered, which has no equivalent. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

It is limited to the technical question, since the indication and the outcome belong to the treatment literature. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no volumes or outcome figures appear either: those are published quantities and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Fluid follows the path of least resistance.
  • A tip in the plane opens it; a tip in the structure distends it.
  • The visual check is immediate and unambiguous.
  • Resistance and the image agree, and either alone is weaker.
  • The technique is safe only under vision.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level this is a technique for a specific problem rather than a general one.

A symptomatic nerve with imaging evidence of surrounding adhesion, after an osteotomy or a corner injury, is the situation where it is considered. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

Imaging identifies the plane and the adhesion, which is the contribution this section owns. 5.4.3 covers the lateral knee.

Whether the procedure helps, and how it compares with surgical release, is a treatment question outside this section. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

A structurally abnormal nerve on ultrasound is better characterised before any procedure, because a lesion within the nerve is a different problem. 5.10.5 covers the common peroneal nerve on magnetic resonance imaging.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is injecting into the nerve rather than around it. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

A test volume is not used, so a misplaced tip is not detected early. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

The needle is out of plane and the tip position is assumed. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

A nerve lesion is treated as an adhesion. 5.10.5 covers the common peroneal nerve on magnetic resonance imaging.

The technical success is reported as a clinical result. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

Key Learning Points

  • The injection goes into the nerve rather than around it.
  • No test volume is used, so a misplaced tip is missed.
  • An out of plane needle makes the tip position an assumption.
  • A nerve lesion is treated as an adhesion.
  • Technical success is reported as a clinical result.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level hydrodissection is the clearest case of imaging creating a procedure, which brings its own problem.

The technique is possible only under ultrasound, so its evidence base is young and its practitioners are the people with the probes, which concentrates both the expertise and the enthusiasm. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

The immediate visual endpoint is highly satisfying and is a technical endpoint, so the procedure can be reported as successful without reference to the patient. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

Whether freeing an adherent nerve changes symptoms durably is not established in the knee, and the imaging's clarity about the plane says nothing about that. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

Distinguishing extraneural adhesion from an intraneural lesion matters before any procedure, and ultrasound does part of that while magnetic resonance imaging and neurophysiology do the rest. 5.10.5 covers the common peroneal nerve on magnetic resonance imaging.

The honest framing is that the imaging makes the technique safe and does not make it indicated. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Key Learning Points

  • The technique exists only under ultrasound, so its evidence base is young.
  • Expertise and enthusiasm are concentrated in the same people.
  • A satisfying visual endpoint is a technical endpoint.
  • Durable symptom change in the knee is not established.
  • The imaging makes the technique safe and does not make it indicated.
