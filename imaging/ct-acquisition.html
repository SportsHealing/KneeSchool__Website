CT Acquisition

Page 5.2.22 · Imaging Physics for Knee Specialists · imaging page

How a computed tomography volume is collected, which decides what can be measured from it.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

CT Acquisition

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Computed tomography rotates an X-ray source and detector around the patient and measures transmission from many directions. 5.2.1 covers the generation of X-rays.

From those measurements a cross sectional map of absorption is computed, which removes the superimposition a radiograph suffers. 5.2.2 covers the image formation.

It matters because the result is a true three dimensional data set, so bone geometry can be measured rather than inferred. 5.16.8 covers three dimensional reconstructions.

How It Is Acquired

The patient moves through a continuously rotating gantry, so the data are collected as a volume rather than as slices. 5.2.23 covers the reconstruction.

Detector coverage and rotation speed set how quickly a volume is acquired, which is why a knee takes seconds. 5.16.1 covers the fracture assessment.

Tube voltage and current set the dose and the noise, exactly as in radiography. 5.2.24 covers the dose in computed tomography.

Thin detector rows give an isotropic volume, which is what allows reformatting in any plane without loss. 5.2.25 covers three dimensional reconstruction.

Metal in the knee degrades the data, and the correction for it is applied at reconstruction rather than at acquisition. 5.2.23 covers the reconstruction.

Key Learning Points

  • Transmission is measured from many directions around the patient.
  • The result is a computed cross sectional map of absorption.
  • Superimposition is removed, so geometry can be measured.
  • Thin detector rows give an isotropic volume.
  • Metal degradation is corrected at reconstruction.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer explains what computed tomography adds over a radiograph and what it costs.

The expected points are the rotating acquisition, the volume, the dose and noise settings, the isotropic data, and metal.

Say that bone geometry is the thing it measures better than anything else. 5.16.6 covers arthroplasty planning.

Say that the cost is dose rather than time. 5.2.24 covers the dose in computed tomography.

Say that soft tissue contrast is poor compared with magnetic resonance imaging. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

How Good It Is

Computed tomography is the best modality for bone geometry in the knee and carries the highest dose of the knee studies.

Its best contribution is cortical detail and three dimensional bony relationships, which radiography infers and magnetic resonance imaging shows less sharply. 5.16.2 covers tibial plateau fractures.

It is limited by dose, by poor soft tissue contrast, and by being acquired supine and unloaded unless a weight bearing scanner is used. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no acquisition parameters appear either, in slice thickness or pitch: those are local protocol quantities and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Best modality for bone geometry in the knee.
  • Highest dose of the knee studies.
  • Cortical detail and three dimensional bony relationships are its strengths.
  • Soft tissue contrast is poor.
  • Acquired supine and unloaded unless a weight bearing scanner is used.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level computed tomography is requested for a geometric question that a radiograph cannot answer.

Articular fracture assessment and surgical planning need the three dimensional relationship, which is the clearest indication. 5.16.2 covers tibial plateau fractures.

Rotational measurement needs a volume, because rotation cannot be measured from a projection. 5.16.3 covers rotational profiles.

Tunnel position and widening before a revision are measured here rather than on magnetic resonance imaging. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

Component position and bone stock before arthroplasty or revision are geometric questions. 5.16.6 covers arthroplasty planning.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is requesting computed tomography for a soft tissue question. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Dose is not considered, particularly in a young patient having repeated studies. 5.2.24 covers the dose in computed tomography.

A supine acquisition is used to answer a loaded question. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

Metal artefact is read as bone loss. 5.2.23 covers the reconstruction.

A measurement is made on a reformatted plane without checking the acquisition was isotropic. 5.2.25 covers three dimensional reconstruction.

Key Learning Points

  • Computed tomography is requested for soft tissue questions.
  • Dose is not considered in a young patient with repeated studies.
  • A supine acquisition answers a loaded question.
  • Metal artefact is read as bone loss.
  • A reformatted measurement is made on non-isotropic data.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level computed tomography is the knee modality whose capability has moved fastest and whose indications have moved least.

Dose reduction, iterative reconstruction and metal artefact reduction have all advanced, so a department's protocol age changes both the dose and the answer. 5.2.23 covers the reconstruction.

Weight bearing computed tomography measures bone geometry under load, which is the combination no other study offers, and its indications in the knee are still being worked out. 5.18.5 covers weight bearing imaging.

Published measurement conventions, for rotation and for tunnel position, were derived on older acquisitions and different reconstruction methods. 5.16.3 covers rotational profiles.

Segmentation for planning and for printing is increasingly automated, and its errors are systematic rather than random, which makes them harder to notice. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

The consistent theme is that the geometry is trustworthy and the conventions applied to it are older than the data, which is the same problem the magnetic resonance pages met. 5.2.12 covers the basic principles.

Key Learning Points

  • Protocol age changes both the dose and the answer.
  • Weight bearing computed tomography offers geometry under load.
  • Measurement conventions were derived on older acquisitions.
  • Automated segmentation errors are systematic rather than random.
  • The geometry is trustworthy and the conventions are older than the data.
