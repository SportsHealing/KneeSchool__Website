Artificial Intelligence in Imaging

Page 5.18.8 · Advanced Imaging · imaging page

Systems trained on what readers said, which are strongest where reading was already reliable and silent about their own uncertainty.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Artificial Intelligence in Imaging

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

These systems detect findings, measure structures and draft reports from the images. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

They are trained on labels that human readers produced, so they learn what readers said rather than what was there. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

It matters because that training sets both their accuracy and their blind spots. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

How It Is Acquired

Establish what the system was trained on, including which departments and which scanners. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Establish where the labels came from, whether from another reader or from an operative finding. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

Establish what it was validated on and whether that set was independent. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

Establish what it does not attempt, which is rarely stated as clearly as what it does. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

Establish whether the output carries an uncertainty. 5.18.1 covers the quantitative MRI.

What Normal Looks Like

A stated training population with its scanners and protocols. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

A validation set independent of the training set. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

An output that marks what it could not assess. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Performance reported by subgroup rather than as one figure. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

What Abnormal Looks Like

A system deployed on scanners unlike those it was trained on. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Performance quoted as a single number for a whole joint. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

An output with uniform confidence across easy and hard cases. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

A measurement produced with no convention named. 5.16.4 covers the TT-TG measurement.

A complete report with nothing marked as unassessable. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

Key Learning Points

  • These systems learn what readers said.
  • The training population sets the blind spots.
  • Validation has to be independent of training.
  • Performance varies by subgroup.
  • An output should mark what it could not assess.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer asks what the system was trained on.

The expected points are the training population, the label reference, the independent validation, the stated scope, and the uncertainty.

Say that a label from a reader inherits that reader's variability. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

Say that a system is strongest where readers already agreed. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

Say that an operative reference beats a reader reference and is harder to obtain. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that a system trained on reader labels can at best reproduce the readers, so it performs best on the complete ACL tear and worst on the partial one, which is where readers disagreed. 5.20.3 covers reporting ACL injuries.

The same argument applies to cartilage grading, to meniscal tear classification, and to any finding whose definition varies. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

Fixing the measurement rules removes variability without settling which convention is correct, so consistency improves and validity does not. 5.16.4 covers the TT-TG measurement.

The departments that supplied the training data supplied their reporting conventions with it, local phrasing included. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

How Good It Is

These systems are the best available way to make reading consistent and are no better than the readers who labelled their training data.

Their best contribution is consistency, triage, and the removal of the missing negative and the hedge. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

They are limited by label quality, by the training population, by uniform confidence, and by being weakest exactly where reading was hardest. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no performance statistics appear either: those are published quantities from particular datasets and this environment cannot check them. The restriction bites hardest here, since performance figures are the main claim made for these systems.

Key Learning Points

  • A system can at best reproduce its labellers.
  • It is strongest where readers already agreed.
  • Fixing the rules improves consistency and not validity.
  • Training departments supply their conventions too.
  • It is weakest where reading was hardest.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level nothing in a report should be acted on differently because a system produced it. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

The gain is in workflow: triage, measurement, comparison with priors, and completeness. 5.1.5 covers the cost-effectiveness of imaging.

A measurement produced automatically still needs its convention stated before it can be compared. 5.16.4 covers the TT-TG measurement.

A finding the system did not report is not a negative unless the system reports negatives. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

The surgeon remains the last check, and that does not change because the report was drafted differently. 4.1.8 covers the examination documentation.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reading machine confidence as accuracy. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

A complete report is read as a thorough study. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Performance from the development dataset is assumed to hold locally. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

Agreement between a system and a reader is counted as validation when both learned the same convention. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

A system silent on a structure is read as having cleared it. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

Key Learning Points

  • Machine confidence is read as accuracy.
  • Completeness is read as thoroughness.
  • Development performance is assumed to hold locally.
  • Agreement with a reader may be shared convention.
  • Silence is read as clearance.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level these outputs arrive inside reports from other departments, usually unmarked as automated. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

Post-operative knees, paediatric knees and revision cases are under-represented in training data and over-represented in a tertiary practice. 5.17.9 covers the revision arthroplasty.

A system updated between two studies of one patient makes the comparison a comparison of systems. 5.18.1 covers the quantitative MRI.

Automated measurement of the patellofemoral geometry inherits the convention problem and returns a number that looks settled. 5.16.4 covers the TT-TG measurement.

Trial outcomes read by an automated system are consistent, which improves the statistics without improving the truth. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

The honest position is that these systems will change the workflow before they change the accuracy, and the accuracy they change will be in the easy cases. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

Key Learning Points

  • Automated output arrives unmarked inside reports.
  • Tertiary cases are under-represented in training data.
  • A system update makes a serial comparison meaningless.
  • An automated measurement looks settled and is not.
  • Workflow will change before accuracy does.
