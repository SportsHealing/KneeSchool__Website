Reflection

Page 5.2.8 · Imaging Physics for Knee Specialists · imaging page

Why an echo comes back at all, and why nothing at all is seen behind a bony surface.

Reflection

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

An echo is produced where two tissues with different acoustic properties meet, and the size of the difference sets the strength of the echo. 5.2.6 covers sound waves.

A small difference reflects a little and lets the rest through, which is what allows layered soft tissue to be imaged. 5.4.1 covers the anterior knee.

It matters because a large difference reflects nearly everything, so a bone or a gas surface ends the image rather than appearing in it. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

How It Is Acquired

Soft tissue interfaces reflect weakly, so the beam continues and deeper structures are still reached. 5.2.6 covers sound waves.

A soft tissue to bone interface reflects almost all of it, producing a bright line and a shadow beyond. 5.3.1 covers the anteroposterior view.

A soft tissue to gas interface does the same, which is why bowel gas and emphysema obscure. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

A smooth interface reflects like a mirror, so the echo returns only when the beam strikes it squarely. 5.2.10 covers anisotropy.

A rough interface scatters, which gives the speckled texture tissue is recognised by. 5.4.6 covers the patellar tendon.

Key Learning Points

  • An echo arises where acoustic properties differ.
  • A small difference reflects weakly and lets the beam continue.
  • Bone and gas reflect nearly everything and cast a shadow.
  • A smooth interface returns an echo only when struck squarely.
  • A rough interface scatters and gives tissue its texture.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer connects the interface physics to the two things ultrasound cannot do.

The expected points are the weak soft tissue interfaces, the bone and gas interfaces, the mirror behaviour of a smooth surface, and tissue texture.

Say that acoustic shadowing behind bone is physics rather than pathology. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

Say that a calcification casts a shadow and that the shadow is a clue. 5.14.4 covers crystal arthropathy.

Say that the mirror behaviour is why probe angle matters so much. 5.2.10 covers anisotropy.

How Good It Is

Reflection is what makes ultrasound superb at a soft tissue boundary and incapable beyond a bony one.

Its best contribution is the tendon and the fluid interface, where the acoustic differences are ideal for imaging. 5.4.6 covers the patellar tendon.

It is limited absolutely by bone and gas, and this limit is the reason most of the knee joint is inaccessible. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no acoustic impedance values appear either: those are tabulated physical quantities and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Acoustic shadowing behind bone is physics rather than pathology.
  • A calcification casts a shadow, and the shadow is a diagnostic clue.
  • Mirror behaviour is why probe angle matters.
  • Tendon and fluid interfaces are ideal for ultrasound.
  • Bone and gas are absolute limits.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the reflection physics draws the boundary of what ultrasound can be asked.

Fluid against soft tissue is the clearest interface there is, so an effusion, a bursa or a cyst is confidently characterised. 5.4.17 covers aspiration.

A calcification within a tendon is confidently identified by its shadow, which is a specific strength. 5.4.20 covers tendon procedures.

Anything deep to a cortical surface is unavailable, which rules out the cruciates, the central meniscus and most cartilage. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

A needle is highly reflective, which is why guided intervention works so well here. 5.4.16 covers the knee injection.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reading acoustic shadowing as a lesion. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

A bright bone surface is read as abnormal cortical thickening. 5.3.1 covers the anteroposterior view.

Reverberation between two strong reflectors produces repeated lines read as structures. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

A structure not seen because of a shadow is reported as absent. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

Posterior acoustic enhancement behind a cyst is read as an adjacent abnormality. 5.4.4 covers the posterior knee.

Key Learning Points

  • Acoustic shadowing is read as a lesion.
  • A bright bone surface is read as cortical thickening.
  • Reverberation lines are read as structures.
  • A shadowed structure is reported as absent.
  • Enhancement behind a cyst is read as an adjacent abnormality.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the reflection artefacts are well described and still account for a recognisable share of ultrasound error.

Each has a name and a mechanism, so an operator who knows the physics does not make the mistake, which makes this a training problem rather than a technical one. 5.2.19 covers artefacts.

The artefacts appear on the recorded images and are interpreted by a reader who was not present, which is where the mechanism gets lost. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Calcification detection by shadow is a genuine advantage over magnetic resonance imaging and is rarely the reason the study was requested, so the advantage is usually unused. 5.14.4 covers crystal arthropathy.

Contrast enhanced ultrasound and elastography extend what reflection alone gives, and neither has an established place in knee practice. 5.18.1 covers quantitative MRI.

The useful summary is that ultrasound's limits here are hard and knowable, which makes them easier to work with than the softer limits of the other modalities. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Key Learning Points

  • The artefacts are named and mechanistic, so this is a training problem.
  • A reader who was not present loses the mechanism.
  • Calcification detection by shadow is an unused advantage.
  • Contrast enhanced ultrasound and elastography have no established knee place.
  • Hard knowable limits are easier to work with than soft ones.
