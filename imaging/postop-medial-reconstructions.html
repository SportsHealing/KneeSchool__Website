Post-operative Medial Reconstructions

Page 5.9.7 · Medial Knee MRI · imaging page

A reconstruction with no published expected appearance, where the useful measurements are the meniscal attachment and the rotation.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Post-operative Medial Reconstructions

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Post-operative imaging of a medial reconstruction shows the grafts, their tunnels and fixation, and the structures that were not reconstructed. 3.9.8 covers the medial reconstruction biomechanics.

Radiographs show the tunnels and the fixation, and magnetic resonance imaging shows the grafts. 5.3.2 covers the lateral view.

It matters because techniques reconstruct different combinations of the medial layers, so without the technique the appearance cannot be classified. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

How It Is Acquired

Establish which operation was performed. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

State the interval, since grafts remodel. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative protocol.

Assess each graft from fixation to fixation. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

Assess the tunnels and whether any converges with a cruciate tunnel. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Assess the meniscocapsular junction, which the approach passes and which may have been repaired. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

What Normal Looks Like

Grafts continuous between their fixation points. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

Tunnels in the femur and the tibia with no convergence. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Fixation in position with no migration. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

A meniscus in position with its attachment restored. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

Signal around the tunnels appropriate to the interval. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative protocol.

What Abnormal Looks Like

A graft discontinuous or pulled off its fixation. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

Tunnel convergence with a cruciate tunnel. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Fixation migration or breakage. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

Persisting or new meniscal extrusion. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

A medial side structurally intact in a knee with recurrent rotatory laxity. 5.9.6 covers the AMRI imaging.

Key Learning Points

  • Techniques reconstruct different combinations of layers.
  • The technique decides what should be present.
  • Tunnel convergence is a technical finding.
  • The meniscal attachment is part of the assessment.
  • An intact construct with laxity points elsewhere.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer asks which layers were reconstructed.

The expected points are the operation, the interval, each graft, the tunnels and convergence, and the meniscal attachment.

Say that the reconstruction restores function rather than anatomy. 3.9.8 covers the medial reconstruction biomechanics.

Say that convergence with a cruciate tunnel is worth reporting whether or not it is symptomatic. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Say that the examination reports the result. 4.12.8 covers the post-reconstruction assessment.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the medial side's three layers do different jobs, so a reconstruction substitutes a graft for one or two of those jobs and leaves the rest, and the imaging describes a construct with no native equivalent. 3.9.9 covers the contributions at different flexion angles.

That makes the expected appearance a property of the technique, so the operative detail is part of the imaging task. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

Extrusion after a medial reconstruction raises whether the meniscal attachment was restored, which is a separate question from the graft. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

Tunnel convergence is created at the index operation and limits a revision. 5.7.14 covers the revision ACL imaging.

How Good It Is

Imaging is a good test of this construct's integrity and no test of the rotational result.

Its best contribution is graft continuity, the tunnel positions and their convergence, and the meniscal attachment. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

It is limited because the expected appearance is technique specific and unpublished, and because the rotational result is clinical. 4.12.8 covers the post-reconstruction assessment.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no tunnel positions, graft dimensions or extrusion values appear either: those are published quantities tied to particular techniques and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • A reconstruction substitutes a graft for one or two jobs.
  • The expected appearance is a property of the technique.
  • Extrusion raises the attachment rather than the graft.
  • Convergence limits a revision.
  • The rotational result is clinical.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the imaging separates a technical failure from an unaddressed problem. 5.9.5 covers posteromedial corner injuries.

A discontinuous graft or migrated fixation is technical. 5.5.6 covers the bones.

An intact construct with persisting laxity points at the layers not reconstructed, the cruciate or the alignment. 5.9.6 covers the AMRI imaging.

Valgus alignment loads the reconstruction and belongs in a failure assessment. 5.17.4 covers the osteotomy.

A revision is planned around the existing tunnels. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting against an anatomy the operation did not restore. 3.9.8 covers the medial reconstruction biomechanics.

Expected peritunnel signal is read as loosening. 5.5.3 covers the post-operative protocol.

A graft running an unfamiliar course is reported as displaced. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

Extrusion is attributed to the reconstruction rather than to an unrestored attachment. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

Convergence is omitted because it was not looked for. 5.7.12 covers the tunnel assessment.

Key Learning Points

  • Reporting against unrestored anatomy is the error.
  • Peritunnel signal is read as loosening.
  • An unfamiliar graft course is read as displacement.
  • Extrusion is misattributed.
  • Convergence is omitted when it is not sought.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the medial side was reconstructed elsewhere and the technique is unknown. 5.20.7 covers reporting the post-operative knee.

Without it the report can describe the construct and cannot say whether it is as intended, which is the closing position the lateral chapter reaches as well. 5.10.6 covers the post-operative PLC imaging.

The pre-operative study is the only record of which layers were injured. 5.9.5 covers posteromedial corner injuries.

Valgus alignment left uncorrected is a recognised reason these reconstructions stretch out. 5.3.6 covers the long leg alignment films.

An examination under anaesthesia reports the result that no imaging finding reports. 4.14.6 covers the examination under anaesthesia.

The useful report gives the operation if known, the interval, each graft, every tunnel with its convergence, the meniscal attachment and the alignment. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Key Learning Points

  • Without the technique the construct cannot be judged.
  • The lateral chapter reaches the same closing position.
  • The pre-operative study records which layers were injured.
  • Uncorrected valgus stretches these reconstructions.
  • The report names every tunnel and its convergence.
