The route a knee takes through imaging, which is a service design question as much as a clinical one.

Imaging Pathways

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

A pathway is the agreed order of investigations for a presenting problem, so it decides what gets done before anyone thinks about the individual knee. 5.1.3 covers choosing the investigation.

Its purpose is consistency and speed, and its cost is that it fits the common case and not the one in front of you. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

A pathway is visible in the request form, the referral criteria and what the department will accept. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

How It Is Acquired

Establish what the pathway is before working around it, since most knees are served well by it. 5.1.3 covers choosing the investigation.

Follow the usual order: radiographs first in almost every knee, then cross sectional imaging where the question needs it. 5.3.1 covers the AP view.

Record the clinical question so the pathway's default protocol can be changed where it needs to be. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Name the previous surgery, since a post operative knee needs a different protocol and often a different modality. 5.5.3 covers the post operative protocol.

Say what is urgent and why, because urgency is the part of a pathway that responds to a clinical reason.

Key Learning Points

  • A pathway decides what happens before anyone considers the individual knee.
  • It fits the common case and not necessarily this one.
  • Radiographs come first in almost every knee.
  • The clinical question is what changes a default protocol.
  • Urgency is the part of a pathway that answers to a reason.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the service question and the expected answer knows the normal route and when to leave it.

The expected points are the standard order, the referral criteria, the protocol modifiers, the urgency, and the cases the pathway does not serve.

Say that you would follow the pathway unless the question needs something it does not offer, and name an example. 5.1.3 covers choosing the investigation.

Say that an acute locked knee and a suspected infection are urgent for different reasons. 5.14.1 covers synovitis.

Say that a pathway without a radiograph is unusual and worth questioning. 5.3.1 covers the AP view.

How Good It Is

A pathway improves the average request and worsens the unusual one, which is the trade it exists to make.

Its best contribution is removing the variability in the common presentations, where most of the volume and most of the avoidable delay sit. 5.1.5 covers cost effectiveness.

It is weakest where a question falls outside its categories, because the pathway's default then answers a different question. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

No figures appear for any accuracy comparison on this page, under decision 028, and the comparison is the claim being made.

Key Learning Points

  • A pathway improves the average request and worsens the unusual one.
  • Its value is in removing variability from the common presentations.
  • It is weakest where the question falls outside its categories.
  • An acute locked knee and a suspected infection are urgent for different reasons.
  • A pathway with no radiograph is worth questioning.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the pathway is something to be designed as well as followed, and the design decisions are the interesting ones.

A pathway that sends every anterior knee pain to magnetic resonance imaging produces incidental findings at scale, which is a design fault rather than a clinical one. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

A pathway that omits weightbearing films answers the alignment question wrongly for every arthritic knee in it. 5.3.6 covers long leg alignment films.

A pathway that does not ask about previous surgery sends post operative knees through a protocol that cannot read them. 5.5.3 covers the post operative protocol.

A pathway's reporting template decides what the clinician is told, which makes the template part of the pathway rather than a detail of it. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is treating the pathway as the decision rather than the default. 5.1.3 covers choosing the investigation.

An individual knee is pushed into the nearest category, and the mismatch disappears once the report arrives.

Urgency is used to get a better scan rather than a faster one, which degrades the signal for everyone.

A pathway designed around availability rather than the question produces consistent answers to the wrong question. 5.1.5 covers cost effectiveness.

The pathway is invisible in the notes, so the reason a particular scan was done cannot be reconstructed later. 4.1.8 covers examination documentation.

Key Learning Points

  • Treating the pathway as the decision rather than the default is the commonest error.
  • A knee pushed into the nearest category loses the mismatch on the way.
  • Urgency used to get a better scan degrades the signal for everyone.
  • A pathway built around availability answers the wrong question consistently.
  • The pathway is invisible in the notes afterwards.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level most patients arrive having already been through somebody else's pathway, and that is the thing to reason about.

The imaging that exists was produced by a pathway you did not design, for a question you did not ask, on a protocol you cannot see. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Repeating it is expensive and sometimes necessary, and the decision is made against what the previous study could not answer. 5.1.5 covers cost effectiveness.

Pathways differ between centres, so a tertiary clinic sees the cases every local pathway failed, which biases the impression of how well pathways work. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

A pathway optimised for throughput and one optimised for the complex case pull in opposite directions, and a tertiary service needs the second while sitting inside the first.

The reporting template is the part of a pathway hardest to change and the part that most shapes what the clinician does. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Key Learning Points

  • Most patients arrive through somebody else's pathway.
  • The existing imaging answered a question you did not ask.
  • Repeating is decided against what the last study could not answer.
  • A tertiary clinic sees the cases local pathways failed, which biases the view.
  • The reporting template is hardest to change and shapes the most.
