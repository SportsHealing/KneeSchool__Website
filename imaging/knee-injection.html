Knee Injection

Page 5.4.16 · Ultrasound of the Knee · imaging page

Putting a needle where it was aimed, which is the one thing guidance reliably improves.

Knee Injection

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

An ultrasound guided injection images the needle and the target together while the needle advances. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

A needle is highly reflective, so it is one of the most conspicuous structures ultrasound shows. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

It matters because guidance improves where the injectate goes, and whether the injection helps is a separate question owned by Section 6. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

How It Is Acquired

Identify the target and plan the approach before the needle is introduced. 5.4.1 covers the anterior knee.

Keep the needle in the plane of the beam, because a needle out of plane appears as a dot rather than a line. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

Watch the injectate spread, which confirms the needle tip is where it appears to be. 5.4.18 covers hydrodissection.

Avoid the vessels and nerves on the planned path, which is why the pre-scan matters. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Record the confirmation of correct placement rather than only the approach. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

What Normal Looks Like

A bright linear needle visible along its length in the plane of the beam. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

Injectate spreading into the intended compartment, which is the confirmation. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

No vessel or nerve on the needle path. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

A target that was identified before the needle entered. 5.4.1 covers the anterior knee.

What Abnormal Looks Like

A needle visible as a dot, which means it is out of the beam's plane and its tip position is unknown. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

Injectate not spreading where expected, which means the tip is elsewhere. 5.4.18 covers hydrodissection.

Injectate within a tendon rather than around it, which is a specific hazard. 5.4.20 covers tendon procedures.

Resistance with no visible spread, which should stop the injection. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

Key Learning Points

  • The needle and the target are imaged together in real time.
  • A needle is one of the most conspicuous structures ultrasound shows.
  • A needle out of the beam's plane appears as a dot.
  • Watching the injectate spread confirms the tip position.
  • Whether the injection helps is a separate question.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer separates accuracy of placement from efficacy of treatment.

The expected points are the pre-scan, the in plane needle, the injectate spread, the vessels and nerves, and the recorded confirmation.

Say that guidance improves placement accuracy. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

Say that improved placement does not by itself establish improved outcome. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

Say that the spread of injectate is the tip confirmation. 5.4.18 covers hydrodissection.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that a needle is a strong specular reflector, so it returns a bright echo when the beam strikes it squarely along its length and almost nothing when it crosses it obliquely. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

That is why in plane technique is the whole skill: the alternative is a tip whose depth is inferred rather than seen. 5.2.9 covers refraction.

Watching the injectate is the independent check, because a bright line can be the shaft with the tip somewhere else. 5.4.18 covers hydrodissection.

Guidance's established benefit is anatomical accuracy, and extrapolating from accuracy to outcome is the inference this page declines to make. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

How Good It Is

Ultrasound guidance places a needle more accurately than a landmark based approach and does not establish that the injection works.

Its best contribution is accurate placement into a small or deep target, and the real time confirmation that the injectate went there. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

It is limited to the placement question, because whether the agent helps belongs to the treatment literature rather than to imaging. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no placement accuracy percentages appear either: those are published quantities and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • A needle returns a bright echo only when struck along its length.
  • In plane technique is the whole skill.
  • A bright line can be the shaft with the tip elsewhere.
  • Watching the injectate is the independent check.
  • Accuracy of placement does not establish efficacy of treatment.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level guidance matters most where the target is small, deep or next to something that must be avoided.

A small bursa or a tendon sheath is a target where a landmark approach misses often enough to matter. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

A target adjacent to a vessel or nerve makes guidance a safety measure rather than an accuracy one. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

A large joint effusion is a target a landmark approach reaches reliably, so guidance adds less. 5.4.17 covers aspiration.

Whether to inject at all, and with what, is a treatment decision this section does not own. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is injecting with the needle out of plane and assuming the tip position. 5.2.8 covers reflection.

The injectate spread is not watched, so misplacement is not detected. 5.4.18 covers hydrodissection.

A pre-scan is skipped and a vessel is on the path. 5.2.11 covers Doppler imaging.

Accuracy of placement is reported as evidence that the treatment worked. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

An intratendinous injection is given where a peritendinous one was intended. 5.4.20 covers tendon procedures.

Key Learning Points

  • The needle is out of plane and the tip position is assumed.
  • The injectate spread is not watched.
  • A pre-scan is skipped and a vessel is on the path.
  • Placement accuracy is reported as treatment efficacy.
  • An intratendinous injection is given unintentionally.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level guidance is an established improvement to a procedure whose own value is often the unsettled part.

The accuracy benefit over a landmark approach is reasonably established, and it is an anatomical endpoint, so a more accurate injection of an agent that does not work is a more accurate nothing. 5.1.7 covers evidence based imaging.

That makes guidance a technique improvement whose clinical value depends entirely on the agent and the indication, neither of which this section owns. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Guidance also changes what is counted as the procedure, because the pre-scan frequently changes the target or finds a reason not to inject, and that diagnostic contribution is rarely recorded. 5.4.1 covers the anterior knee.

Nothing in the record distinguishes a guided injection confirmed by injectate spread from one where a needle was merely visualised, which is the same recording weakness this chapter keeps finding. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

The defensible position is that guidance should be used where the target is small or hazardous, and that the imaging claim stops at placement. 5.5.12 covers the bursa.

Key Learning Points

  • The accuracy benefit is established and is an anatomical endpoint.
  • A more accurate injection of an ineffective agent is a more accurate nothing.
  • Guidance's value depends on the agent and the indication.
  • The pre-scan's diagnostic contribution is rarely recorded.
  • The imaging claim stops at placement.
