What It Shows

This page's subject is a named research programme at a named institution, which makes it the only page in the section whose content is a claim about a real organisation. 5.19.7 covers the current research.

The site holds no verified information about that programme. Evidence verification has not run, so nothing here has been checked against a source. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

Writing an investigator, a date, a grant, a trial name or a result from memory would be the same class of failure as an invented citation, and the Operations Handbook treats that as critical rather than untidy. 5.19.7 covers the current research.

The page therefore carries none of those things. What follows is what such a programme consists of and what a reader should ask of it. 5.19.1 covers the physics of magic angle imaging.

How It Is Acquired

A research programme on this subject would be built from a small number of identifiable parts, and naming them lets a reader judge the real one when they reach it.

A sequence development strand, because using orientation as a measurement needs acquisition that controls it. 5.2.21 covers the advanced MRI techniques.

A positioning strand, because the angle has to be set deliberately rather than accepted from the couch. 5.18.5 covers weight-bearing MRI.

A validation strand, comparing the imaging measure against tissue examined directly. 5.19.2 covers the collagen architecture.

A clinical strand, asking whether the measure changes a decision rather than whether it correlates with one. 5.19.9 covers the clinical translation.

Key Learning Points

  • This is the section's only page whose subject is a real organisation.
  • The site holds no verified information about the programme.
  • An investigator or result written from memory is a critical failure.
  • The page carries no name, date, grant or result.
  • It describes the parts such a programme would have instead.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva no candidate is asked about a particular department's programme, which is a fair indication of where this page sits in the curriculum.

The expected points, if the subject arises at all, are the distinction between a mechanism and a validated measurement, and the questions that distinction generates.

Say that an institutional programme is judged by its validation strand rather than by its sequence work. 5.18.1 covers quantitative MRI.

Say that a measure correlating with disease is not yet a measure that changes management. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

Say that this page states no finding because none has been checked. 5.19.7 covers the current research.

How Good It Is

As a page this one is a declared gap, and it is published as a gap rather than filled with plausible content.

Its best contribution is the question list, which is usable against any programme in this field and does not depend on which programme it is. 5.19.9 covers the clinical translation.

It is limited completely on its nominal subject. A reader who came here to learn what this programme has done will not find out. 5.19.7 covers the current research.

It is limited in a way the rest of the section is not, because its siblings can stand on standard teaching and a page about one organisation's work cannot. 5.1.6 covers imaging pitfalls.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no date, publication count, cohort size, funding figure or result appears either: in this environment every one of them would be invented.

Key Learning Points

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level this page is the clearest instance in the site of a constraint that applies everywhere and is usually invisible.

Every other page in the section is written from standard teaching, which is weaker than a cited source and strong enough to be useful. This page has no standard teaching to fall back on, because one department's programme is not standard teaching. 5.19.7 covers the current research.

The constraint is the same one that leaves the whole section uncited, and it shows here because the subject is specific rather than general. 5.1.7 covers evidence-based imaging.

The remedy is the same too: source access, then evidence verification, then a consultant's signature, in that order. 5.19.9 covers the clinical translation.
