Subchondral Sclerosis

Page 5.3.19 · Plain Radiography · imaging page

Denser bone under a loaded surface, which is the bone responding to the load it now carries.

Subchondral Sclerosis

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Subchondral sclerosis is increased bone density immediately beneath an articular surface. 5.3.1 covers the anteroposterior view.

It appears where the surface carries more load than it used to, so it marks the loaded compartment. 5.3.20 covers the alignment changes.

It matters because it is a bone response to mechanics, which makes it the radiographic feature most directly about load. 3.4.9 covers the force transmission.

How It Is Acquired

Assess the subchondral region on the standing anteroposterior view, in the compartment that is narrowed. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

Compare with the opposite compartment, which is the reference that needs no calibration. 5.2.5 covers magnification.

Look for subchondral cysts alongside it, which accompany advanced change. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Remember that apparent density depends on exposure and post-processing, so comparison within one film beats comparison between films. 5.2.1 covers the generation of X-rays.

Assess the patellofemoral compartment separately on the skyline view. 5.3.3 covers the skyline view.

What Normal Looks Like

A thin, even subchondral line of uniform density across both compartments.

No focal density increase beneath either surface.

No subchondral cysts. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Symmetry between compartments and with the other knee. 5.3.20 covers the alignment changes.

What Abnormal Looks Like

Increased density beneath the narrowed compartment's surface. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

A thickened subchondral plate with loss of the normal thin line.

Subchondral cysts, which indicate advanced change. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Flattening or collapse of the subchondral contour in advanced disease. 5.13.5 covers osteonecrosis.

Key Learning Points

  • Sclerosis is increased density immediately beneath a surface.
  • It appears where the surface carries more load than before.
  • It is the radiographic feature most directly about load.
  • Apparent density depends on exposure and post-processing.
  • Comparison within one film beats comparison between films.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected answer links sclerosis to load and warns about comparing density across films.

The expected points are the narrowed compartment, the contralateral comparison, the cysts, the exposure dependence, and the patellofemoral assessment.

Say that density is not comparable between studies from different departments. 5.2.2 covers the image formation.

Say that sclerosis marks which compartment is loaded. 5.3.20 covers the alignment changes.

Say that the magnetic resonance equivalent is the subchondral marrow lesion, which is a different measurement of a related thing. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that bone adapts its density to the load it carries, so sclerosis is the bone's record of a mechanical change rather than a disease process of its own. 3.4.9 covers the force transmission.

That makes it a confirmatory feature: it should sit in the compartment the alignment loads and the joint space has narrowed. 5.3.7 covers the mechanical axis.

Sclerosis without narrowing or malalignment is therefore discordant and worth a second thought, because the load explanation is absent. 5.13.5 covers osteonecrosis.

Subchondral cysts form where the plate is breached, which is why they indicate advanced change rather than a separate lesion. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

How Good It Is

Sclerosis is a reliable qualitative sign of a loaded compartment and a poor quantitative measure of anything.

Its best contribution is confirming the mechanical story that the alignment and the joint space already tell. 5.3.7 covers the mechanical axis.

It is limited by density on a radiograph depending on acquisition and processing, so it cannot be tracked over time or between departments. 5.2.2 covers the image formation.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no density or thickness values appear either: radiographic density is not a calibrated quantity and this environment cannot check published measures.

Key Learning Points

  • Bone adapts its density to the load it carries.
  • Sclerosis is a record of mechanical change, not a disease of its own.
  • It should sit where the alignment loads and the space has narrowed.
  • Sclerosis without narrowing or malalignment is discordant.
  • Cysts form where the plate is breached and indicate advanced change.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level sclerosis is a cross check on the mechanical interpretation rather than a decision input of its own.

Sclerosis in the compartment the alignment loads supports the realignment argument, because the bone agrees with the axis. 5.16.5 covers osteotomy planning.

Sclerosis in a compartment the alignment does not load is discordant and should prompt a different explanation. 5.13.5 covers osteonecrosis.

A collapsed subchondral contour changes the diagnosis from osteoarthritis to osteonecrosis or insufficiency, which changes the operation entirely. 5.13.4 covers insufficiency fractures.

Extensive subchondral cysts affect fixation and bone stock in a replacement, which is a planning consideration. 5.16.6 covers arthroplasty planning.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is comparing density between films from different departments. 5.2.2 covers the image formation.

Post-processing changes the apparent density and the change is read as progression. 5.2.1 covers the generation of X-rays.

A subchondral collapse is read as advanced osteoarthritis rather than osteonecrosis. 5.13.5 covers osteonecrosis.

Sclerosis is used as a severity grade. 5.3.16 covers Kellgren-Lawrence grading.

Subchondral cysts are reported as separate lesions. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Key Learning Points

  • Density is compared between films from different departments.
  • Post-processing change is read as progression.
  • A collapse is read as osteoarthritis rather than osteonecrosis.
  • Sclerosis is used as a severity grade.
  • Cysts are reported as separate lesions.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level subchondral bone is where radiography and magnetic resonance imaging describe the same tissue in incompatible terms.

Radiographic sclerosis and the magnetic resonance subchondral marrow lesion occur in the same place and measure different properties, and the relationship between them is not simple. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

The marrow lesion carries prognostic associations in osteoarthritis and the radiographic sclerosis does not, despite sclerosis being the longer established finding. 5.21.5 covers the osteoarthritis imaging literature.

Radiographic density is not calibrated, so it cannot be used longitudinally at all, which rules out the one use that would make it a mechanical measure. 5.2.2 covers the image formation.

Computed tomography does measure density quantitatively and is not used for this question in routine knee practice, so a calibrated option exists and is unused. 5.2.23 covers the computed tomography reconstruction.

The sensible position is to read sclerosis as confirmation of a mechanical story and to go to cross sectional imaging for anything quantitative. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Key Learning Points

  • Sclerosis and the marrow lesion are the same place and different properties.
  • The marrow lesion carries prognostic associations and sclerosis does not.
  • Radiographic density is uncalibrated and cannot be used longitudinally.
  • Computed tomography measures density quantitatively and is unused here.
  • Read sclerosis as confirmation and go cross sectional for measurement.
