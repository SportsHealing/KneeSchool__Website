Reconstruction

Page 5.2.23 · Imaging Physics for Knee Specialists · imaging page

Turning the measurements into an image, which is where most of the modern improvement in computed tomography has happened.

Pipeline status. Editorial draft. Evidence verification did not run. Not consultant reviewed. Not for clinical use.

Reconstruction

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

Reconstruction is the computation that converts transmission measurements into a cross sectional image. 5.2.22 covers the computed tomography acquisition.

The same raw data can be reconstructed in different ways, producing images that look different and support different measurements. 5.2.25 covers three dimensional reconstruction.

It matters because the reconstruction choices, not the acquisition, determine much of what a modern knee computed tomography study can show. 5.16.1 covers the fracture assessment.

How It Is Acquired

A reconstruction kernel is chosen, sharp for bone detail and smooth for soft tissue, and both can be produced from one acquisition. 5.16.2 covers tibial plateau fractures.

Slice thickness is chosen at reconstruction, so thin slices can be produced retrospectively from a volume. 5.2.25 covers three dimensional reconstruction.

Iterative methods reduce noise, which allows a lower dose acquisition for the same image. 5.2.24 covers the dose in computed tomography.

Metal artefact reduction is applied here, and it removes streaks while altering the bone immediately around the metal. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

Dual energy acquisitions allow material separation at reconstruction, which is how crystal deposits can be characterised. 5.14.4 covers crystal arthropathy.

Key Learning Points

  • Reconstruction converts transmission measurements into an image.
  • One acquisition supports several reconstructions.
  • The kernel is chosen sharp for bone or smooth for soft tissue.
  • Iterative methods reduce noise and allow lower dose.
  • Metal artefact reduction alters the bone around the metal.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the page behind the answer that a computed tomography study can be reprocessed without rescanning.

The expected points are the kernel, the retrospective slice thickness, iterative reconstruction, metal artefact reduction, and dual energy material separation.

Say that a sharper kernel can be requested without a repeat study. 5.16.2 covers tibial plateau fractures.

Say that metal artefact reduction changes the appearance of bone near metal. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

Say that dual energy can characterise crystal deposition. 5.14.4 covers crystal arthropathy.

How Good It Is

Reconstruction is where a computed tomography study is salvaged or improved, and it is almost never asked for.

Its best contribution is that a study already acquired can answer a second question without more dose. 5.2.24 covers the dose in computed tomography.

It is limited by metal artefact reduction introducing its own distortion, which is a trade rather than a fix. 5.7.14 covers revision cruciate imaging.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no kernel or slice values appear either: those are vendor specific protocol quantities and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • A study already acquired can answer a second question without more dose.
  • A sharper kernel can be requested without a repeat.
  • Metal artefact reduction introduces its own distortion.
  • Dual energy can characterise crystal deposition.
  • Reconstruction is almost never asked for.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level knowing that reconstruction can be revisited changes what to do with an inadequate study.

A study reported as limited by metal can often be reprocessed with artefact reduction, which is faster and cheaper than a repeat. 5.7.14 covers revision cruciate imaging.

A fracture needing finer cortical detail can be re-reconstructed with a sharper kernel from the same data. 5.16.2 covers tibial plateau fractures.

A planning request made after the diagnostic study can usually be met from the stored volume. 5.16.6 covers arthroplasty planning.

Bone immediately adjacent to metal is altered by the reduction algorithm, so tunnel wall and implant interface assessment needs both versions. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is repeating a study that could have been reprocessed. 5.2.24 covers the dose in computed tomography.

Metal artefact reduction output is read as unaltered anatomy. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

A smooth kernel study is used for cortical assessment. 5.16.2 covers tibial plateau fractures.

Noise from an iterative reconstruction has a different texture and is read as disease. 5.3.19 covers subchondral sclerosis.

Measurements are compared between studies reconstructed differently. 5.16.3 covers rotational profiles.

Key Learning Points

  • A study is repeated when it could have been reprocessed.
  • Artefact reduction output is read as unaltered anatomy.
  • A smooth kernel study is used for cortical assessment.
  • Iterative noise texture is read as disease.
  • Measurements are compared across different reconstructions.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level reconstruction is the part of computed tomography that most affects a measurement and least appears in a report.

Iterative and deep learning reconstruction change noise texture and edge behaviour, so a measurement at a bone margin is reconstruction dependent. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

Metal artefact reduction alters the bone it is applied to, which means tunnel widening and implant interface measurements are being made on modified data. 5.7.12 covers tunnel assessment.

The published conventions for rotational and tunnel measurement predate all of this, so a figure from the literature and a figure from a modern study are not the same measurement. 5.16.3 covers rotational profiles.

Deep learning reconstruction can produce plausible structure that was not in the data, which is a different class of error from noise and is not visible as one. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

The discipline this argues for is that a planning measurement should name the reconstruction it was made on, which no report currently does. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Key Learning Points

  • Noise texture and edge behaviour are reconstruction dependent.
  • Artefact reduction means tunnel measurements are made on modified data.
  • Published conventions predate modern reconstruction.
  • Deep learning reconstruction can produce plausible absent structure.
  • A planning measurement should name its reconstruction.
