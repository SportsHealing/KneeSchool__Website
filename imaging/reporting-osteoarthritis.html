Reporting OA

A grade that describes the film rather than the patient, and the compartment pattern that decides the operation.

Reporting OA

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What the Report Is For

An osteoarthritis report states which compartments are affected, how severely, and what the alignment is. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

It is built from weight bearing radiographs rather than from a scan. 5.3.5 covers the Rosenberg view.

It matters because the compartment pattern rather than the severity decides which operations are available. 5.3.20 covers the alignment changes.

How the Report Is Built

Report each compartment separately, the patellofemoral joint included. 5.12.7 covers the patellofemoral arthritis.

State the loading conditions and the view each film was taken with. 5.3.5 covers the Rosenberg view.

Report the osteophytes, the sclerosis and the cysts as findings of their own. 5.3.18 covers osteophytes.

Report the alignment from a long leg film wherever the question is surgical. 5.3.6 covers the long leg alignment films.

Name the grading system if a grade is given at all. 5.3.16 covers the Kellgren-Lawrence grading.

What a Good One Looks Like

Three compartments reported separately with the pattern stated. 5.12.7 covers the patellofemoral arthritis.

The loading conditions and the views stated in the technique. 5.3.5 covers the Rosenberg view.

The individual features reported rather than collapsed into a grade. 5.3.19 covers subchondral sclerosis.

The alignment given with the film it was measured on. 5.3.6 covers the long leg alignment films.

A conclusion naming the pattern rather than the word osteoarthritis alone. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

What a Poor One Looks Like

A grade with no compartment pattern behind it. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

An unloaded film reported as though the space it shows were the loaded one. 5.3.1 covers the AP view.

The patellofemoral joint omitted because no skyline view was taken. 5.3.3 covers the skyline view.

Alignment inferred from a short film. 5.3.6 covers the long leg alignment films.

Magnetic resonance findings reported as the severity of the arthritis. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Key Learning Points

  • The compartment pattern decides which operations are available.
  • Weight bearing films carry the joint space question.
  • The loading conditions belong in the technique.
  • The patellofemoral joint needs a view of its own.
  • A grade with no pattern behind it says little.

MRCS Level

How the Report Is Built

In a viva the expected answer reports the pattern and the alignment rather than a grade.

The expected points are the three compartments, the loading conditions, the individual features, the alignment, and the named system.

Say that a grading system compresses several features into one number and loses the pattern. 5.3.16 covers the Kellgren-Lawrence grading.

Say that joint space on an unloaded film is not the loaded space. 5.3.5 covers the Rosenberg view.

Say that symptoms and radiographic severity correspond loosely. 3.12.9 covers patellofemoral pain.

What a Good One Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that an operation is chosen by which compartments are spared, so a report giving severity without pattern answers the wrong question. 5.3.20 covers the alignment changes.

A flexion weight bearing view reaches the posterior condyle where loss begins, which a standing extended film can miss. 5.3.5 covers the Rosenberg view.

Alignment decides whether the wear has a mechanical cause, which changes the conversation entirely. 5.3.7 covers the mechanical axis.

Osteophytes, sclerosis and cysts carry different information and a single grade discards it. 5.3.18 covers osteophytes.

How Good It Is

Weight bearing radiography is the best available test of the osteoarthritic knee and says nothing about the symptoms.

Its best contribution is the compartment pattern and the alignment, under load and cheaply. 5.3.6 covers the long leg alignment films.

It is limited by positioning, by the single flexion angle each view gives, and by the loose relation between appearance and symptoms. 5.21.5 covers the osteoarthritis imaging literature.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no joint space measurements or grade boundaries appear either: those are published quantities measured under particular conditions and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • Operations are chosen by which compartments are spared.
  • A flexion weight bearing view reaches the posterior condyle.
  • Alignment decides whether the wear has a mechanical cause.
  • A single grade discards the individual features.
  • Appearance and symptoms correspond loosely.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the report should let a surgeon choose between an osteotomy, a partial replacement and a total replacement without looking further. 5.16.5 covers osteotomy planning.

That needs the pattern, the alignment and the state of the other compartments. 5.16.6 covers arthroplasty planning.

A spared compartment matters as much as an affected one and is the finding most often left unstated. 5.3.17 covers joint space narrowing.

Magnetic resonance imaging is rarely needed here and is requested when the radiograph and the symptoms disagree. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

A report that grades and stops leaves the surgeon to read the films, which was the point of the report. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting a scan's findings as the severity of established arthritis. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Marrow oedema is described in a vocabulary that suggests injury. 5.13.1 covers bone marrow oedema.

A meniscal tear in an arthritic knee is reported as a surgical target. 5.6.2 covers the meniscal signal grading.

Narrowing on an unloaded film is read as preserved space. 5.3.1 covers the AP view.

An inflammatory arthropathy is reported as osteoarthritis because the pattern was not considered. 5.14.2 covers rheumatoid arthritis.

Key Learning Points

  • Scan findings are reported as arthritis severity.
  • Marrow oedema is described as though it were an injury.
  • A meniscal tear in an arthritic knee is not a target.
  • An unloaded film understates narrowing.
  • An inflammatory pattern is reported as osteoarthritis.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the films arrive from several departments and were taken in several positions, so the series documents radiography as much as progression. 5.3.5 covers the Rosenberg view.

Progression measured across differently positioned films is not progression. 5.21.5 covers the osteoarthritis imaging literature.

A grade recorded years ago cannot be compared with a different system used today. 5.3.16 covers the Kellgren-Lawrence grading.

Quantitative measures of cartilage and bone are offered for trials and are not decision grade in a clinic. 5.18.2 covers T2 mapping.

Automated grading reproduces the compression that made grades unhelpful, now at scale. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

The useful report gives each compartment, the loading conditions and views, the individual features, the alignment with its film, and the spared compartments stated as spared. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Key Learning Points

  • A series across departments documents radiography as much as progression.
  • Differently positioned films cannot show progression.
  • Grades from different systems are not comparable.
  • Quantitative measures are not decision grade in a clinic.
  • Spared compartments should be stated as spared.
