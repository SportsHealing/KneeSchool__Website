Reporting Meniscal Tears

The four facts that decide repair or resection, and the report that gives a tear with none of them.

Reporting Meniscal Tears

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What the Report Is For

A meniscal tear report states the pattern, the location, the extent and whether any fragment has displaced. 5.6.3 covers horizontal tears.

It is built from signal and morphology across all three planes rather than from one image. 5.6.2 covers the meniscal signal grading.

It matters because repair is possible for some patterns in some locations and not for others, so the words decide the operation. 5.17.2 covers the meniscal repair.

How the Report Is Built

Describe the pattern in the recognised terms rather than as a tear. 5.6.5 covers radial tears.

Locate it by meniscus, by horn and by zone, since the blood supply varies across the zones. 2.5.3 covers the vascular supply.

State the extent and whether the tear reaches a surface. 5.6.4 covers vertical tears.

State whether a fragment is displaced and where it has gone. 5.6.6 covers bucket handle tears.

Report the root separately, because a root tear behaves unlike a body tear. 5.6.9 covers root tears.

What a Good One Looks Like

A named pattern, a named location and a stated extent in the findings. 5.6.8 covers complex tears.

The surfaces involved stated explicitly. 5.6.3 covers horizontal tears.

The root and the meniscocapsular junction addressed as their own items. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

Extrusion reported with the convention used to measure it. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

A conclusion that says whether the appearance is repairable in principle. 5.17.2 covers the meniscal repair.

What a Poor One Looks Like

A report that says meniscal tear with no pattern and no location. 5.20.8 covers common reporting errors.

Internal signal change reported as a tear where no surface is reached. 5.6.2 covers the meniscal signal grading.

A root tear buried inside a sentence about the posterior horn. 5.6.9 covers root tears.

A ramp lesion missed because the posteromedial corner was not examined. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

A displaced fragment described without saying where it now lies. 5.6.6 covers bucket handle tears.

Key Learning Points

  • Pattern, location, extent and displacement are the four facts.
  • Zone matters because the blood supply varies across the meniscus.
  • A root tear is reported as its own item.
  • Internal signal reaching no surface is not a tear.
  • The report should say whether repair is possible in principle.

MRCS Level

How the Report Is Built

In a viva the expected answer gives the four facts before any opinion.

The expected points are the pattern, the location by zone, the extent and the surfaces, the displacement, and the root.

Say that the report should name the plane each finding was seen on. 5.5.8 covers the menisci.

Say that a tear seen on a single image is reported with that limitation. 5.6.2 covers the meniscal signal grading.

Say that extrusion is a separate finding from the tear. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

What a Poor One Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the pattern maps onto a mechanical consequence, so a radial tear and a horizontal tear of the same length mean different things. 3.6.12 covers the effects of radial tears.

A tear in the vascular zone can heal and one in the avascular zone cannot, which makes the zone the most consequential word in the report. 2.5.3 covers the vascular supply.

A displaced fragment produces mechanical symptoms, and its position decides the approach. 4.2.5 covers locking.

Extrusion changes load transmission whatever the tear's pattern. 3.6.10 covers meniscal extrusion.

How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance reporting of the meniscus is the most reliable soft tissue reporting in the knee and is still weakest on the findings that decide repair.

Its best contribution is the presence of a tear and its location. 5.6.1 covers the normal meniscus.

It is limited by pattern classification, by root and ramp detection, and by signal change reaching no surface being called a tear. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no agreement statistics for tear classification appear either: those are published quantities from particular reader panels and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • The pattern maps onto a mechanical consequence.
  • The zone decides whether healing is possible.
  • A fragment's position decides the approach.
  • Extrusion is a separate finding from the tear.
  • Pattern classification is the weakest part of the report.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the report either supports a repair discussion or it does not. 5.17.2 covers the meniscal repair.

A vertical tear in the peripheral zone with a stated length supports one. 5.6.4 covers vertical tears.

A degenerate horizontal cleavage tear in an older knee does not, and saying so plainly is useful. 5.6.3 covers horizontal tears.

A root tear changes the operation and the prognosis, so it belongs in the conclusion rather than the findings. 5.6.9 covers root tears.

Extrusion with intact tissue raises a different question from a tear and should not be merged with one. 5.6.11 covers meniscal extrusion.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting degenerative signal in an older knee as a tear that explains the symptoms. 5.6.2 covers the meniscal signal grading.

A post-operative meniscus is reported as re-torn when the appearance is expected. 5.6.12 covers the post-operative meniscus.

A ramp lesion is absent from the report because it was absent from the search. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

A discoid meniscus is reported as a displaced fragment. 5.15.4 covers the discoid meniscus.

The tear is reported and the chondral surface it sits against is not. 5.20.5 covers reporting cartilage lesions.

Key Learning Points

  • Degenerative signal is reported as a symptomatic tear.
  • An expected post-operative appearance is called a re-tear.
  • A lesion absent from the search is absent from the report.
  • A discoid meniscus mimics a displaced fragment.
  • The cartilage against the tear belongs in the same report.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the referral arrives with a report that has already set the patient's expectation of an operation. 5.1.2 covers clinical examination versus imaging.

The words repairable and irreparable appear in reports although neither is a radiological finding. 5.17.2 covers the meniscal repair.

A report from a department that does not treat ramp lesions rarely mentions them, so absence of the words is not absence of the lesion. 5.6.10 covers ramp lesions.

After a repair no agreed normal appearance exists, so a scan read against an intact meniscus reports failure in a healed one. 5.6.13 covers the meniscal repair imaging.

Automated meniscal reporting is trained on classifications that readers disagree about, so it reproduces the disagreement with added confidence. 5.21.6 covers the artificial intelligence literature.

The useful report gives the four facts, the root, the extrusion, the cartilage, and a statement of what the sequences could not assess. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

Key Learning Points

  • Repairable is not a radiological finding.
  • Absence of the words is not absence of the lesion.
  • A repaired meniscus has no agreed normal appearance.
  • Automated reporting reproduces reader disagreement.
  • The report should state what could not be assessed.
