Normal ACL

Page 5.7.1 · ACL MRI · imaging page

The normal anterior cruciate ligament on magnetic resonance imaging, which has to be known before an abnormal one means anything.

Normal ACL

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

The anterior cruciate ligament runs from the medial wall of the lateral femoral condyle to the tibia in front of the intercondylar eminence. 2.7.1 covers the gross anatomy.

On magnetic resonance imaging it is a dark band of fibres with thin bright lines between them, which is normal rather than degenerate tissue. 2.7.11 covers its MRI anatomy.

It matters because a ligament made of separate bundles can look split or striated in health, and that appearance is the commonest reason a normal cruciate is called abnormal. 2.7.8 covers the anteromedial bundle.

How It Is Acquired

Look at the sagittal images through the intercondylar notch, where the ligament runs along its own length. 5.5.1 covers the standard protocol.

Confirm on the coronal and axial images, because the sagittal plane alone cuts the ligament obliquely and can make it disappear. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

Use a proton density or intermediate weighted sequence for the fibre pattern and a fluid sensitive one for oedema. 5.2.15 covers proton density imaging.

Follow the ligament from the femoral attachment to the tibial attachment without losing it, which is what a normal reading requires. 2.7.6 covers the femoral footprint.

Assess its line against the roof of the notch, because a normal ligament runs parallel to it. 2.7.12 covers the surgical anatomy.

What Normal Looks Like

A continuous dark band running from the lateral femoral condyle to the tibia, parallel to the roof of the intercondylar notch.

Fine bright striations between the fibre bundles, giving the ligament a striped appearance along its length. 2.7.9 covers the posterolateral bundle.

A slightly fanned tibial attachment, which is broader than the femoral end. 2.7.7 covers the tibial footprint.

Some increased signal near the tibial attachment in an otherwise normal ligament, which is a recognised normal appearance. 5.2.20 covers the magic angle effect.

What Abnormal Looks Like

Loss of the continuous dark band, with fluid signal filling its place.

A ligament that no longer runs parallel to the notch roof, which is the line sign rather than a signal change. 5.7.3 covers the complete tears.

Fibres that stop short of either attachment, leaving a gap.

Secondary findings in the same study: marrow signal in the lateral femoral condyle, a tibial translation, or a deep lateral femoral notch. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Key Learning Points

  • The normal ligament is a dark band with fine bright striations.
  • The striations are the bundles and are not degeneration.
  • It runs parallel to the roof of the intercondylar notch.
  • It must be followed from one attachment to the other on more than one plane.
  • Increased signal near the tibial attachment can be normal.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva the expected opening is that the ligament is assessed in three planes and not on the sagittal images alone.

The expected points are the sagittal long axis view, the coronal and axial confirmation, the fibre pattern, the line against the notch roof, and the attachments.

Say that you would describe the ligament's continuity, its signal and its line as three separate observations. 5.20.3 covers reporting cruciate injuries.

Say that the striations are normal bundle anatomy. 2.7.8 covers the anteromedial bundle.

Say that you would look for the secondary signs whatever the ligament looked like. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

What Normal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that the ligament is two functional bundles in a common sheath, so the bright lines between them are interfascicular tissue rather than a tear. 2.7.3 covers the histology.

The bundles tighten at different flexion angles, which is why the ligament's apparent tension changes with knee position in the scanner. 3.7.4 covers the bundle function.

Its line parallel to the notch roof follows from the femoral attachment being high and posterior on the lateral wall, so a ligament lying flatter than the roof has lost length somewhere. 2.7.6 covers the femoral footprint.

The tibial attachment is broad and fans out, which is why signal there is less specific than signal in the mid substance. 2.7.7 covers the tibial footprint.

How Good It Is

Magnetic resonance imaging reads the intact cruciate ligament more reliably than it reads any other state of it.

Its best contribution is exclusion: a continuous dark band following a normal line on all three planes is a strong normal. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

It is limited by the oblique course, by partial volume averaging in a thick slice, and by the magic angle effect near the tibial attachment. 5.2.20 covers the magic angle effect.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028. The claim being made is that the normal ligament is read better than the injured one, and that is a comparison rather than a number.

Key Learning Points

  • The ligament is two bundles in a common sheath, so striations are anatomy.
  • The bundles tighten at different flexion angles.
  • The normal line follows from a high, posterior femoral attachment.
  • The broad tibial attachment makes signal there less specific.
  • A normal appearance on three planes is a strong normal.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level a confident normal cruciate changes the differential rather than confirming it, and that is most of its value.

A normal ligament in an acute haemarthrosis moves the question to the meniscus, the corners and the cartilage rather than closing it. 6.2.5 covers the acute haemarthrosis.

A normal ligament with a positive examination is a disagreement to resolve rather than a report to accept, because the examination grades function and the image grades structure. 4.10.4 covers the Lachman test.

A ligament reported as degenerate in a patient with no instability is usually mucoid change rather than injury, and the two have different answers. 5.7.6 covers mucoid degeneration.

A normal ligament with a Segond fracture says the anterolateral structures took the load instead. 2.11.4 covers the Segond lesion.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is calling normal bundle striation a partial tear. 5.7.2 covers the partial tears.

Signal near the tibial attachment is read as a tear when the ligament is continuous. 5.2.20 covers the magic angle effect.

The sagittal plane alone is used, and an obliquely cut normal ligament appears absent. 5.5.9 covers the ligaments.

A thick slice averages the ligament with the notch fat and loses it. 5.5.2 covers the sports knee protocol.

A normal cruciate is taken as a normal knee, and the secondary signs go unexamined. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Key Learning Points

  • Normal bundle striation is called a partial tear.
  • Signal at the tibial attachment is read as a tear in a continuous ligament.
  • The sagittal plane alone can make a normal ligament disappear.
  • A thick slice averages the ligament into the notch fat.
  • A normal cruciate is not a normal knee.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the normal cruciate is the appearance against which every claim in this chapter is measured, and it is less standardised than that role requires.

What counts as normal signal in the ligament has no agreed definition, so the boundary between normal striation and a partial tear is set by the reader rather than by a rule. 5.21.2 covers the cruciate imaging literature.

Knee position in the scanner changes the ligament's apparent tension and its line, and position is not recorded on the study. 3.7.4 covers the bundle function.

A structurally normal ligament can be functionally lax after a reconstruction or a healed injury, and the image cannot show that. 5.7.8 covers cruciate healing.

The secondary signs carry more of the diagnosis than the ligament's own appearance in the equivocal case, which inverts the usual reading order. 5.11.7 covers bone marrow lesions.

Automated reading is trained on studies labelled by the same reader judgement that lacks a rule, so it reproduces the inconsistency rather than resolving it. 5.18.8 covers artificial intelligence in imaging.

Key Learning Points

  • Normal ligament signal has no agreed definition.
  • Knee position changes the line and tension and is not recorded.
  • A structurally normal ligament can be functionally lax.
  • The secondary signs carry the equivocal case.
  • Automated reading reproduces the reader inconsistency it was trained on.
