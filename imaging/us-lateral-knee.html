Lateral Knee

Page 5.4.3 · Ultrasound of the Knee · imaging page

The lateral structures, which converge in a small space and are harder to separate than the medial ones.

Lateral Knee

Choose a depth

For Medical Students

What It Shows

The lateral side holds the collateral ligament, the popliteus tendon, the biceps femoris and the common peroneal nerve, converging towards the fibular head. 2.10.1 covers the LCL anatomy.

The structures are shallow and run in several directions, so each needs its own probe orientation. 5.2.10 covers anisotropy.

It matters because the common peroneal nerve is here, and ultrasound images a peripheral nerve better than any other modality. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

How It Is Acquired

Start at the fibular head, which is the landmark everything else is found from. 2.10.1 covers the LCL anatomy.

Follow the collateral ligament from the fibular head to the femoral epicondyle, reorienting as it runs obliquely. 5.4.10 covers the LCL.

Follow the biceps femoris to its insertion and the common peroneal nerve around the fibular neck. 2.10.5 covers the biceps femoris.

Find the popliteus tendon in its groove, which requires a posterolateral approach. 5.4.8 covers the popliteus tendon.

Reorient for every structure, because they do not share an axis. 5.2.10 covers anisotropy.

What Normal Looks Like

A thin bright collateral ligament running obliquely from fibular head to femur. 2.10.1 covers the LCL anatomy.

The common peroneal nerve as a small fascicular structure at the fibular neck. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

The biceps femoris tendon inserting at the fibular head. 2.10.5 covers the biceps femoris.

A popliteus tendon within its groove, seen from behind. 2.10.2 covers the popliteus tendon anatomy.

What Abnormal Looks Like

Thickening or discontinuity of the collateral ligament. 5.4.10 covers the LCL.

An enlarged or hypoechoic common peroneal nerve, or a ganglion displacing it. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

Fluid or thickening around the biceps insertion. 2.10.5 covers the biceps femoris.

A fluid filled popliteus sheath or an absent tendon in its groove. 5.4.8 covers the popliteus tendon.

Key Learning Points

  • The structures converge towards the fibular head.
  • The fibular head is the landmark everything is found from.
  • Each structure needs its own probe orientation.
  • The common peroneal nerve is better imaged here than by any other modality.
  • The popliteus tendon needs a posterolateral approach.

MRCS Level

How It Is Acquired

In a viva this is the region to name when asked about imaging the common peroneal nerve.

The expected points are the fibular head landmark, the oblique collateral, the biceps and nerve, the popliteus approach, and the reorientation.

Say that the nerve is better seen here than on magnetic resonance imaging. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

Say that each structure runs in a different direction, so anisotropy applies differently to each. 5.2.10 covers anisotropy.

Say that the corner as a whole is assessed better on magnetic resonance imaging. 5.10.3 covers posterolateral corner injuries.

What Abnormal Looks Like

The reasoning expected is that a peripheral nerve has an internal fascicular pattern that high frequency ultrasound resolves and magnetic resonance imaging does not, which is a genuine superiority. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

That matters clinically because a nerve lesion after a corner injury or an osteotomy is a specific question with a specific answer. 5.10.5 covers the common peroneal nerve on magnetic resonance imaging.

The corner's ligaments converge in a small volume and run obliquely, so separating them needs continuous reorientation and knowledge of the anatomy rather than pattern recognition. 2.10.4 covers the posterolateral corner.

The popliteus tendon's course makes it the hardest of the group to image completely. 5.4.8 covers the popliteus tendon.

How Good It Is

Lateral knee ultrasound is excellent for the nerve and the superficial ligaments and incomplete for the corner.

Its best contribution is the common peroneal nerve, where it outperforms every other modality. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

It is limited for the corner as a functional unit, because the deeper structures and their attachments are not all reachable. 5.10.3 covers posterolateral corner injuries.

No figures appear for accuracy on this page, under decision 028, and no nerve calibre or ligament thickness values appear either: those are published quantities and this environment cannot check them.

Key Learning Points

  • A peripheral nerve's fascicular pattern is resolved here and not on magnetic resonance.
  • A nerve lesion after a corner injury or osteotomy is a specific question.
  • The corner's ligaments converge obliquely and need continuous reorientation.
  • The popliteus tendon is the hardest of the group to image completely.
  • Excellent for the nerve, incomplete for the corner.

FRCS Level

How Good It Is

At decision level the lateral ultrasound has one strong indication and several weak ones.

A suspected common peroneal nerve lesion, after a corner injury or an osteotomy, is the clearest indication for knee ultrasound outside the extensor mechanism. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

A superficial collateral or biceps insertion question is answerable. 5.4.10 covers the LCL.

A corner injury as a whole needs magnetic resonance imaging, because the operative decision depends on structures ultrasound cannot complete. 5.10.3 covers posterolateral corner injuries.

A nerve ganglion or a mass displacing the nerve is characterised and can be aspirated under guidance. 5.4.18 covers hydrodissection.

Where It Misleads

The commonest error is reporting the corner as intact when only its superficial structures were seen. 5.10.3 covers posterolateral corner injuries.

Anisotropy in an obliquely running ligament is read as injury. 5.2.10 covers anisotropy.

The nerve is not examined when a corner injury is found. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

The popliteus is reported from an incomplete view. 5.4.8 covers the popliteus tendon.

A normal lateral ultrasound is taken to exclude a rotational instability. 5.10.3 covers posterolateral corner injuries.

Key Learning Points

  • The corner is reported intact when only its superficial structures were seen.
  • Anisotropy in an oblique ligament is read as injury.
  • The nerve goes unexamined when a corner injury is found.
  • The popliteus is reported from an incomplete view.
  • A normal study is taken to exclude rotational instability.

Fellowship Level

Where It Misleads

At fellowship level the lateral knee shows ultrasound at its most and least useful within a few centimetres.

The common peroneal nerve is the one knee structure where ultrasound's resolution advantage translates into a diagnosis nothing else makes as well, and it is rarely the reason the study was requested. 2.10.6 covers the common peroneal nerve.

The posterolateral corner is the one structure group whose functional assessment matters most and whose components ultrasound cannot complete, so the two sit side by side. 5.10.3 covers posterolateral corner injuries.

Operator dependence is higher here than anywhere else in the knee, because the structures require continuous reorientation and are identified by anatomy rather than by appearance. 5.2.10 covers anisotropy.

That makes a negative lateral ultrasound the weakest negative in knee ultrasound, and the report does not distinguish a thorough examination from a cursory one. 5.20.1 covers structured reporting.

The reasonable position is to use it for the nerve and for a named superficial structure, and not for the corner. 5.1.3 covers choosing the correct investigation.

Key Learning Points

  • The nerve is the clearest resolution advantage and rarely the reason requested.
  • The corner matters most functionally and cannot be completed here.
  • Operator dependence is higher here than anywhere else in the knee.
  • A negative lateral ultrasound is the weakest negative in knee ultrasound.
  • Use it for the nerve and a named structure, not for the corner.
