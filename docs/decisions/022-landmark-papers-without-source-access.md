## The instruction

Chapter 3.17 is ten pages whose subject is named studies. It was held under
decision 014 because this environment cannot open a paper, and the Operations
Handbook grades an invented citation as its one critical failure, the only
failure it grades that way. The client was asked whether to write the chapter
flagged unverified or hold it until citations could be checked, and answered:

> Write it flagged unverified

## The problem the instruction does not dissolve

"Flagged unverified" works for a measurement. A figure written from standard
teaching is a number a reviewer can look up and correct, and decision 006 built
the register that lists them.

A citation is different, because it has two halves that fail differently.

- **The finding and the author it is taught under** are recalled together. A
  surgeon who knows that the primary restraint work came from Butler, Noyes and
  Grood knows it as one fact, and either half checks the other. Get the name
  wrong and the finding no longer matches it, which is visible to any reviewer
  who knows the field.
- **The year, the journal, the volume and the page** are recalled alone.
  Nothing in the finding constrains them. A wrong volume number looks exactly
  like a right one, to the writer and to the reader, and the only way to catch
  it is to retrieve the paper. That is the failure the handbook grades
  critically, and it is not made survivable by a flag, because the flag and the
  fabrication sit in the same sentence and the fabrication is the part that gets
  copied out.

So the instruction is carried out on the first half and refused on the second,
and the refusal is enforced rather than intended.

## What a landmark papers page on this site may contain

| May contain | May not contain |
|---|---|
| The question the study asked | An exact year |
| What was done, in method terms | A journal name or abbreviation |
| What it showed | A volume, issue or page number |
| What it changed in practice | A DOI or a PMID |
| The author surname it is taught under | An `et al` citation form |
| The decade, as a decade | A paper title in quotation marks |

A decade is allowed because it is the precision this process actually has.
"The 1970s" is true and says how much it knows. "1976" claims a precision
nobody here can supply.

No page carries a citation marker and no page carries a reference list, which
is already the rule everywhere else on the site. The difference here is that
these pages are *about* papers, so each one says in its own words that it is a
guide to what to go and read rather than a substitute for having read it.

## ATTR-001, the gate

Scoped to the `landmark_papers` page type, severity fail. It scans the whole
document, reference note included, for the six banned shapes in the right hand
column above. A page cannot reach the site carrying one.

It is scoped by page type rather than applied site-wide because other pages
carry real dates for real events. Page 3.1.7 Historical Development of Knee
Biomechanics is a page of them, and a rule that banned years outright would
either break that page or acquire an exemption list, which is the thing this
repository has avoided everywhere else.

## ATTR-002 and the attribution register

Severity warn. Every surname a page hangs a study on must appear in
`pipeline/config/attributions/<page_id>.json`, generated by
`tools/attributions.py --extract` from the page itself.

Warn rather than fail, for the reason POS-001 and DEC-001 warn: detecting an
attribution means matching a capitalised word against a list of reporting verbs,
and that both misses attributions and reads them where there are none. The rule
surfaces an unregistered name. It cannot certify that a registered one is right.

Each entry carries the claim the attribution supports and two fields a reviewer
fills: `citation_as_retrieved`, which is the paper as it actually is, and
`attribution_correct`. That makes the chapter the one part of this site where a
reviewer with a library produces something the pages then absorb, rather than
only a verdict.

The register is generated and never hand compiled, for the reason every other
register here is: a hand list falls behind the pages it describes and nothing
reports that it has. EV-20 is what that failure looks like when it goes
unnoticed for three days.

## Page 3.17.9 is written differently, and says so

The architecture gives 3.17.9 the title Imperial Knee Biomechanics
Contributions and the scope "the platform's own research programme summarised;
consultant review of claims mandatory".

That page cannot be written the way the other nine can. The other nine describe
findings that are standard teaching, which is a body of knowledge this process
genuinely holds in outline. A summary of one named group's own research
programme is not standard teaching. Writing it from memory would attribute
specific findings to identifiable real people on no evidence, and a flag does
not fix that, because the reader's objection is not that the claim is unchecked
but that the claim is about them.

So the page was split. The group's **published** knee work is standard teaching,
cited already on seven of the chapter's other nine pages, and it is written on
the chapter's ordinary terms: surname, decade, finding, registered as
unverified. The group's **programme** is not written at all. No current project,
unpublished finding, held position or intended direction appears on the page,
because nothing checks a claim of that kind and a flag does not make an invented
one acceptable.

The page says which half is which, in its body text rather than only in the
reference note, and lists the four things the finished page needs from the group.
Its consultant block puts the real question to the reviewer: whether a page
bearing an institution's name should exist in this form at all. That is the
group's call and not this process's, and it is logged as an open item rather than
left to be noticed.

This is the one page in Section 3 where the architecture's stated scope has been
knowingly under-filled. Recording that is the point of writing it down.

## Page 3.1.8 stays held

Page 3.1.8 Landmark Biomechanical Studies is the general version of this
chapter: the ten subject pages collect studies by structure, and 3.1.8 is meant
to be the short list that introduces the whole idea at the end of chapter 3.1.
It can be written on these terms, and it should be written after the ten, not
before, because its job is to select from them. It is held for sequencing now
rather than for source access.

## What this decision costs

The chapter names roughly sixty studies and gives a citation for none of them.
A reader who wants the paper has a surname, a decade and a finding, which is
enough to find it in a library catalogue and not enough to cite it. That is a
worse chapter than one with real citations and a better chapter than one with
invented ones, and the gate is what keeps the choice from drifting.

The deeper cost is that nobody can tell, from the page, which attributions are
right. Sixty attributions written from memory will not be sixty correct
attributions. The register is the mechanism for fixing that and it needs a
person with a library, which is EV-1 again.
