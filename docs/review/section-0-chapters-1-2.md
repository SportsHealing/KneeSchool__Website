## Why only eleven of thirty eight

The remaining 27 pages are careers, study skills, teacher resources and
assessment pages. The architecture marks all four of those page types "variant
pending", and the Operations Handbook has not defined a template for any of
them. Writing them to the anatomy template would produce headings like
"Structure and Location" on a page about choosing A levels.

That is handbook open question 5 and architecture open question 6. It is with
the editor.

## What this tier is and why it reads differently

Everything here targets a reading age of 12 to 14. Sentences are short.
Analogies are concrete. Nothing assumes prior knowledge.

Governance on every page: no commercial content of any kind, no clinical advice,
no citation markers, and a closing line pointing the reader to a parent,
teacher, coach or doctor.

## What you are being asked to confirm

**Is it accurate at this level of simplification?** That is the real risk in a
junior tier. Simplifying accurately is harder than writing accurately, and the
failure mode is a sentence that is easy to read and slightly wrong.

Five substantive claims per page are listed in each handoff under
`claims_needing_verification`. No page carries a reference, because the
verification stage could not run.

## Pages that need particular attention

**0.2.4 Girls, Boys and Knees.** This page tells thirteen year old girls that
they tear the ACL more often than boys. It then says movement patterns can be
trained, that training works particularly well in female athletes, and that part
of the gap may reflect unequal access to coaching rather than biology. That last
point is a judgement as much as a fact. Please read the whole page and confirm
the framing, not just the claims.

**0.2.5 When to Tell an Adult.** This is the page most likely to change what a
young person actually does. It lists same day signs and within a few days signs.
Please confirm both lists are right, complete, and in the right urgency bracket.

**0.2.2 Osgood-Schlatter.** States that it is not causing damage that will matter
later, and that it resolves when the growing area closes. Confirm both.

**0.2.3 Warm-Ups That Work.** States that static stretching before sport does not
prevent injury and may briefly reduce strength. Confirm the wording.

**0.1.5 Growing Knees.** Repeats the two thirds of limb length figure from 1.2.10
and the claim that growth plate cartilage does not appear on an x-ray.

## Specific questions

1. Is the reading age consistently hit, and is anything simplified past the point
   of being true?
2. 0.2.4: is the framing right for the audience?
3. 0.2.5: are the two urgency lists correct and complete?
4. Several pages tell readers that certain things are not their fault and not
   permanent. Is that reassurance accurate in each case?
5. 0.2.6 Athlete Stories describes a page that will carry interviews, including
   from people who did not return to their sport. Is that the right editorial
   call for this audience?

## Governance confirmed on all eleven

- No commercial content. The brief refuses it because the junior tier is present.
- No clinical advice, and no page tells a reader what to do about a symptom.
- No citation markers anywhere.
- Every page closes with the standard speak to an adult message.
- British English, no em or en dashes.
