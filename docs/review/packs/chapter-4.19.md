## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.19.1 Growth Plate Assessment**

- Chronological age, bone age and pubertal stage diverge.
- A healed physeal injury can examine normally while a deformity develops.
- Partial arrest is detected by an alignment change over time.
- Ligament and physeal injury coexist near maturity.
- Every conclusion about the physis is an inference from tenderness and mechanism.

**4.19.2 Paediatric ACL Injury**

- Physiological laxity varies with age and the examination has no age adjusted reference.
- Partial and complete tears are not reliably separated clinically.
- The physeal status, not the laxity, governs the operation.
- Examination under anaesthesia removes guarding and changes what is measured.
- Parent and patient reports of instability differ.

**4.19.3 Discoid Meniscus**

- Presence on imaging does not establish causation.
- The three findings together make the case, and one alone does not.
- A treated discoid meniscus has a post operative baseline, not a normal one.
- Long term lateral compartment change overlies the mechanical findings.
- The peripheral attachment, which determines stability, is not assessable clinically.

**4.19.4 Patellar Instability**

- Trochlear dysplasia is the strongest predisposing feature and only inferred clinically.
- Patellar height is not reliably measurable clinically and changes the treatment.
- Opposing femoral and tibial torsion can hide each other.
- The laxity threshold is a convention rather than a biological boundary.
- A previously operated knee has its own baseline.

**4.19.5 OCD**

- The mechanical history is an imperfect proxy for fragment stability.
- Clinical assessment of skeletal maturity is approximate.
- A healed lesion can remain tender without being active.
- Pain from the lesion and from the altered loading are not separable clinically.
- Repeated examination over time beats any single assessment.

**4.19.6 Developmental Variants**

- The trajectory is the finding, and it needs more than one visit.
- Angular and rotational components can compensate and hide each other.
- The normal range for an age is a population statement, not a decision rule.
- A laxity threshold is a convention rather than a boundary.
- A dysplasia can present as a symmetrical variant, and stature separates them.

**4.19.7 Torsional Assessment**

- The clinical measurement locates the twist only approximately.
- Compensating torsion gives a normal gait with an abnormal limb.
- Rotational and coronal deformity interact and neither describes the load alone.
- Torsion's contribution to patellar instability is not apportionable clinically.
- Contracture limits the measurement, so finding and limitation are recorded together.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.19.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
