## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**2.12.1 Trochlea**

- The junction of proximal trochlea and anterior cortex is what dysplasia descriptions describe.
- Bony and soft tissue restraint hand over rather than add.
- Groove geometry sets direction; soft tissue sets permitted deviation.
- Contact pressure peaks in mid flexion, so the cartilage at risk is not the cartilage examined.
- A patella that dislocates in full extension has a soft tissue story.

**2.12.2 MPFL**

- Three structures within a few millimetres at the femur, distinguished by measurement.
- A graft replaces a broad attachment with a point one, changing load distribution.
- A normally tracking patella does not load this ligament, so a taut graft is wrong.
- Femoral point error tightens or slackens the graft through flexion, and the two fail differently.
- A 1 mm thick check cannot correct bony malalignment.

**2.12.3 MQTFL**

- The femoral point decides length behaviour and is shared, so anterior naming arguments miss the point.
- Patellar fracture after reconstruction is a complication of bone, not of ligament.
- A tendon attachment adds a small dynamic element that a bone graft does not reproduce.
- Over-tensioning loads the medial facet every step.
- Neither structure corrects bony malalignment.

**2.12.4 Retinacula**

- Lateral patellar tightness can originate above the knee, through the iliotibial band.
- Retinacular and capsular procedures are less distinct than their names suggest.
- The deep transverse layer is a ligament, so releasing it is a decision.
- Medial plication reproduces little of what the medial patellofemoral ligament does.
- Retinacular transmission to the tibia changes what a straight leg raise tests.

**2.12.5 Dynamic Stabilisers**

- Muscle and ligament are mechanically coupled at the medial patella.
- A lateral release divides a muscular expansion as well as a ligament.
- Dynamic control sets the position from which passive restraints are loaded.
- The oblique fibres are a restraint, not a corrective force.
- Selective strengthening in isolation is not supported by the shared nerve and tendon.

**2.12.6 Surgical Anatomy**

- A femoral error is invisible at the time and discovered weeks later; checking takes one image.
- Patellar fracture is a bone complication, and the tendon attachment is the anatomical alternative.
- The patella has one anastomosis and combination procedures spend it.
- A perfectly reconstructed 1 mm sheet fails against a bony problem.
- Over-constraint presents as anterior pain in a knee that no longer dislocates.
