## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.13.1 Varus Stress Test**

- The varus test is usually normal in the knees that get referred, which is why they arrive late.
- A stretched posterior cruciate graft with a normal varus test and an abnormal dial test is the classic case.
- A chronically varus knee with lateral laxity is an alignment problem first.
- A previous lateral reconstruction can hold varus firm while rotation stays abnormal.
- A partly recovered nerve palsy produces a varus thrust that is not ligamentous.

**4.13.2 LCL Assessment**

- A reconstructed collateral with persistent rotational abnormality means the popliteus was not addressed.
- A reconstruction in an uncorrected varus limb stretches, whatever the surgery.
- Previous lateral extra-articular surgery makes an absent cord less informative.
- A united but displaced avulsion gives a palpable, slack ligament, and the varus test is the one to believe.
- A recorded absent cord is strong evidence of a complete injury at that time.

**4.13.3 Popliteus Assessment**

- The popliteus is the structure that was left out, and the stretched graft is the consequence.
- A persistent dial abnormality at thirty degrees after corner reconstruction means the popliteofibular part was not restored.
- Over constraint on this side presents as lateral pain and lost internal rotation.
- After a proximal tibial osteotomy the dial test needs a post-operative baseline.
- A nerve palsy mimics a corner injury in gait and does not increase rotational laxity.

**4.13.4 Posterolateral Corner Assessment**

- The corner explains most failed posterior cruciate surgery.
- A stretched graft, a dial difference at both angles and a varus thrust are one diagnosis.
- A residual difference at thirty degrees after reconstruction is incompleteness, not failure.
- After an osteotomy the alignment correction is assessed before any ligament revision.
- An unrecovered nerve palsy limits what any reconstruction can achieve and belongs in the consent.

**4.13.5 Dial Test**

- The dial test is usually the test that was not done, and doing it answers most referral questions.
- A stretched cruciate graft with a difference at both angles means the revision must address the corner.
- A difference at thirty degrees only after corner reconstruction is an incomplete operation, not a new injury.
- No dial difference with a rotational complaint points at the anterolateral structures.
- After an osteotomy the other leg is no longer a valid comparison.

**4.13.6 Reverse Pivot Shift**

- A fixed posterior subluxation cannot shift, so the most deficient knees read as reassuring.
- A previous tenodesis removes the mechanism, so a negative result says nothing.
- An over constrained corner reconstruction gives a negative test and a stiff lateral side.
- In a hypermobile patient the test is positive bilaterally and contributes nothing.
- The test is too unreliable to carry a medicolegal opinion, and the dial test is not.

**4.13.7 External Rotation Recurvatum Test**

- A positive test after corner reconstruction means the reconstruction has stretched.
- It is easier than the dial test to reproduce identically at successive visits.
- A recurvatum that persists after reconstruction is usually an unaddressed bony problem.
- Hyperextension from a previous osteotomy is abnormal for a reason unrelated to the corner.
- A recorded positive test at the time of injury is useful evidence, because it is hard to produce in a normal knee.

**4.13.8 Combined PLC Injuries**

- The examination's job in revision is to establish what was not treated.
- A stretched posterior cruciate graft with a difference at both angles is a pattern that was missed.
- A failed cruciate graft with a dial difference and a persisting pivot shift has an unaddressed corner.
- Two intact reconstructions with a varus thrust is an alignment problem, and the osteotomy is the operation.
- An unrecovered nerve palsy is often the main determinant of function in a multiply reconstructed knee.
