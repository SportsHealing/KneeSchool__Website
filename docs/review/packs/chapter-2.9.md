## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**2.9.1 Superficial MCL**

- In front of the merge point the ligament is isolable; behind it, an injury is a corner injury.
- The proximal tibial attachment is a dynamic coupling to semimembranosus, not just a second anchor.
- Valgus restraint is shared, so laxity in extension means more than this ligament has failed.
- The two tibial attachments split rotation restraint between external and internal.
- Landmarks, not residual tissue, locate the attachments in a reconstruction.

**2.9.2 Deep MCL**

- Skin to joint on the medial side crosses five named structures, not three.
- This is the only continuous ligamentous attachment either meniscus has.
- It converts the medial meniscus from a spacer into a loaded wedge.
- A peripheral detachment is vascular tissue and reattaches.
- Deep ligament and posteromedial corner injury can be one injury seen from two sides.

**2.9.3 Posterior Oblique Ligament**

- The ligament has no posterior border; it becomes the posteromedial capsule.
- Its femoral attachment shares bone with the medial head of gastrocnemius.
- It and the superficial collateral ligament are a reciprocal pair across the range.
- Rotation near extension is its job, so a 20 degree valgus test cannot see it.
- Advancing the ligament also moves the posterior horn of the medial meniscus.

**2.9.4 Posteromedial Corner**

- Five structures and three findings, so a stage is an inference the plan must survive.
- Acute capsule repairs; remodelled capsule has healed at the wrong length.
- The corner sets the rotational position in which the rest of a multiligament knee is fixed.
- Two reconstruction points compete for one small field of femoral bone.
- No graft reproduces the dynamic tension semimembranosus supplies.

**2.9.5 Semimembranosus**

- Every other posteromedial component attaches to, arises from, or is tensioned by this tendon.
- Dividing one arm changes structures the approach never touched.
- The direct arm's tubercle is the fixed posteromedial reference.
- Ligaments set the limit; this muscle sets the tension at which it is reached.
- In a multiligament knee it is often the one intact medial structure.

**2.9.6 Dynamic Stabilisation**

- The harvest interval and the saphenous nerve interval are the same interval.
- The pes bursa is the plane between dynamic and static medial restraint.
- Gastrocnemius origin and the posterior oblique ligament compete for the same femoral bone.
- A knee lax asleep and stable awake has a working dynamic component.
- Harvesting two pes tendons removes dynamic restraint from a possibly injured medial side.

**2.9.7 Surgical Anatomy**

- A collateral ligament and a corner need different incisions, decided by anatomy not preference.
- In a revision the prominences remain and the intervals do not, so plan from bone.
- Medial femoral tunnels converge in three dimensions with the cruciate tunnel and the gastrocnemius origin.
- The medial side is often repairable when the lateral is not, and that decides sequence.
- The chronic medial knee is a length problem, so reattaching without shortening restores a failing anatomy.
