## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.2.1 Degrees of Freedom of the Knee**

- Constraining one degree of freedom reduces range in others; over-constraint presents as stiffness.
- The small range degrees of freedom carry importance out of proportion to their magnitude.
- Robotic cadaveric testing is the reference method and carries an order effect.
- No clinical instrument measures rotation reliably, which is why it is graded by hand.
- A knee called stable on the one measurable degree of freedom may not be.

**3.2.2 Flexion and Extension**

- The flexion arc is surface geometry and ligament length, and surgery can alter either.
- Rollback asymmetry is the knee's rotation; reproducing one without the other reproduces neither.
- Tibial slope changes the relationship between flexion and anteroposterior position.
- Passive asleep, passive awake and active are three measurements, and the gaps carry information.
- Published implant flexion is usually passive and best case.

**3.2.3 Internal Rotation**

- The motion patients complain about is the one with no number.
- Its restraints are distributed, so no single reconstruction reliably normalises it.
- The margin between restoring and over-constraining is small, because the structures have little compliance.
- A three point scale assessed unblinded measures less than it appears to.
- A normal arthrometer reading does not mean a stable knee.

**3.2.4 External Rotation**

- Increased external rotation localises; it does not grade severity.
- Three structures within two centimetres share the restraint, so the test localises to a region.
- A varus limb loads a corner reconstruction at every step, so alignment comes first.
- Corner tissue that healed long gives laxity with continuity on imaging.
- The 10 degree threshold is a convention rather than a derived cut off.

**3.2.5 Varus Motion**

- Alignment, laxity, thrust and compartment state are four measurements of one problem.
- A lateral reconstruction in an uncorrected varus limb is loaded by the limb, not the injury.
- Osteotomy has the largest mechanical effect per unit of surgery at this joint.
- Sectioning figures describe a knee without the alignment the patient has.
- Reconstructing laxity in a limb whose alignment caused it repeats the failure.

**3.2.6 Valgus Motion**

- Medial laxity is a question about length, not continuity.
- The corner restrains in extension and the collateral in flexion, a reciprocal pair.
- Time changes this injury in a way it changes no other knee ligament.
- Sectioning figures describe an acutely cut ligament, not a chronically lengthened one.
- Continuity on imaging is not competence.

**3.2.7 Anterior Translation**

- The clinical instrument measures one component of a coupled event.
- Secondary restraint failure is progressive, which is the mechanical case for early meniscal preservation.
- Slope is a bony contribution to a problem usually described as ligamentous.
- Arthrometry is adequate for groups and weak for individuals, which is the reverse of its common use.
- Translation and rotation are reported with different precision, which shapes trial conclusions.

**3.2.8 Posterior Translation**

- The cruciate is the restraint and the corner the modifier; flexion angle separates them.
- Partial healing is why this ligament's natural history differs from the anterior cruciate's.
- Posterior sag changes resting contact and loads compartments differently over time.
- Stress radiography is underused here, because the clinical finding depends on starting position.
- A varus limb with posterolateral laxity is a different problem from the same injury in a neutral one.

**3.2.9 Coupled Motion**

- Rotatory and translational laxity dissociate after reconstruction because coupling splits them.
- The anterolateral structures restrain the rotational half of the coupled pair.
- Residual symptoms with a normal arthrometer reading are a coupling problem.
- Posterior tibial slope and lateral plateau shape change the coupled rotation.
- Sectioning order changes apportionment, which is a limitation of the method.

**3.2.10 Knee Stability During Motion**

- Stability is a redundant system; redundancy preserves function and hides injury.
- The cost of redundancy is transferred load, which is the mechanical route to degeneration.
- A multiligament knee is a system failure rather than a sum of single injuries.
- Bony morphology and alignment change the demand on the soft tissue restraints.
- No single measurement captures stability.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.2.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.

## The same pack in two other formats

A spreadsheet, which is easier to fill, and a Word file, which is easier to print:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --chapter 3.2 --format all

All three carry the same rows and the same references, and `--ingest` reads any of them.
