## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.11.1 Posterior Sag Sign**

- A recurrent sag after reconstruction is either a stretched graft or a missed corner injury.
- A sag appearing between two visits is a trajectory, and it changes plans more often than a single reading.
- Reconstructing the cruciate into an unaddressed corner injury is a common cause of graft stretch.
- A visible sag is often the first finding in a patient referred with patellofemoral pain.
- The sign needs the earlier examination to have been recorded as a step off, not as a grade.

**4.11.2 Posterior Drawer Test**

- A grade recorded without the step off and the rotation is near useless at the second assessment.
- After reconstruction, expect a restored step off with a firm endpoint and slightly more translation.
- A soft endpoint after reconstruction is graft failure; a progressive increase is graft stretch.
- An unaddressed posterolateral corner is the commonest cause of that stretch, and the dial test finds it.
- Examination under anaesthesia is where second and third degree are genuinely separated.

**4.11.3 Quadriceps Active Test**

- A positive test recorded acutely is good evidence the tibia was subluxed at the time.
- A negative test recorded acutely is weak evidence, because non cooperation is rarely written down.
- After reconstruction the test loses its meaning, since a holding graft leaves nothing to reduce.
- A positive result never licenses the word isolated.
- In medicolegal work the absent test and the negative test are the distinction that matters.

**4.11.4 Reverse Pivot Shift**

- The classic revision case is a stretched cruciate graft in a knee whose corner was never addressed.
- A positive test after corner reconstruction suggests rotational control has failed.
- A persistent positive result in a stable, symptom free knee is not a reason to reoperate.
- A fixed posterior subluxation cannot shift, so the worst knee can read as negative.
- Previous lateral soft tissue surgery removes the mechanism the test depends on.

**4.11.5 Dynamic PCL Examination**

- Watching a patient descend a step separates a mechanical problem from a confidence problem.
- Deteriorating gait with an unchanged drawer points at the corner or at compartment change.
- Unrecovered quadriceps wasting is the finding most predictive of persistent complaint.
- A knee that walks worse than it measures, with no corner signs, raises whether the knee is the problem.
- An athlete who passes a step down can still fail a deceleration, which the clinic cannot show.

**4.11.6 Chronic PCL Deficiency**

- The chronic knee usually arrives under a different label, and the examination relabels it.
- A fixed posterior subluxation makes every test in the chapter read as mild.
- Tenderness sitting posteriorly on the medial joint line is a clue to the pattern of wear.
- Unchanged anterior pain after a sound reconstruction usually means the problem was never the ligament.
- Recording function separately from laxity is what makes a medicolegal opinion defensible.

**4.11.7 Combined Ligament Injury Assessment**

- In revision practice the examination reconstructs what was missed rather than what was torn.
- A stretched cruciate graft and an uncontrolled corner are separated by the dial test, not the drawer.
- A tight lateral reconstruction can mask rotational laxity.
- Alignment and tibial slope are assessed before further soft tissue work.
- An incompletely recovered nerve palsy changes the dynamic examination permanently.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.11.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
