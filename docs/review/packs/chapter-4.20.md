## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.20.1 ACL Reconstruction**

- Graft laxity is judged against the same knee's earlier examinations.
- An intact but malpositioned graft produces stiffness rather than laxity.
- Residual rotational instability has several sources the examination cannot separate.
- Alignment contributes to graft load and is examined alongside it.
- Laxity and readiness to return to sport are different judgements.

**4.20.2 Meniscal Repair**

- Clinical success and structural healing diverge.
- Joint line tenderness after meniscal surgery has little discriminating value.
- A re-tear and an unhealed repair present identically.
- A concurrent reconstruction makes attribution unreliable.
- Root repairs and peripheral repairs are not the same examination.

**4.20.3 Root Repair**

- A repair can heal while the compartment continues to deteriorate.
- Extrusion, the real measure of success, is not clinically assessable.
- A combined osteotomy changes every alignment finding.
- Posterior tenderness is non specific after posterior work.
- The flexion restriction records a trade off rather than a result.

**4.20.4 Osteotomy**

- The intended correction is radiographic and the examination only estimates it.
- Joint line obliquity changes and the examination does not describe it.
- Collateral tension after correction can be asymmetric without being lax.
- Pain has at least five candidate sources and the examination separates some.
- Patellar height changes in some techniques and is not clinically detectable.

**4.20.5 Cartilage Surgery**

- Repair tissue quality is invisible to the examination.
- Lesion size and containment govern the result and are not clinically measurable.
- Lesion, surrounding cartilage and subchondral pain are not separable clinically.
- A concurrent osteotomy makes the cartilage result harder to isolate.
- Function and structure diverge in both directions.

**4.20.6 MPFL Reconstruction**

- Failure, over tightening and the wrong operation overlap in presentation.
- Translation at one or two angles does not describe the whole range.
- Cartilage damage from previous dislocations explains much of the later pain.
- Uncorrected torsional and coronal contributions are read as graft failure.
- Fixation and tunnel position matter more than clinically assessed tension.

**4.20.7 Partial Knee Replacement**

- Progression and loosening both produce loading pain.
- A well fixed malpositioned implant produces pain with no nameable sign.
- Low grade infection presents identically to mechanical causes.
- Ligament balance is assessed against a baseline that is often not recorded.
- Pain with a normal examination is a recognised and unresolved problem.

**4.20.8 Total Knee Replacement**

- No combination of clinical findings excludes low grade infection.
- Component rotation is not clinically measurable, only its consequence.
- Flexion instability has no reliable test, so the history carries equal weight.
- Pain with a normal examination is its own category.
- Expectation predicts the reported result more than the findings do.

**4.20.9 Revision Knee Surgery**

- The contralateral knee is often operated too, removing the comparison.
- An abnormal finding may be the expected state of that knee.
- Pain has more candidate sources than the examination can separate.
- Bone stock, fixation and tunnel position govern feasibility and are not clinical.
- Expectation after a failed operation shapes the result as much as the findings.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.20.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
