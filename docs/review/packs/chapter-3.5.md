## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.5.1 Contact Areas**

- Contact area is what these procedures restore and nothing clinical measures it.
- The evidence is cadaveric for mechanism and cohort for outcome, with nothing in between.
- A transplant restores less area than a native meniscus, by conventional teaching.
- Tissue present is not necessarily tissue working; the ring must be anchored.
- A pressure sensor alters the contact it is measuring.

**3.5.2 Contact Pressures**

- Peak contact stress is the common currency of mechanical arguments in the knee.
- No in vivo stress threshold is established.
- Cadaveric thresholds come from single impacts; degeneration is cumulative.
- A stress figure argues mechanism; a cohort argues treatment.
- Repair tissue of different stiffness distributes load differently.

**3.5.3 Load Distribution**

- The division is the target of unicompartmental and realignment surgery.
- Overcorrection transfers an excessive share by the same arithmetic that justified correction.
- Unicompartmental replacement relies on the other compartment taking its share.
- No clinical measurement of the division in an intact knee exists.
- The division changes as the unoperated compartment progresses.

**3.5.4 Effects of Flexion**

- Deep flexion loads a small posterior contact area; the bearing has to tolerate it.
- Posterior condylar offset decides both achievable flexion and deep flexion contact.
- The structure under the highest deep flexion load is the posterior horn root.
- Retrieval analysis shows where deep flexion load actually fell.
- Measured range and reported deep flexion ability correlate imperfectly.

**3.5.5 Effects of Meniscal Loss**

- The contact studies and the clinical cohorts agree, which is unusual and useful.
- Transplantation restores less area than a native meniscus, and sizing decides how much.
- Anatomic root repair is specified because position and tension restore the hoop mechanism.
- A restored meniscus in a malaligned limb is overloaded.
- Healing on imaging is not restored contact mechanics.

**3.5.6 Effects of Malalignment**

- Alignment is the only variable here that can be changed by a large amount.
- Osteotomy precedes or accompanies cartilage and meniscal work rather than following it.
- Slope is a separate mechanical risk acting on translation and graft failure.
- Stress radiography separates bony deformity from soft tissue laxity.
- Confirm the correction radiographically rather than assuming it.

**3.5.7 Effects of Obesity**

- Mass has the largest achievable effect size and the lowest achievement rate.
- It acts on construct loads and on operative complications, which are separate arguments.
- Higher mass is associated with poorer osteotomy and unicompartmental outcomes.
- Body mass index is a poor description of load distribution.
- No measurement predicts which heavier patient will do well.

**3.5.8 Contact Mechanics After ACL Injury**

- The contact shift is the mechanical account of post-traumatic degeneration.
- Reconstruction restores stability reliably and contact position only partially.
- Stability and long term joint health are different outcomes.
- Slope correction addresses a shift driven by bony morphology.
- No clinical measurement confirms restored contact mechanics.

**3.5.9 Contact Mechanics After Meniscectomy**

- The shift from resection to repair was mechanically predicted before it was demonstrated.
- Transplantation relieves symptoms more reliably than it is proven to protect cartilage.
- A resected meniscus cannot be undone; the decision is permanent.
- No measurement confirms an individual knee's contact mechanics before or after.
- Record the resection precisely, because the next surgeon's options depend on it.

**3.5.10 Contact Mechanics After Osteotomy**

- Osteotomy is the foundation other procedures are built on, not an alternative to them.
- A correction is a commitment that changes any later arthroplasty.
- Slope correction is a separate operation with a separate mechanical target.
- Conversion to arthroplasty is the usual survivorship endpoint.
- Localise the deformity before correcting and confirm the axis afterwards.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.5.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
