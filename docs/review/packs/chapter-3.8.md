## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.8.1 Functions of the PCL**

- The position function explains the natural history.
- A chronically sagged knee loads the medial and patellofemoral compartments differently.
- Partial healing in continuity at a greater length separates this ligament from the anterior.
- Imaging can look normal in a measurably lax knee.
- Reconstruction competes with a ligament that is partly working.

**3.8.2 Posterior Stability**

- The grade is the decision variable and the method of getting it is the weak point.
- Conventional teaching reads above roughly 12 millimetres as suggesting a combined injury.
- A grade converts into an anatomical inference.
- Partial healing is more likely in a lower grade injury.
- Serial measurement is more informative than a single reading.

**3.8.3 Bundle Function**

- Here the dominant bundle really is dominant.
- Double bundle restores translation closer to normal in cadavers, with clinical equivalence.
- The mechanical gap to close is smaller than anteriorly.
- Femoral tunnel position within the anterolateral footprint matters most.
- Tension in flexion, since that is where the dominant bundle works.

**3.8.4 AL Bundle Mechanics**

- A graft in the right place tensioned at the wrong angle behaves like one in the wrong place.
- Conventional teaching tensions in flexion, commonly around 70 to 90 degrees.
- Reducing the sag before fixation distinguishes this reconstruction.
- Fixing with the tibia sagged locks the deformity in.
- No measurement confirms the tension once the knee is closed.

**3.8.5 PM Bundle Mechanics**

- The double bundle question is settled unsatisfyingly: a cadaveric advantage without a clinical one.
- The combined injury is where the mechanical case is strongest.
- Conventional teaching reserves double bundle for selected cases.
- Two bundles need more graft and more femoral tunnel space.
- The subgroup with the strongest mechanical case has no trial evidence.

**3.8.6 Posterior Drawer Mechanics**

- The error that misleads a drawer is the error that fails a reconstruction.
- Reduce the tibia with an anterior drawer before fixing the graft.
- Residual posterior laxity after reconstruction is common.
- It is attributed to the start position, graft creep and unaddressed corner injury.
- No measurement confirms the tibia was reduced at fixation.

**3.8.7 Gait Following PCL Injury**

- The adaptation is why non operative management succeeds more often here.
- The knee functions on a strong quadriceps and pays in compartment loading.
- Reconstruction improves laxity without a clear effect on the degenerative natural history.
- Varus with posterolateral laxity needs the alignment addressed.
- No measurement predicts which deficient knee will degenerate.

**3.8.8 PCL Reconstruction Biomechanics**

- This reconstruction is rarely performed alone in the multiligament knee.
- Tunnel convergence is the specific mechanical risk.
- Trajectories are planned in advance, which is geometry rather than preference.
- Inlay fixation avoids the tibial tunnel altogether.
- No measurement establishes the optimal sequence for a given combination.

**3.8.9 Double Bundle Reconstruction**

- The knee that would benefit most has the least graft and tunnel space.
- That is a resource argument rather than a mechanical one.
- Bone bridge and tunnel convergence decide feasibility.
- Multiligament series do not separate bundle technique.
- The point at which added complexity stops being worth it is not established.

**3.8.10 Inlay Techniques**

- The inlay is a tool for the compromised tibia.
- It is a planning decision made from imaging before theatre.
- The approach and position have to be set up for it.
- Both techniques are accepted as reasonable, so judgement decides.
- No measurement predicts which technique suits a given compromised tibia.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.8.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
