## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**2.6.1 Gross Anatomy**

- At the hiatus a tear is assessed on what is left to fix rather than on distance from a margin.
- Mobility is normal by design, so the line between mobile and hypermobile is a judgement.
- The lateral compartment has no concavity of its own, which is the primary argument for preservation.
- Assess lateral meniscal mobility deliberately at arthroscopy.

**2.6.2 Popliteomeniscal Fascicles**

- An arthroscopy that did not test lateral meniscal mobility has not excluded this lesion.
- The Wrisberg type discoid has no posterior capsular attachment at all.
- Saucerisation without stabilisation leaves a mobile remnant.
- At revision, scar can stabilise or tether, and probing distinguishes them.

**2.6.3 Meniscofemoral Ligaments**

- Their femoral attachments constrain posterior cruciate tunnel placement.
- Their posterior stability contribution is real and small; it explains variability rather than substituting.
- A lateral meniscus with failed fascicles and intact meniscofemoral ligaments behaves differently from one with both gone.
- An oblique signal next to the posterior horn is a pseudotear until these are accounted for.

**2.6.4 Root Attachments**

- An unrecognised lateral posterior root tear is the commonest reason a sound reconstruction has residual rotational laxity.
- Anterior root detachment by a tunnel is a managed cost if recognised and a later problem if not.
- In revision the previous tunnel may already have taken the anterior root.
- Lateral allograft horn fixation competes for a space the cruciates already occupy.

**2.6.5 MRI Anatomy**

- Imaging sees the posterolateral periphery better than the arthroscope and also misreads it more.
- Peripheral attachment and mobility are not reliably assessable on imaging.
- Plan a lateral meniscal procedure to allow for an attachment problem rather than a tear.
- In discoid meniscus, expect abnormal peripheral attachment more often than imaging suggests.

**2.6.6 Surgical Anatomy**

- At the hiatus the options are limited resection, repair to fascicles and sheath, or acceptance.
- Make that judgement with the probe before any tissue is removed.
- Saucerisation reshapes and does not stabilise; peripheral attachment is often abnormal.
- Lateral allograft transplantation is an access problem before it is a graft problem.
