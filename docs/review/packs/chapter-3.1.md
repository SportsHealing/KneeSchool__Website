## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.1.1 What Is Biomechanics?**

- Kinematics is motion without force; kinetics is force and its effects.
- Viscoelasticity gives creep, stress relaxation and rate dependence.
- Conventional teaching puts tibiofemoral joint reaction force at about three times body weight in walking.
- The instant centre of rotation moves through flexion, which is why the knee is not a hinge.
- A cadaver figure, a laboratory figure and an in vivo figure are three different kinds of number.

**3.1.2 Why Biomechanics Matters**

- Mechanism applies load, load produces stress, stress exceeds strength, pattern identifies mechanism.
- Conventional teaching gives anterior cruciate ultimate load of the order of 2,000 newtons.
- The margin between pivoting load and ultimate load is small, which is why the injury is common.
- Mechanism studies and intervention studies answer different questions.
- Surgery moves a variable from unreachable to reachable.

**3.1.3 Biomechanics in Everyday Life**

- Joint reaction force is the vector sum of external load and muscle force, so it exceeds both.
- Pressure is force over area, and area changes through the range, so the two do not track.
- Contact area rising with flexion partly offsets rising patellofemoral force.
- Co-contraction improves stability at a mechanical cost.
- A figure without its activity and method attached is not usable.

**3.1.4 Biomechanics in Sport**

- Load arrives faster than a reflex can modify it, so protection happens before contact.
- The mechanism is deceleration, near extension, valgus collapse with internal rotation.
- Anterolateral structures and the lateral meniscus load in the same event.
- Prospective designs link mechanics to injury; cross sectional ones cannot.
- Reinjury risk is the highest risk a returning athlete carries.

**3.1.5 Biomechanics and Injury Prevention**

- The causal chain assumes training changes movement, movement changes loading, loading changes injury.
- Conventional teaching reports a third to a half relative risk reduction with good adherence.
- Adherence is the dominant moderator and falls over a season.
- Injury definitions vary between studies more than readers notice.
- Individual screening does not perform as well as the group association suggests.

**3.1.6 Biomechanics and Surgical Reconstruction**

- A reconstruction alters one term in a system of restraints that varies with flexion.
- Nothing restores the native pattern; claiming otherwise misdescribes the operation.
- Alignment has the largest effect per step and is most often left uncorrected.
- Tibial slope is now part of the revision conversation.
- A construct can restore laxity measurements and leave a patient unsatisfied.

**3.1.7 Historical Development of Knee Biomechanics**

- The field moves when a method arrives, not when a question is asked.
- Questions answered by inference are where the next correction will come from.
- Simple memorable figures outlive the methods that produced them.
- Sectioning apportionment depends on cutting order, so read several studies.
- A figure with no method attached is unusable however often it is repeated.
