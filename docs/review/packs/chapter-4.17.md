## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.17.1 Clinical Presentation**

- The commonest problem is a lesion on imaging that does not fit the symptoms.
- An incidental lesion with diffuse pain means a finding and a separate problem.
- A matching arc with a malaligned limb means the lesion is being loaded, and the correction is treatment.
- After a cartilage procedure, the alignment and the meniscal status determine survival.
- An osteochondral injury after a dislocation has a time limited repair window.

**4.17.2 Mechanical Symptoms**

- The mechanical symptom is the claim an operation was built on, so it is re-examined first.
- A normal arthroscopy after reported locking means the symptom was not mechanical.
- Recurrent true locking after a cartilage procedure is an unstable graft or an unaddressed fragment.
- Locking stopping and pain persisting is a result worth framing before the operation.
- A recorded description of a block is evidence; the recorded word locking is not.

**4.17.3 Osteochondral Lesions**

- A fragment loose for months has lost the fixation option.
- A haemarthrosis attributed to a cruciate injury can be the wrong diagnosis from the right finding.
- Recurrent effusions and catching after a dislocation suggest a missed fragment.
- After fixation the alignment and the meniscal status determine survival.
- A missed fragment after a documented dislocation is the most defensible criticism here.

**4.17.4 Osteochondritis Dissecans**

- An adolescent with a stable lesion and remaining growth has a good prognosis.
- An adult with a healed lesion and compartment pain has an early degenerative problem.
- An adult with an unhealed lesion and a loose body is a reconstruction decision.
- Bilateral lesions with a family history put the other knee's prognosis in the conversation.
- The condition is developmental and is not attributable to a single injury.

**4.17.5 Articular Cartilage Defects**

- A resurfaced compartment with an uncorrected alignment treated the surface and left the mechanics.
- A deficient meniscus produces the same failure through a different route.
- Symptoms that never matched the compartment mean an incidental finding was treated.
- A large defect in a well aligned knee with an intact meniscus is the best case for resurfacing.
- Attribution to a single injury needs an acute mechanism and early imaging.

**4.17.6 Functional Assessment**

- This is the assessment that was not done before the operation that did not work.
- Without a pre-operative functional record, change cannot be assessed.
- A limitation that was always a strength problem persists after the surface is treated.
- A limitation in a different compartment means an incidental lesion was treated.
- A claim concerns the functional assessment and the record rarely contains it.

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.17.md
