## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.6.1 Systematic Palpation**

- Repeating palpation properly answers a surprising number of referrals.
- Tenderness over the pes insertion or the collateral's course is a soft tissue problem.
- Localised tenderness over a tunnel or a device is a hardware problem only palpation finds.
- Tenderness sitting more posteriorly on the medial joint line suggests a root or meniscotibial problem.
- Tenderness over several unrelated structures is a pain presentation, not a structural one.

**4.6.2 Temperature Assessment**

- A knee still warm months after surgery raises low grade infection.
- A normal inflammatory marker does not exclude it in that setting.
- A warm knee with a recurrent effusion and normal mechanics is not helped by arthroscopy.
- Immunosuppression blunts the usual signs and lowers the threshold for investigation.
- The absence of a documented vascular examination in a multiligament injury is a serious gap.

**4.6.3 Effusion Assessment**

- A persistently effused knee is usually not a mechanical problem.
- An effusion after reconstruction or arthroplasty raises low grade infection.
- A chronically effused knee cannot be rehabilitated until the effusion is addressed.
- A knee that effuses only after activity may be dry in clinic.
- The documented presence or absence at presentation is frequently disputed and decisive.

**4.6.4 Joint Line Tenderness**

- The precise position is often the most useful thing in the examination and is almost never recorded.
- Tenderness posterior to the posterior horn suggests a root avulsion or meniscotibial separation.
- Tenderness at an old meniscectomy site in a young patient raises early compartment change.
- Whole joint line tenderness with a normal arthroscopy raises the surface or a synovial process.
- After a repair, persistent local tenderness is expected for months.

**4.6.5 Patellar Tenderness**

- Palpation localises the anterior problem that has been treated as something else.
- Facet tenderness with stair pain in a meniscal referral changes the plan.
- Facet tenderness with posterior cruciate deficiency is a consequence of the deficiency.
- Tenderness over a tendon harvest site is graft site pain, not a patellar problem.
- Medial retinacular tenderness recorded acutely is useful evidence of a dislocation.

**4.6.6 Tendon Tenderness**

- Tendon tenderness explains the knee investigated intra-articularly with an extra-articular problem.
- Donor site pain years after a reconstruction is common and under recognised.
- Pes tenderness with compartment change means two sources of medial pain.
- In an athlete the management belongs to load rather than to surgery.
- A late diagnosed tendon rupture usually traces to a missing active extension test.

**4.6.7 Tibial Tubercle Tenderness**

- The commonest relevance is the knee that has had a tubercle osteotomy and still hurts.
- Localised tenderness over a screw is prominent hardware, with a reliable small operation.
- A bone patellar tendon bone harvest site sits at exactly this point.
- Tenderness with movement at an osteotomy site suggests a non union.
- A late diagnosed avulsion usually traces to a missing active extension test.

**4.6.8 Fibular Head Tenderness**

- Persisting tenderness over a device after corner reconstruction is prominent hardware.
- A partial nerve deficit after corner surgery is an operative injury or a compressive problem.
- A foot drop with no injury is a compressive lesion until proven otherwise.
- Previous fibular surgery means the attachments may not be where they are palpated for.
- An undocumented nerve examination in a lateral injury is a serious gap in a record.

**4.6.9 Posterior Knee Palpation**

- Doing the posterior examination answers a proportion of referrals outright.
- A persisting posteromedial complaint after reconstruction is an unaddressed corner.
- A popliteal mass that does not change with knee position needs imaging rather than reassurance.
- Distal symptoms with a popliteal mass mean a compressive lesion.
- The absence of any recorded posterior examination is a reasonable criticism of a record.

**4.6.10 Popliteal Fossa Examination**

- A recurrent cyst after excision has an unaddressed intra-articular cause.
- A firm mass called a cyst for months has had a diagnosis rather than an examination.
- A foot drop with a mass at the fibular neck is a compressive ganglion.
- After posterior surgery a mass may be a pseudoaneurysm rather than a cyst.
- An undocumented vascular examination in a dislocated knee is among the most serious omissions.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.6.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
