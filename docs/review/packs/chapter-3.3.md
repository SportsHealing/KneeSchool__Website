## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.3.1 Introduction to Kinematics**

- Stability is a kinetic claim and motion a kinematic one; an operation can restore one alone.
- Abnormal kinematics with normal laxity is a recognised post-reconstruction pattern.
- An implant imposes a path rather than negotiating one.
- Biplanar fluoroscopy is the in vivo reference method, limited by equipment not by question.
- A kinematic study generalises only as far as the task it measured.

**3.3.2 Roll and Glide Mechanisms**

- Rollback is a design requirement; retaining and stabilised designs produce it differently.
- Paradoxical anterior sliding loses the lever arm deep flexion needs.
- A reconstruction can restore the limit and leave the contact path shifted.
- Sagittal radius and insert conformity decide how much the path is imposed.
- No routine clinical measurement of contact path exists.

**3.3.3 Femoral Motion on Tibia**

- Closed chain is the condition most surgical decisions are really about.
- The ground reaction force line relative to the knee exists only in closed chain.
- A chain has several solutions, which is why function and laxity dissociate.
- Static long leg alignment and dynamic measurement can disagree.
- Compensation is visible in a kinematic record and not separable from capacity by it.

**3.3.4 Tibial Motion on Femur**

- Open chain asks a structural question and closed chain a functional one.
- A graft can measure tight in open chain and be inadequate in closed chain.
- The two chains load a graft at different parts of the range.
- Examination under anaesthetic is the cleanest clinical open chain measurement.
- No open chain measurement reliably predicts a closed chain symptom.

**3.3.5 Screw Home Mechanism**

- An arthroplasty without full extension has lost the locking position.
- The coupled rotation constrains component rotation in replacement.
- A graft placed too anteriorly limits terminal extension because it blocks coupled motion.
- Full extension has no partial version; achieve it on the table.
- Lost extension after reconstruction is investigated, not accepted as stiffness.

**3.3.6 Reverse Screw Home Mechanism**

- Anatomic corner reconstruction addresses the complex rather than a subset, by conventional teaching.
- The deficit is rotational and positional, so symptoms appear near extension.
- A missed corner injury is a recognised cause of cruciate graft failure.
- Varus alignment loads a reconstructed corner and is addressable.
- No measurement shows whether a reconstructed popliteus is functioning.

**3.3.7 Instant Centre of Rotation**

- Single radius and multi radius designs disagree about the centre path; both approximate.
- Hinged implants abandon the native centrode deliberately for constraint.
- An off axis hinged fixator translates the joint while it flexes.
- A well designed implant placed off axis imposes the wrong path.
- No routine postoperative measurement of the centrode exists.

**3.3.8 Changes Throughout Flexion**

- Mid flexion is where reconstructions and replacements fail quietly.
- Mid flexion instability is laxity at 30 to 60 degrees with stable extension and flexion.
- Conventional teaching attributes it to femoral component position and radius of curvature.
- Joint line position shifts the whole arc and the ligament tension through it.
- Mid flexion instability has no agreed diagnostic threshold.

**3.3.9 Kinematics During Walking**

- Walking is the patient's benchmark and a weak discriminator between constructs.
- The adduction moment discriminates, and it is kinetic rather than kinematic.
- Stiff knee gait has several causes; separating them is the clinical work.
- Wearable monitoring answers the real world question that laboratory gait does not.
- No kinematic measure substitutes for whether walking is acceptable to the patient.

**3.3.10 Kinematics During Running**

- Return to running is a mechanical decision, not a time based one.
- Repeated loading distinguishes running from any single cycle clinical test.
- Cumulative cycles, not peak load, drive the cartilage consequence.
- Performance tests are proxies; they do not measure joint kinematics.
- No measurement predicts an individual's tolerance.

**3.3.11 Kinematics During Squatting**

- Deep flexion loads a small posterior contact area; the design question is tolerance.
- High flexion demand is cultural and occupational, so the goal is set per patient.
- Posterior condylar offset and joint line position determine achievable flexion.
- Retrieval analysis shows where deep flexion loading actually fell.
- Measured range and the ability to kneel correlate imperfectly.

**3.3.12 Kinematics During Stair Climbing**

- Stair function discriminates because it needs range and eccentric control.
- Anterior knee pain from patellofemoral overload presents on stairs.
- Stairs expose mid flexion instability and an unfavourable contact path.
- Fluoroscopic step up studies underlie several arthroplasty kinematic comparisons.
- No clinical measurement separates quadriceps capacity from mechanical restriction here.

**3.3.13 Kinematics During Landing**

- The pattern is associated with injury; screening an individual has limited predictive value.
- Prevention evidence rests on injury rates rather than on measured kinematic change.
- Asymmetrical landing kinematics persists after reconstruction with normal laxity.
- Limb symmetry indices have a ceiling effect when both limbs deconditioned.
- No measurement identifies the individual who will reinjure.

**3.3.14 Kinematics During Cutting**

- Cutting is the task a rotationally deficient knee fails.
- Lateral augmentation's mechanical argument rests on the coupled rotation this task demands.
- Increased posterior tibial slope is identified as a graft failure risk in pivoting athletes.
- Instrumented pivot shift measures the relevant laxity without an agreed threshold.
- No clinical measurement reproduces an unanticipated sporting cut.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.3.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.

## The same pack in two other formats

A spreadsheet, which is easier to fill, and a Word file, which is easier to print:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --chapter 3.3 --format all

All three carry the same rows and the same references, and `--ingest` reads any of them.
