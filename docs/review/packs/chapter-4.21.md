## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.21.1 Single Leg Squat**

- Observational scoring varies between examiners and over time.
- The pattern is coachable within a session, so it measures strategy not capacity.
- Dynamic valgus predicts at a population level and not in an individual.
- Pain and control are confounded in the recorded result.
- Single plane observation misses the rotational component.

**4.21.2 Single Leg Hop**

- A ratio can normalise while both limbs stay below their original capacity.
- A forward hop does not test rotational or lateral demand.
- Observational landing grades reintroduce subjectivity.
- Fear and capacity both limit the result and are not separated.
- Passing a hop battery does not establish readiness.

**4.21.3 Triple Hop**

- The test samples the fatigue axis rather than testing it.
- Total distance masks the distribution across the three hops.
- Observational control grading is the least reliable part of the test.
- Within session learning is a systematic bias in a paired comparison.
- The battery's relationship to reinjury is a literature question.

**4.21.4 Crossover Hop**

- The test imposes a planned change of direction and injuries follow unplanned ones.
- Technique and familiarity weaken the paired comparison here most.
- The test scores the outcome without locating the rotational deficit.
- A symmetrical result does not establish a restored rotational restraint.
- Thresholds and their link to reinjury are literature questions.

**4.21.5 Y Balance Test**

- Slow controlled demands carry little predictive weight for fast injuries.
- Reach is confounded by ankle, hip and trunk factors.
- The unaffected limb is not unaffected after a long rehabilitation.
- Normative thresholds come from specific populations.
- Reproducibility is not validity.

**4.21.6 Landing Error Scoring System**

- The faults are associated with injury in groups, not in individuals.
- A planned self initiated landing is a different event from an injury.
- Observational scores are not comparable between clinics.
- The pattern is modifiable within minutes, so the score is performance not habit.
- Whether improving a score reduces injury is a literature question.

**4.21.7 Return to Sport Testing**

- The battery describes a state rather than forecasting an outcome.
- Thresholds are conventions from specific populations.
- Symmetry criteria are satisfied by bilateral deconditioning.
- The components become limiting in turn rather than together.
- The decision involves risk that belongs to the athlete.

**4.21.8 Return to Play Decision Making**

- The link between criteria and reinjury is contested and no figure is supplied here.
- External pressure is context and not assessment.
- A clinician's risk tolerance is an uncontrolled source of variation.
- Different advice at different points in a season can be legitimate.
- Deferring has a cost that is rarely weighed.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.21.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
