**0.2.1 Common Knee Injuries in Young Athletes**

- This is for recognising a problem, not for diagnosing one.
- A pop and swelling within hours usually means something significant.
- Front of knee pain, worse on stairs and after sitting, is very common in teenagers.
- Overuse pain almost always follows a change in training, surface or footwear.
- Locking, giving way, and not bearing weight are the signs that need checking.

**0.2.2 Osgood-Schlatter and Growing Pains**

- The bump below your kneecap is where the thigh tendon attaches to your shin.
- While you are growing, that attachment point is softer than the tendon pulling on it.
- Repeated hard pulling during a growth spurt irritates it, causing a tender lump.
- It settles when the growing area turns to solid bone, though the bump may remain.
- Pain in one exact spot after sport is different from vague evening growing pains.

**0.2.3 Warm-Ups That Work**

- A warm up prepares you to play and teaches your body how to move.
- Most knee injuries in sport happen with nobody touching the player.
- Programmes that work include landing, cutting, strength and single leg balance.
- Hip strength matters, because the hip controls where the thigh points on landing.
- Doing it twice a week all season matters more than which programme you pick.

**0.2.4 Girls, Boys and Knees**

- Female athletes tear the ACL more often than male athletes in the same sports.
- Pelvis width, landing patterns, muscle balance and hormones probably all contribute.
- Dynamic valgus means the knee collapsing inwards during landing or turning.
- Movement patterns and strength can be trained, and training works well.
- Part of the gap may reflect unequal access to coaching rather than biology.

**0.2.5 When to Tell an Adult**

- Cannot bear weight, rapid swelling, a pop, or a locked knee all mean tell someone today.
- A hot red swollen knee with feeling unwell needs seeing the same day.
- Numbness, coldness or colour change in the foot after a knee injury is urgent.
- Giving way, disturbed sleep, or soreness lasting weeks means tell someone soon.
- Say when it started, what you were doing, where it hurts and how fast it swelled.

**0.2.6 Athlete Stories**

- The injury moment is often less dramatic than people expect.
- The hard part is usually the middle months, not the operation.
- Trusting the knee again takes longer than being physically ready.
- Return is judged on what the knee can do rather than on the calendar.
- Accounts here will include people who did not return to their sport.
