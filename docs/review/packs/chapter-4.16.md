## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.16.1 Quadriceps Tendon**

- A missed rupture gives a harder repair and a worse result.
- An extension lag after arthroplasty may be a tendon problem, separated by palpation.
- A quadriceps graft harvest leaves a tender donor site that is not a reconstruction failure.
- Bilateral spontaneous ruptures mean a systemic problem.
- A missed rupture traces to the absent active extension test.

**4.16.2 Patellar Tendon**

- Donor site pain after a graft harvest is common, under recognised and not a reconstruction failure.
- A normal arthroscopy with lower pole tenderness is a tendinopathy the arthroscopy could not address.
- A rupture after a previous harvest is a different repair proposition.
- Bilateral insertional symptoms with systemic features is an enthesopathy.
- A missed rupture traces to the same absent test as at the quadriceps tendon.

**4.16.3 Jumper's Knee**

- A normal arthroscopy with lower pole tenderness had the diagnosis available at the first consultation.
- An athlete whose programme failed may not have had a programme.
- Donor site pain after a graft harvest is a different problem with a different prognosis.
- In an elite athlete the surgeon's role is the surgical decision rather than the programme.
- As an overload condition it is hard to attribute to a single event.

**4.16.4 Osgood-Schlatter Disease**

- Tubercle pain with an effusion and night pain in a growing patient needs a different assessment.
- Persistent symptoms after maturity suggest a separate ossicle, a tendinopathy or something unrelated.
- A prominent tubercle with pain on kneeling is a mechanical consequence, not an active apophysitis.
- A normal joint is what allows load to be worked with rather than rest alone.
- The condition is developmental and is not attributable to an injury.

**4.16.5 Sinding-Larsen-Johansson Syndrome**

- The referral usually needs explanation rather than investigation.
- A separate ossicle persisting after maturity is a mechanical residue, not an apophysitis.
- Bilateral symptoms with systemic features and several tender insertions is an enthesopathy.
- Symptoms recurring each season are a load pattern problem rather than a recurring disease.
- It is developmental and is not attributable to an injury.

**4.16.6 Hamstring Tendinopathy**

- Hamstring problems usually explain posterior pain investigated as meniscal.
- Donor site symptoms after a graft harvest are common and not a reconstruction failure.
- Biceps tenderness with a nerve palsy after corner surgery needs the operation note to separate.
- Recurrent hamstring problems in a cruciate deficient knee may be substitution rather than coincidence.
- As an overload condition it is hard to attribute to a single event.

**4.16.7 Pes Anserinus Pathology**

- The pes explains medial pain in a patient whose joint has been found acceptable.
- Donor site symptoms after a hamstring harvest sit here and are under recognised.
- With medial compartment arthritis, an operation on the joint only partly addresses the complaint.
- After a medial reconstruction the tenderness may be at a fixation point.
- As an overload condition it is hard to attribute to a single event.

## How to return this
