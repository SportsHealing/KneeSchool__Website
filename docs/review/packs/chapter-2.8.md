## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**2.8.1 Gross Anatomy**

- Posterior restraint shifts from capsule and corners near extension to the ligament by 90 degrees.
- The bundles load reciprocally: anterolateral with flexion, posteromedial with extension.
- The meniscofemoral ligaments carry a real share of the posterior restraint.
- The distance to the popliteal artery rises with flexion and falls with posterior capsular injury.
- A combined injury is more likely than an isolated one after high energy trauma.

**2.8.2 AL Bundle**

- The anterior tunnel limit at the femur is set by the cartilage margin, not by bone.
- The bundle is the flexion half of a reciprocal pair with the posteromedial bundle.
- Figures quoted for the whole ligament approximate this bundle, because it dominates the area.
- An intact anterior meniscofemoral ligament is restraint the knee still has.
- Graft behaviour follows the flexion angle at fixation.

**2.8.3 PM Bundle**

- The femoral footprint is the posterior and distal part of the crescent, away from cartilage.
- Figures for the whole ligament understate what is lost when this bundle alone fails.
- It restrains in the position most used walking on the flat.
- Isolated injury is an imaging diagnosis, not an examination one.
- An intact posterior meniscofemoral ligament restrains in the same part of the range.

**2.8.4 Blood Supply**

- The sheath carries the supply a partial injury depends on, and stripping it removes that route.
- The middle genicular artery is short and tethered, so it does not move with the capsule.
- Receptor population is smaller than the anterior cruciate's, so proprioceptive arguments carry less weight.
- Healing in continuity at greater length restores appearance, not function.
- The fat plane behind the capsule is what a capsular injury takes away.

**2.8.5 MRI Anatomy**

- Posterior capsule, fat plane and vessels are visible and their state changes the plan.
- The footprint to cartilage distance is measurable on a coronal slice before operation.
- Continuity, uniformity and calibre are three separate observations.
- An intact meniscofemoral ligament, visible on the study, is restraint the knee still has.
- A curved ligament in an extended knee is normal, not a healed injury.

**2.8.6 Surgical Anatomy**

- A transtibial graft turns at the posterior cortex; an inlay graft does not turn, and that is the anatomical argument.
- A previous tunnel removes the landmark it was drilled from, so revision anatomy is its own anatomy.
- In a multiligament knee the planes behind the capsule are displaced or absent.
- Quoted distances to the artery come from intact knees and do not apply to an injured capsule.
- A plane that cannot be identified cannot be protected.
