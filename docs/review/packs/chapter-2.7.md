## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**2.7.1 Gross Anatomy**

- The notch is a fixed volume and everything added to it competes for space.
- The lateral wall's obliquity determines the trajectory any femoral tunnel can take.
- A continuum of fibres with differing length patterns is the framing that predicts correctly.
- Graft obliquity, tunnel position and notch volume are three expressions of one anatomy.
- A graft much larger than the native ligament fills a notch rather than restoring anatomy.

**2.7.2 Embryology**

- The footprint to physis relation changes with age, so the same operation carries different risk at different skeletal ages.
- A paediatric cruciate is better vascularised, less maturely inserted, and surrounded by growing bone.
- Congenital absence reconstruction differs because the notch itself is abnormal.
- The repair argument in proximal avulsion rests partly on a developmental property.

**2.7.3 Histology**

- An intact synovial sheath is what makes repair biologically plausible.
- Ligamentisation sets the rehabilitation timeline, not the fixation.
- Tunnel healing gives an indirect insertion, so the four zone enthesis is never reproduced.
- Failure timing separates technique, biology and new injury.

**2.7.4 Blood Supply**

- The artery is a relation of posterior cruciate surgery as well as a supply of the anterior.
- A preserved looking remnant in a revision knee may not be vascularised.
- The supply is why reconstruction rather than repair became standard.
- Remnant preservation trades visualisation for vascularity and proprioception.

**2.7.5 Innervation**

- The sheath carries the sensory function and is the thing sacrificed for a clear footprint view.
- Mechanical and sensory recovery run on different timescales.
- The sensory gap is a reasonable explanation for stability based return to sport criteria performing poorly.
- Neuromuscular rehabilitation addresses the sensory deficit, not the graft.

**2.7.6 Femoral Footprint**

- Whether a new femoral tunnel communicates with the old one decides one versus two stage, and computed tomography decides that.
- Revising a vertical graft means moving the attachment posteriorly and distally on the wall.
- With no identifiable ridge, the articular margin and the wall's extent are the fallback references.
- In the immature knee the footprint to physis relation decides the technique.

**2.7.7 Tibial Footprint**

- Tunnel widening is commoner at the tibia and a graft cannot be fixed in a cavity.
- An anterior tunnel causes roof impingement; notchplasty treats the symptom and revision the cause.
- Root repair fixation can occupy the cruciate tunnel's path, so sequence matters.
- Stage the revision on preoperative imaging, not at arthroscopy.

**2.7.8 Anteromedial Bundle**

- A surviving bundle is both the thing preserved and the thing obscuring the footprint.
- Two cords predicts that reproducing both restores native behaviour, and that has not borne out cleanly.
- The useful judgement is whether a surviving bundle is functional, and it is made arthroscopically.
- In chronic partial injury the surviving bundle may have adapted.

**2.7.9 Posterolateral Bundle**

- An anatomical tunnel with a compromised wall is not better than a slightly anterior one in intact bone.
- A vertical graft cannot reproduce an oblique bundle.
- The pivot shift is the sign that tracks this bundle and translation testing misses it.
- In revision for rotational laxity, consider the lateral structures and the lateral root too.

**2.7.10 Proprioception**

- The ligament's contribution cannot be isolated clinically, which limits how much the anatomy can carry.
- Where remnant preservation compromises footprint placement, placement wins.
- A reconstructed knee is sensorily altered, probably permanently; rehabilitation trains compensation.
- Expect bilateral deficits, so test the uninjured side before using it as a reference.

**2.7.11 MRI Anatomy**

- Tunnel position, diameter, convergence and bone quality are the revision questions, and computed tomography answers them.
- An anterior tunnel with a continuous graft is a technical failure; an anatomical tunnel with a torn graft is not.
- Graft signal increases with remodelling in the first year and does not mean failure.
- Assess graft orientation against the notch roof as for a native ligament.

**2.7.12 Surgical Anatomy**

- Tunnel convergence is geometry and computed tomography answers it before the drill is in hand.
- A notchplasty alters the wall the footprint sits on, so it is not a free choice.
- In the multi-ligament knee the limit is behind the landmarks, so vascular assessment comes first.
- Both physes bound the trajectory in an immature knee, and the femoral footprint is closer to its physis.
