## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.4.1 Normal Gait**

- A normal gait in a patient referred as disabled is the consultation, not a dismissal.
- A knee that fails in sport needs the assessment moved to the sport's demand.
- A normal gait with gross laxity and a wasted quadriceps is worth distrusting.
- In a multiply operated knee a normal gait is the strongest evidence against further surgery.
- A recorded normal gait is hard to reconcile with a claim of severe disability.

**4.4.2 Antalgic Gait**

- A persisting limp with a normal laxity examination is pain, not instability.
- An antalgic gait after arthroplasty or reconstruction raises low grade infection.
- An intermittent limp with a normal examination deserves more than one observation.
- An antalgic pattern replacing an avoidance pattern indicates compartment change.
- Recording the distance walked and the conditions makes the observation defensible.

**4.4.3 Quadriceps Avoidance Gait**

- A persisting pattern with a competent graft means the quadriceps has not recovered.
- Losing an established pattern and starting to limp means pain or compartment change.
- An athlete can walk with a normal wave and still avoid loading at the intensity that fails.
- A lateral extra-articular procedure can make the adaptation persist for mechanical reasons.
- In a posterior cruciate deficient knee the quadriceps is the compensation rather than the problem.

**4.4.4 PCL Deficient Gait**

- Deteriorating gait with an unchanged drawer is compartment change or a stretched corner.
- A varus thrust after reconstruction is an unaddressed corner or an uncorrected alignment.
- A fixed posterior subluxation measures deceptively well, and the gait is more honest.
- An unrecovered nerve palsy produces a thrust that is not ligamentous.
- In the athlete the failing task is deceleration, which the clinic cannot reproduce.

**4.4.5 Trendelenburg Gait**

- In an exhaustively investigated knee, this finding revises the referral's premise.
- Persistent giving way with a competent graft may be proximal control rather than instability.
- The single leg tasks separate those two better than the ligament tests do.
- After a hip arthroplasty the pattern is expected and does not indicate new pathology.
- With a spinal cause the examination has to widen rather than deepen.

**4.4.6 Arthritic Gait**

- It reframes a referral from a ligament question into a joint preservation one.
- A varus thrust with reduced excursion in a revision referral points at the compartment, not the graft.
- After meniscectomy in a young patient this gait is often the earliest sign of a narrowing window.
- A thrust after an osteotomy is under correction or a ligament problem the correction exposed.
- The development of this gait after an injury is the strongest observational evidence of progression.

**4.4.7 Post-operative Gait**

- The post-operative gait is usually the only objective record of progress, and it usually does not exist.
- Without an earlier record, change cannot be assessed, and that is a limit to state.
- A gait that normalised and deteriorated is graft failure, compartment change or a stretched corner.
- In a multiply operated knee an abnormal gait may be the best achievable result.
- An unrecovered nerve palsy gives a permanent abnormality that is not a reconstruction failure.

**4.4.8 Running Gait Assessment**

- Giving way only when turning at speed is graded under anaesthesia, not on a treadmill.
- Pain at a reproducible distance is a management question rather than a diagnostic one.
- A patient who will not run on the knee has a confidence problem, and that is the finding.
- In the elite athlete the surgeon interprets what the team's staff report.
- A claim about running capacity is almost never supported by an observation of running.

**4.4.9 Single Leg Landing Assessment**

- The assessment separates the two commonest reasons a reconstructed knee disappoints.
- A competent landing with no trust in the knee is a confidence problem, and surgery worsens it.
- Medial collapse or an inability to land needs the anaesthetised examination to localise.
- In an athlete a competent landing is necessary and not sufficient.
- A recorded single leg task is objective and reproducible, and its absence weakens a record.

**4.4.10 Functional Movement Screening**

- Screening is most useful as what has not been done to a patient who has been investigated instead.
- A movement problem appears on a screen rather than on a further scan.
- Whether an established asymmetry is still modifiable is a rehabilitation judgement.
- In the elite athlete the surgeon interprets the team's data rather than generating it.
- In a hypermobile patient only the comparison with their own other leg is interpretable.
