## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**5.3.1 AP View**

- Standardised acquisition protocols exist and are used in trials, not clinics.
- Repeat films differ by about as much as a year of progression.
- Clinic films cannot substitute for a standardised acquisition.
- The position is not recorded in any usable form.
- Compartment appearance and symptoms correlate loosely.

**5.3.2 Lateral View**

- Several patellar height ratios are in use and do not agree.
- Tibial slope depends on tibial length included and on the axis chosen.
- The slope thresholds come from small series measured differently.
- Cross sectional measurement is more reproducible and not what thresholds used.
- Report the method alongside the value.

**5.3.3 Skyline View**

- The angle thresholds derive from a flexion angle rarely used now.
- The measurements driving instability surgery are made cross sectionally.
- The plain film's role has narrowed without the conventions following.
- Dynamic tracking is the clinic's question and no static view answers it.
- Read this view for the articular surface and go elsewhere for position.

**5.3.4 Tunnel View**

- Real detection advantage and no characterisation ability.
- A positive leads to another study and a negative excludes less than it seems.
- The notch width literature predates routine magnetic resonance imaging.
- The view survives on cost and availability rather than capability.
- It is redundant where cross sectional imaging is already planned.

**5.3.5 Rosenberg View**

- Its advantage has been established for decades and the default has not changed.
- The grading systems were built on extended views.
- Clinic reproducibility is below the published performance.
- Weight bearing computed tomography removes the projection entirely.
- Request it by name and use the same protocol every time.

**5.3.6 Long Leg Alignment Films**

- Rotation is the dominant error and is hard to detect from the image.
- Coronal, sagittal and rotational deformity coexist and this film measures one.
- Weight bearing computed tomography measures all three under load.
- Alignment targets have moved towards patient specific goals.
- Say so when the film was not acquired to protocol.

**5.3.7 Mechanical Axis**

- Neutral alignment is now one target among several.
- A deviation can be constitutional, and one film cannot date it.
- Standing alignment includes compartment laxity as well as bone.
- Dynamic alignment predicts load better and is rarely measured.
- The measurement is reliable and the target is contested.

**5.3.8 Anatomical Axis**

- Several conventions exist on each axis system with different normal ranges.
- Alignment philosophies reference different axes.
- A navigated system's axes are not the radiographic ones.
- Three dimensional planning measures the geometry directly.
- Name the axis and the convention with every angle.

**5.3.9 Joint Line Obliquity**

- Alignment philosophies differ in how much obliquity they accept.
- In a worn knee the femoral and tibial definitions are not parallel.
- Standing obliquity mixes bone, cartilage and soft tissue.
- A stress view would separate them and is not routine.
- Measure it, state the definition, and locate its source.

**5.3.10 Deformity Analysis**

- Rotational deformity interacts with coronal correction.
- Population normal ranges are applied to individuals.
- Standing films include soft tissue laxity the analysis attributes to bone.
- Three dimensional planning measures all three planes directly.
- State the planar analysis's limits, and go three dimensional for multiplanar cases.

**5.3.11 Tibial Plateau Fractures**

- The common classifications were defined on radiographs.
- They group fractures computed tomography shows to be mechanically different.
- Soft tissue injuries are under-recognised because the fracture dominates.
- An unaddressed root or corner injury outlasts the fracture.
- A classification should match the study it was derived from.

**5.3.12 Patellar Fractures**

- Articular step and comminution determine later patellofemoral arthritis.
- Neither is measured well on plain films nor imaged routinely.
- Functional outcome depends on something no imaging measures.
- Fixation is assessed for position rather than for articular reduction.
- A bipartite patella can itself become symptomatic.

**5.3.13 Femoral Condyle Fractures**

- A fracture plane aligned with the beam produces no edge to see.
- Reading harder does not help; a different projection or a volume does.
- Imaging further is a protocol decision rather than a judgement.
- Recognition improved because computed tomography became routine.
- The associated soft tissue injury is still under-recognised.

**5.3.14 Avulsion Injuries**

- The fragment's diagnostic weight comes from anatomical knowledge.
- A descriptive report loses the implication entirely.
- The paediatric versions carry a growth consequence as well.
- Magnetic resonance imaging shows the soft tissue and the fragment less well.
- Read by attachment site and name the structure implicated.

**5.3.15 Segond Fractures**

- The avulsed structure's identity is less settled than the fragment's origin.
- Extra-articular augmentation is an active question and this is one input.
- The fragment is often absent on later films, so the injury films matter.
- Cross sectional imaging shows the anterolateral structures inconsistently.
- A well established sign of a contested structure.

**5.3.16 Kellgren-Lawrence Grading**

- It was built to classify populations and is used to grade individuals.
- Several modified versions exist and are not interchangeable.
- Reader agreement is worst in the middle of the scale.
- Automation improves consistency without improving validity.
- Describe the compartments and give the grade as a convenience.

**5.3.17 Joint Space Narrowing**

- It remains the regulatory endpoint for structural progression.
- Trial grade and clinic measurements are different quantities with one name.
- Meniscal extrusion confounds it substantially.
- Neither half of the mechanism is measured under load.
- Better measures exist and have not displaced the surrogate.

**5.3.18 Osteophytes**

- The scale weights the feature with no mechanical consequence earliest.
- Osteophyte may be adaptive, making its presence a mixed signal.
- Magnetic resonance imaging detects it earlier without changing its meaning.
- Its interference with the extrusion landmark has no agreed solution.
- Report it as diagnostic and carry severity in the compartment description.

**5.3.19 Subchondral Sclerosis**

- Sclerosis and the marrow lesion are the same place and different properties.
- The marrow lesion carries prognostic associations and sclerosis does not.
- Radiographic density is uncalibrated and cannot be used longitudinally.
- Computed tomography measures density quantitatively and is unused here.
- Read sclerosis as confirmation and go cross sectional for measurement.

**5.3.20 Alignment Changes**

- Alignment, wear and laxity are three contributors to one measurement.
- Patient specific targets make the pre-arthritic alignment valuable and unrecoverable.
- Whether correction changes the natural history is uncertain for most patients.
- The decision rests on the oldest imaging technology in this section.
- Radiography answers load and alignment questions only when position is controlled.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-5.3.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
