## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**0.4.1 Choosing Subjects**

- Medical schools set their own requirements and they differ, so there is no single list to learn.
- Chemistry is the subject most often required outright and the riskiest one to drop.
- Requirements come in three layers: required, preferred, and not counted.
- Choose a combination that satisfies every school you might apply to, not most of them.
- Read requirements from the university's own pages, because they change each cycle.

**0.4.2 Work Experience and Volunteering**

- Work experience does not have to be in a hospital, and most medical schools say so.
- Care experience and positions of responsibility count alongside clinical observation.
- What is assessed is the reflection, not the list of places.
- Writing a short note after each session is far easier than remembering it a year later.
- Depth in one or two settings beats a week in six.

**0.4.3 UCAT and Admissions Tests**

- Admissions tests for medicine test information handling under time pressure, not recall.
- The UCAT is used by most United Kingdom medical schools; a few courses use another test.
- How much a score counts differs sharply between schools and each one publishes its method.
- Official free practice material exists, and format familiarity matters more than anything else.
- A bursary covers the fee for eligible candidates.

**0.4.4 Personal Statements and Interviews**

- Statements and interviews assess reasoning, not enthusiasm, because every candidate has enthusiasm.
- A specific question you followed is worth more than a declared ambition.
- Multiple mini interviews and panel interviews are scored differently and schools publish which they use.
- Ethical scenarios usually have no right answer; the marks are in the reasoning.
- Practise out loud with interruptions rather than silently.

**0.4.5 Widening Participation**

- Widening participation adjusts for circumstance; it does not lower the standard of the degree.
- Support comes as contextual offers, foundation years, outreach programmes and financial help.
- Criteria are broader than most students assume and the schemes are often under subscribed.
- Some programmes must be joined before the application year, so check two years ahead.
- Each university publishes its own criteria and they differ.

**0.4.6 Gateway and Graduate Entry Routes**

- Gateway, graduate entry, standard entry as a graduate and Access routes all lead to the same qualification.
- Gateway courses usually require widening participation eligibility before grades are considered.
- Standard entry has far more places than graduate entry and graduates often overlook it.
- Several schools accept a non science degree for graduate entry.
- Eligibility criteria are published by each university and differ between them.
