## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**5.20.1 Structured Reporting**

- Report words have no shared definition between departments.
- An automated report is complete by construction.
- Serial comparison across departments compares templates.
- Re-reporting is a clinical act and belongs in the notes.
- A conclusion that commits is the point of the exercise.

**5.20.2 Reporting Meniscal Tears**

- Repairable is not a radiological finding.
- Absence of the words is not absence of the lesion.
- A repaired meniscus has no agreed normal appearance.
- Automated reporting reproduces reader disagreement.
- The report should state what could not be assessed.

**5.20.3 Reporting ACL Injuries**

- Graft reporting needs the interval to mean anything.
- Tunnel position decides a revision and is often unreported.
- A primary report is reused for a revision question.
- Automation is strongest where reading was already reliable.
- The report should state the interval since injury or surgery.

**5.20.4 Reporting MCL Injuries**

- Absence of the corner from a report carries no information.
- The scan and the examination use different vocabularies.
- A reconstructed medial side has no published reference appearance.
- Serial scans measure interval rather than outcome.
- The report should name the layer and the site.

**5.20.5 Reporting Cartilage Lesions**

- Imaging and arthroscopic grades disagree.
- Quantitative measures vary between departments.
- A repair has no agreed reference appearance.
- Automated grading returns no uncertainty.
- The report should name the sequences relied on.

**5.20.6 Reporting OA**

- A series across departments documents radiography as much as progression.
- Differently positioned films cannot show progression.
- Grades from different systems are not comparable.
- Quantitative measures are not decision grade in a clinic.
- Spared compartments should be stated as spared.

**5.20.7 Reporting Post-operative Knees**

- The technique is rarely identifiable from the images.
- Serial studies across departments compare protocols.
- A bony procedure makes later measurements describe the operation.
- Cartilage near metal is the least reliable assessment.
- The report should state what the artefact prevented.

**5.20.8 Common Reporting Errors**

- Inherited reports arrive as constraints rather than corrections.
- Re-reporting is a decision and belongs in the notes.
- A tertiary practice cannot see the base rate.
- Automation removes the hedge and keeps the wrong finding.
- A confident wrong finding is not fixable by a template.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-5.20.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
