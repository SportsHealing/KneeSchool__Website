## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.12.1 Patellar Tracking**

- The engagement arc is where the mechanics matter and where most indices do not look.
- Before engagement the medial soft tissue restrains; after it, the lateral trochlear facet.
- The J sign is the handover failing visibly.
- Late engagement, from height or a shallow groove, lengthens the soft tissue window.
- No routine index reports the flexion angle at which the patella engages.

**3.12.2 Contact Areas**

- Area and quadriceps force rise together, and their ratio is what cartilage experiences.
- The ratio is least favourable in early to mid flexion.
- The lateral facet bias matches the wear found at arthroscopy.
- Quadriceps tendon articulation in deep flexion is genuine load sharing.
- Contact area figures are comparable within a study and poorly comparable between studies.

**3.12.3 Contact Pressures**

- Pressure rises with flexion and rises faster in open chain terminal extension.
- Absolute peak figures are rig dependent; the direction of change between conditions survives.
- Surgery reduces pressure by moving or enlarging contact, not by reducing extensor force.
- In vivo estimation accumulates error across motion capture, inverse dynamics and a model.
- Pressure in a loaded, moving, native, living joint is not measurable.

**3.12.4 Trochlear Function**

- Trochlear morphology is the strongest single predictor of recurrent instability.
- It is the one factor no soft tissue procedure addresses.
- Constraint is lost most in the first 30 degrees, which soft tissue already covered alone.
- A supratrochlear spur turns engagement into a climb over a ridge.
- Shape predicts better across a cohort than for the individual, because shape is not constraint.

**3.12.5 MPFL Function**

- The ligament and the trochlea cover different parts of the arc, and a knee needs both.
- A reconstruction restores restraint only where the ligament was tight.
- Tensioning in flexion overconstrains the medial facet and raises medial pressure.
- The femoral origin is close to the physis in a growing knee.
- No measured optimum for graft tension exists, and it is set by feel.

**3.12.6 Q Angle**

- The Q angle survived because it was easy, not because it worked.
- Its failure is structural: it omits a plane and uses a mobile landmark.
- The tubercle to groove distance replaced it by measuring bone to bone.
- That replacement has its own limits and a soft threshold.
- A vector with components in three planes cannot be reduced to one number.

**3.12.7 Dynamic Valgus**

- Dynamic valgus produces relative femoral internal rotation under a patella that does not follow.
- That is the same geometric error as a lateralised tubercle, and it adds to it.
- Displacement and pressure rise together, unlike in static malalignment.
- The patellofemoral evidence for modifying the pattern is weaker than the cruciate evidence.
- Patellofemoral consequences are modelled rather than measured in a living athlete.

**3.12.8 Patellar Instability**

- Instability is a competition between a lateral vector and the restraint at that angle.
- Indices predict in groups because each measures one term in an inequality.
- Addressing two mild factors often beats overcorrecting one severe factor.
- Patellar height ratios disagree, so the ratio must be named with the number.
- No clinical measurement expresses vector and restraint in the same units.

**3.12.9 Patellofemoral Pain**

- The condition has mechanical contributors and is not a mechanical diagnosis.
- Realignment surgery for pain alone has performed poorly.
- Loading programmes outperform mechanical corrections, suggesting tolerance is the modifiable variable.
- Cumulative load and loading rate matter beyond peak pressure.
- Reserve realignment for instability rather than for pain.

**3.12.10 Patellofemoral Arthritis**

- The mechanical account of degeneration is more secure than that of pain.
- Site of wear follows pressure distribution; symptom severity follows neither site nor grade.
- Offloading has a defensible rationale for a mapped lesion and a poor record for undifferentiated pain.
- Instability related degeneration is the clearest mechanical story in this joint.
- No prospective study shows a predicted high pressure site becoming a worn site in an individual.

**3.12.11 Effects of Malalignment**

- The transverse plane is under recognised because the coronal plane was measurable first.
- Femoral anteversion is reported in a substantial minority of recurrent dislocators.
- No soft tissue or tubercle procedure addresses femoral version; a derotation osteotomy does.
- Coronal malalignment matters more for the tibiofemoral joint than for this one.
- Normal ranges for version are wide, so the threshold question is partly about the reference range.

**3.12.12 Tibial Tubercle Position**

- Tubercle transfer is the most mechanically direct operation in this joint.
- Medialisation reduces the lateral vector and raises medial facet pressure.
- Distalisation advances engagement, which is a different mechanism from medialisation.
- Cadaveric work supports each effect; clinical results are more modest.
- No measurement confirms the intended pressure redistribution after surgery.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.12.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
