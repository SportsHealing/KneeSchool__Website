## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.13.1 Mechanical Axis**

- The mechanical axis is a static surrogate for a dynamic quantity, correlating moderately.
- A standing film records a loading condition that occupies a small part of the gait cycle.
- The measurement mixes bony deformity with intra-articular collapse.
- Projection error with rotation can reach several degrees.
- The adduction moment predicts progression best; the angle is what surgery plans to.

**3.13.2 Anatomical Axis**

- Load follows the mechanical axis; instruments reference the shaft.
- Canal based techniques work in the anatomical frame and correct by a fixed offset.
- The fixed offset fails in the bowed femur, in the direction of residual deformity.
- Navigated techniques reference the mechanical frame and add registration error.
- Measuring each patient's own offset is the step most often skipped.

**3.13.3 Varus Alignment**

- Varus increases medial load; the static angle predicts weakly in an individual.
- The dynamic adduction moment is the stronger predictor and correlates only moderately.
- Varus thrust during gait predicts progression independently and is a clinical observation.
- Correcting the angle does not guarantee changing the moment.
- The angle is corrected because it is the variable that can be corrected.

**3.13.4 Valgus Alignment**

- Varus and valgus differ in compartment area, congruence, meniscal contribution and soft tissue.
- Equalising load share does not equalise pressure, because the lateral surface is smaller.
- Chronic valgus can leave a knee that is aligned and unstable.
- Distal femoral osteotomy is the usual correction site.
- Most alignment and load data comes from varus knees.

**3.13.5 Constitutional Varus**

- If neutral is an average rather than a norm, correcting everyone to it overcorrects a third of men.
- A knee aligned away from where its ligaments grew needs release or accepts imbalance.
- The argument supports kinematic alignment in arthroplasty and cuts the other way in osteotomy.
- Teaching has moved from one target to a range whose boundaries are unsettled.
- There is no per patient measurement of constitutional alignment once disease is present.

**3.13.6 Joint Line Obliquity**

- Obliquity turns a one variable correction into a two variable one.
- A tibial correction for a femoral deformity gives a neutral axis loaded in shear.
- The accepted limit of a few degrees is a convention, not a measured threshold.
- Double level correction buys joint line preservation at the cost of a second osteotomy.
- The intra-articular component sets a floor on achievable obliquity.

**3.13.7 Dynamic Alignment**

- The variable that predicts is neither the one measured nor the one surgery corrects.
- Correcting geometry changes the moment arm and leaves force magnitude and timing untouched.
- The moment changes by less than the radiographic correction suggests.
- Gait retraining, wedges and weight loss change mechanics without changing a degree on a film.
- The dynamic quantity is known and simply not measured in practice.

**3.13.8 Alignment and OA**

- Alignment predicts progression of established disease; its role in incidence is weaker.
- Correction has its best rationale with a deformity and a preserved opposite compartment.
- A severe varus knee carries most of its deformity inside the joint.
- Correcting late moves load onto a compartment that may also be worn.
- The mechanical and biological literatures use different endpoints and have not converged.

**3.13.9 Osteotomy Biomechanics**

- The osteotomy is a three plane operation and the indication decides which plane leads.
- Coronal leads for compartment disease; sagittal leads for a steep slope in a revision cruciate knee.
- Combined procedures change sequencing, fixation and rehabilitation.
- A failed previous correction is a new deformity with a surgical component.
- No series reports adduction moment, slope and patellar height together before and after.

**3.13.10 Alignment Correction Principles**

- The fellowship question is which plane's target leads and what the reference should be.
- Kinematic alignment takes the patient's own alignment as the target within limits.
- The same logic argues for less correction in a constitutionally varus knee, unsupported by data.
- A sagittal target has entered practice in the revision cruciate knee with a steep slope.
- No study sets a target in one plane and measures the consequences in the other two.
