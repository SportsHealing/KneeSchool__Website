## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.7.1 Functions of the ACL**

- The four functions are unequally restorable by a graft.
- Anterior restraint restores well, rotation partly, guidance less, sensation hardly at all.
- A stable knee that does not feel normal is the predictable disappointment.
- Muscle has to cover the functions a graft does not restore.
- No instrument captures whether the knee feels normal.

**3.7.2 Primary Restraints**

- Replacing a primary restraint and augmenting a secondary are different operations.
- The anterolateral augmentation debate is a question about the rotational share.
- A well placed single bundle graft restores translation near normal and rotation less so.
- Slope sets the load the restored restraint meets.
- Every construct argument inherits the sectioning order limitation.

**3.7.3 Secondary Restraints**

- Unaddressed secondary restraint injury is the commonest non technical reason for underperformance.
- Ramp lesions and lateral posterior horn tears are the two most often missed.
- They are sought rather than noticed.
- Slope has become a modifiable secondary contributor.
- Revision series are the evidence that secondaries were missed the first time.

**3.7.4 Bundle Function**

- The bundle model is the mechanical content of the tunnel position debate.
- Double bundle restores rotation better in cadavers without a consistent clinical advantage.
- Anatomic single bundle in the footprint centre is the common resolution.
- Graft diameter determines how much of the footprint is spanned.
- The cadaveric and clinical results disagree, which is the honest position.

**3.7.5 AM Bundle Function**

- A single bundle reconstruction most nearly reproduces this bundle's behaviour.
- That restores the translation clinical testing samples and can leave rotational symptoms.
- A transtibial tunnel tends to land more anteromedially than an anatomic one.
- Postoperative computed tomography identifies a non anatomic position.
- A normal arthrometer reading is evidence about this bundle only.

**3.7.6 PL Bundle Function**

- This is the half a reconstruction most often fails to restore.
- Residual rotational laxity is the expected sign of it.
- A lower, more posterior femoral tunnel reproduces this bundle's territory better.
- Robotic testing in rotation at low flexion distinguishes constructs here.
- Slope raises the demand on near extension rotational control.

**3.7.7 Rotational Stability**

- Rotational control decides satisfaction in a pivoting athlete and is measured least well.
- The augmentation literature moved on cadaveric and registry evidence, not a clinical measure.
- The indications are defined by rotational demand rather than translation.
- Lateral posterior horn repair has a rotational rationale.
- Judge the construct by reinjury and return to sport.

**3.7.8 Anterior Stability**

- Restored anterior translation is necessary and insufficient.
- A normal reading with an unstable knee is the apportionment argument in clinical form.
- Instrumented laxity correlates weakly with patient reported outcome.
- Serial measurement is more informative than a single reading.
- Progressive laxity without a new injury prompts a slope measurement.

**3.7.9 Pivot Shift Mechanics**

- The pivot shift corresponds to what the patient reports and is measured worst.
- Construct comparisons rest on reinjury rates because the sign has no threshold.
- A residual pivot shift is associated with poorer outcome and graft failure.
- Check it under anaesthetic after fixation.
- Slope and lateral meniscal pathology are part of abolishing it.

**3.7.10 ACL Strain During Activity**

- The strain data is the basis of rehabilitation protocols, which have outrun it.
- Graft protection matters most in the first months, while remodelling.
- Protocols have moved towards earlier loading on outcome evidence, not new strain data.
- No measurement of graft strain in a patient exists.
- Every protocol argument crosses that gap.

**3.7.11 ACL Loading During Running**

- Running is the benchmark the other activities are read against.
- The graft, not the activity, is the limiting variable in the first months.
- The criteria rather than the interval carry the argument.
- Concurrent meniscal repair delays loading for its own reasons.
- No measurement of graft load in a running patient exists.

**3.7.12 ACL Loading During Landing**

- Landing mechanics are modifiable risk with modest individual predictive value.
- Programmes reduce population rates; screening does not identify the individual.
- Those two statements are often conflated.
- Landing asymmetry persists after reconstruction with normal laxity.
- The graft does not address this loading risk.

**3.7.13 ACL Loading During Cutting**

- Cutting defines the reconstruction's job.
- A construct adequate for running is not necessarily adequate here.
- This is the activity the registry reinjury data is largely about.
- Increased posterior tibial slope is a graft failure risk in pivoting athletes.
- No assessment reproduces an unanticipated sporting cut.

**3.7.14 ACL Injury Mechanisms**

- The mechanism is settled in outline and unsettled in detail.
- Multi component programmes hedge across the candidate mechanisms.
- Slope and lateral plateau morphology are now partly modifiable.
- Previous injury is the strongest single risk factor for another.
- No measurement predicts an individual injury.

**3.7.15 Female ACL Biomechanics**

- The rate difference is established; the mechanism is partly explained.
- It is a basis for prevention rather than for prognosis.
- Registry data reports higher graft failure and contralateral injury in young female athletes.
- Previous injury outweighs sex as a risk factor.
- No measurement predicts which individual athlete will reinjure.

**3.7.16 Graft Biomechanics**

- Revision and the unusual case push the choice away from the primary algorithm.
- Allograft is accepted more readily in revision and multiligament reconstruction.
- Tunnel condition determines what fixation is possible.
- Fixation is the early limit on the construct.
- Construct testing in compromised bone is sparse.

**3.7.17 Hamstring Graft Biomechanics**

- Regenerated tendon is unreliable graft material.
- Where diameter cannot be achieved, each option is a stated mechanical compromise.
- Tunnel widening determines whether single stage revision is possible.
- No measurement establishes a regenerated tendon's mechanical adequacy.
- Plan the graft source before theatre in any revision.

**3.7.18 Patellar Tendon Graft Biomechanics**

- A reharvested patellar tendon is feasible and less predictable.
- Patella baja from a previous harvest changes any later arthroplasty.
- A bone plug can be sized to a defect a soft tissue graft will not hold.
- Record patellar height after harvest.
- No measurement establishes a reharvested tendon's adequacy.

**3.7.19 Quadriceps Tendon Graft Biomechanics**

- It is often the revision solution: an untouched donor site with controllable diameter.
- It suits multiligament reconstruction, where graft quantity matters.
- The extensor mechanism has a finite amount that can be taken across a surgical history.
- Tendon thickness is measured on imaging to plan the diameter.
- No measurement establishes the total extensor cost a patient can afford.

**3.7.20 ACL Reconstruction Biomechanics**

- The fellowship problem is the compromised construct.
- Staging is a mechanical decision, not a cautious one.
- Maximal environmental intervention is reserved for a knee that failed a conventional revision.
- Tunnel imaging drives single or two stage.
- No measurement predicts whether a compromised construct will hold.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.7.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.

## The same pack in two other formats

A spreadsheet, which is easier to fill, and a Word file, which is easier to print:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --chapter 3.7 --format all

All three carry the same rows and the same references, and `--ingest` reads any of them.
