## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.14.1 Pivot Shift**

- A firm Lachman with a persisting shift is an anterolateral problem, not a graft problem.
- That knee is the main indication for a lateral extra-articular procedure at revision.
- A persisting shift with a loose Lachman is a failed graft, distinguished by the translation.
- After a tenodesis a negative pivot shift no longer excludes a failed reconstruction.
- A corner injury gives a larger shift for the same cruciate injury.

**4.14.2 Slocum Test**

- Its main remaining use is interpreting old letters.
- A recorded anteromedial pattern before a failed reconstruction suggests the corner was identified and not addressed.
- A recorded anterolateral pattern means less, because the pivot shift grade is the better record.
- After a meniscectomy the translation is larger for reasons unrelated to the ligaments.
- The findings recorded without the label are worth more than the label.

**4.14.3 Anterolateral Rotatory Instability**

- It explains the commonest referral in Section 4: a firm Lachman and a knee the patient does not trust.
- The pattern is established under anaesthesia rather than in clinic.
- A normal shift under anaesthesia with the same complaint is a confidence problem, and surgery makes it worse.
- After a tenodesis the shift can be abolished over an incompetent graft.
- A deficient lateral meniscus gives a large shift after sound surgery.

**4.14.4 Anteromedial Rotatory Instability**

- It explains the patient whose medial injury was treated correctly and whose knee still fails on turning.
- A corner healed long gives a firm endpoint over a rotationally lax joint.
- A stretched graft with this pattern has its explanation, not a second diagnosis.
- A varus limb makes a medial reconstruction a poor proposition.
- A previous lateral procedure constrains the rotation and makes the assessment under read.

**4.14.5 Combined Instability Patterns**

- The examination is better evidence of the original pattern than the operation note.
- Two sound reconstructions with a persisting complaint usually means a bony factor.
- A pattern that fits no recognised combination raises generalised hypermobility.
- Previous surgery constrains the pattern, so the operation notes are needed to interpret it.
- An unrecovered nerve palsy changes the dynamic examination permanently and gets blamed on laxity.

**4.14.6 Examination Under Anaesthesia**

- The anaesthetised examination from the first operation is often the most useful document in the file.
- A recorded grade zero shift then and a high grade now establishes that something changed.
- An examination after the tourniquet or a long arthroscopy is worth discounting.
- In a multiply operated knee the other knee is no longer a comparison with normal.
- This is the strongest examination evidence that exists, which is an argument for recording it properly.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.14.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
