## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.9.1 Clinical Presentation**

- After resection the examination has to separate a new tear from altered mechanics.
- Joint line tenderness after resection is common, persistent and poorly specific.
- A mechanical block after a repair still stands alone as a finding.
- The examination is substantially less useful after meniscal surgery than before it.

**4.9.2 Joint Line Tenderness**

- After resection, tenderness is frequent, persistent and non-discriminating.
- After a repair, tenderness at six weeks means little and at six months means more.
- Record where and when rather than whether, so the next examination can compare.
- In a transplant or scaffold, early tenderness is expected and late tenderness is a concern.

**4.9.3 McMurray Test**

- After resection a negative test is expected and uninformative.
- A positive after resection may reflect altered mechanics rather than a new tear.
- After a repair the manoeuvre loads the repair, so performing it is a decision.
- The useful post-operative test is the search for a mechanical block.

**4.9.4 Thessaly Test**

- The test retains value after surgery because it measures function rather than trapping a fragment.
- Rotating under load without symptoms after resection is a functionally satisfactory knee.
- A positive does not say whether it is a new tear, altered mechanics or cartilage.
- Do not use it during the protected period after a repair.
- At this level it is better treated as a functional measure than a diagnostic test.

**4.9.5 Apley Compression Test**

- The structure the compression half was trapping may no longer be there.
- The distraction half retains value, since a symptomatic collateral after meniscal surgery is under-diagnosed.
- Persistent medial pain after resection may mean the original problem was never the meniscus.
- In a stiff knee the prone test reports the flexion rather than the meniscus.

**4.9.6 Childress Squat Test**

- After surgery the squat is a functional measure rather than a provocative test.
- A regained full painless squat is a good functional result whatever the imaging shows.
- After a root repair deep flexion is restricted, so the test is a protocol decision.
- In the degenerate knee an inability to squat is near universal and contributes nothing diagnostically.

**4.9.7 Ege Test**

- After surgery it is a functional measure rather than a provocative test.
- A positive in the untouched direction points away from the operated compartment.
- After a root repair or transplant the test is a protocol decision rather than a routine.
- The provocative group was designed to detect tears; imaging now does that better.
- What the examination contributes is attribution and function, which these tests were not designed for.

**4.9.8 Mechanical Locking**

- A block after surgery has its own differential and the meniscus is not at the top of it.
- A cyclops lesion blocks near extension and is frequently recorded as stiffness.
- Whether the knee was ever straight after the operation separates a retained fragment from a contracture.
- After arthroplasty a block raises impingement, osteophyte, cement or component issues.
- The endpoint character is the main clinical discriminator between a block and arthrofibrosis.

**4.9.9 Meniscal Root Tears**

- The common referral is a rapidly deteriorating knee labelled as arthritis.
- How quickly the knee changed is what identifies it.
- A failed repair looks like the original tear, and imaging usually separates them.
- A root repair in a knee that was not realigned is a recognised reason for failure.
- The examination raises the question here and almost never answers it.

**4.9.10 Meniscal Deficiency**

- Deficiency is the predictable end state of the resection the chapter's tests lead to.
- The assessment is alignment, cartilage, symptom localisation and function.
- The provocative tests contribute nothing to this knee.
- The hardest judgement is timing, between too early to need anything and too late for anything but arthroplasty.
- The best examination of a deficient knee is the one that prevented it.

**4.9.11 Meniscal Examination in OA**

- In this group saying no is the service, and the examination makes it defensible.
- A recorded absence of a mechanical block is a reason rather than an opinion.
- The patient who does need an operation is identified by the same examination.
- Eleven pages of tests lead to one finding that acts alone and a combination better than any single test.
- In the degenerate knee the whole group performs poorly, which is not how it is usually taught.
