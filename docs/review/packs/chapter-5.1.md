## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**5.1.1 Why Imaging Matters**

- The imaging is usually already done, and often on an unknown protocol.
- Two scans on different protocols are not a series.
- A report written without the clinical question answers a different one.
- Prevalent findings accumulate with age.
- Re-imaging is decided against what the last study could not answer.

**5.1.2 Clinical Examination Versus Imaging**

- The imaging usually arrives first, so the examination is not independent.
- A reported finding biases the examination towards it.
- The bias is strongest for the findings that most need a check.
- Neither instrument establishes causation.
- The cases where both are wrong together are invisible.

**5.1.3 Choosing the Correct Investigation**

- The real choice is usually whether to repeat an existing study.
- Protocol differences between departments are invisible on the report.
- Substituting an available modality silently changes the plan's basis.
- Advanced techniques answer questions that are unreachable in most places.
- Responsibility for the answer is split between requester and department.

**5.1.4 Imaging Pathways**

- Most patients arrive through somebody else's pathway.
- The existing imaging answered a question you did not ask.
- Repeating is decided against what the last study could not answer.
- A tertiary clinic sees the cases local pathways failed, which biases the view.
- The reporting template is hardest to change and shapes the most.

**5.1.5 Cost-effectiveness of Imaging**

- The marginal scan is usually a repeat, valued by what the first could not answer.
- Advanced techniques carry benefits established in research populations.
- An incidental finding transfers cost to the patient and the system.
- Published work is bound to a health system and a price list.
- Not requesting saves the most and is the hardest to defend afterwards.

**5.1.6 Imaging Pitfalls**

- The prevalence problem has no clinical solution, only a judgement each time.
- Protocol variation between centres is invisible on reports.
- Advanced sequences bring new artefacts rather than fewer.
- Assisted reading changes the error pattern rather than removing it.
- The pitfalls recur across the section, which is why they have a chapter.

**5.1.7 Evidence-Based Imaging**

- This section states comparisons and refuses figures, which illustrates its own subject.
- A reader wanting numbers is told where to go rather than given an approximation.
- Shifting reference standards make old and new accuracy work hard to compare.
- Automated reading is evaluated against readers rather than outcomes.
- The strongest claim available is about which question to ask.

**5.1.8 Future of Knee Imaging**

- Whether a quantitative measure predicts progression in an individual is open.
- Whether loaded imaging adds to a weightbearing radiograph is open.
- Whether automated reading reduces or relocates error is open.
- Whether the magic angle effect can be exploited is this site's own question.
- Whether any of it reaches general practice is not a clinical question.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-5.1.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
