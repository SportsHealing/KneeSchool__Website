## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.1.1 Why Clinical Examination Matters**

- A revision assessment is an examination problem, because the images are full of the previous operation.
- Rotational laxity is missed when only anterior translation is tested.
- A posterolateral injury is missed when the dial test is done at one flexion angle.
- These are failures of completeness rather than of technique, which is why the sequence matters.
- Examination under anaesthetic is the most informative laxity assessment and costs an anaesthetic to obtain.

**4.1.2 Principles of Knee Assessment**

- In a multiligament knee each test loads an unknown combination of failed structures.
- Record what the knee does rather than what you think is torn, because the attribution can be revised and the finding cannot be redone.
- Generalised laxity makes every comparison in this section unreliable, side to side included.
- In both cases the examination's job moves from diagnosis to documentation.

**4.1.3 History Versus Imaging**

- When all three disagree, weighting imaging because it is concrete is wrong more often than right.
- Predict from history and examination before reviewing images, so a disagreement is a finding rather than a correction.
- In revision assessment the previous operation guarantees abnormal imaging.
- None of this is measurable, and nothing on this page is cited.

**4.1.4 Structured Examination Approach**

- In referred practice the examination checks a previous one rather than performing a first.
- Do the parts most likely skipped: rotation at two angles, the hip, and the other knee.
- Examination under anaesthetic is a different instrument, not a more careful version.
- Whether the answer is worth an anaesthetic is a judgement the structure does not contain.

**4.1.5 Common Examination Pitfalls**

- In referred practice the pitfalls are inherited, and the skill is recognising which one.
- Persistent rotational complaints after a well placed graft usually mean rotation was never examined.
- A late posterolateral presentation almost always had a dial test at one angle or none.
- A contracture is invisible if range was recorded without end feel.
- Every claim on this page is a teaching pattern rather than a measured frequency.

**4.1.6 Examination in the Era of MRI**

- In the revision knee imaging is least useful and examination most.
- Once the decision to operate is made, imaging drives the plan and examination contributes little.
- A patient with several findings and an unclear picture is usually better served by a repeat examination than a further scan.
- No frequency for any of these errors is cited here, because none could be retrieved.

**4.1.7 Examination for Different Audiences**

- In referred practice the examination is distorted by arriving with someone else's diagnosis attached.
- Examine before reading the referral in full, which is awkward and is the only clean option.
- An expert audience encourages pattern language and discourages the plain record.
- A conclusion cannot be rebuilt into evidence; evidence can always be rebuilt into a conclusion.

**4.1.8 Examination Documentation**

- In revision practice the old record is often the only evidence of the pre-operative knee.
- A pre-operative examination with no rotational assessment makes a later rotational complaint undatable.
- Range recorded without end feel makes a contracture impossible to date.
- An examination under anaesthetic not labelled as such is non-comparable with every other.
- The pre-operative record is part of the operation, because a procedure that cannot be audited cannot be improved.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.1.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
