## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**0.5.1 How We Know What We Know**

- Evidence comes in kinds and they are not equally strong.
- Most knee problems improve with time, so any claim without a comparison group is unreliable.
- Randomisation exists to make groups similar in the things nobody thought to measure.
- A systematic review is strongest because it accounts for the studies that disagreed.
- Compared with what, how many, who was left out, and who paid, will carry you a long way.

**0.5.2 EPQ and Science Fair Project Ideas**

- A project succeeds or fails on whether the question is narrow enough to answer.
- Knee projects divide into measurement, literature review, and modelling.
- Any project involving people needs informed consent, anonymity and school approval.
- Checking whether your own measurement repeats is the single most valuable addition.
- A school project cannot establish that a treatment works, and should say so.

**0.5.3 Reading a Science Paper for the First Time**

- Almost every paper follows the same five part structure, which makes a first read possible.
- Read the abstract, the aim, the participant numbers, the figures, then the limitations.
- The limitations paragraph is the most useful part and the most commonly skipped.
- An abstract is written to persuade and often omits the limitation that matters.
- Statistically significant and large enough to matter are two different questions.

**0.5.4 Simple Biomechanics Experiments**

- The knee rotates slightly as it straightens, and you can see it on your own leg.
- Measuring the same thing several times shows you the error in your own measurement.
- How hard standing up is depends on where the weight sits relative to the joint.
- Stop at pain, never use an injured knee, and get consent before measuring anyone else.
- These experiments measure movement and cannot tell you whether a joint is healthy.

**0.5.5 Meet the Researchers**

- Knee research is done by clinical groups, biomechanics laboratories, imaging groups and engineers.
- Most of it is clinics, data and long follow up rather than laboratory bench work.
- Many knee researchers are not doctors.
- Statistics is the tool the whole field depends on.
- Carrying out one small project is the most useful preparation available at school.
