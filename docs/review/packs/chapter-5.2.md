## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**5.2.1 Generation of X-rays**

- Two exposures of one knee support different density conclusions.
- Post-processing can make a poor acquisition look correctly exposed.
- Bone texture is a weak longitudinal measure across departments.
- Automated analysis inherits the acquisition variation.
- The request should name the question, because the question sets the exposure.

**5.2.2 Image Formation**

- Post-processing differs per department and per vendor.
- The processing is not recorded where the reader looks.
- Projected joint space depends on beam angle, rotation and flexion.
- The published conventions each fix some of those variables and not others.
- Radiographic measurement is a protocol question.

**5.2.3 Radiation Dose**

- No routine knee pathway records cumulative exposure.
- An older computed tomography protocol delivers more dose for the same question.
- The risk model behind low dose estimates is contested.
- Weight bearing computed tomography competes with a radiograph.
- Dose should choose between modalities rather than decline a useful study.

**5.2.4 Scatter**

- Grading conventions do not record the scatter conditions.
- Radiographic grading is systematically harder in larger patients.
- That is the population in which osteoarthritis is commonest.
- Automated grading degrades invisibly across populations.
- Judge whether the study supports the measurement before making it.

**5.2.5 Magnification**

- Calibration practice varies and is not recorded on the image.
- A plan mixing angles and lengths inherits the length's uncertainty.
- Computed tomography planning trades projection for dose and segmentation.
- Automated measurement can be precise on an unknown scale.
- Prefer an angle where either would answer, and state what calibrated a length.

**5.2.6 Sound Waves**

- A live examination is recorded as a few images and cannot be re-read.
- A negative is a statement about one examination, not about the knee.
- Published accuracy reflects the operators as much as the technique.
- The dynamic examination is both its best contribution and least standardised.
- Treat the study as a consultation with the person holding the probe.

**5.2.7 Frequency Selection**

- Two frequencies support two conclusions and the report distinguishes neither.
- Published accuracy is tied to the equipment of the study.
- Older figures were obtained at frequencies no longer in use.
- Almost no report names the probe and frequency.
- Ask the operator what they could see.

**5.2.8 Reflection**

- The artefacts are named and mechanistic, so this is a training problem.
- A reader who was not present loses the mechanism.
- Calcification detection by shadow is an unused advantage.
- Contrast enhanced ultrasound and elastography have no established knee place.
- Hard knowable limits are easier to work with than soft ones.

**5.2.9 Refraction**

- The disproving test exists only during the examination.
- The evidence is the examination and the record is a selection from it.
- Video capture would help and is not standard.
- Automated interpretation sees the selection rather than the examination.
- The examination and not the image is the study.

**5.2.10 Anisotropy**

- Two different physics produce the same orientation dependent error.
- Both are resolved by changing the relationship, not by looking harder.
- Both are well described and both still generate reports of false tears.
- The report's silence about the angle test loses real information.
- An imaging appearance is a relationship between tissue and instrument.

**5.2.11 Doppler Imaging**

- The vascularity to symptom relationship is inconsistent across studies.
- Grading scales are not interchangeable, so a grade needs its scale.
- Probe pressure alone can change the result.
- The two available measures of activity have never been properly compared.
- It answers a unique question less reproducibly than its use implies.

**5.2.12 Basic Principles**

- Every sequence measures a property of water and every question is mechanical.
- The inference is strongest for continuity and weakest for function.
- Field strength has delivered resolution rather than new answers.
- Composition is not mechanics.
- A report that separates structure from inference is more useful.

**5.2.13 T1 Imaging**

- Normal red marrow varies with age, smoking, anaemia and training.
- The discriminating features are symmetry, distribution and the fluid sequence.
- Quantitative marrow assessment is not in routine knee practice.
- A marrow question is systemic and a knee protocol images a joint.
- Resist a marrow diagnosis from a knee protocol.

**5.2.14 T2 Imaging**

- Intermediate signal has no agreed meaning and is where older knees sit.
- The same ambiguity recurs for grafts, repairs and degenerate menisci.
- Quantitative relaxation measurement does not resolve a mechanical question.
- Sequences have improved faster than the grading conventions.
- Report intermediate signal as intermediate.

**5.2.15 Proton Density Imaging**

- The grading conventions were derived on this weighting.
- Protocols differ in whether they include it, and reports do not say.
- Three dimensional isotropic sequences change the contrast again.
- Serial assessment across departments is less comparable than it looks.
- A meniscal report's authority rests on an omitted protocol detail.

**5.2.16 Fat Suppression**

- Marrow oedema is found in asymptomatic and nearly all symptomatic knees.
- Population level prognostic associations are applied to individuals.
- Suppression quality varies and is not reported.
- The finding stays qualitative while being used quantitatively.
- Report the pattern, and say when the pattern names nothing.

**5.2.17 STIR Imaging**

- A standard protocol fails suppression exactly where the answer is.
- The sequence choice is the diagnostic decision in a painful arthroplasty.
- Metal artefact reduction is advancing and unevenly available.
- The request rarely names the implant, so the protocol cannot be selected.
- Naming the implant on the request is the cheapest improvement here.

**5.2.18 Gradient Echo Imaging**

- An isotropic volume changes the contrast each plane is read on.
- The grading systems were written for the older contrast.
- Reformatted images carry different noise and partial volume behaviour.
- Progression measures came from cohorts with different sequences.
- A thinner image is not automatically a more interpretable one.

**5.2.19 Artefacts**

- The artefact set belongs to a sequence and a field strength.
- New acquisition methods change which artefacts appear.
- An artefact is never recorded as such, so its frequency is unknown.
- Automated reading has no artefact concept without labelled training.
- An appearance is a product of the tissue and the instrument together.

**5.2.20 Magic Angle Effect**

- Everything needed to avoid this has been established for decades.
- The persistence locates the problem in practice rather than in knowledge.
- The affected knee structures are the ones ultrasound images best.
- Ultra short echo time imaging will change its relevance rather than remove it.
- Ask what the instrument contributed before believing an appearance.

**5.2.21 Advanced MRI Techniques**

- Each quantitative measure needs a validated link to function that mostly does not exist.
- Thresholds do not transfer between sequences and field strengths.
- Accelerated reconstruction improves access rather than adding a measurement.
- Weight bearing acquisition addresses the mechanical gap directly.
- Better measurement of the wrong quantity is not progress.

**5.2.22 CT Acquisition**

- Protocol age changes both the dose and the answer.
- Weight bearing computed tomography offers geometry under load.
- Measurement conventions were derived on older acquisitions.
- Automated segmentation errors are systematic rather than random.
- The geometry is trustworthy and the conventions are older than the data.

**5.2.23 Reconstruction**

- Noise texture and edge behaviour are reconstruction dependent.
- Artefact reduction means tunnel measurements are made on modified data.
- Published conventions predate modern reconstruction.
- Deep learning reconstruction can produce plausible absent structure.
- A planning measurement should name its reconstruction.

**5.2.24 Radiation Dose**

- No part of a knee pathway owns the cumulative dose.
- Dose reduction has improved unevenly and invisibly between hospitals.
- Weight bearing computed tomography will increase study numbers.
- The risk estimates at these exposures rest on a contested model.
- Respond with protocol and modality choice, not by declining a useful study.

**5.2.25 3D Reconstruction**

- A segmentation error becomes a bone cut rather than a misread image.
- Automated segmentation errors are systematic by anatomy and population.
- The threshold and method are not recorded in the plan.
- The surface carries no uncertainty and nothing restores it.
- An appearance is made jointly by the tissue and the instrument.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-5.2.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
