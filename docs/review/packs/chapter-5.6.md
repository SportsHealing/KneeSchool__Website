## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**5.6.1 Normal Meniscus**

- A meniscus after partial meniscectomy has no normal reference appearance.
- Normal and abnormal are a spectrum in a degenerate meniscus.
- Protocol differences change what counts as confidently normal.
- The roots and the ramp area are where a normal report most often hides a lesion.
- Normal is a statement about a particular knee at a particular age.

**5.6.2 Meniscal Signal Grading**

- The surface rule identifies mechanical significance, not symptoms.
- In a degenerate knee attributing pain to the meniscus is a choice.
- Agreement falls for the degenerate grades, so a grade is not a measurement.
- Extrusion is a better mechanical marker in the worn knee and is reported less.
- The scheme was defined on images that are not the ones being graded.

**5.6.3 Horizontal Tears**

- A reported horizontal tear carries almost no weight on its own.
- The compartment's cartilage is the useful information and is reported less.
- Resecting it removes tissue from a compartment already losing load distribution.
- Extrusion predicts the compartment's course better than the morphology.
- The negative literature is about this pattern, not about all meniscal tears.

**5.6.4 Vertical Tears**

- Imaging estimates length and position and says little about tissue quality.
- The peripheral margin that matters is a few millimetres wide.
- A chronic tear in a cruciate deficient knee is a different lesion.
- The posteromedial meniscocapsular junction is where it is most often missed.
- A repairable appearance is not a prediction of repair outcome.

**5.6.5 Radial Tears**

- A complete radial tear makes the compartment behave as meniscus deficient.
- Repair is mechanically unfavourable and often still the better option.
- Where a partial tear becomes mechanically complete is not visible.
- Extrusion is the best marker available and the least standardised.
- Which came first, the tear or the wear, is not answerable from the images.

**5.6.6 Bucket Handle Tears**

- An intermittently locking knee is caught in one state by the scan.
- A chronically displaced fragment remodels and is harder to recognise.
- Repair depends on tissue quality the imaging cannot assess.
- A previous meniscectomy and chronic displacement look alike.
- The fragment's position at scanning is not where it will be at operation.

**5.6.7 Flap Tears**

- Detection depends on the request more than on the protocol.
- A scan is one sample of a mobile fragment.
- The gutter is anatomically variable, so a small leaf is hard to call.
- In a degenerate knee attributing the symptom to the flap is a judgement.
- Resecting it relieves the catching and does not change the compartment.

**5.6.8 Complex Tears**

- The classification was built for tears in otherwise normal menisci.
- Extrusion, cartilage and alignment carry the prognosis and are reported less.
- A complex tear in a young knee after injury is a different lesion with the same name.
- Resecting here removes tissue from an already compromised compartment.
- The question worth asking is whether anything is displaced.

**5.6.9 Root Tears**

- Detection improved through a reporting change, which is not uniform between centres.
- A degenerate root and a torn root are a continuum.
- Repair restores the anchor and does not reliably reverse extrusion.
- Whether repair changes the long term course is a literature question.
- The root tear is the nameable part of a larger mechanical failure.

**5.6.10 Ramp Lesions**

- Imaging and arthroscopic detection differ enough to justify inspecting regardless.
- The lesion's definition is not uniform across the literature.
- Whether an asymptomatic ramp lesion needs repair is open.
- Secondary signs give a probability rather than a diagnosis.
- Improving detection is a protocol and a reporting problem together.

**5.6.11 Meniscal Extrusion**

- The measurement depends on a slice chosen by eye.
- Osteophyte corrupts the landmark in the knees where it matters most.
- The relevant quantity is the loaded position, which is rarely imaged.
- Published thresholds come from specific cohorts and protocols.
- Whether reducing extrusion changes the course is unanswered.

**5.6.12 Post-operative Meniscus**

- The operation note changes the report, so it is part of the investigation.
- There is no agreed imaging definition of a re-tear.
- Contrast helps and is rarely available where the question arises.
- The compartment's state predicts more than the search for a tear.
- Automated reading is weakest on the studies it was not trained on.

**5.6.13 Meniscal Repair Imaging**

- Repairs that look unhealed are often intact at arthroscopy.
- There is no agreed imaging definition of healing.
- Normal repair signal is long lived and variable.
- Root repair is judged by a supine measurement of a standing problem.
- Quantitative sequences do not answer a mechanical question.

**5.6.14 Meniscal Transplantation Imaging**

- There is no established normal appearance for a transplanted meniscus.
- Graft extrusion is near universal and its meaning is unsettled.
- The outcome that matters is chondroprotection measured over years.
- Alignment and cartilage state determine the result more than graft appearance.
- The population is too small to have validated quantitative assessment.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-5.6.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
