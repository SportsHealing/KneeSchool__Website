## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.8.1 Normal Fluid Physiology**

- A knee producing fluid faster than it clears it has a synovial process.
- A popliteal cyst's behaviour reflects the joint's production, so treating the cyst alone recurs.
- Repeated aspiration without addressing the cause refills predictably.
- A scarred capsule alters the usual relationship between volume and tension.
- The quadriceps cannot be rebuilt while the effusion persists.

**4.8.2 Clinical Significance**

- A persistent effusion with normal ligaments means a mechanical opinion about a non mechanical problem.
- Stalled rehabilitation with an effusion needs the effusion addressed first.
- An effusion with a reconstruction is infection, a mechanical lesion, or compartment change.
- A ligament examination performed on an effused knee may be understated and is worth repeating.
- The documented timing of the first effusion is often the most useful fact in the record.

**4.8.3 Bulge Sign**

- It explains the knee recorded as dry at every visit and still not settling.
- A persistent small effusion through rehabilitation means the joint is being irritated.
- A stalled post-operative range with a small effusion has a treatable reason.
- Repeated normal mechanical examinations with a small effusion are a clue, not reassurance.
- A scarred capsule makes a negative result less informative.

**4.8.4 Patellar Tap**

- The tap is often the test that produced the dry knee recorded in the notes.
- A persistent small effusion recorded as dry explains a stalled range.
- A tense effusion that cannot be tapped may need aspirating for diagnosis and relief.
- A grinding patella is an incidental but useful finding.
- An effusion recorded as absent on a tap alone is a weaker observation than it looks.

**4.8.5 Sweep Test**

- This test and the bulge sign change the record from a dry knee to an effused one.
- A persistent small effusion through rehabilitation means the programme cannot succeed yet.
- A record of dry knees based on the tap alone cannot be relied on.
- A scarred suprapatellar pouch makes a negative result less informative.
- The distinction between absent and not looked for is rarely recoverable from a record.

**4.8.6 Ballottement**

- A tense effusion is usually acute and a large compliant one usually chronic.
- A tense effusion after a reconstruction is bleeding or infection.
- A large compliant effusion with a normal mechanical examination is a synovial process.
- A tense effusion with a locked knee raises a displaced tear with bleeding.
- A scarred capsule means a modest volume can be tense.

**4.8.7 Acute Haemarthrosis**

- A haemarthrosis attributed to a cruciate rupture may have been a patellar dislocation.
- An osteochondral fragment identified late has lost its repair window.
- A ligament examination normal acutely and abnormal at six weeks was unreliable the first time.
- A recurrent haemarthrosis without injury has a vascular or haematological cause.
- A vague recorded timing is one of the commonest weaknesses in a record.

**4.8.8 Chronic Effusions**

- The chronic effusion is the commonest presentation in which the referral's premise is wrong.
- Several arthroscopies for a recurrent effusion is a history that is itself the diagnosis.
- A chronic effusion after surgery is infection until excluded, however many years have passed.
- An excised and recurred cyst means the consequence was treated twice.
- Attribution to an old injury is hard to support without a demonstrable mechanical cause.

**4.8.9 Synovitis**

- Synovitis explains the knee with several normal arthroscopies.
- Synovitis after a reconstruction is infection, a reaction, or an inflammatory process, in that order of exclusion.
- Localised rather than generalised synovitis raises a focal process.
- A haemorrhagic synovitis has its own differential, distinguished by aspiration.
- The distinction between a post traumatic and a systemic process is the usual contested point.

**4.8.10 Crystal Arthropathy**

- It explains the knee whose effusions never fitted a mechanical pattern.
- Chronic crystal disease damages cartilage, so a mechanical complaint on top is compound.
- With a prosthesis every hot episode is possible infection and the history offers a tempting alternative.
- Chondrocalcinosis with a mechanical complaint is two findings, not one.
- A septic knee treated as crystal disease is criticised on the absent aspiration.
