## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**2.1.1 Gross Anatomy**

- There is no safe posterior plane at the distal femur; the bone is approached from the side.
- The distal articular surface sits in valgus to the shaft axis, and that angle varies between people.
- Rotational references disagree because the condyles are asymmetrical, so the choice is which disagreement to accept.
- The epiphysis is supplied through capsule and retinacula, not through the shaft.

**2.1.2 Distal Femur**

- The notch is a fixed volume whose contents compete for space.
- Condylar asymmetry means a symmetrical implant cannot reproduce both sides.
- The changing radius of curvature is why ligament tension varies through range on the native knee.
- The cartilage to artery distance behind the condyles is small and flexion does not reliably increase it.

**2.1.3 Medial Femoral Condyle**

- The posteromedial corner is layered and the condyle anchors it; the plane anterior to gastrocnemius stays safe.
- The medial compartment carries more and moves less, so it tolerates malalignment poorly.
- The load bearing zone is the contact area near extension, not the geometric centre of the condyle.
- The medial patellofemoral ligament attachment lies close to the physis in a growing knee.

**2.1.4 Lateral Femoral Condyle**

- Posterolateral reconstruction restores two or three fixed points rather than every structure.
- Lateral restraint is soft tissue dependent, so failing to restore it costs more than on the medial side.
- The popliteus hiatus is both the tendon's route into the joint and a route for a tear to extend.
- In a lateral approach the order of depth is band, superior lateral genicular artery, capsule, popliteus.

**2.1.5 Trochlea**

- The region with the least bony restraint is also where most of the soft tissue restraint attaches.
- A deep groove makes stability a function of engagement; a flat one makes it soft tissue dependent throughout.
- Trochlear shape is the instability factor that soft tissue surgery does not change.
- A shallow native trochlea is replaced by a component groove that is not, and the retinacula must adapt.

**2.1.6 Femoral Attachments of Ligaments**

- The epicondyles and adductor tubercle are the reference of last resort when previous surgery has altered the surface.
- A non-anatomical femoral attachment produces a graft that is tight somewhere in the range.
- A proximally placed medial patellofemoral ligament attachment tightens with flexion.
- This page deliberately carries no distances; they could not be verified. See decision 005.

**2.1.7 Surgical Anatomy**

- A proximal extensor lengthening heals in muscle; a distal one has to heal in bone.
- Any plan leaving a narrow skin strip between two incisions relies on a watershed.
- Resection proximal and distal to the adductor hiatus are different operations, because the artery is tethered there.
- Sinus tracts mark the planes already compromised and constrain which flap survives.

**2.1.8 Imaging Anatomy**

- Vessel position, physeal scar level and epicondylar to posterior condylar rotation all vary and are all visible preoperatively.
- Rotational assessment is the imaging question with the most direct surgical consequence.
- Tunnel and hardware position relative to native attachments decides single versus two stage revision.
- Where imaging and textbook disagree, the patient's anatomy wins.

**2.1.9 Biomechanical Relevance**

- Resecting the distal femur moves the surface while the ligament attachments stay put, which is why the joint line matters.
- Distal cut governs the extension gap, posterior cut the flexion gap, and size and position govern both.
- Mid flexion is hardest to control because it is governed by the transition between the cut surfaces.
- Single and multi radius designs are competing answers to the native changing radius.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-2.1.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
