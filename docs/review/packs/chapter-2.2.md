## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**2.2.1 Proximal Tibia**

- The posteromedial interval behind the pes anserinus and in front of medial gastrocnemius is the safe route to the posterior plateau.
- The common peroneal nerve is a relation of the fibula, not the tibia.
- Increasing posterior slope increases cruciate demand; reducing it does the reverse.
- Moving the tubercle changes the sagittal moment arm and the axial direction of pull at once.
- Subchondral bone is denser medially, which affects fixation and subsidence.

**2.2.2 Tibial Plateau**

- Capsule alone separates the posterior plateau margin from the popliteal artery.
- Slope is the variable with the most mechanical effect per degree and is altered inadvertently most often.
- Coronal joint line obliquity and sagittal slope are independent questions.
- Reducing a plateau fracture anatomically and restoring slope can conflict.

**2.2.3 Tibial Spine**

- Capsule alone separates the posterior cruciate attachment from the popliteal artery, and flexion does not change that reliably.
- Every translational restraint and every meniscal root is anchored in the same small area.
- Previous tibial tunnels remove part of the spine and decide whether revision is one stage.
- This is the page where the absence of verified dimensions costs the reader most.

**2.2.4 Gerdy's Tubercle**

- The identity of the anterolateral capsular structure is disputed; the tubercle is not, which is why reconstructions reference it.
- Lateral tenodesis alters the moment arm of an existing restraint rather than adding one.
- Almost every described technique uses the tubercle tibially and varies at the femoral end.
- This page describes the uncontested anatomy only.

**2.2.5 Tibial Tubercle**

- The tubercle is the only place extensor geometry can be changed without touching the joint surface.
- Medialisation can overload the medial facet; anteriorisation moves the contact area; distalisation tightens the mechanism.
- Soft tissue surgery cannot change tubercle position or trochlear shape, which is what decides whether bony surgery is added.
- A tubercle osteotomy for exposure must heal in bone, unlike a proximal lengthening.

**2.2.6 Surgical Anatomy**

- With a compromised anterior envelope the perforator anatomy decides the approach, not the fracture pattern.
- As a posterior exposure extends laterally the limiting structure changes from gastrocnemius to the bundle itself.
- In the periprosthetic tibia the subcutaneous medial surface is the thinnest envelope and the only accessible cortex.
- Resection proximal and distal to the anterior tibial artery's passage are different operations.
