## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.7.1 Active Motion**

- A persistent active deficit with a full passive range and a competent graft is a quadriceps problem.
- A deteriorating active range with a stable passive one is inhibition, pain or weakness.
- A patellofemoral problem can drive the inhibition, and the sitting examination finds it.
- After an extensor mechanism repair the active range may be the best achievable.
- The active range is the figure that corresponds to a claimed disability.

**4.7.2 Passive Motion**

- The passive range and its end feel separate the two stiff knees that get referred.
- A firm abrupt block after reconstruction is an impinging lesion with a straightforward operation.
- A leathery global restriction is arthrofibrosis, with a less certain result.
- A reducing passive range with a stable active one indicates a developing structural problem.
- A range recorded without an end feel cannot have its cause determined later.

**4.7.3 Hyperextension**

- Increasing hyperextension after a corner reconstruction is easy to reproduce and shows stretch.
- Losing hyperextension in a knee that previously had it is an early contracture.
- A knee tensioned to neutral in a hypermobile patient is asymmetrical by design.
- A bony hyperextension will not be controlled by a soft tissue reconstruction.
- A recorded pre-operative figure is rare, which makes claims about a changed range hard to support.

**4.7.4 Flexion Assessment**

- A firm block in flexion after reconstruction may have a mechanical cause.
- A global leathery restriction in both directions is arthrofibrosis.
- Flexion limited by anterior pain rather than resistance is a patellofemoral problem.
- Without a pre-operative figure, loss cannot be assessed.
- After an arthroplasty the target is not the other knee.

**4.7.5 End Feel**

- The end feel converts a vague stiffness referral into a specific plan.
- A firm abrupt block after reconstruction has a good prognosis and is often referred late.
- A leathery restriction begins the honest conversation about arthrofibrosis.
- A developing block between visits is more concerning than a stable one.
- A pain limited range with a normal range under anaesthesia is an irritable knee, not a stiff one.

**4.7.6 Painful Arc**

- The arc often explains why an arthroscopy did not help.
- A meniscal resection for an anterior mid flexion arc treated the wrong compartment.
- A terminal extension arc after reconstruction is an impinging lesion, frequently referred late.
- A deep flexion arc after a meniscal repair has a timeline rather than a treatment.
- A recorded arc is more informative than a recorded pain score.

**4.7.7 Contractures**

- An isolated extension contracture with a firm end feel may be a mechanical lesion instead.
- A global leathery restriction begins the conversation about partial improvement.
- A contracture present for years is unlikely to regain a normal range.
- A contracture with posterior cruciate deficiency makes the ligament measurements unreliable.
- Without early recorded ranges, the contracture's development cannot be dated.

**4.7.8 Extension Lag**

- A persisting lag with a competent graft is a quadriceps problem no surgery addresses.
- A lag that will not respond to a programme needs inhibition sought.
- After an extensor mechanism repair a lag may be the best achievable.
- A lag with posterior cruciate deficiency removes the compensating mechanism.
- A recorded lag is objective evidence of reduced quadriceps function.

**4.7.9 Functional Range**

- A textbook range with one necessary task missing explains the unexplained referral.
- An occupational requirement should set the pre-operative conversation.
- A small extension deficit with a limp is worth treating and is often dismissed.
- In a multiply operated knee the achievable range may be below the requirement.
- A claim concerns the functional range and the record contains the measured one.

**4.7.10 Post-operative Range Assessment**

- The stiff knee after surgery arrives late, and the lateness is a monitoring failure.
- A firm abrupt block referred at a year has been treatable for most of that year.
- Arthrofibrosis referred late has a worse prognosis, which makes the monitoring interval clinical.
- Without a pre-operative range, loss cannot be assessed.
- A persisting lag with a full passive range is made worse by further surgery.
