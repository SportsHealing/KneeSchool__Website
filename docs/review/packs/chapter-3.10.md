## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.10.1 Varus Stability**

- Lateral laxity is harder to detect and more consequential than its medial counterpart.
- Most clinically significant lateral injuries involve the corner.
- Varus alignment is the environmental factor, equivalent to slope anteriorly.
- A lateral reconstruction in a varus limb carries the load that stretched the original.
- The lateral structures stretch rather than heal.

**3.10.2 External Rotation Stability**

- External rotation laxity defines the corner injury and drives the operation.
- A missed corner injury is a leading cause of graft failure on both cruciates.
- The assessment is routine in cruciate surgery, not a specialist extra.
- Chronic corner laxity in a varus limb defeats reconstruction.
- No clinical measurement quantifies the rotatory component reproducibly.

**3.10.3 LCL Function**

- This ligament is the easier half of a lateral reconstruction; the corner is the harder.
- Reconstructing it alone leaves rotational laxity.
- That is the lateral equivalent of reconstructing the superficial medial ligament alone.
- Poor healing is why lateral injuries are operated on more often than medial ones.
- A chronic lateral laxity rarely improves with time.

**3.10.4 Popliteus Function**

- Anatomic corner reconstruction is a three component operation.
- Reconstructing all three restores restraint better than a subset.
- The active component cannot be reconstructed.
- A graft replaces the passive restraint only.
- The femoral origins are close together, so position needs confirming.

**3.10.5 Popliteofibular Ligament Function**

- Reconstructing it distinguishes an anatomic corner procedure from a tenodesis.
- A limb in the wrong orientation restrains the wrong motion.
- The fibular head is crowded and fragile.
- A styloid avulsion changes the operation.
- No measurement confirms this limb carries the intended load.

**3.10.6 Posterolateral Corner Function**

- Corner assessment belongs in every cruciate operation.
- A missed corner injury is a leading cause of graft failure for both cruciates.
- Chronic corner deficiency is least forgiving: it heals badly and varus loads it.
- Reconstruction is anatomic and three component where graft allows.
- Revision findings are the evidence the corner was missed.

**3.10.7 Dial Test Biomechanics**

- The dial test is an intraoperative check as well as a clinical one.
- It says whether the corner reconstruction worked.
- Having no instrument limits its use as an outcome measure, not as a check.
- A residual positive test after reconstruction predicts a poorer result.
- Investigate a residual positive test before leaving theatre.

**3.10.8 PLC Reconstruction Biomechanics**

- The problem is a multiligament knee with a varus deformity and a stretched corner.
- Staging is the main tool and the sequence follows the mechanics.
- Corner tunnels compete with a posterior cruciate tibial tunnel.
- An inlay for the posterior cruciate frees tibial tunnel space.
- No measurement establishes the optimal sequence.

**3.10.9 Combined PCL and PLC Injury**

- The environment, the structures and the alignment all have to be addressed.
- Reconstructing one leaves the loads that defeated it.
- Tunnel convergence decides the technique and argues for an inlay.
- Multiligament series are small and heterogeneous.
- Outcomes in this combination are worse than in isolated injury.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.10.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
