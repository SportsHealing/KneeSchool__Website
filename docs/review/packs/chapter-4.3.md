## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.3.1 Observation from the Door**

- A patient walking in unaided with a disabling referral is describing a mismatch to be worked out.
- Performing differently once asked is a question to handle, not an inconsistency to record.
- In a multiply operated knee this separates stiffness from instability better than the couch.
- An athlete walking normally is uninformative; the task has to approach the demand that fails.
- A slapping foot or catching toe points at the nerve rather than the joint.

**4.3.2 Gait Observation**

- A stretched graft with a varus thrust has its mechanical explanation, which no laxity measurement supplies.
- A gait that deteriorates while measurements do not is compartment change seen early.
- A thrust after an osteotomy is under correction or a ligament problem the correction exposed.
- An unrecovered nerve palsy gives a permanent gait abnormality that is not ligamentous.
- In an athlete the clinic gait is uninformative and the task has to be raised.

**4.3.3 Standing Alignment**

- The referral is often a soft tissue question with a bony answer.
- Two sound reconstructions with a persisting complaint usually means alignment or slope.
- A stretched lateral reconstruction in a varus limb is a sequencing error, not a technical one.
- A flexion deformity with posterior cruciate deficiency makes every measurement unreliable.
- Alignment at the time of injury is almost never recorded, which complicates attribution.

**4.3.4 Sitting Assessment**

- It catches the patellofemoral problem masquerading as a meniscal complaint.
- Anterior pain with posterior cruciate deficiency is patellofemoral loading seen as a consequence.
- A persisting extension lag after sound surgery is the finding most predictive of dissatisfaction.
- Previous surgery alters patellar height and track, so the other side is no longer normal.
- Failure to hold extension with no effusion and normal bulk needs a neurological explanation.

**4.3.5 Supine Assessment**

- Normal supine findings with a disabling complaint means a loaded problem, a rotational one, or not the knee.
- An abnormal supine examination in a patient who works without difficulty is a reason not to operate.
- In a multiply operated knee the resting position is the most reliable finding left.
- A pillow under the knee is the commonest reason a posterior sag is missed in a bed.
- A recorded resting position is more useful than a recorded grade, and harder to dispute.

**4.3.6 Skin Changes**

- Several scars with thin skin constrain a revision more than the ligament findings do.
- A chronically warm, mildly swollen knee after surgery raises low grade infection.
- A rash and nail changes with recurrent effusions is an inflammatory arthropathy.
- A swollen limb is not the same finding as a swollen knee.
- Photographs of the skin at presentation carry more weight than any written finding.

**4.3.7 Surgical Scars**

- Three or more incisions with an adherent anterior scar can outweigh the gain from further surgery.
- A sinus or puckered scar with a previous infection has to be excluded before any revision.
- An osteotomy scar in a varus limb means a correction that did not hold or was incomplete.
- A graft harvest scar on the other limb is easy to miss and changes the options.
- Scars are the least disputable evidence about what was done.

**4.3.8 Swelling**

- A recurrent effusion with normal ligaments and no mechanical symptoms is not a surgical problem.
- An effusion after arthroplasty or reconstruction raises low grade infection.
- A quadriceps cannot be rehabilitated while an effusion persists.
- An effusion appearing only after activity is worth timing in the history, not chasing in clinic.
- The timing of the first swelling after injury is often the most useful fact in the record.

**4.3.9 Muscle Wasting**

- Unrecovered wasting is the commonest explanation for a mechanically satisfactory, functionally poor knee.
- Further surgery for a rehabilitation problem makes the knee worse.
- Wasting that will not respond raises pain inhibition, an effusion, or a nerve problem.
- After a nerve palsy the wasting extends below the knee.
- Measured wasting is objective evidence of reduced use and is hard to dispute.

**4.3.10 Deformity**

- A stretched lateral reconstruction with a fixed varus deformity needs an osteotomy, not another graft.
- A fixed flexion deformity after reconstruction has three causes separated by the end feel and the notes.
- In a chronically deficient knee the deformity is often a consequence of the laxity.
- After an osteotomy the question is whether the correction achieved what was planned.
- Pre-injury alignment is almost never recorded, which complicates attribution.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.3.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
