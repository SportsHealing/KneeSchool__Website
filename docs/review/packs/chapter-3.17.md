## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.17.1 ACL Landmark Studies**

- The decision these studies inform is what a reconstruction should reproduce, and it is a balance rather than a rule.
- Tunnel position has the strongest mechanical support and the weakest patient reported signal, which is the tension to weigh.
- The five patient factors change the answer more than the laboratory evidence does.
- Read contested work for its comparator, since that is usually where the disagreement sits.
- The field's best evidence comes from specimens with no muscle, and teaching should say so.

**3.17.2 PCL Landmark Studies**

- Whether to operate on an isolated PCL injury is a balance the literature does not settle.
- The mechanical case is strong, the natural history case is weak, and the two are often quoted as though they were one body of evidence.
- The five patient factors carry more weight here than the laboratory findings.
- Recognising a combined injury changes management more than any isolated PCL decision.
- Teach improvement rather than restoration as the stated aim, because that is what the literature reports.

**3.17.3 Meniscal Landmark Studies**

- Whether to resect a fragment is a balance argument, and the mechanical evidence weighs against resection in every case where the symptom is not obstructive.
- Every millimetre removed is permanent, which is the asymmetry that should shape the conversation.
- The five patient factors decide most of these consultations.
- The trials are a verdict on one procedure in one population, not on arthroscopy.
- Mechanical and trial evidence agreed here and practice still lagged, which is the chapter's real lesson.

**3.17.4 Medial Knee Landmark Studies**

- What to do with a combined medial and cruciate injury is a balance the mechanical literature does not settle.
- Healing capacity is the argument for waiting and it varies between patients more than the mechanics do.
- The five patient factors are what separates the three defensible plans.
- Rotation, not valgus, is where the management argument sits.
- A strong mechanical hierarchy and an unsettled management question can coexist.

**3.17.5 PLC Landmark Studies**

- Whether to add a lateral procedure is the least settled balance in this chapter.
- Rotational contribution argues for it and disputed anatomy with unknown long term constraint effects argues against.
- The five patient factors, and rotational demand in particular, do most of the deciding.
- Record the balance, because the evidence is moving and the record is what makes a decision reviewable.
- Practice moved ahead of anatomy here, which is a pattern to recognise rather than condemn.

**3.17.6 Patellofemoral Landmark Studies**

- What to do for anterior knee pain with measured maltracking is a balance, not a rule.
- A measured mechanical abnormality is not by itself an indication.
- Psychological state carries unusual weight in this presentation and should be weighed as evidence rather than as courtesy.
- How a maltracking finding is explained to a patient becomes part of their problem.
- Instability predicts structurally; pain does not, and the two literatures should be read apart.

**3.17.7 Osteotomy Landmark Studies**

- Osteotomy against arthroplasty against waiting is a close balance in the younger patient.
- Joint preservation and a sound rationale weigh against a long recovery and absent disease modification evidence.
- The five patient factors, and tolerance of recovery in particular, decide most of these consultations.
- A correct mechanical argument is not evidence that an operation works.
- Absent evidence and negative evidence are different, and this chapter is where the difference matters.

**3.17.8 Arthroplasty Landmark Studies**

- Alignment strategy is a balance between long survival evidence and short functional evidence.
- The kinematic case is strongest where the patient's demands are highest.
- Expectation is the one factor here known to change the outcome being measured.
- An operation can be reliable and mechanically unsuccessful at once, and both claims are supported.
- The gap between kinematics and satisfaction is the field's live question, not an inconsistency.

**3.17.9 Imperial Knee Biomechanics Contributions**

- The decision here is about attribution rather than about a patient, and it is still a balance.
- Publishing gathers useful standard teaching; holding avoids implying an institutional account of itself.
- The discoverable flag being false is what makes publishing defensible, and it is weaker than review.
- The limit is stated in the page body rather than only in the register, deliberately.
- Whether the page should exist in this form is the group's call, and it is logged as open.

**3.17.10 Future Research Priorities**

- What to tell a patient when the evidence stops short of their question is itself a balance.
- Plain uncertainty supports shared decisions; unstructured uncertainty damages them.
- Elicit what the patient wants to know first, because it determines which uncertainty is worth raising.
- This site's measurements are unchecked and its clinical directions unsigned, and both are listed so they can be worked rather than admired.
- A page that lists what it does not know is more useful than one that does not.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.17.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.

## The same pack in two other formats

A spreadsheet, which is easier to fill, and a Word file, which is easier to print:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --chapter 3.17 --format all

All three carry the same rows and the same references, and `--ingest` reads any of them.
