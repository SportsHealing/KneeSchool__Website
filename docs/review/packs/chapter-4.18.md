## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.18.1 Early OA**

- The question is whether one compartment or the whole knee is involved.
- Tenderness on the opposite joint line changes the picture and is easy to omit.
- Stiffness lasting beyond an hour belongs to an inflammatory process.
- A deformity correctable supine can be fixed under load.
- The focal or diffuse distinction is not available from the examination.

**4.18.2 Established OA**

- The clinical and the radiographic deformity differ, and each governs a different question.
- Varus with medial laxity is an unusual combination and deserves rechecking.
- A fixed flexion deficit can be mostly bony or mostly capsular, and the end feel is the clue.
- Rotational deformity is not captured by a varus and valgus description.
- The share of pain from the patellofemoral joint is not available from the examination.

**4.18.3 Varus OA**

- Bone loss, contracture, lateral lengthening and thrust coexist and mask each other.
- A knee that corrects fully and still thrusts is giving two different signals.
- Lateral laxity may be relative rather than absolute, and the examination cannot tell.
- A coexisting cruciate deficiency alters the thrust and is attributed to the deformity.
- Rotational components are outside the coronal description.

**4.18.4 Valgus OA**

- Valgus deformity is more often multiplanar than varus deformity.
- Medial lengthening and lateral bone loss give the same clinical laxity.
- A post fracture joint line is not where the localising signs assume.
- Peroneal nerve function is a preoperative baseline in a valgus knee.
- Bone loss and cartilage loss are not distinguishable clinically.

**4.18.5 Patellofemoral OA**

- Apportioning symptoms between the front and the compartments stays an estimate.
- Tracking, not pain, separates secondary from primary patellofemoral wear.
- Trochlear dysplasia is inferred clinically and the inference is often wrong.
- Sitting pain and stair pain can come from different parts of the joint.
- Previous realignment surgery changes the examination baseline.

**4.18.6 Functional Limitation**

- The same walking distance can mean different function in two patients.
- Reports drift as adaptation progresses, which flattens the trajectory.
- Contributions from the hip, spine and foot are not separable by the knee examination.
- Expectation and limitation are different quantities, recorded together.
- The weighting behind any functional score is not settled here.

**4.18.7 Arthroplasty Assessment**

- Clinical and radiographic deformity answer different questions.
- Correctability is a continuum and two examiners will record it differently.
- A multi joint patient needs a sequence, so the examination establishes the order.
- Bone quality and bone loss are not assessable clinically.
- Expectation is recorded loosely and predicts the reported outcome.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.18.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
