## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.10.1 History of ACL Injury**

- In referred practice ask what has changed and whether the symptom resembles the original.
- A returned original symptom suggests graft failure; a new pivoting symptom suggests rotation.
- A knee that was never confident with a stable graft is a rehabilitation problem.
- That group is larger than the structural failures.
- The rehabilitation history is part of the knee's history and is usually missing.

**4.10.2 Acute ACL Injury**

- Recording that rotation could not be assessed is different from recording that it was normal.
- In a multiligament knee the acute laxity pattern is often the only pre-operative account.
- An examination under anaesthetic during an urgent procedure is a free and under-used opportunity.
- The referred patient's pre-operative rotational state was frequently never assessed.

**4.10.3 Chronic ACL Deficiency**

- The question in a long-deficient knee is whether reconstruction is still appropriate.
- Degeneration, meniscal deficiency and malalignment all argue against it and are assessable in clinic.
- Pain after twenty years of coping is usually degenerative disease.
- Instability after a change of occupation is a genuine new indication.
- The examination establishes what the joint can still tolerate.

**4.10.4 Lachman Test**

- A small increase in translation with a firm endpoint is a satisfactory graft result.
- A soft endpoint after reconstruction indicates graft failure.
- Progressive increase across visits indicates a stretching graft, which needs earlier records.
- A normal Lachman with instability points at rotation rather than translation.
- That gap between translation and rotation is this chapter's central problem.

**4.10.5 Anterior Drawer Test**

- At this level it is mainly a vehicle for the rotational modifications.
- The ninety degree position assesses translation and rotation in one setup.
- A gross drawer with rotation suggests an unaddressed medial or lateral component.
- A test can survive in practice for reasons unrelated to its accuracy.

**4.10.6 Pivot Shift Test**

- The test measures what the patient complains about and is the least obtainable finding here.
- That gap is why rotational instability is missed before surgery and complained of after it.
- The pre-operative grade is frequently absent, so a later complaint cannot be dated.
- Quantified measurement exists and has not entered routine practice.
- Record an examination under anaesthetic whenever the rotational state is relevant.

**4.10.7 Lever Sign Test**

- Its interest at this level is methodological rather than clinical.
- A well designed test with results that have not been uniformly reproduced is a common sequence.
- Its post-operative interpretation is unestablished and this page claims none.
- Perform it, record it, and weight it alongside rather than above the Lachman.

**4.10.8 Dynamic Assessment**

- It answers why a technically sound reconstruction is not better.
- A stable graft with poor control and a hop deficit is a rehabilitation problem.
- A stable graft with good control and pivoting instability is a rotational problem.
- It separates the two commonest causes of a disappointing reconstruction.
- The tasks are not standardised between clinics, so compare with the patient's own other leg.

**4.10.9 Rotational Instability**

- The best performing examination in the knee measures the wrong variable.
- Translation is accurate and is not the complaint; the test for the complaint needs anaesthesia.
- Almost every dissatisfied reconstruction sits in that gap.
- The available responses are all partial.
- The field's best examination has a known blind spot and no instrument closes it.

**4.10.10 Examination Under Anaesthesia**

- It is the only reliable rotational assessment available, which is why it resolves referrals.
- In revision it establishes what the current laxity actually is.
- In a multiligament knee it determines the staging and the order of reconstruction.
- In a hypermobile patient it is most likely to overstate the problem.
- It is frequently the best record a knee will ever have, and summarising it as a grade wastes it.

**4.10.11 Post-Reconstruction Assessment**

- The main limitation is the absence of a pre-operative comparator, which is a records problem.
- Increased translation without a pre-operative measurement cannot be called a stretched graft.
- A pivot shift without a pre-operative grade cannot be called a new problem.
- Record the examination under anaesthesia at the reconstruction, with grades, endpoints and angles.
- That remedy helps the next patient rather than this one.

**4.10.12 Revision ACL Assessment**

- Most revisions are for a rotational component or an environment the first assessment did not record.
- Both were assessable before the first operation.
- The revision clinic is where the cost of an incomplete pre-operative examination is paid.
- After several reconstructions the job is establishing what the joint can still tolerate.
- Frequently the answer is that no further reconstruction is appropriate, and the examination makes that defensible.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.10.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
