## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**1.1.1 What Is the Knee?**

- The knee is the largest synovial joint and contains two articulations within one capsule.
- It is a modified hinge: flexion and extension with axial rotation that increases with flexion.
- Articular surfaces are incongruent, so bone contributes little to stability.
- Stability is conferred by ligaments, menisci, capsule and muscle rather than by bony fit.
- One shared capsule means an effusion from any source distends the entire joint.

**1.1.2 Why the Knee Is Important**

- The knee is the principal joint of walking and of the sit to stand transfer.
- It lies between the two longest bones, so forces act through long lever arms.
- Joint reaction forces in ordinary activity are several times body weight.
- Functional consequences of knee disease extend to fitness, falls risk and wellbeing.
- Structural appearance correlates imperfectly with symptoms and with function.

**1.1.3 Functions of the Knee**

- The knee meets three conflicting demands: range, load transmission and stability.
- Range is roughly zero to 135 degrees, with slight hyperextension in many people.
- Terminal extension involves obligatory tibial external rotation, locking the joint.
- Load is shared between articular cartilage and the menisci, which raise contact area.
- Separating deficits of range, load tolerance and stability directs assessment.

**1.1.4 Evolution of the Human Knee**

- The human knee shows specific adaptations to habitual bipedalism.
- The bicondylar angle keeps the knee near the midline while the pelvis stays wide.
- Terminal extension and locking permit standing with minimal quadriceps activity.
- The bicondylar angle creates a lateral vector on the patella during extension.
- Medial load concentration in stance is consistent with medial compartment predominance.

**1.1.5 Common Knee Problems**

- Knee presentations group by mechanism more usefully than by structure.
- Haemarthrosis within hours implies bleeding and a significant structural injury.
- True locking implies a mechanical block. Giving way implies instability or inhibition.
- Age materially shifts the differential diagnosis.
- Septic arthritis must be excluded in any hot swollen knee with systemic upset.

**1.1.6 Lifelong Knee Health**

- Cartilage is avascular and depends on cyclical loading for nutrition.
- Activity within tolerance maintains cartilage health; immobilisation impairs it.
- Quadriceps weakness is both a consequence of knee pathology and a risk for progression.
- Previous injury is the strongest single predictor of subsequent injury.
- Painless crepitus and recreational running are poorly supported as causes of harm.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-1.1.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
