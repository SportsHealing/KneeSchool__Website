## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**3.9.1 Valgus Stability**

- Valgus laxity is easy to measure and easy to over-read in isolation.
- Opening in full extension indicates injury beyond the superficial ligament.
- The ligament heals with length rather than failing to heal.
- That healing is why the medial side is treated differently from the lateral.
- Serial examination captures the natural history that defines management.

**3.9.2 Rotational Stability**

- Rotational laxity decides whether a medial injury is operative.
- Isolated valgus laxity usually is not operative.
- Addressing rotation means reconstructing the posterior oblique ligament or the deep layer.
- No clinical measurement quantifies medial rotatory laxity reproducibly.
- A chronically lax knee has stretched its remaining restraints.

**3.9.3 Superficial MCL Function**

- Healing capacity is the governing fact of medial side management.
- The exceptions are displacement, chronic length and rotatory laxity.
- Reproduce the native length; shortening over-constrains and restricts flexion.
- Position sets the length change pattern, so the femoral attachment matters.
- No measurement confirms a graft reproduces the native pattern.

**3.9.4 Deep MCL Function**

- The meniscotibial attachment links medial ligament injury to meniscal extrusion.
- Extrusion is the imaging sign that it has failed.
- A medial injury treated as a ligament problem can leave a meniscal one.
- The meniscal consequence has the degenerative endpoint.
- No measurement confirms the deep layer's function has been restored.

**3.9.5 POL Function**

- Its involvement converts a medial injury from non operative to operative.
- Examination in extension is how that is established.
- Reconstructing the superficial ligament alone leaves near extension and rotation uncontrolled.
- In a chronic injury it is the structure most likely to have stretched.
- No measurement confirms a reconstructed limb is functioning.

**3.9.6 Posteromedial Corner Function**

- The corner decides the medial side's management.
- An isolated superficial injury heals; a corner injury often does not.
- Anatomic medial reconstruction is a two structure operation.
- In combined injury the corner's state decides whether the medial side needs surgery.
- No measurement confirms a reconstructed corner functions as a unit.

**3.9.7 AMRI Biomechanics**

- The pattern is why a medial injury alongside a cruciate tear sometimes needs surgery.
- The graft restores the anterior component and leaves the rotational one.
- Residual anteromedial rotatory laxity predicts a poorer result.
- It is an indication to address the medial side at the same sitting.
- The rotatory component cannot be quantified clinically.

**3.9.8 Medial Reconstruction Biomechanics**

- The chronic valgus knee with medial laxity has two wrong things reinforcing each other.
- A graft in an uncorrected valgus limb carries the load that stretched the original.
- Correct the alignment before or with the ligament reconstruction.
- Stress views separate bony deformity from soft tissue laxity.
- Combined procedure series do not separate the components.

**3.9.9 Contributions at Different Flexion Angles**

- Angle dependence makes medial reconstruction a two limb operation.
- One limb at one angle cannot reproduce two reciprocal patterns.
- The result has to be measured at both angles.
- Over-constraint of either limb shows as lost range.
- No measurement attributes a residual laxity to a specific limb.

**3.9.10 Combined ACL and MCL Injury**

- Medial healing favours delay; graft protection favours addressing residual laxity.
- The resolution is brace, mobilise, reassess, then reconstruct.
- Restore motion before cruciate surgery.
- Acute medial repair is accepted for displacement or gross instability.
- Outcome series are heterogeneous in grade and timing.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-3.9.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.
