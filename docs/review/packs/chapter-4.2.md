## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.2.1 Pain**

- In referred practice the account has hardened, so ask what has changed rather than what it is like.
- Pain after a reconstruction or a replacement needs its own differential.
- Pain with no mechanical pattern after surgery is most likely to be operated on and least likely to benefit.
- No figure in this chapter supports which patterns discriminate; the patterns are standard teaching.

**4.2.2 Instability**

- Persistent instability after reconstruction has four common causes and the history separates them.
- An unaddressed rotational component is the commonest and the easiest to miss.
- Quadriceps deficit produces pseudo-instability that responds to rehabilitation, not revision.
- A subluxing patella can present as the knee giving way.
- Ask for the event, then the activity, then the direction, then what happened afterwards.

**4.2.3 Swelling**

- Recurrent effusion is a common post-operative referral and the history separates its causes.
- An effusion that never settled behaves differently from one that settled and returned.
- After arthroplasty, exclude infection before entertaining a mechanical explanation.
- An effusion after a repair may be a rehabilitation problem rather than a structural one.

**4.2.4 Stiffness**

- A knee that never achieved range differs from one that achieved it and lost it.
- Ask what rehabilitation was done rather than what was prescribed.
- Pain before stiffness suggests a cause; stiffness before pain suggests a capsular process.
- A release for stiffness driven by unexplained pain is the intervention most likely to disappoint.

**4.2.5 Locking**

- After a failed arthroscopy for locking, establish whether the symptom was ever a block.
- If it was not, a second arthroscopy will not help either.
- A resected fragment leaves a mechanically different knee, which can itself produce symptoms.
- A locked knee after a repair may be repair failure rather than a new event.

**4.2.6 Giving Way**

- Ask whether the episode resembles the original symptom: a return suggests graft failure.
- A new pivoting symptom suggests a rotational component that was never addressed.
- In a multiligament knee the complaint is distrust in every direction, not a plane.
- In a hypermobile patient compare against their own other knee and their own past function.

**4.2.7 Mechanical Symptoms**

- After resection the symptom may be altered mechanics rather than a new tear.
- A cyclops lesion produces a reproducible block near extension and is often called stiffness.
- A knee arthroscoped repeatedly without benefit usually had a symptom that was never mechanical.
- That conclusion comes from the history, not from a further arthroscopy.

**4.2.8 Location of Pain**

- After surgery the site of pain points at what was done rather than at what was there.
- Harvest site, tunnel, device and scar pain are all reported simply as knee pain.
- Joint line pain after a repair may be the repair, and the rehabilitation timing settles it.
- In a painful arthroplasty, diffuse pain suggests infection or sensitisation over a mechanical cause.

**4.2.9 Onset**

- A patient referred after a reconstruction has two onsets, and conflating them is the common error.
- A symptom that began when loading increased differs from one present from the first week.
- In a multiply operated knee, a timeline of operations and symptom changes is the history.
- Ask about the onset of the current complaint as well as the original injury.

**4.2.10 Severity**

- Severity without a comparator is the least useful part of a referral letter.
- The useful version is severity relative to before the last intervention.
- Worse than before an operation and better but not enough are different problems.
- Severe pain with no mechanical pattern after multiple operations is the commonest avoidable harm here.

**4.2.11 Aggravating Factors**

- After surgery a change in the provoking pattern is more informative than the pattern.
- New flexion-provoked anterior pain after reconstruction is usually patellofemoral or harvest site.
- New lateral pain after an osteotomy is transferred load behaving excessively.
- Establish the pre-operative pattern first, which is frequently not recorded.

**4.2.12 Relieving Factors**

- After surgery, relieving factors are the clearest guide to whether the symptom is mechanical.
- Pain unaffected by rest, position, load and immobilisation is not mechanical.
- Position dependent relief points at impingement, a cyclops lesion or a component issue.
- In revision assessment, whether anything relieves it frequently decides whether to operate.

**4.2.13 Night Pain**

- Night pain since an operation needs infection excluded however long ago it was.
- Low grade chronic infection can present with pain, stiffness and night pain and little else.
- Night pain with a stable knee and no swelling after reconstruction more often reflects a pain process.
- When to stop investigating night pain with no cause found is a judgement the history cannot settle.

**4.2.14 Inflammatory Pain**

- A knee that returned to a stiff, swollen, morning-worse state after arthroscopy was probably inflamed before it.
- A pattern that developed after an operation raises infection, reactive synovitis or a material reaction.
- Persistent synovitis after multiple operations is a recognised and often unexplained end state.
- The history identifies who needs reactive and infective synovitis distinguished; it cannot distinguish them.

**4.2.15 Referred Pain**

- After surgery, referred causes need revisiting, because they were excluded before and not since.
- A spinal cause arising later will be attributed to the operation by everyone.
- Complex regional pain, neuropathic pain and referred pain are three different things.
- Infrapatellar branch neuroma presents as localised numbness or hypersensitivity, not joint pain.

**4.2.16 Non-contact Injury**

- The mechanism of a re-injury points at what the first operation did not address.
- Failure during the original movement raises rotational control and tunnel position.
- Failure during a trivial movement raises biology, fixation, alignment or meniscal deficiency.
- The two scenarios lead to different operations, so reconstruct the mechanism carefully.

**4.2.17 Contact Injury**

- The original event's history is more useful than the examination performed at presentation.
- Reconstruct the energy, the direction, and whether the knee reduced spontaneously.
- A spontaneously reduced dislocation can look like a ligament injury with a normal radiograph.
- Any peroneal deficit indicates a lateral injury of higher energy than it appears.

**4.2.18 Twisting Injury**

- A reconstructed knee twisting and giving way suggests the rotational component was missed.
- A deficient meniscus alters rotational constraint as well as load.
- In hypermobility, compare against the patient's own other knee and prior function.
- A twisting history with the kneecap moving points at the patella, which referrals often miss.

**4.2.19 Hyperextension Injury**

- A graft that stretched out in a hyperextending knee raises whether the hyperextension was addressed.
- A late posterolateral problem often traces to a hyperextension injury assessed as cruciate alone.
- Correcting hyperextension may be a bony problem rather than a soft tissue one.

**4.2.20 Direct Blow**

- A chronic extensor mechanism injury is far harder than an acute repair.
- The question that catches it is whether the patient can straighten the knee.
- Anterior pain after a blow with normal imaging sometimes has a chondral lesion and sometimes nothing.
- A reproducible symptom at a defined angle with fixed localised tenderness favours looking.

**4.2.21 Dashboard Injury**

- Chronic posterior cruciate deficiency often presents years later with an adapted gait.
- An unassessed posterolateral corner is the common reason a posterior cruciate reconstruction fails.
- A varus thrust changes the operation from a reconstruction to a reconstruction plus realignment.
- Many chronically deficient knees function well, so the mechanical finding is not the indication.

**4.2.22 Patellar Dislocation Event**

- Continued dislocation after a soft tissue procedure usually means the bony cause was not addressed.
- Pain without instability after stabilisation raises over-constraint or chondral injury.
- When the pain began relative to the operation separates those causes.
- Medial instability after a lateral release is iatrogenic and diagnosed almost entirely from the history.

**4.2.23 Football**

- Failure during the original cutting movement raises rotational control and tunnel position.
- Failure during a trivial movement raises graft, fixation, alignment or meniscal deficiency.
- A player who never returned with a stable knee usually has a confidence problem.
- That group is larger than the structural failures.
- The history decides whether the next step is an operation, rehabilitation or a conversation.

**4.2.24 Cricket**

- Establish what rehabilitation was done, not what was prescribed.
- A tendinopathy that never had a progressive loading programme is the commoner presentation.
- Pain that no longer settles between sessions suggests bone stress rather than tendon.
- An old diving injury is often the only clue to a patellofemoral chondral lesion.

**4.2.25 Rugby**

- The chronic presentation is usually a medial or posterolateral component never addressed.
- The original mechanism is more useful than the acute examination, which under-reported laxity.
- A varus thrust traces back to a varus or hyperextension injury assessed as cruciate alone.
- Recurrent instability in a contact athlete may be exposure rather than a surgical failure.

**4.2.26 Skiing**

- A knee stable in daily life and unstable on skis is being loaded by the sport's lever.
- Chronic medial laxity with a reconstructed cruciate is the pattern that stretches grafts.
- Equipment remains modifiable in a way the knee is not.
- Ask whether the rotational component was ever addressed.

**4.2.27 Running**

- A runner unchanged after a structural intervention almost always had an overuse problem.
- A runner who relapsed on returning to the same load has had the diagnosis confirmed.
- The useful next step is usually a loading programme rather than a further procedure.
- The history separates persistent anterior pain better than further imaging.

**4.2.28 Tennis**

- Pain unchanged after arthroscopy was degenerative, and the next step is not another arthroscopy.
- A persisting mechanical symptom raises a retained fragment, a new tear or a chondral lesion.
- A player wanting to return to tennis after arthroplasty needs an explicit conversation.
- Their intended level of play is what makes that conversation concrete.

**4.2.29 Basketball**

- A stable knee that cannot land confidently is a rehabilitation and psychological problem.
- That group is larger than the structural failures.
- Giving way on cutting raises an unaddressed rotational component.
- Anterior pain after a patellar tendon graft is often the harvest site, worse with kneeling.

**4.2.30 Previous Surgery**

- In revision practice the surgical history is the assessment.
- Build a timeline: operation, date, indication, finding, and symptom before and after.
- Repeated operations for the same symptom with brief benefit means the diagnosis was wrong.
- A symptom returning at six weeks, six months and six years has different causes.
- The timeline identifies what cannot be undone, which bounds everything that follows.

**4.2.31 Previous Injections**

- The date of the last intra-articular steroid is frequently missing from a referral letter.
- A low grade infection after an injection can present late and quietly.
- A diagnostic injection answers questions the examination cannot, such as hip against knee.
- Using an injection diagnostically is often quicker than further imaging.

**4.2.32 Previous Physiotherapy**

- Many courses with no progression are not a trial, however many sessions are recorded.
- A structured, progressed, documented programme that failed is a trial.
- The distinction is in the progression rather than the session count.
- In a multiply operated knee a further programme is often the better option.

**4.2.33 Previous Imaging**

- Establish the sequence of imaging against the sequence of operations and symptoms.
- A post-operative report written without the operative detail is frequently misleading.
- The imaging that answers the question is often not the imaging that has been done.
- A referred patient frequently carries several incompatible explanations.

**4.2.34 Previous Rehabilitation**

- The history determines the plan more often than the imaging, and the treatment history most.
- The commonest finding here is that no criteria-based programme was ever delivered.
- Establishing that changes the plan and is usually unwelcome.
- A properly delivered programme that failed is the strongest argument for surgery available.
- No claim in this chapter about which question discriminates best is supported by a figure.

## How to return this

1. Fill the Decision column on any row you have an answer for. Leave the rest blank; a blank row stays outstanding and comes back in the next pack.
2. Put your name in the Reviewer column, and a source in the Source column for a measurement.
3. Send the file back, then run:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --ingest docs/review/packs/chapter-4.2.md

That writes each decision into the page's own register. The style gate then enforces it: a page carrying a recommendation that is not in its register fails POS-001, and a figure that is not in its register fails FIG-002, so a signed chapter cannot drift back to unsigned without the gate saying so.

Amending a sentence or correcting a figure needs the page edited as well as the register. Put the replacement wording in the Decision cell after `AMENDED:` or `CORRECTED:` and it is carried into the register's note field for whoever makes the edit.

## The same pack in two other formats

A spreadsheet, which is easier to fill, and a Word file, which is easier to print:

       python3 tools/review_pack.py --chapter 4.2 --format all

All three carry the same rows and the same references, and `--ingest` reads any of them.
