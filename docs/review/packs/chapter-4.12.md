## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**4.12.1 Valgus Stress Testing**

- The question in referred practice is whether the remaining laxity matters, not whether it exists.
- Persistent laxity with a rotatory pattern means the posteromedial corner did not heal.
- Medial laxity after a cruciate reconstruction is often dismissed and often why the graft stretched.
- Medial opening with varus alignment is a mechanical problem as well as a ligament one.
- Scar after previous medial surgery gives a firm endpoint over a joint that still opens.

**4.12.2 Assessment at 0 Degrees**

- A persistent valgus complaint after treatment usually sits on the posterior oblique ligament.
- A cruciate graft into an uncontrolled medial side takes load it does not resist and stretches.
- A valgus thrust with opening in extension makes alignment the first question.
- Scar can give a firm endpoint that holds on the couch and fails under load.
- A firm endpoint recorded elsewhere is worth repeating, because the knee was often not in extension.

**4.12.3 Assessment at 30 Degrees**

- A residual grade is usually asymptomatic, and the work is deciding whether it explains the complaint.
- A firm endpoint over a lax joint usually means healing in a lengthened position.
- Pain on twisting with a firm endpoint points at the meniscus rather than the ligament.
- Alignment decides whether the medial side is loaded at all.
- After reconstruction, a grade better than the other side is a tight reconstruction, not a success.

**4.12.4 Deep MCL Assessment**

- Meniscal extrusion with an intact meniscus is a meniscotibial problem, not a meniscal one.
- Persistent posteromedial pain after a meniscal repair may be an unaddressed separation.
- A knee reconstructed for valgus laxity alone can stay rotationally lax.
- Early medial compartment wear produces the same joint line tenderness.
- The honest medicolegal statement is that the examination cannot identify this structure.

**4.12.5 POL Assessment**

- It explains the patient whose medial reconstruction worked and whose knee still turns badly.
- The finding is on the anteromedial drawer rather than on any valgus test.
- A corner healed long gives a firm endpoint over a rotationally lax joint.
- Where the extension test and the rotational test disagree after surgery, believe the rotational one.
- In the multiligament knee this is routinely the structure not recorded.

**4.12.6 AMRI Assessment**

- It is found in the knee with a sound cruciate reconstruction that still feels unsafe on turning.
- A stretched graft with this pattern has its explanation rather than a second problem.
- Easy to produce in a hypermobile patient and easy to miss in a muscular one.
- Under anaesthesia the pattern becomes readable where the awake examination failed.
- A lateral extra-articular procedure constrains the rotation the test depends on.

**4.12.7 Medial Meniscus Versus MCL Injury**

- The usual referred error is the ligament blamed when the meniscus was the problem.
- A knee too painful to provoke acutely is where the meniscal tear hides.
- Persisting pain after a normal arthroscopy is often an untested posteromedial corner.
- A meniscal root avulsion gives medial pain with a normal valgus examination and rapid wear.
- In the chronic knee the question becomes whether the compartment is worth preserving.

**4.12.8 Post-Reconstruction Assessment**

- The treated medial side is usually examined because a cruciate reconstruction failed.
- Valgus laxity in extension with a rotatory pattern explains a stretched cruciate graft.
- Scar can give a reassuring endpoint over a joint that fails under load.
- An over constrained reconstruction presents as medial pain and lost flexion, not instability.
- A reconstruction in an uncorrected valgus limb will stretch, which is an examination failure.
