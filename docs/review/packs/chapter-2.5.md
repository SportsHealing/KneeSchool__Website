## Claims to confirm

The deepest tier's key learning points on each page, which are the chapter's substantive claims. Every other statement in the body needs the same check; these are where to start.

**2.5.1 Gross Anatomy**

- Repairability is decided by the peripheral relations: capsule, coronary ligament and vascular plexus.
- Meniscal function is an all or nothing property of the hoop, not a graded property of the tissue.
- Root repair restores the mechanism; debriding a tear does not.
- In chronic cruciate deficiency the posterior horn is doing a job it was not designed for.

**2.5.2 Histology**

- The meniscus is one end of a continuous fibrous system, not a free body.
- Any repair must restore circumferential fibre continuity; restoring bulk does not restore function.
- Repairability is a histological judgement made at arthroscopy.
- Biological augmentation acts on the cellularity gradient, so it affects healing rate rather than mechanics.

**2.5.3 Vascular Supply**

- Repairability is a distance from the peripheral border, not a position relative to a landmark.
- Previous peripheral surgery can narrow the zone, so judge the tissue rather than the geometry.
- A red zone failure is mechanical; a white zone failure is biological.
- Document the zone and the distance in the operation note for whoever revises the knee.

**2.5.4 Biomechanics**

- A cushion can be trimmed; a tensile ring cannot be cut. The cushion model predicts the wrong things.
- In a cruciate deficient knee the medial posterior horn is doing a job badly and wearing out.
- An extruded meniscus is mechanically closer to absent than to intact.
- No meniscal procedure corrects malalignment, which is why osteotomy is considered alongside.

**2.5.5 MRI Anatomy**

- The imaging question before meniscal surgery is where and how peripheral, not whether torn.
- Imaging cannot reliably separate a healed repair from a recurrent tear.
- Assess extrusion, root integrity, rim width and tear location before arthroscopy.
- Intrasubstance change is a property of an ageing meniscus rather than a lesion.

**2.5.6 Surgical Anatomy**

- Releasing the medial collateral for access is a small reversible injury that can save a meniscus.
- The ramp lesion sits in the posteromedial recess and is missed from the standard anterior view.
- Finding it needs a transnotch view or a posteromedial portal.
- In a revised posteromedial corner the saphenous structures may be tethered to the capsule.

**2.5.7 Root Attachments**

- Restoring the hoop helps a joint that still has a surface to protect.
- In the varus knee the root and the alignment are one problem.
- The posterior intercondylar area holds both roots and the posterior cruciate, so trajectories are planned together.
- An iatrogenic anterior root injury can be repaired if recognised at the time, and usually not later.
